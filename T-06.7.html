Assessing a new client · T-06.7

If she has told you she wants to build, build

She has already said what she wants. Get on with it. Do not offer a recommendation she did not ask for.

If she has clearly stated she does not want to diet, telling her you would cut first is not advice. It is pushback. She will read it that way, and she will be right to.

This rule governs every client, not only the one who has ruled a cut out. A client who says nothing either way does not get a cut recommendation. Saying nothing is not an invitation.

A recommendation is appropriate in three situations and no others.

  • She asks for one.
  • She has questions about the process or about the road ahead.
  • Her goal genuinely needs more than one phase. "Build muscle and get lean" contains both, so laying out the order is coaching rather than overriding.

    • Outside those three it is your preference, and she did not ask for it.

    Say it like this

    Perfect. We're building. We'll prime first so I can see how you move and how you recover, then we get into it properly.

    Nothing about body fat. She did not ask.

    Why it works. She came to us knowing what she wanted. Meeting that with a counter-proposal tells her that what she wants was the opening position in a negotiation. Nothing you gain from making the point is worth what it costs you in the first month.

    Never say this

    "Happy to build, though I do think a cut first would serve you better." That sentence undoes the whole rule. You have technically honoured her goal while making sure she knows you disagree with it. That is worse than either honouring it properly or arguing openly.
