Setup

Set her up in this order

Pulleys as close together as possible, without the hands or cables colliding at the end of the lowering phase (eccentric). Stand slightly back from the crossover so the cables do not foul each other at the start.

Grab the cable end fittings directly. Do not use handles. It means the fitting on the end of the cable, gripped in the hand. Not a handle, and not a wrist cuff.

A high-to-low line of pull, with the exact height set to the individual. High-to-low is comfortable. It matches the natural fibre path and it stops the upper traps shrugging or pulling the blades back hard (retraction).

Stand tall, torso upright, rib cage stacked over the pelvis. She will want to lean forward. Push her back.

If you use a pad, it is a reference, not a brace. A flat bench placed in front of her is there so she can feel whether the chest has come off it. The loads here are light, so the chest pad is not something she pushes into (counterforce).

Say which kind of pad is in play

On a rear delt fly the pad is a reference. She feels whether her chest has left it.

On a chest press the back pad is a counterforce. She pushes into it, and adding it raises the load.

Whenever a pad appears in a setup, say which of the two it is.

Terms on this page

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.

retraction: The shoulder blade sliding back toward the spine, so the two blades pinch together.

counterforce: A pad or support she can push into, so that the load she moves can go up without her losing position.
