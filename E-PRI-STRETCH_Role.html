What it's for

A stretch is step one of three. It is never the whole intervention. STS uses the same framework for the front of the hip, the ankle and the shoulder. That is what makes it a framework rather than a one-off.

The three steps

Passive work. A stretch or a release restores access to the range.

Active drill. She moves into the range under her own control.

Load. Full-range loaded patterning owns the range. This is the point.

What step one buys. It clears the extra nervous-system tension she carries from a lack of movement. She can then reach a range in the warm-up that she could not reach walking in. The range is reinforced with a better pattern and some load afterwards.

How it works. Sensors in the muscle and tendon respond to the stretch (mechanoreceptors). The tendon's sensor (Golgi tendon organ) and the muscle's stretch sensors (muscle spindles) send the signal for the muscle to relax generally.

The ceiling, stated plainly. Do not expect a long-term change from stretching unless she stretches for tens of minutes a day. Stretching is not bad. It is oversold as commonly applied.

The named failure is getting stuck at step one

Manual therapy, stretching and drilling can each quietly become the whole intervention. The layer exists to make itself redundant. Step three is the point.

Tightness is often a perception rather than a length problem. A pelvis tipped forward (anterior pelvic tilt) leaves the hamstrings already lengthened and still reported as tight. Stretching them is unlikely to change anything.

The IT band cannot be stretched (IT band). It is connective tissue doing the bidding of three hip muscles. Address those instead. Soft tissue work explains.

Terms on this page

mechanoreceptors: Sensors in muscle, tendon and skin that respond to pressure and stretch. Stimulating them changes what the nervous system lets the muscle do.

Golgi tendon organ: A sensor in the tendon that reads how hard the muscle is pulling. When it fires strongly, the muscle is told to relax.

muscle spindles: Sensors inside the muscle that report how far and how fast it is stretching.

anterior pelvic tilt: The pelvis tipped forward, so the lower back arches more.

IT band: The long strip of connective tissue down the outside of the thigh, from the hip to below the knee. It is not a muscle, so it cannot be stretched or released.
