Who gets a deficit pull, and how high

The client who gets a deficit pull rounds through her lower back from weak erectors, and she is in a peaking cycle. She is chosen because the deficit pull is closest to the lift itself (specificity), and the deficit is set at 1–2 inches.

Picture the lifter who loses back tightness in a deadlift and rounds through the lower back. Her abs and the muscles down the sides of her trunk (obliques) are strong. Her back muscles (erectors) are weak, and she is peaking for a competition. Three options work the lacking area.

Three options, one winner

# Back extensions

sub: Isolate the erectors

Why not first: They work the muscle alone, away from the movement where it fails.

# Good mornings

sub: A rigid torso through a plane of hip flexion

Why not first: More specific than a back extension, because the torso has to stay rigid while the hips bend. Beginners almost always butcher good mornings, which is why the Romanian deadlift sits above it as STS's hinge.

# Deficit deadlifts

sub: A rigid torso through an extended range of the exact movement where the breakdown occurs

Why first: Performed with a weight light enough to prevent the technical breakdown, they train the position in the lift itself. Chosen on specificity, not on preference.

Load it light enough that technique does not break. That condition is part of the prescription, not an aside.

How high, and why. The purpose of a deficit is to get her to hold the starting position longer. So the deficit must not be so high that it changes the mechanics of the movement. 1–2 inches is fine. Performed with a weight where she can hold correct technique through the movement, it carries over well to deadlifts from the floor.

When should I program a deficit deadlift? Four answers

In the first block of a peaking cycle. Peaking for a meet names it there.

Never for a deadlift that hurts off the floor. Pull from blocks and progress down instead. A deficit lengthens the range at the position that already hurts.

When the goal is time in the start position. That is its stated purpose. Expect carryover to floor pulls only while her technique holds.

Set the deficit at 1–2 inches. Higher changes the mechanics, which defeats the purpose.

Sets, reps and load are yours to set. Outside the rehabilitation protocol on The deficit pull, take them from Volume and Effort and intensity, and record them as your own decision. Her training age and readiness are your judgement too. Write the reasoning into the program.

Terms on this page

obliques: The abdominal muscles down each side of the trunk. They twist and side-bend the trunk, and they resist both.

specificity: The closer the training is to the quality or lift she wants to improve, the more it carries over. It is how closely a training movement matches the lift you want to improve.

erectors: The long muscles either side of the spine that hold the back straight and extend it.
