A goal that looks like someone else's gets an open-ended question

Naming a goal that looks like someone else's uses the same tool as any other hard opening. An open-ended question, not an accusation. This is a judgement call.

What belongs here. Whether the goal is actually hers to assess belongs to the intake work in Assessing a new client. What belongs here is how to raise the suspicion once it exists.

Ask around the goal rather than at it. Something closer to whether this is the outcome she actually wants, rather than a direct claim that it belongs to somebody else.

Asking around the goal

Before we build for this, I want to check something. If nobody else ever saw the result, is this still what you'd want?

Why it works. An open-ended question makes her supply the observation. A verdict makes her defend a goal she may not even have chosen. If the goal is genuinely hers, the question costs nothing. If it is not, she is the one who says so, which is a stronger foundation for changing course than being told.

Watch for

"That sounds like your partner's goal." True or not, she will defend it now. Ask instead.
