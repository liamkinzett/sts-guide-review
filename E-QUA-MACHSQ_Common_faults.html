Common faults

These faults were found on the hack and the pendulum squat. The Smith machine squat has no back pad, so the butt-wink row does not apply to it.

Six common faults

# Knee travelling inside the foot (knee valgus)

sub: The knees shift inward

Why it happens: The standard is knees tracking slightly outward, following the toes. Past a small band inward, the hip has stopped steering the knee.

Do this: Judge it against the standard. Cue full foot contact and knees forward, and watch whether it changes. Cueing explains why "knees out" is not the instruction.

# Butt wink (butt wink)

sub: The pelvis rounds off the back pad. Hack and pendulum only

Why it happens: She has gone past the depth her hips own for that stance, so the pelvis leaves the pad to find range.

Do this: Stop above it. On a back-supported machine this is a fault every time. The pelvis leaving the pad means the lower back loses its support while the machine keeps driving load through it.

# Tension dumped at an end range

sub: Locking out at the top

Why it happens: She rests at the top between reps.

Do this: Stop short of lockout.

# Momentum at the turnaround

sub: Dive-bombing when fatigued

Why it happens: The control has gone before the reps have.

Do this: End the set.

# Weight into the heels

sub: Feet set too high on the plate, driving the shin backwards (negative shin angle)

Why it happens: High feet feel stronger because the hips take over, but the movement stops being a quad exercise.

Do this: Lower the feet.

# Base too wide or too long

sub: A narrow stance on a hack squat. The lost depth is the consequence, not the fault

Why it happens: She has copied a narrow stance without the ankle range to squat deep in it.

Do this: Widen the stance.

Terms on this page

knee valgus: Her knees fall inwards, inside the line of her toes, as she squats or stands up.

butt wink: At the bottom of the squat her pelvis tucks under and her lower back rounds. It usually shows up when she runs out of hip range or loses her brace.

negative shin angle: Her front shin leans back, so the knee sits behind the ankle rather than out over the toes.
