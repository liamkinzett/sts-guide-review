A passive hold never earns a place in the prep slot

A passive held stretch is out of the prep slot at any duration. The inclusion test decides what goes in the slot. The sixty-second threshold only decides what will not cost her the session.

Two rules were being read as if either could admit a movement. They answer different questions.

Compared

columns: The inclusion test | The sixty-second threshold

The question it answers: Does this earn a place in the warm-up? | Will this cost her the session?

The answer: Yes if it is dynamic, with real intent | No, if the hold is under sixty seconds

What it decides: What goes in the slot | What she may do without being weaker for it

Passing the threshold means a stretch will not hurt her. It never meant the stretch belongs in the prep slot. A thirty-second hold had been admitted on that basis. That is a ceiling being read as an admission ticket.

The one item this looks like it removes is not a passive hold. A PNF stretch with a contract-relax has an active contraction and real intent. It satisfies the inclusion test rather than failing it. The test is the contraction, not the clock. It stays.

Run every PNF with an explicit contract-relax

Five seconds of pushing into the stretch, then release. That makes the label true and keeps it inside the threshold either way.

Never run it as a silent hold of 30 seconds. Without the contraction it is the passive hold this rule expelled, kept in on the strength of its name.

What is out either way: any passive hold, at any duration, in this slot.

A note on the roller. Arching the upper back over a foam roller (thoracic extension) is a mobilisation and it stays. A mobilisation is a movement that takes a joint through its range with intent. That is not the same as foam rolling as tissue work, which is not prep work.

Anything loaded in a deep stretch gets prep for that position wherever it sits in the session. A seated hamstring curl late in a leg session has a warm muscle and a hamstring that has not been loaded at length all day.

Terms on this page

PNF: A stretch where she takes the joint to end range, pushes against resistance for a few seconds, then relaxes into the new range. The contraction is what makes it PNF.

prep work: The movements inside the warm-up slot that get the first loaded movement ready. Chosen for the session in front of you.

thoracic extension: Straightening the upper back so the chest comes up and the rounding between the shoulder blades disappears. Over a foam roller it is a standard prep movement for a hinge or a pull.
