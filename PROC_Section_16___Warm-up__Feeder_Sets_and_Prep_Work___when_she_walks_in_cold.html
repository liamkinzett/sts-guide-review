From the door to the first working set

This page is the whole topic in the order a session uses it. Nothing here is new: it runs from the moment she walks in to her first working set.

Priming is a different thing

Priming is a phase. It is the readiness block a new client runs for weeks. The phases owns its length.

The warm-up is a slot. It sits at the top of every session, before the first working set. Building a session places it and this topic fills it.

The five moves, in order

Test. Three facts decide the whole slot before she touches anything.

Read. The ramp is a test of the day as well as preparation for it.

Do. Prep work, then the ramp, then on the strength path one clean single.

Change. What moves in the session around this slot, and what does not.

If she has come straight from something active, the joint circles come out. The point is joints that have not moved, not a ritual.

Read. The ramp is itself the second test. Preparation is only one of the warm-up's two jobs. The other is telling you what kind of day it is before you find out the expensive way. A feeder that felt wrong is data rather than a nuisance. A joint that objects during the ramp has answered a question you would otherwise have asked with the working weight.

Do. In this order.

What she does before the first working set

No general warm-up. No ten minutes on a bike or treadmill. Test day is the only exception.

Two to three minutes of joint circles, at the joints this session will load.

The prep menu for the first movement, dynamic and with real intent. A passive hold earns no place at any duration. A PNF stretch stays only with an explicit contract-relax.

The ramp. The ramp table owns the percentages. It differs three ways: a full ramp above the heavy threshold, one feeder for mid-range work, none for late isolation on a muscle already worked.

Feeder reps: three to eight. One to eight on squat, bench and deadlift, trending down as the load climbs.

On the strength path only, one clean single at roughly 90% of the day's top set. The strength path means a client training for squat, bench and deadlift numbers. Ninety percent of the day's top, not of her one rep max. Then the working sets.

Change. What moves in the session around this slot.

  • A feeder producing fatigue has too many reps in it, whatever the table says. The ramp never costs reps from the first working set. A feeder at 90% of the working load is a working set that has been mislabelled.
  • A movement paused in the stretch, or built to load a muscle at its longest, moves down the order, whatever the prep covered. That longest point is the muscle's lengthened position (lengthened position). Its prep runs wherever it lands. Anything loaded in a deep stretch gets one feeder wherever it sits in the session.
  • A grinding potentiator changes the day's numbers. The single has to be clean and fast. If she has to fight it, the working weight is already wrong. A client training for muscle rather than strength never uses one.
  • The ramp says it is a bad day and nothing hurts. Adjust the session rather than push on. How far you adjust is your call, made on what the ramp showed you.

    • What does not change is what she does on her own. A client who likes her walk keeps it. A client who finds foam rolling useful is not doing herself harm and does not need talking out of it. A static stretch held under sixty seconds costs her nothing. None of that admits any of it into the prep slot. Only the inclusion test does that: dynamic, with real intent.

    Terms on this page

    feeder set: A light set done before the working sets to rehearse the movement and find the day's load. It is never counted as volume.

    prep work: The movements inside the warm-up slot that get the first loaded movement ready. Chosen for the session in front of you.

    PNF: A stretch where she takes the joint to end range, pushes against resistance for a few seconds, then relaxes into the new range. The contraction is what makes it PNF.

    potentiator: A single heavy, clean rep done before the working sets so the working sets move better. It wakes the nervous system up without tiring her.

    lengthened position: The point in the rep where the working muscle is at its longest while it carries the load. It describes the muscle's length, not whether she feels a stretch.
