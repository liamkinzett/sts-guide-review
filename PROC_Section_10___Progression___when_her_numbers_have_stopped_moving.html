What to do when her numbers have stopped moving

This page is the whole topic in the order you use it. The situation is a logbook: a lift that has not moved, and a coach deciding what to change. Most weeks the correct change is nothing. The steps below are why.

The five moves, in order

Test. Open the logbook before you open the program.

Read. A number standing still is information, not a fault.

Do. Diagnose first. The levers come last, on purpose.

Change. One lever. The rest of the program holds still.

Escalate. Know what this topic does not cover.

Test. Open the logbook before you open the program.

Look or ask

Two consecutive flat exposures on this lift, inside the same block? That is the trigger to diagnose. One flat session is a bad night. Count exposures, never weeks.

Did anything around the lift change? Its slot in the session, the work before it, the block itself. Across a block boundary you should expect nothing in particular.

Flat by the band, or flat by tonnage? The band at the load is the judge. A heavier load for fewer reps inside the band is progress. Every metric that multiplies weight by reps scores it as a loss.

How hard is she actually training it? Effort is checked before the program is touched. Effort and intensity owns the check.

What phase is she in? A deficit makes retention the job. A deload lowers volume and holds the load. After a deload she resumes exactly where she left off, at pre-deload loads and the block's ongoing effort, with no ramp back in.

Read. A number standing still or going down is information. Read it against the list of causes on the decline page. Life, under-fuelling, a deficit, a deload or phase change, a context reset, correct coaching, genuine regression.

More than one at once is normal. Only the last is a problem. It is a working read, never a diagnosis, and never a name given to the client.

Read her training age too. An advanced lifter adds across blocks. She sometimes holds a number for a month while something else improves. That is her classification being correct, not a broken program.

Do. In order. The levers come last on purpose.

What to do

Work the stall order first: effort, then technique, then recovery, then volume. Volume owns that order. Three of the four are free to fix. For a beginner the order inverts and execution comes first. The most common correct answer to a single stalled exercise is that she is not training it hard enough. Effort and intensity has that answer.

Several movements stalled together, with recovery markers to match, is a deload question. The phases owns it.

Only when the diagnosis lands on progression itself, pull the cheapest lever first. On the hypertrophy path: reps within the band, then load when her working sets reach the top of it, then a set. The levers grow the same muscle and cost different amounts.

Load is earned, not triggered. The top of the band makes her eligible. The current weight moving with quality makes her ready.

A set is the last lever, and a response rather than a plan. It is never written into week four in advance. It spends room inside her weekly band and her per-session default.

On the strength path the order inverts. Load comes first there.

Change. What moves and what holds still.

Compared

columns: Moves | Holds still

The lever: The one lever the diagnosis called for | The rest of the program. Rebuilding a working advanced program off a slow logbook is the most common way one gets ruined

The read: The diagnosis, if the lift stays flat for two more exposures | The comparison window. Judge the lift inside its block only

The metric: Nothing | The band stays the judge. Tonnage stays out of it

Two things this page does not print. No rate of expected progress is given anywhere, on purpose. Her own history in the current block is the comparison. And sometimes the smallest jump she has access to overshoots the band, which is ordinary on dumbbells. Then the answer is a bridging lever, not the jump. The page on bridging a plate jump has the menu.

Escalate. What this topic does not cover.

  • Pain anywhere in the story goes to Pain rules and when to refer. It is not a progression problem.
  • An under-fuelling read belongs to The female body. The logbook is the only place it shows, and hormonal contraception hides the visible sign for over half the roster. What she then eats is the Nutrition Manual's job.
  • Where the cause sits in her life rather than her training, that is the Life row on the decline page. Where it stops being a coaching conversation at all, The coaching relationship owns the line.
