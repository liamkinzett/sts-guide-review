Cues

Cues go in the order printed. Try the first, and only move on when it fails.

Cues

When: The landmine press, in the session

Say: Tall as you can. Tall as you can.

Why: External cue. Start here. It fixes the lean and the rib flare together.

When: Any delt or press variation, setting the rib cage

Say: Slight forward reach.

Why: External cue. The serratus cue, used on every delt variation. The reach buys a stable rib cage position through the obliques.

When: The blades ride up at the end of the range

Say: Keep those scaps down. Cue more depression than retraction.

Why: Internal cue. End range of retraction becomes elevation. On paper that reads as a detail. She feels it immediately.

When: The whole press needs organising

Say: spine tall, don't overly flare, don't overly press, keep those scaps more pinned down.

Why: Internal cue. The full pressing string.

Where the four above do not land, these are also used. "Top of the head to the ceiling" (external). "Pulling your shoulder blades into your pockets" (external). "Posterior expansion" (internal). "Driving your elbows down toward your pockets" (external). Cueing explains why an external cue is tried before an internal one.
