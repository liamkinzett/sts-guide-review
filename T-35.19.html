Pain rules and when to refer · T-35.19

Reading a label: hers or a clinician's

Nothing diagnostic is ever taken from a label, whether it arrives as her own word or as six pages from a clinician. Not a stage, not a grade, not a prognosis. What you extract is behavioural.

The same four moves, whatever the label

Take nothing diagnostic from it. "I have arthritis in my hands" and a six-page report are read the same way.

Extract the behaviour. Which movements are restricted, which are cleared, and what she reports about each.

Feed it into the bucket sort. A report that says she is cleared answers the medical question and nothing about the loading one. The loading question runs on the buckets, the same as for a client with no letter at all.

Take the full history the same way, regardless of what she calls it.

Where a referral row exists for the joint named, it governs the referral threshold. The osteoarthritis row sends late-stage, severe mechanical symptoms to a GP or sports physician. A hand has no regional page, and you do not invent a test to fill that in.

A pattern with no label at all gets no new screen invented for it either. Easy bruising, joints that "always hurt", no diagnosis offered or sought. The nearest case is the hypermobile client in Mobility and stretching: no diagnosis made, no test added. The response is the same, strength built through whatever range she already owns. The referral triggers, a joint giving way, night pain, locking, still set the bar. Nothing about an unlabelled presentation lowers it or raises it.
