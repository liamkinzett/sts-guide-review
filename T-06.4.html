Assessing a new client · T-06.4

Everyone primes first, for at least four weeks

Every client starts with a readiness block. We call it priming (priming). No client goes straight into a hypertrophy, strength or fat-loss block. Priming comes first and runs a minimum of four weeks.

Key figure

value: 4 weeks

unit: minimum

note: The floor, not the length. The phases sets how long it actually runs.

This rule decides who gets one. The phases decides how long it runs and what goes in it.

"Everyone" means everyone at intake. That includes a client who has trained for years, and a former client coming back to us. She still primes. If she is genuinely proficient, she clears the gate at the minimum.

The client you inherit mid-phase is the one look-alike, and Inheriting a client governs her. She is not re-primed. Her first month is reading rather than rewriting.

Pulling her back into a priming block would cost her a month, and cost the handover the trust it can least afford.

Why it works. Priming is a readiness gate, not a warm-up block. It establishes technical competence. It tells you how she recovers. And it gives you the observation window that turns your intake guesses into something you actually know.

End it later if she is still learning the patterns, still inconsistent, or still giving you a picture you cannot read. Four weeks is the floor, not the length.

Watch for

Priming a client who arrives technically proficient and treating the four week minimum as time to fill. The gate is about readiness, not attendance. If she clears it at the minimum, move on.

Terms on this page

priming: The opening phase of a client's program, where she learns the lifts and earns her positions before the load is pushed. It is the first block every new client runs, a minimum of four weeks, and it shows you how she moves and recovers and gates what comes next.
