Shoulders · E-SHO-REARDELT#Role

What it's for

The rear delt fly is isolation for the rear delt, which runs from the shoulder blade to the upper arm bone. That attachment pair governs every standard on these pages. If the shoulder blade travels with the arm, origin and insertion move together. The muscle is not loaded.

Why the arm path matters more than the machine. The rear delt fibres do not run on one fixed plane. Their direction changes with where the upper arm is placed. So the arm path, not a fixed machine angle, decides what gets loaded.

That is also why cable height is set per lifter and never to a number. A higher rear-delt origin needs a higher pulley. A lower attachment needs it lower. It is the reason STS prefers a cable to a fixed machine here: the line of pull can be made to match her fibres.

The slot. The rear delt is one of the "odds and ends", the stubborn or underloved groups worked into a session rather than given a headline slot. Forearms, calves, traps and abs sit in the same class.

The rear delt fly also appears in a warm-up slot, where it confirms she can hold core and shoulder position. It is the entry point for upper-body rib-cage work with the ribs held still. Keep it to the shoulder blade sliding forward and back (protraction) before anything more dynamic.

Effort. The rear delt fly is one of the few movements myo-reps are allowed on: local fatigue is high and the systemic cost is near zero. Rest and intensity techniques holds the rule.

The three versions STS programs

# Single arm cable rear delt fly

sub: The default, and the one that stayed in the worked case of a provoked shoulder

Why: One arm lets her match the line of pull to her own fibre direction and brace the other hand.

# Dual cable rear delt fly

sub: The same standards with both arms at once

Why: Nothing about the rep changes. Pulleys as close together as possible, cable ends in the hands, high-to-low line.

# Reverse pec deck

sub: The machine option when there is no crossover

Why: It fixes one angle for everyone, which is why the cable is preferred. Take it when the cable is not there.

Terms on this page

protraction: The shoulder blade sliding forward and out around the rib cage, away from the spine.
