Rest and intensity techniques · T-14.13

1.5 reps and quarter reps stay out

The one-and-a-quarter rep is the technique clients arrive already believing in. So-called 1.5 reps and quarter reps stay out. More reps with heavier weight does the same job better.

What the extra part-rep actually does. It keeps people on the hook. It does increase metabolic stress, but not to the point where it makes a difference. Its real function is sensation and engagement rather than stimulus.

The alternative is not a technique at all. The 1.5 rep is usually prescribed in a 10 to 12 rep range. In that range, just do more reps with heavier weight. That buys the same metabolic stress plus more mechanical tension, with better movement control.

Why the technique fails in practice. The half rep she performs is rarely a controlled half rep. It becomes a bounce, using elastic momentum to get in and out of the bottom position. Control around the quarter rep is almost non-existent. That defeats the technique's own reason for existing, which was time at long muscle length.

The one form of extra bottom-range work that is allowed. A block of reps in the stretched part of the range (lengthened partials), after she has failed at full range. Only on an exercise that actually reaches the lengthened position. That is a post-failure block on a lengthened-biased exercise, not interleaved 1.5s.

It is still an intensifier. It goes in only when you can say what it buys, and only on the final working set.

And no intensifier goes in until she has learned to take a straight set to the prescribed effort. Effort and intensity is that subject.

Watch for

The half rep that is really a bounce. On video it shows as an elastic rebound out of the bottom. A real half rep is controlled and held to the same standard as the full ones.

Terms on this page

lengthened partials: Reps performed only in the stretched part of the range. The one form we allow is a block of them after full-range failure, on an exercise that reaches the lengthened position.
