Four faults with a twist that changes what you do

Four named faults carry a qualifier. Each one changes what you do about it. None of them is a new fault.

Four qualifiers

# Rib flare has an opposite, and half a roster has it

sub: Check which one she has before cueing either

What changes: A client whose sternum sits down needs "chest up". A client who is overly flared needs "chest down a little". A team that names only the flare will produce the stuck-down fault in the other half of its roster. Cueing chest-up at a flared client makes it worse, and so does chest-down at a stuck-down one.

# Lumbar finish on an overhead press is usually a shoulder fault

sub: Screening it as a bracing problem sends you to the wrong joint

What changes: The shoulder blade will not rotate upward enough to get the arm overhead, and the lower back supplies the rest (lumbar finish). Train the shoulder. It is the clearest example of a fault appearing where it is not caused.

# Shruggie is a range signal as well as a form fault

sub: Stop the rep there

What changes: When she gets shruggy on her pressing or her flyes (shruggie), she has probably run out of the range where she adds real tension. Read as a form fault alone, it gets cued. Read as a range signal, it ends the set at the right place.

# Grip width is the upper-body version of base width

sub: The same reasoning covers both

What changes: What "base too wide or too long" does for a stance, grip width does for a press or a pull. One fault, both ends of the body.

Terms on this page

lumbar finish: Finishing a hip movement by arching the lower back instead of extending the hip.

shruggie: Her shoulders climb toward her ears mid-rep, so the upper traps take over work they were not given. It shows up during a press, raise or fly.
