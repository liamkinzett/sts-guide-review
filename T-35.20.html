Where the answer is already refer, not judge

Six places where the call has already been made: the answer is refer, not assess. None of these is a screen you run to decide whether the hold applies. You check whether her presentation matches a row.

Red flags

title: Hard stops already decided. No deviation

Spine, with known bone loss in the spine (vertebral osteoporosis) or an exaggerated upper-back curve (kyphosis): no loaded forward flexion, no rapid explosive spinal twisting

Tendon: the stage decides the intervention, and a coach never names the stage

Energy availability: persistent absent periods and the other signs of low energy availability. A medical hold, not a training decision

Pelvic floor: internal examination, grading strength, diagnosing prolapse. Never a coach's

Pregnancy: the full stop-the-session list

Eating disorder: you do not treat it. Refer, to a psychologist or a specialised dietitian. Noticing the signs is not diagnosing

A coach reading a client against this table is matching, not deciding. She checks whether the presentation matches one of these rows. She does not decide from first principles whether it should.

This table is not exhaustive, by design. It collects the hard stops that are firm and locked. A presentation that fits none of these rows is still triaged. Missing row is not missing risk.

Terms on this page

vertebral osteoporosis: The bones of the spine have lost density and can fracture under loads a healthy spine would tolerate. Loaded forward bending and fast twisting are off the table.

kyphosis: An exaggerated forward rounding of the upper back. Alongside known bone loss it makes loaded forward bending and fast twisting a hard stop.
