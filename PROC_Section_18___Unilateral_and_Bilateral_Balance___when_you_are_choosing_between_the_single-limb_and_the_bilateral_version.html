Choosing between the single-leg and the two-legged version

This page is the whole topic in the order you use it at the moment of selection. Nothing here is new.

The five moves, in order

Test. Ask what the single-leg version buys that the two-legged one cannot.

Read. Adaptations are the same either way. Judge an asymmetry by function, not looks.

Do. Meet the per-session and per-week requirements.

Change. Know what does not move.

Escalate. Know what belongs to another topic.

Test. One question decides it. What is the single-leg version (unilateral) buying that the two-legged one (bilateral) cannot? Two answers qualify. A position a two-legged stance cannot reach, and loadability when a two-legged pattern has run out of room. Everything else is habit or a bonus. Then check it is actually single-leg. A dumbbell in each hand is bilateral. A movement is unilateral because one limb is doing the work, not because the load is in one hand.

Read.

What you find, and what it means

# You are choosing between the two versions

sub: Neither is better

Read: The adaptations are comparable either way, in trained and untrained lifters. The read is about coverage, not superiority.

# You think you can see an asymmetry

sub: Function is the test, not symmetry

Read: Nobody is symmetrical. A difference that is not changing how she moves, what she can load or what hurts is information, not a problem.

# She has a reconstruction or surgery in her history

sub: Post-injury asymmetry is real, large and lasting

Read: It comes from her history, not the footage. Both legs can look symmetrical while one is doing far less of the work. She is carrying it whether or not anything looks wrong.

Do.

Every program meets these

Every lower session gets at least one unilateral movement. The reason is lengthened glute coverage, not adaptation. The one carve-out is the strength path's realisation weeks (realisation), which are tested lifts only by design.

At least one unilateral pull somewhere in the week. Unilateral pressing is optional.

No more than three unilateral movements in a session. The reason is the clock. Every unilateral set is really two sets.

Teach the pattern with external stability, then load it. The loaded compound is the stability work.

Change. What does not move.

  • There is no correct unilateral percentage. A published proportion becomes a quota. The per-session requirements above do the same job without that failure mode.
  • Depth language does not transfer. "Full" and "parallel" name different knee angles on one leg and on two. The page on knee angles has the definitions.
  • The split squat and lunge are already counted at half glutes and half quads in Volume. This topic does not create a second accounting.
  • When she asks why she is doing split squats, she hears about her glutes, not her core. The extra core demand is a bonus, not the reason.

    • Escalate.

  • A specific post-surgical presentation belongs to Pain rules and when to refer, which owns rehabilitation loading. The post-injury rule here is a principle, not a protocol.
  • A client who cannot hold position goes to the stabiliser fix in Building a session. That is a fix for a specific fault, not a stage every client passes through.
  • Uneven bar travel read as a technique fault belongs to Diagnosing from video.
    • Terms on this page

    unilateral: One limb doing the work, as in a split squat or a single-arm row. Where the load is held does not decide it.

    bilateral: Both limbs working together, as in a two-footed squat or a standard Romanian deadlift.

    realisation: The last four weeks of a strength phase.
