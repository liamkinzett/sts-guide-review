Shoulders · E-SHO-FRONTRAISE#Where tension peaks

Where it's hardest

Across the range

title: Where it's hardest

note: At the top, at shoulder height, with the front delt short (shortened position)

from: Dumbbell in front of the thigh

to: Shoulder height

mark: 92

label: Hardest

Like every free-weight raise, the front raise is heaviest at the top. Every free-weight raise loads the shortened position hardest. The one exception is a raise done lying on her side, and the alternating dumbbell front raise is not that exception. The front delt is at mid-range there (shoulder flexion), 0 to 90 degrees, not at its longest.

Do not read the pause at the top as the reason for that. The pause at shoulder height is the standard for the rep, not a mechanical claim about where tension peaks. The one mechanical statement about the bottom is that letting the dumbbells rest on the thighs dumps the tension. That tells you where tension is lost, not where it peaks.

The slow eccentric is the point. Where the movement is used for shoulder function, loading the delt on the way down (eccentric) is part of the stated target. Tempo and pauses sets how long.

Terms on this page

shortened position: The muscle is working while it is bunched short.

shoulder flexion: The arm travelling forward and up in front of the body, all the way to overhead.

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.
