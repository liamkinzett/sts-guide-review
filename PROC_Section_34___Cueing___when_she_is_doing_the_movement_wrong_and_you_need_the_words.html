What to do when she is doing the movement wrong and you need the words

This page adds nothing. It is this topic's rules in the order you use them, when the video shows a fault and you have to say something.

Two routes out before any cue

Pain of any grade is Pain rules and when to refer, and never a cueing problem.

A fault you cannot yet name goes to Faults and what you can actually see first. Read the video the way Diagnosing from video teaches. Come back here with a name.

Step 1. Test. Two gates come before any cue, and neither is reachable by cueing.

Check

Have you asked "where do you feel this?" as a question, not a confirmation?

Was the position right before the rep started?

Can she physically hold the position at this load?

yes: Awareness or coordination is the gap. Cueing is the right tool. Go to step 3.

no: A setup fault is fixed in the setup. A strength gap is fixed by regressing the exercise. Do that instead of talking.

Step 2. Read. A fault that exists before rep one looks like a cueing problem and is a setup problem. A position that collapses because she lacks the strength to hold it is capacity, not awareness. A strap, a wedge or a different bar height that fixes it in one move outranks any cue. Only when the setup is sound and the position is holdable is cueing the right tool.

Step 3. Build the one cue

Find the fault with the largest downstream effect and cue that. One cue. Two is the ceiling, and the second is generally not another phrase.

Test it by trying to do it wrong. If she can comply completely while nothing you wanted happens, it has already failed.

Make it executable now. As easy as making a fist. If it needs explaining, teach it between sets, in a video or in her notes.

Pick the focus by the job. External for force on this rep. Internal for upper-body muscle growth. No basis to choose for lower-body growth.

If she is biased away from the position, over-cue past it and tell her in advance it will feel wrong.

Check it against the thirteen. Using one of those cues is fine. Using one without knowing how it fails is not.

Step 4. Change. Re-test the thing you cued before deciding anything else. Fixing one fault often cleans up others. You do not know what the second cue should be until you have seen what the first one fixed. A cue that failed is a result: record which cue you tried and what happened, so the next coach knows what has been ruled out. If the movement did not change, the first hypothesis is that you said the wrong thing. Say it differently.

What does not change: the count. Past two cues, stop. An escalating cue list is not a difficult client. It is the wrong exercise.

Step 5. Escalate. Past two cues, walk down the ladder instead of talking more. Constrain the movement so the position is enforced instead of requested, then change the exercise. Choosing exercises owns selection from there, and a capacity failure routes the same way through a regression. Any pain is Pain rules and when to refer. A question that is not a training question is escalated, never improvised.

The words for a named fault live on the exercise's own page, in its cues and its fix column. This topic tells you how to choose between them.
