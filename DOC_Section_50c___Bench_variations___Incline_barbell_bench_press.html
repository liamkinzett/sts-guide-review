Incline barbell bench press

On an incline, what changes with a barbell is the bar rather than the bench. The fixed path, the higher loading ceiling and the locked shoulder blades all carry over from the flat version, and so does the elbow tuck. On a higher incline, tuck the elbows slightly. Then the line of force meets the upper fibres of the chest (clavicular fibres) rather than the front delt.

The angle. Choose and record it exactly as you do on the dumbbell incline, which has its own page in Chest. At a low incline, dumbbell, barbell, machine and Smith are accepted interchangeably. Record the angle as a number so the exercise is repeatable.

Selection. Take the barbell incline where the loading ceiling is the point. That is the same reason the flat barbell sits at the top of the ladder. Take the dumbbell where arm path and range are the point. In the strength system an incline bench press is one of the two options for a competitive powerlifter's second bench exposure of the week.

Two more jobs. An externally supported low-incline press is the constraint for the client whose sternum is stuck down. Gravity supplies the position she cannot find lying flat, until she can hold it herself. And where she cannot reach the overhead position, overhead pressing is gated. Incline and neutral-grip pressing remain available.

Tempo. `2110`, with the pause at the chest.

Terms on this page

clavicular fibres: The upper part of the chest muscle, running from the collarbone to the upper arm. An incline press meets it more directly than a flat press does.
