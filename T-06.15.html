Assessing a new client · T-06.15

When the signals disagree, classify down

When the signals disagree, classify down. Under-classifying costs her a few weeks of slower progress. Over-classifying costs her junk volume, a technique breakdown or an injury.

The errors are not the same size, so do not treat them as if they are.

Compared

columns: Classify too low | Classify too high

What it costs her: A few weeks of slower progress | Junk volume, a technique breakdown or an injury

How you find out: Within weeks, from a logbook that moves too easily | When technique or recovery breaks under a program she was not ready for

What you do: Revise up and carry on | Rebuild, and win back the trust the injury cost

A beginner's program given to an intermediate still moves her forward, and you find out within weeks. An intermediate's program given to a beginner asks her for technique and effort she does not yet have.

What disagreement looks like. Her form says three years of training, but she answers in exercises rather than numbers. She names a split, but cannot recall a recent lift or say what has stalled. Read the row that points lower, and classify there. Priming will show you soon enough if you were wrong, and you revise up.
