When to use it

The problem you notice. Her shoulder is cranky and you need a press she can do today. Or she is advanced and you want her at genuine failure without paying for it at the joint. Or she is fighting the whole stack on a standing cable press with her hips. The machine and cable chest press answer each of these.

Reach for it when

# Her shoulder is cranky and you are entering the pressing ladder at the top rung

sub: The barbell and the dumbbells both provoke it

Confirm it: Ask her to press on the machine. If a machine feels good, do it. If a machine does not feel good, she needs more freedom of motion: a cable press, or a dumbbell incline press.

What it gets her: A press she can still do today, inside the same pattern.

# She is advanced and you want genuine failure without paying for it at the joint

sub: The last rep should be a pec failure, not a balance failure

Confirm it: Her free-weight sets end with position going before the muscle does.

What it gets her: Maximum force into the pecs rather than into holding herself together. A highly braced machine gives more stimulus for the fatigue it costs than a free-weight barbell press.

# She is counterbalancing the whole stack on a standing cable press

sub: Hips shifting, torso bracing, the "Spider-Man" look

Confirm it: Film from the side. Her body is moving as much as the handles.

What it gets her: The pec back as the limiting factor instead of her balance. Sit her against a back pad and the load goes up.

# A flat machine press has stalled and you do not want to change the pattern

sub: Same family, different line of force

Confirm it: Her log shows the same weight for the same reps across the block.

What it gets her: A different line of force inside the same family. Change the seat height, or move to the machine incline chest press.

Fit is the constraint, not effort

Where a fixed path does not match her structure, change the equipment rather than the person. A machine is not automatically the braced case for a short client. If the handles sit wrong for her frame, it is a different exercise.

A kettlebell bottoms-up press is a test, not a treatment. It is a good test of relative shoulder function. For a bugging shoulder, this is not the way to make it feel better.

Setup. Seat height to the part of the chest you want, flush against the pad, ribs stacked, hands squeezed, blades down. Setup has the list.
