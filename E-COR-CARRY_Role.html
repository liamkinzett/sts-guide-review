Core · E-COR-CARRY#Role

What it's for

A carry is a coordination and load-tolerance tool. It is not a core-capacity exercise. It teaches a trunk to accept load. It does not build the capacity to produce force through a range.

The reason is mechanical. Ask of any carry: where is the stretch, where is the shortening, where is the contraction, where is the growth? There is none. The same is true of the whole family of holds (isometric): bird dog, curl-up, side plank, Pallof press, carries. Stop selling carries as core work.

Where each job now sits

# Building core capacity

sub: Not here

Where: Loaded flexion and extension through a range, on the trunk flexion page. Capacity is built from insertion to origin.

# Teaching load acceptance

sub: Here

Where: The carry. For a novice it comes before the Roman chair, for exactly this reason.

# Getting off the plank

sub: A sideways step, not the next rung up

Where: The plank's exit is a loadable exercise. Nobody should progress to holding a plank for 10 minutes. The carry is the other way out. Pick it when the demand you want is holding the rib cage and pelvis organised while walking under load, not while still.

So the client who gets a carry has already earned it. She can already hold a position; the plank is the entry point and the carry is one of the things the plank is for. A beginner looking for core work belongs on the plank first.

The lats are part of this and are routinely left out. In deadlifts, squats and heavy carries the lats help stabilise the trunk. Most coaches think of the core as the rectus abdominis, the transverse abdominis and the obliques, and never count the lats.

Where a carry appears

# Slot six of the strength system's lower session

sub: Core, or a carry

Where: The suitcase carry is the strength system's stated favourite for the trunk slot. See The strength system.

# A rung in a pain ladder

sub: Mild rather than severe referred pain

Where: Floor work, then a front-loaded carry with a gentle flexion moment to resist, then a staggered-stance RDL, then onward. The rung is chosen by how bad her symptoms are and traded out as soon as it stops being a stimulus she adapts to. Running that ladder is a decision for Pain rules and when to refer, not for this page.

# The first rung of a pressing re-entry

sub: Pressing provokes the shoulder and the pattern has to be rebuilt

Where: A loaded farmer's carry, palm turned up and the shoulder turned outward, is the first rung. A neutral-grip dumbbell floor press follows it, and the press angles climb from there. Pain rules and when to refer decides whether she is on the ladder at all.

Where the carry stands

A carry has no place of its own among STS's exercises yet, so it is not written into a client's program. This page teaches you to coach one for when it appears: in the strength system's lower session, or in a client's existing training.

The strength system's trunk slot reads "core, or a carry", and the suitcase carry is its stated favourite. Until STS programs a carry, fill that slot with core work.

Terms on this page

isometric: A hold. The muscle works against the load but the joint does not move.
