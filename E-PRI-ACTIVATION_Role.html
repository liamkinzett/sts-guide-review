Priming lifts · E-PRI-ACTIVATION#Role

What it's for

Not warmth. Not tissue change. The purpose of prep work is sensation (prep work). Plant a feeling in the warm-up, then demand that she recreate it in the first working exercise. The two are placed close together on purpose.

The construction principle. A warm-up is built from components that lead into the main exercise of the day.

Position rehearsal. A position she struggles with goes into the warm-up or into an early exercise. She can then call back on it later in the session.

The drills and what each one feeds

# Kneeling landmine press

sub: The arm raised forward (shoulder flexion) and turned outward (external rotation), with the shoulder blade rotating, all against a stacked rib cage (stacked rib cage)

What it feeds: The overhead position. It doubles as the warm-up and the first working exercise, which makes it both the prep and the stimulus.

# Scap raises

sub: Captain's chair and hanging

What it feeds: The beginner tier of a pull-up progression. They teach the shoulder blade to pull down, slide forward and turn up before the arm is asked to pull (depression) (protraction) (upward rotation).

# Serratus press

sub: Face down, forearms flat. STS doesn't program it

What it feeds: Activation of the muscle that holds the shoulder blade flat to the ribs (serratus anterior). She pushes the shoulder blades forward to lift the chest off the floor and holds the peak protraction.

The slot. Ahead of the movement the drill feeds. Warming up owns whether the drill goes into the warm-up at all.

The set ends on sensation rather than on a rep count. Once the sensation goes, the set is over. Volume owns the count everywhere else.

Terms on this page

prep work: The movements inside the warm-up slot that get the first loaded movement ready. Chosen for the session in front of you.

stacked rib cage: The rib cage sitting directly over the pelvis, ribs down, neither flared nor slumped.

depression: The shoulder blade pulled down, away from the ear. The opposite of a shrug.

protraction: The shoulder blade sliding forward and out around the rib cage, away from the spine. The opposite of pinching the blades together.

upward rotation: The shoulder blade turning so its outer corner rises and the socket tilts upward. It is what lets the arm travel overhead. A bench press holds it in the opposite position.

serratus anterior: The muscle along the side of the rib cage that pulls the shoulder blade forward and holds it flat to the ribs.

shoulder flexion: The arm travelling forward and up in front of the body, all the way to overhead.

external rotation: Turning a limb outward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face forward or up, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more to the side.
