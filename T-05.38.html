Training while trying to conceive

Trying to conceive changes nothing about her training on its own. What might is the same under-fuelling screen every client gets.

Screen her fuelling and her cycle regularity, the way you do for every client. Do not calculate her energy availability yourself. If her cycle is regular (eumenorrhoeic) and she is adequately fuelled, notice it and move on. If it is not, this is the existing under-fuelling referral, not a new one.

Why under-fuelling matters here. Not eating enough for what she trains (low energy availability) turns down the hormone chain that runs her cycle (HPG axis).

How under-fuelling turns the cycle down

The brain's pulse signal drops.

The two pituitary hormones drop with it.

Oestrogen and progesterone drop in turn.

It shows up three ways. Infrequent periods (oligomenorrhoea). A short or weak second half of the cycle (luteal-phase defect). Or a cycle that has shut down (functional hypothalamic amenorrhoea).

Exercise itself does not impair fertility. The old fear that training causes miscarriage is disproven. A client trying to conceive does not need to reduce training on that basis alone.

One intensity ceiling is worth naming. Repeated bouts at 95% to 100% of her maximal aerobic capacity (VO2max), taken to exhaustion, trigger a stress-hormone surge. That surge measurably drops placental blood flow and raises uterine contractility. That is a mechanism of pregnancy: there is no placenta before conception.

The source still names the same intensity ceiling as relevant before conception. Hold the ceiling there, not the placental explanation. Untrained clients can train up to 70% to 75% VO2max without changing anything for this reason alone. Trained clients can train up to 85% to 90%.

Aerobic intensity, as a share of her VO2max

Untrained clients | 70-75 | Train up to here without changing anything for this reason

Trained clients | 85-90 | Train up to here without changing anything for this reason

Repeated bouts to exhaustion | 95-100 | The stress-hormone surge. Not here, repeatedly

Verdict

An irregular or absent cycle in a client trying to conceive is the under-fuelling referral.

Notice it, do not calculate it. Pain rules and when to refer holds the routing.

Say it like this

If your cycle's regular and you're eating enough for what you're training, nothing needs to change yet. The thing worth watching is training right at the edge repeatedly, not training itself.

Terms on this page

eumenorrhoeic: Having regular, normal periods.

low energy availability: Not enough food coming in to cover both her training and the rest of what her body needs to run.

HPG axis: The hormone chain from the brain to the pituitary to the ovaries that runs her cycle. Under-fuelling turns it down.

oligomenorrhoea: Infrequent periods, with cycles running well past their usual length.

luteal-phase defect: The second half of her cycle is too short or produces too little progesterone to support a pregnancy.

functional hypothalamic amenorrhoea: Her periods stop because the brain has switched off the cycle in response to stress, usually under-fuelling or overtraining. Nothing is structurally wrong.

VO2max: The most oxygen her body can use per minute at all-out effort. It is the standard measure of aerobic capacity.
