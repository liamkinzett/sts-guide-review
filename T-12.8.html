Building a session · T-12.8

Supersets are not a default and never a time-saver

Supersets (superset) are not programmed by default. Logistically they can be a nightmare, so unless one is absolutely necessary, no supersets. When session time is the constraint, cut sets. Never pair exercises to save minutes.

The one case that survives. Where continuous work on a muscle is the mechanism you want, pair two exercises for that muscle. They must have two different resistance profiles (resistance profile). Rest and intensity techniques explains when continuous work is the mechanism. The next page explains how the pair is chosen. The other kind of pairing, opposing muscles (antagonists) such as biceps with triceps, is not used.

Compared

columns: Same muscle, two profiles | Opposing muscles

Use it when: Only where continuous work on that muscle is the mechanism | Not used to save time. Cut sets instead

What it does: Concentrates the stimulus on one muscle, so the whole-body cost stays proportional to what you wanted | More total work in the same minutes. Exactly what you do not want when minutes are not scarce

What it costs: Volume load, the total weight moved across the pair. Each exercise in the pair moves less weight or fewer reps than it would alone. The trade is real, though her growth does not suffer for it | Two muscle groups accumulate fatigue instead of one. You are buying minutes with recovery

Say it like this

These two are paired because they hit the same muscle from different angles. Go straight from one to the other. It's not to save you time, it's to finish the job on that muscle before you move on.

Watch for

A superset written to make a session fit the clock. If the session does not fit, cut accessory sets or move work to another day.

Terms on this page

superset: Two exercises done back to back, with the rest taken after the pair rather than between them.

resistance profile: Where in the range an exercise is hardest and where it is easiest. It is set by the line of the load against the joint, so a dumbbell and a cable can load the same muscle very differently. A dumbbell is the same weight everywhere; a cable on a pulley gets heavier as the stack rises.

antagonists: Muscles that do the opposite job to each other across a joint. Biceps and triceps, chest and back.
