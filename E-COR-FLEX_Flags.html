Watch for

Red flags

title: Never load spinal flexion if she has either of these

Vertebral osteoporosis (vertebral osteoporosis)

Kyphosis (kyphosis), which like osteoporosis sits mostly in the upper back

Pain rules and when to refer decides whether she trains at all.

Take the contraindication first

You will hear that the upper back can be loaded in deep flexion. The claim is that the rib cage gives it the stability the neck and lower back lack. It comes with no caveat about who it applies to. And it collides with the contraindication above, which is itself mostly about the upper back. The contraindication wins.

Red flags

title: Pregnancy and after

Pregnant: refer. The female body governs, and no programming answer lives here.

The one sign you act on: a ridge or dome down the midline of her belly under load (coning). It changes the set, and it is not a referral on its own. You never assess it yourself.

Pelvic floor symptoms, pain at the front of the pelvis (pubic symphysis) or bleeding are a referral, not a modification.

After a baby: refer. Returning to training owns the return. After a caesarean, any deep core or abs work comes out for 12 weeks, and technique is adjusted so nothing pulls on the scar.

When you see coning, the response is graded. Change the tempo. Reduce the range. Change how the movement is done. Abandon the movement only when those have not resolved it. The worry is not that a rep does damage. It is that she wires in a habit she will have to retrain later.

When you see coning mid-set

I want to change how that one looks. Same exercise, slower, with the range shortened a little.

Terms on this page

vertebral osteoporosis: The bones of the spine have lost density and can fracture under loads a healthy spine would tolerate.

kyphosis: An exaggerated forward rounding of the upper back. Loaded spinal flexion is contraindicated with it.

coning: A ridge or dome rising along the midline of the belly when the abdominal wall is loaded. It is a sign the deep wall is not holding. Change the set when you see it.

pubic symphysis: The joint at the very front of the pelvis where the two pubic bones meet.
