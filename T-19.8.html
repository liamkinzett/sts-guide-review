How to read an exercise page · T-19.8

Swaps come in three kinds, and each has a home

A Swaps section answers three situations. The equipment is missing, a joint says no, or she is training at home. Each has its own source, and a page never improvises one.

The three kinds of swap

# Equipment missing

sub: The rack is busy or the gym has no such machine

Where it comes from: The exercise list. Every programmed movement is tagged with the range it loads (lengthened, mid or shortened), how it is stabilised, and its equipment. A swap keeps the first two tags and changes the third.

Do this: Look it up. Same range tag, same stabilisation tag, different equipment. It is a lookup, not a judgement.

# A joint says no

sub: A knee, a hip, a back or a shoulder will not tolerate the movement

Where it comes from: Region by region, which holds a playbook for each region, and Pain rules and when to refer for the general triage.

Do this: Go to the region's page. Never invent an injury swap because the field looked empty. A plausible guess about someone's knee is worse than a blank.

# Home

sub: No gym access

Where it comes from: Nowhere. STS coaches clients who train in equipped gyms, so there is no home ladder to give.

Do this: Say so. The row stays on the page so a coach who looks for it finds the reason rather than a gap.

Why the equipment swap is a lookup. The range tag and the stabilisation tag describe what a movement does to her. The range tag says which muscle length it loads (lengthened position). The stabilisation tag says whether the machine holds her position (externally stabilised) or she does (internally stabilised). Two movements sharing both tags do the same job. Changing only the equipment keeps the job and changes the tool.

Watch for

A travelling client is not a home client. She still trains in a gym, just not hers. The home rule does not answer her question. Training around life is where it goes.

An injury swap invented on the page. Point at Region by region for every region, the knee and the wrist included.

Terms on this page

lengthened position: The point in the rep where the working muscle is at its longest while it carries the load. It describes the muscle's length, not whether she feels a stretch.

externally stabilised: The machine, bench or support holds her position so she does not have to.

internally stabilised: Her own trunk and bracing hold the position under load.
