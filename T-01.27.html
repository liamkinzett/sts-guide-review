Muscle and growth · T-01.27

How does training change a muscle's architecture?

Training changes a muscle's shape as well as its size. One change packs in more force. The other adds speed. A block built purely for size leans toward force, at some cost to speed.

Muscle growth is not only about total size. Training can also change the internal arrangement of the fibres, changes referred to as muscle architecture. Two useful concepts are pennation angle (pennation angle) and fascicle length (fascicle length).

Pennation angle. Muscle fibres do not always run in exactly the same direction as the tendon. In many muscles, fibres meet the tendon at an angle: the pennation angle. A steeper angle can pack more fibres into a given muscle volume, adding contractile tissue arranged in parallel and supporting force production. The trade-off is that not all of each fibre's force acts directly along the tendon's line.

Fascicle length. A fascicle is a bundle of muscle fibres. Longer fascicles hold more contractile units arranged in series, an arrangement linked to shortening across a greater distance and at higher speeds. So very broadly: more tissue in parallel supports force, and more units in series support shortening speed.

Training can bias these adaptations. Size-focused training may increase pennation angle as the muscle thickens. Heavy strength and speed-oriented work can be linked with longer fascicles and a shallower angle. These are not perfectly separate adaptations, and STS does not ask coaches to chase architectural numbers directly.

Compared

columns: A steeper fibre angle (pennation angle) | Longer fibre bundles (fascicle length)

What changes: Fibres meet the tendon at a steeper angle. More fibres fit into the same volume. | More contracting units are added end to end. Each bundle is longer.

What it buys: More force in parallel. But each fibre sends less of its force along the tendon's line, so the muscle shortens more slowly at the tendon. | Faster shortening. Speed adds up along the units in series, so the whole muscle moves faster.

Built by: Size-focused training that steepens the angle without lengthening the bundles. | Heavy strength training. It is linked to longer bundles and a shallower angle.

Adding contracting units end to end (sarcomeres in series) is the mechanism under longer fascicles. The page on eccentrics covers how thin the human backing for driving it is.

What this means for coaching. A pure size block can leave the muscle biased toward force in parallel, at some cost to shortening speed. That is a real trade, not a flaw. It is why a pure size phase and a speed-strength phase are not interchangeable, even at the same muscle size. Do not quote a size for the trade. The phases covers which block she is in.

Terms on this page

pennation angle: The angle at which muscle fibres meet the tendon. A steeper angle packs more fibres in but sends less of each fibre's force along the tendon.

fascicle length: The length of a bundle of muscle fibres. Longer bundles let the muscle shorten faster.

sarcomeres in series: More contracting units added end to end along a muscle fibre, making the fibre and the muscle longer rather than thicker.
