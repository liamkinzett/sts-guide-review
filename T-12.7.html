Building a session · T-12.7

Cover the muscle with compounds, then one or two isolations

Choose compounds (compound) that cover different parts of the target muscle rather than repeating the same stimulus. Then finish with one or two isolations (isolation) for what the compounds missed. A flat press and an incline press cover different portions of the chest. Two flat presses cover one portion twice. The first is a session. The second is a longer session that achieves less.

One or two isolations, not five. They exist to fill in what the compounds could not reach and to complete the volume target. They do not exist to add exercises because the session looks short.

How to read "different parts". The exercise pages tell you where each movement is hardest: in the stretch, at mid-range or short. Two compounds from opposite ends of that range cover the muscle. Two from the same end do not.

Watch for

Session bloat. A long exercise list feels thorough and usually means the selection was not doing its job. Choosing exercises is where to fix it.

Terms on this page

compound: An exercise that moves several joints and loads several muscles at once, like a squat, a deadlift, a press or a row.

isolation: An exercise that works one muscle across one joint, like a leg extension or a cable curl. Nothing else can take the set over.
