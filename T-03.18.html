Fatigue and recovery · T-03.18

Does active recovery speed up repair?

Light movement on a rest day helps how someone feels. It does not speed up the repair inside the muscle. Prescribe it for the feeling, if she enjoys it, and never sell it as recovery.

Why the usual claim is half right. Light movement raises local blood flow and clears blood lactate faster than sitting still. But clearing lactate is not what restores performance. Faster clearance does not become better performance the next day, faster structural repair or restored force.

What it does do. Light lifting-only movement (concentric) gives a genuine, temporary easing of soreness. It feels better. Active cool-downs do not change performance recovery, next-day soreness (DOMS) or injury risk beyond that feeling.

Two results. In one group of netball players, active recovery left them sorer next day than passive rest, cold water or contrast therapy. Performance did not differ. In professional footballers, active recovery and full rest made no difference to soreness or to muscle function. The consistent finding is a psychological benefit without a physical one. That is the summary to give her.

Say it like this

It'll make you feel better today. It's not speeding up what your muscle is actually doing to repair itself. Do it if you enjoy it.

When to say no. Some clients are under-fuelled (low energy availability) or already running a sleep debt. For them, active recovery on a rest day adds real strain to a stretched recovery budget. Full passive rest is the better call there, not the default. The female body covers energy availability. The page on sleep as a training variable covers the debt.

The cold water page carries the 12-week comparison of active recovery against cold water.

Terms on this page

concentric: The lifting half of a rep, where the muscle shortens under load.

doms: Delayed onset muscle soreness. The soreness that arrives a day or two after training and fades over the next few days.

low energy availability: Not enough food coming in to cover both her training and the rest of what her body needs to run. Recovery, hormones and the period all suffer.
