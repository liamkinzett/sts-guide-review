Tempo and pauses · T-15.8

A fatigued client gets a longer eccentric, at a cost

When a client turns up carrying fatigue, move to the long end of the eccentric range. The slower descent forces her to pick a lighter weight to stay in control, which regulates the session's stress without you rewriting the program. Fresh client, short end.

How it works. She has not slept, or the week has been heavy. Rather than having the conversation about dropping weight, write the eccentric at three seconds instead of two. The load comes down on its own because she cannot control the heavier weight at that speed. The same tool works at the other extreme. Take a very strong lifter's Romanian deadlift from 320kg to 280kg on a 3 to 4 second eccentric. Orthopaedic wear drops while the tissue still gets loaded.

What it does not do. Tempo is growth-neutral inside the band, so the manoeuvre costs nothing in growth terms. It does not follow that a longer eccentric grows more. It does not.

What it costs. This is the part that gets missed. Changing the eccentric changes two things at once.

  • The logbook comparison ends. Progression says hold the context still or the number tracks the change rather than the client. Tempo is part of that context.
  • Her reps in reserve stop meaning what they meant. A three second eccentric at two reps in reserve (RIR) is not the same relative effort as a two second eccentric at the same RIR. The slower set is heavier relative to what she could lift at that tempo. The prescription from Effort and intensity has quietly moved.

    • Use it knowingly. Expect the logged load to fall and do not read that fall as a stall.

    Terms on this page

    eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.

    RIR: Reps in reserve. How many more reps she could have done when the set stopped.
