What she will ask you · T-47.16

"I wasn't sore. Did it work?"

Soreness indexes novelty, not productivity. Wrecked is a dosing error, not a badge.

The claim, named and dismissed. The idea that being sore means the session was productive, and not being sore means it was not, still permeates from the less informed.

Soreness tracks how new the stimulus is. It does not matter how much she has trained in her life. Do a new kind of training for a week and she can be extremely sore. A trained athlete gets destroyed by any new stimulus. Soreness indexes unfamiliarity.

Chronic soreness inverts the myth. If she feels destroyed every session and every week, she is not recovering and she is not adapting. Some soreness (DOMS) is fine to have and feel. What she does not pursue is constant soreness. She pursues the adaptation.

The strongest frame, spoken as the programmer.

Say it like this

That first week of a new block, you'll probably be a bit sore, because the volume has gone up. I don't want you wrecked. If you're wrecked, that's on me. I got the dose wrong.

Mild soreness after a volume jump is an expected side effect. Being wrecked is the coach's mistake. Own it as yours when it happens. Fatigue and recovery owns fatigue and recovery as programming.

Compared

columns: Mild soreness after a jump | Wrecked every week

What it means: The stimulus was new. Expected. | She is not recovering. A dosing error.

Whose it is: Nobody's. It passes. | Yours. Fix the dose.

Watch for

A client who chases soreness as proof. She will add volume until she gets it, and the soreness she gets is the failed recovery, not the result.
