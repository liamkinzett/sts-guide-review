Progression · T-10.4

On the strength path, load comes first

In a strength block the order inverts. Load progression comes first. It is not a last resort there.

Why it works. Maximal strength is not indifferent to the lever the way hypertrophy is. It is highly sensitive to which lever you use. Adding load beats adding reps even when volume and effort are matched. Strength is a nervous-system and coordination adaptation as much as a tissue one. It is specific to the loads she actually handles. Adding reps at a lighter weight trains something adjacent to what she is trying to improve.

This is the same principle as the three paths in Assessing a new client, applied to progression rather than exercise selection. Adaptation is specific to what you ask for. The hypertrophy client can be progressed on whichever lever costs least. The strength client cannot. The lever is part of the stimulus.

Compared

columns: Hypertrophy path | Strength path

First lever: Reps inside the band | Load

Why: The levers grow the same muscle and cost different amounts | Strength is specific to the load she handles

The set lever: Last, as a response to a stall | Volume is set by the block's percentages in The phases

Watch for

Carrying the hypertrophy habit into a strength block. A coach who has internalised reps-first keeps a strength client at the same weight adding repetitions, then wonders why the tested lift has not moved.
