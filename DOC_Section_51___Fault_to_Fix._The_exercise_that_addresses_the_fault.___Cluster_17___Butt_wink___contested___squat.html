Butt wink on the squat: heel, front load, tempo

Heel elevation, plus a load in front of her, plus a slow heavy tempo. All three, before any cue. Then build capacity in the bottom position with the accessories below. Quads carries the test procedure; this page is the fix list it points to.

Her pelvis tucks under at the bottom of the squat and her lower back rounds (butt wink). On a free squat, whether that matters depends on the client. What to do about a butt wink, in Quads, finds what her wink responds to. These are the fixes that page names.

Setup changes

# Heel elevation, anterior load and a slow heavy tempo

sub: All three together, before any cue

What you see: The pelvis tucks under at depth.

Do this: Slow the descent and give her a heavy tempo. Add a heel elevation. Then load her in front: goblet squat, front squat, even a Zercher. All of these put her in a better position to control her centre of mass.

Exercises: Goblet squat; Barbell front squat

# Stop forcing her vertical

sub: Let the squat be hingier

What you see: A lifter who needs more hinge in her high bar squat, trying to stay super vertical, winks because she is being super vertical.

Do this: Undo the "stay upright" cue. Let her hinge a bit more.

# A heeled shoe, then stance width

sub: The mechanism is the thigh meeting the pelvis, not the ankle

What you see: In a flat shoe she kicks the pelvis back as she reaches depth. Then either a big wink, or she folds in half and gets pinchy at the front of the hips.

Do this: Put a heel on her and it often goes away. Then adjust stance width. The heel does not add ankle or hip range. It moves where the stability demand sits.

# A pause at the bottom

sub: A corrective, not only a stimulus

Do this: A pause at the bottom teaches an unstable lifter to hold tension in the correct position without collapsing or winking.

The heel has a contraindication

If a heeled shoe alters the hip angle unfavourably and causes hip pain, the heel is contraindicated. Take it off. Pain rules and when to refer takes over.

Accessories that build capacity in the bottom position

# Counterbalance squat hold

sub: 2 to 3 minutes at the bottom, then an inch up

Do this: Find the bottom of a counterbalance squat and hold it, 2 to 3 minutes. Then keep the legs active and lift about an inch off the bottom, holding tension there.

# Long-hold static pigeon

sub: For the muscles that turn the thigh out (external rotators)

Do this: A long hold in the pigeon position. Mobility and stretching governs how it is done, and it comes last in the procedure's order.

# Unilateral work for active hip control

sub: Lunges, split squats, single-leg hinges

Do this: Most single-leg movements teach that hip control. STS programs the B-stance RDL rather than the single-leg version. If she cannot stand on one leg, she should not be squatting on two.

Exercises: DB reverse lunge; DB Bulgarian split squat; DB B-stance RDL

Terms on this page

butt wink: At the bottom of the squat her pelvis tucks under and her lower back rounds. It usually shows up when she runs out of hip range or loses her brace.

external rotators: The muscles that turn the thigh outward at the hip, or the upper arm outward at the shoulder. A long hold in the pigeon position stretches them.
