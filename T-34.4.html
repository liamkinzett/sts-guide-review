Cueing · T-34.4

A long cue list is not a difficult client, it is the wrong exercise

If you have to shout a laundry list of cues to keep her in position, you have selected the wrong exercise. Cueing volume is a symptom of bad exercise selection.

The fix is to constrain the movement rather than describe it. Move her somewhere the position is enforced instead of requested. A front-loaded squat is a physical constraint, not a cue: she cannot sit back and shear under a load held in front. The exercise punishes the fault without anyone saying anything. A narrower grip on an overhead press forces the elbow tuck the cue was asking for. A chest-supported setup removes the torso sway you were cueing against.

That frees her attention for output instead of position, which is the actual purpose of the set.

Why it works. A cue asks her to solve the problem every rep, forever. A constraint solves it once. And the constraint survives you not being there, which for a remote roster is most of the time.

Constrain it instead of cueing it

# She sits back and shears in the squat

Swap to: Front squat; Goblet squat; Safety bar squat

Why: A load in front will not let her sit back. The exercise holds the position.

# Her elbows flare on the overhead press

Swap to: Narrower grip

Why: The grip forces the tuck you were asking for.

# Her torso sways on a row

Swap to: Chest-supported row

Why: The pad removes the sway. She just pulls.

Watch for

Reading an escalating cue count as evidence she is trying. It is evidence you picked wrong. The right response is to change the exercise, not to keep talking.

Say when you swap the exercise

We're swapping this. Not because you can't do it. Because the machine will hold that position for you and you can just push.
