Variations

STS programs four drills on this page. The purpose governs all of them: sensation, not warmth and not tissue change. Plant a feeling in the warm-up, then demand she recreates it in the first working exercise.

Scap is short for scapula, the shoulder blade (scapula). Every drill here moves it on its own before the arm joins in.

The four drills

# Kneeling landmine press

sub: The one that doubles as a working exercise

What changes: It is both the prep and the stimulus. Setup, How to do it and Cues are written for it.

# Captain's chair scap raise

sub: Trunk supported, grip demand removed

What changes: Shoulder-blade work (scapular) with the body held by the chair, so all of her attention goes to the shoulder blade.

# Hanging scap raise

sub: The same shoulder-blade work with a grip and an overhead shoulder demand added

What changes: She hangs from the bar, so the grip and the overhead position are part of the drill. It is STS's form of the scapula pull-up, the middle rung of the pull-up progression, and it scales with the heels on a bench.

# Incline prone Trap 3 raise

sub: Warming up's shoulder blade prep for pressing and pulling sessions

What changes: The pressing prep runs in an order: rib cage, then shoulder blade, then rotation. This drill is the shoulder blade step. Coach it to the shared standards on this page: scaps set before load, depression before retraction, intent, and the set ending on sensation.

The scap raises are not progressed by load. They are progressed by the position she can own and the feeling she can recreate.

Terms on this page

scapula: The shoulder blade. The flat bone on the back of the rib cage that the arm hangs from. "Scap" is the gym short form, so a scap raise or a scapula dip moves the shoulder blade on its own before the arm joins in.

scapular: To do with the shoulder blade, the scapula. Scapular work moves or holds the shoulder blade on purpose.
