Calves and adductors · E-ADD-ADDUCT#When to program it

When to use it

The problem you notice. Her squat cannot find the inward turn of the hip (hip internal rotation). Cueing it inside the squat has changed nothing. Or one hip has less inward turn than the other and your instinct is to stretch. Hip adduction is the loaded corrective for both, and it is also a test.

Reach for it when

# Her squat cannot find hip internal rotation

sub: You have cued it inside the squat and nothing has changed

Confirm it: Film the squat. The knees will not track where you have asked them to, set after set, even with the cue.

What it gets her: The option built, so her nervous system has one to take. Under a genuinely heavy compound the nervous system does not have an option. It is not going to take it. You have to build that option. You cue internal rotation in her squat by doing the single-leg seated calf raise and the adductor machine. These correctives are done loaded, in the same session, before or after the squat. Never during it.

# One hip lacks internal rotation

sub: The instinct is to stretch the muscles that turn the hip out (external rotators)

Confirm it: Compare left against right. One side turns in less.

What it gets her: The deficit worked from the only side that can balance it. She needs to activate into internal rotation. She does not need to stretch into it. There are eight or nine external rotators and very few muscles to balance them, so the fix is to strengthen the adductors. This is the STS answer where others give a 90/90 stretch.

# You want a left-against-right read on adduction (hip adduction) before loading her

sub: A test, not just a drill

Confirm it: Use the Copenhagen hold as the test, on a bench rather than with a partner. Involved leg on a bench, top leg lifted. Her bodyweight is held by the down-side obliques and the up-side adductors.

What it gets her: A side-to-side comparison you can see. The side lacking internal rotation is the side lacking adduction activation.

# She plays a field sport and groin problems are what you want out of her season

sub: Prevention, not treatment

Confirm it: Her sport involves sprinting, kicking or cutting.

What it gets her: Fewer groin problems across a season. Never quote her a percentage risk reduction. Those figures were measured in male footballers, and Region by region owns them.

Where it sits

Before or after the session, never inside the lift. Every use above is a loaded corrective around the main work. The main thing needs to be the main thing. Adduction work is the side dish, or even the seasoning on top. Building a session owns the order.

This is not an injury page. An injured adductor goes to Region by region, hip and groin. A prevention effect is not a rehabilitation ladder.

Setup. Back on the pad, no lower-back arch, start width at a light stretch. Setup has the list.

Terms on this page

hip internal rotation: The thigh turning inward at the hip. A squat needs some of it for the knees to track and the hip to load.

external rotators: The muscles that turn the thigh outward at the hip, or the upper arm outward at the shoulder. There are many of them at the hip and few muscles to balance them.

hip adduction: Bringing the thigh in toward the midline of the body. The inner-thigh muscles do it. The adductor machine loads it.
