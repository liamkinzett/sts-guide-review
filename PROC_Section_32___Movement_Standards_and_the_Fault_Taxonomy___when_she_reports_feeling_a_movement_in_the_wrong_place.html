What to do when she reports feeling a movement in the wrong place

She tells you the work went somewhere you did not intend. Treat it as data, not as chat. A reported change in where she feels a movement changes the program.

She feels a squat all in her quads. Her lower back pumps on RDLs. The row moved into her biceps halfway through the set. These are Tier 3 faults: you find them by asking, never by watching.

Route two look-alikes first

Pain, anywhere, of any grade. Pain rules and when to refer. Pain is never a felt-in-the-wrong-place problem.

A set she logged as near-failure that was not. The repeat-set protocol, then Effort and intensity. That is an effort-calibration problem.

Step 1. Test. Ask, do not watch. That is the whole definition of a Tier 3 fault. A coach who watches for it misses it because she watched instead of asking.

Ask, in this order

"Where did you feel that set?" Open, before you name a muscle. Naming one leads her to it.

"When did it move, if it moved?" Rep one against rep eight separates a setup fault from a fatigue fault.

"Is it the same on both sides?" A one-sided report is an asymmetry question, not a technique one.

Step 2. Read. The exercise's own page owns the answer for the movement she is describing. This list is a router, not a diagnosis.

What she reports, and where the answer lives

# The low back on a hip extension or kickback

Read: Movement through the lower back rather than the hip.

Where: Glutes

# A lower-back pump on an RDL

Read: A diagnostic, not a verdict. Four causes in order: heel-heavy pressure, hinging through the knee, going past active hip range, bracing failure.

Where: Glutes

# A pinch in the hip crease on a leg raise, not the lower abs

Read: Hip-flexor dominance.

Where: Core

# Upper-back achiness on an RDL

Read: With the load in front the lats cannot engage. Traps and rhomboids take the job.

Where: Back

# The working feel migrating mid-set on a machine row

Read: It has moved to rear delts or biceps. Check also for position degrading across the set.

Where: Back

# Where a single-leg RDL is felt

Read: Glute reads as good pelvic stability. Entirely hamstring reads differently.

Where: Hamstrings

# A pump in a secondary or tertiary muscle

Read: Self-report, then adjust the setup.

Where: The exercise's own page

Step 3. Do. Change the setup before you change the exercise. Every row above resolves to a position, a load or a range, and the exercise page names which. Choosing exercises owns selection if the setup change does not hold.

Do not cue her toward the muscle you wanted her to feel

A cue that tells her where to feel it makes her report useless as a signal from then on. Cueing covers when a felt-muscle cue helps and when it does not.

Step 4. Change. Re-ask after the change, in the same words. A shift in her answer is the sign the change worked. It arrives before anything shows in the numbers.

Step 5. Escalate. Any pain is Pain rules and when to refer. A report that does not change across two full exposures after a setup change is a selection question for Choosing exercises, not a coaching one.
