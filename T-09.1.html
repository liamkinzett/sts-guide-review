Effort and intensity · T-09.1

Everything is prescribed in reps in reserve

Effort is written, spoken and logged in reps in reserve, across the whole system.

Reps in reserve (RIR) means how many more reps she could have done when the set stopped. A set at 2 RIR stopped two reps short of failure. One language, so a program written by one coach reads the same to the next.

Not rate of perceived exertion. That scale (RPE) measures the same thing from one to ten, and most clients invert it under fatigue.

Not percentages of a one rep max, except inside a strength block, where a later page says when percentages take over.

Why it works. A percentage is a prediction about a day that has not happened yet. Reps in reserve describes the set she just did. When her sleep, food or stress moves, the percentage is wrong and the reps in reserve is still right. That is the whole argument for letting the day's set decide the load (autoregulation).

A worked example. She is written 3 sets of 8 to 10 at 2 RIR. She finishes a set of ten and could have done two more. That set was 2 RIR, on target. On a bad night the same weight leaves her at eight with two in reserve. Still 2 RIR, still on target. A percentage would have called the second set a miss.

Watch for

A coach who avoids the language while still training clients hard. If she stops her sets somewhere, that somewhere has a number. Refusing the vocabulary does not opt you out of the decision. It just means the decision is made without a record.

Say it like this

Reps in reserve is how many more you could have done. Two in reserve means you stopped two short of the point where the next rep wouldn't move. That's the number I want from you after every working set.

Terms on this page

RIR: Reps in reserve. How many more reps she could have done when the set stopped. Zero RIR is failure.

RPE: Rating of perceived exertion. How hard a set felt to her, on a scale out of 10, where 10 is nothing left. STS writes reps in reserve instead.

autoregulation: Letting how she performs on the day set the load, instead of a number fixed in advance.
