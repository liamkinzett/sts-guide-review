What she is told, and the three things you never say

When she feels good and wants to push, tell her we go off symptoms, not feel. That sentence is the whole model said out loud.

The client who feels good and wants to push. It is the sentence a coach needs when nothing is wrong and the client wants more. Nothing has changed, so nothing changes.

Say it like this

We go off symptoms, not how you feel on the day. Feeling great is good news. It isn't the signal we change the plan on.

What she can be told during pregnancy. Activity at guideline level is not associated with miscarriage, stillbirth, preterm birth, low birth weight or birth defects. It reduces the risk of pre-eclampsia, gestational diabetes, caesarean delivery, lower back and pelvic pain (lumbopelvic pain) and depression severity.

What she can be told after birth. Postpartum activity goes with substantially lower odds of depression and of leaking urine (urinary incontinence). Exercise does not change breast milk quality, quantity or infant growth.

Three things a coach never says

# "Leaking is normal after a baby"

sub: Common is not normal

Why: It is treatable. Saying otherwise stops her seeking care.

# "You're fully cleared at six weeks"

sub: The six-week check is a medical screen

Why: It is not a tissue-capacity clearance.

# Any return-to-performance date

sub: There is no date to give

Why: Every return is individual. Load climbs as long as it stays symptom-free.

Terms on this page

lumbopelvic pain: Pain across the lower back, the pelvis or both, common in and after pregnancy.

urinary incontinence: Leaking urine when she does not mean to, often on a cough, a jump or a heavy lift.
