Watch for

  • Injury caution. Shoulder blades sliding forward or rising under a heavy barbell jams the shoulder joint and makes a rotator cuff injury highly likely. 90 degrees of sideways elbow lift pinches the top cuff tendon (supraspinatus).
  • Populations. A history of pec tear, anterior shoulder pain, long limbs, and triceps-dominant pressers. The flat barbell bench is orthopaedically unfriendly for a large population because of the fixed bar path.
  • Pain is Pain rules and when to refer. Female physiology is The female body. Neither is decided on an exercise page.
  • Session consequence. Because this movement pins the shoulder blade, overhead pressing or a pulldown later in the same session restores its upward turn (upward rotation). That is a requirement, not an option, and Building a session holds the rule. A bench session with no pulling in it breaks it.
  • Programming interaction. The pec major and the lat both turn the upper arm inward (internal rotators). Heavy lat rowing immediately before pressing compromises shoulder-blade stability and drops pressing performance. Building a session holds this rule too.
  • Upstream. Upper-back rounding on a squat or deadlift destroys the ability to pull the blades back and down here. Fix it there.
  • Beginners get one cue, at most two. Start light so her mistakes are cheap. Advanced lifters get the opposite. Small deviations carry large risk at the top end.
  • Prerequisites. There is no gate before the barbell bench. STS sequences push-ups before dips and treats bodyweight pressing competence as foundational.
    • Terms on this page

    supraspinatus: The rotator cuff muscle that runs over the top of the shoulder. Its tendon is the one pinched when the elbows flare to shoulder height.

    internal rotators: The muscles that turn the upper arm inward in the socket, mainly the chest and the lats.

    upward rotation: The shoulder blade turning so its outer corner rises and the socket tilts upward. It is what lets the arm travel overhead.
