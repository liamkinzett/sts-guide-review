Fatigue and recovery · T-03.16

Training after a short night of sleep

After one short night of sleep, she trains. A single restricted night barely lowers her strength. Keep the session and name what she should expect.

Total sleep deprivation is different, and so are two or three restricted nights in a row. Those do lower strength, and mostly in one place.

Where a short night shows up

# Time of day

sub: Afternoon and evening sessions are hit harder than morning ones

Do this: If her heavy session is late in the day, warn her it may grind.

# Lift type

sub: Lower-body compound work drops first and fastest

Do this: Expect the squat and the deadlift to feel it. Upper-body and single-joint work barely moves.

# Consecutive nights

sub: One bad night is not the trigger. A second and third in a row is

Do this: Track the run. Across three restricted nights, deadlift and leg press strength fell night by night. Bench held until the third night. A biceps curl was unaffected throughout.

Caffeine is not a footnote. Caffeine before training restored the full workload lost to a restricted night. Offer it before you offer a lighter day.

Say it like this

Train today. If it's a heavy squat or deadlift session this afternoon, expect a bit more grind than usual. That's the sleep, not you losing fitness overnight.

Watch for

A client using one rough night as a reason to skip. One night does not support that decision. Name the two conditions above and move on.

When the answer changes. Where the night was total deprivation rather than a short sleep, expect a real drop. The same holds where this is the second or third short night running. The drop concentrates in lower-body compound lifts. At that point "push through it" stops being the right coaching line.

One rough session is not a verdict. A poor session from a bad night does not erase what her training has already built. A single off day most often reflects last night. A repeated run of poor sessions is more likely to reflect her program, her recovery or her life context. Look for the pattern before you rewrite anything.
