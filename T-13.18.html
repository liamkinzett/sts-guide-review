Choosing exercises · T-13.18

Assisted dips and cable pushdowns buy different things

Both train the triceps. They are not a straight substitute for each other. One buys cheap volume. The other buys a compound's fatigue bill.

An assisted dip is a loadable compound. It carries tricep work alongside chest and shoulder demand, and the whole-body recovery bill comes with it. A cable pushdown is an isolation movement, cheap on whole-body fatigue. Because that cost is low, it is one of the movements that can be taken to true failure. Effort and intensity has that rule.

Compared

columns: Assisted dip | Cable pushdown

Use it when: The session or the week has fatigue budget to spend. Her chest and shoulders also need the volume | Fatigue is the constrained resource. Priming, a cut, or a week already carrying heavy compounds elsewhere

What it buys: More than tricep stimulus for the cost. Chest and shoulder work travel with it | Isolated tricep volume without touching the fatigue budget. It can be pushed closer to failure than the dip can

Why it works. Treating these as interchangeable "tricep exercises" hides the real decision: whether this session can afford a loadable compound's fatigue cost right now. Loadability equals fatigue is the rule that makes that cost visible before the choice is made.
