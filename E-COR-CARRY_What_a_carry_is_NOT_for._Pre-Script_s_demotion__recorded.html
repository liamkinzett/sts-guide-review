What a carry is not for

A carry is not core work. Stop selling farmer's carries as a core exercise. Compared with challenging the trunk from insertion to origin, a carry is not a good core exercise.

The whole family of holds (isometric) gets the same question: the bird dog, the curl-up, the side plank, the Pallof press, the farmer's carry. Where is the stretch? Where is the shortening? Where is the contraction? Where is the growth?

Growth is the answer, and none of them has it. So carries and Pallof presses are coordination and load-tolerance tools. Capacity is built by loading the trunk through a range, which is the trunk flexion page.

What a carry is for

# Teaching load acceptance

sub: The novice's first heavy hold

Do this: For a novice, the carry comes before the Roman chair. A net downward force to accept before she is asked to resist an upward one.

# Integrating a cranky shoulder

sub: Before pressing

Do this: A loaded heavy farmer's carry with the palm turned up (supinated) and the shoulder turned outward (external rotation), placed as the integrated movement before pressing.

# Trap loading by net force

sub: Shrugs and carries

Do this: Traps grow from shrugs and carries by net force. The shoulder blade being pulled off the rib cage loads the muscle maximally and on the lengthening.

# A load-management input

sub: Heavy carries change the next choice

Do this: If she did a lot of heavy farmer's carries, skip the overhead press that day. Give a cable lateral raise in the stretched position instead.

# Ordering

sub: Lunge before carry

Do this: She should own a walking lunge before anyone sees her do a farmer's carry.

The carry is a sideways step off the plank, not the next rung up a core ladder. Once she holds a plank for thirty seconds to a minute, the loadable exit is trunk flexion. The carry is the other exit, for walking under load.

Remember where the carry stands. STS does not yet write a carry into a client's program. This page is for coaching one when it appears in the strength system's lower session or in a client's existing training.

Terms on this page

supinated: Palm turned up or forward, knuckles back.

external rotation: Turning a limb outward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face forward or up, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more to the side.

isometric: A hold. The muscle works against the load but the joint does not move.
