The bad-week conversation. You adjust the plan out loud

In the bad-week conversation, you adjust the plan out loud. Her half of it was scripted weeks earlier, at onboarding. She never failed, and this is the conversation the fixed word happens inside.

The conversation itself

Okay, you've had a rough week. You got several hours less sleep. We were going to have an intense workout today, but we're not going to. We're going to push that back. There's time. There's time we can get back, especially for something performance-based. Let's play the long game. There's no need to rush this.

Name the week. Name the data. Name the change. Close with time.

Read who moves. The coach adjusts the plan. She is not asked to try harder. The session is pushed back as strategy rather than forfeited as failure. Training around life owns a week that genuinely compressed. Adherence as a signal owns the pattern where bad weeks are the finding.

The embarrassment is won at onboarding. Script both halves of the future confession and hand her hers in advance.

At onboarding, weeks before the bad week

There's no judgment here. I'm not going to shout at you, I'm not going to discipline you, I'm not going to fire you as a client. I want you to be able to come to me and say: hey, I messed up. This is why. This is what I did. Can you help me fix it?

The first half is your wording. The second half is hers, licensed in advance. If that expectation is set at the very beginning, the confession arrives instead of the silence. This is the coaching relationship's intake agreement aimed at shame rather than at hard talk. It uses the same day-one timing as pre-framing the dip.

Then score the week she actually had against the environment she was in.

Watch for

Answering the embarrassment with reassurance and leaving the plan untouched. The move is structural. The plan visibly absorbs the week. That is what tells her the bad week was priced in.
