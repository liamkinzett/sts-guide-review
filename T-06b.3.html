Her first program · T-06b.3

Effort: below 3 RIR where she is stable, 2-3 RIR where she is not

She does not know where failure is, and she thinks she is closer to it than she is. A prescribed 1-2 RIR arrives as a real 3-4 RIR.

Two cases, two prescriptions. Where a machine holds her position (externally stabilised), the overshoot is harmless. Prescribe below 3 RIR so that she lands at 3. Where her own bracing holds her position (internally stabilised), the overshoot is not harmless. Hold her at 2-3 RIR and accept that she lands further out.

What you write, and where she actually lands

1-2 | What you write on a machine. She lands at a real 3-4

2-3 | What you write on a squat or a deadlift. She lands further out, and that is fine

Why the two cases differ. On a leg press or a machine row, the weight stops if she misjudges. On a squat or a deadlift, a beginner who does reach 1 RIR is a beginner whose form breaks under load. The cost of being wrong is not the same, so the prescription is not the same.

Effort and intensity owns this rule, the overshoot figure and the reasoning. It is repeated here because it is one of the three things you aim off. A coach writing program one needs all three in one place.

Terms on this page

externally stabilised: The machine, bench or support holds her position so she does not have to.

internally stabilised: Her own trunk and bracing hold the position under load.
