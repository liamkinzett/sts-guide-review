Split design · T-11.21

A rolling split: track it by position, not by the calendar

A rolling five-day push/pull/legs rotation is judged by the same arithmetic as a fixed week. Track it by position in the rotation, not by the calendar day. Nothing about a rotation that drifts across the week changes how many exposures (exposures) each muscle needs.

Compare her numbers by slot. Read a lift against its own history at the same position in the rotation. Leg day two of the cycle against leg day two, not against whatever fell on a Tuesday. Progression compares an exercise within its block, and here the block's slot is the position. The context that matters is the slot, not the date.

The genuine risk arrives less predictably. The five-day cycle drifts against the seven-day calendar. So two demanding sessions that a fixed week would keep apart can land closer together as the cycle laps the week. That is crossover fatigue (crossover fatigue), not a flaw in the rolling structure itself.

Watch for

Trusting the shape to keep hard sessions apart. A fixed week does that automatically. A rotation does not. Check the actual spacing between hard sessions each time the rotation would otherwise compress two of them.

Terms on this page

exposures: The number of separate sessions in a week in which a muscle is trained.

crossover fatigue: Fatigue that shows up in a session other than the one that caused it. Heavy hinging on a pull day limiting her bracing on a later hinge session is an example.
