Common faults

Thirteen common faults

# No wedge

sub: Rep one jerks, later reps do not. The click arrives with the pull

Why it happens: She pulls before the slack is out, so the bar snaps taut under her instead of before her.

Do this: Lat tension and floor push before the bar moves. Cue "it's a push and a pull together". Kill the click at the floor.

# Knees straighten before the hips

sub: She gets shot forward at the floor

Why it happens: The legs fire first and the hips are left behind, so the bar swings forward and the back finishes the lift.

Do this: The wedge first, then cue "leg drive" so hips and knees extend together.

# Shoulders too far over the bar, or behind it

sub: Centre of mass off the midfoot

Why it happens: She sets up by feel rather than by the bar. Too far over and the bar swings; too far behind and the legs are blocked.

Do this: Reset with the shoulders just in front of the bar, over the midfoot.

# Grip and rip

sub: Soft elbows, momentum, the bar banged off the floor

Why it happens: Nobody has taught her the set-up as part of the lift. She treats the bar like something to snatch.

Do this: Take the slack out. Cue "arms long until it clicks".

# Squatting too deep

sub: Trying to make it a pure push

Why it happens: "Bum down" is taken literally. Extra depth adds travel and pulls her out of position.

Do this: Bum down only as far as leg drive needs. Reset the hips higher.

# Bar drifting forward off the midline

sub: The bar leaves the body

Why it happens: The lats are off, so nothing holds the bar to her. There is no exception here. If the bar drifts away from the body, she is doing it wrong.

Do this: Lats on before the pull. Cue "pull your scaps into your back pockets".

# Toes lifting

sub: The toes come off the floor as she pulls

Why it happens: Her weight has gone into the heels, usually from sitting back to "push".

Do this: Cue "toes off the ground, the devil". Weight across the whole foot.

# Huge arch, head looking up

sub: Lower back over-arched, chin up

Why it happens: "Chest up" and "look up" have been coached into her. The arch replaces a braced, neutral trunk.

Do this: Head slightly forward as a counterbalance, pelvis close to neutral.

# "Shoulders back" as a posture fix

sub: Upper traps working overtime

Why it happens: Pulling the shoulders back creates a new deviation and loads the upper traps. Lifters who do it literally give themselves a headache.

Do this: Cue "crown of the head to the ceiling" instead. It fixes posture up-and-down.

# Wide conventional stance copied from strongmen

sub: Feet set well outside the jump stance

Why it happens: She has watched giants pull on television and copied the stance. She is not Thor, she is not six foot nine and she is not 200 kilograms.

Do this: Back to the jump stance, naturally close.

# Sumo stance too wide

sub: The knee cannot sit over the foot, or the stance is wider than her mobility

Why it happens: Wider looks stronger, but past a point the shin can no longer be vertical and the hips cannot open.

Do this: Narrow it to where the shin can sit perpendicular over the foot.

# Excessive sumo toe flare

sub: Feet turned out well past 45 degrees

Why it happens: More flare feels like more hip opening, but it costs balance. She will fall over straight away.

Do this: Bring the feet back to roughly 45 degrees.

# Over-leaning at lockout

sub: She finishes leaning back, hips pushed forward past neutral

Why it happens: She mistakes a lean-back for a finished lift.

Do this: Stand straight. Hips through, not back.
