A braced wrist takes grips and planes off the table, not pressing

A wrist in a brace forbids what the brace forbids: sideways bend and load through the joint. It does not forbid elbow or shoulder movement. Pressing stays. Certain grips and planes go.

Why. Neutral in the sideways plane is already the standard at the bottom of any press. A brace enforces that neutrality by construction. What it removes is her ability to grip and stabilise the bar the way an unbraced wrist would. The pressing pattern survives.

What to change, in the lever order

Change the position. Machine presses with a fixed handle path, or a neutral-grip dumbbell press. Both ask less of the wrist's own stabilisation than a straight bar or a palm-down dumbbell grip.

Change the exercise. Where grip load itself is the problem rather than the press pattern, a chest-supported or machine variant removes the demand the brace cannot.

Go single arm, outside the AC joint plane. The single-arm exception to the AC joint rule exists for exactly this. The unaffected side loads normally while the braced side is managed on its own terms.

What does not come out. Lower body, and any upper-body pattern that does not load the wrist in extension under a compressive load. The method is train what does not hurt, not train around the upper body entirely because one joint in it is braced. Four days a week stays achievable on that basis.

Watch for

Reading "wrist sprain" as "no pressing". The hand and wrist are an assessment site, not a fixed obstacle. Programming around one changes the grip and the plane before it changes the session count.

What we do not give her. No return timeline for coming out of the brace. Her physio decides that. You keep everything else moving.

Say it like this

The brace tells us which grips are out, not that pressing is out. We'll swap to handles and machines that keep your wrist straight, and your other arm trains as normal.
