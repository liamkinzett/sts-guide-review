One leg or two · T-18.11

A single-leg squat is not a shallower two-legged squat

The same depth words describe different knee angles on one leg (unilateral) and on two (bilateral). The angles are what they are. The two definitions below are either applied or got wrong.

TermUnilateralBilateral
"Full"Internal knee angle 60 to 70 degrees40 to 45 degrees
"Parallel"80 to 100 degrees60 to 70 degrees

A unilateral "parallel" squat sits at the same knee angle as a bilateral half squat. The restrictions are structural. Less torso lean is available, and sometimes the rear leg is up on a support.

Why it works. Depth language does not transfer between the two. A coach who writes "to parallel" on a split squat and on a back squat has written two different prescriptions. Volume already counts the split squat and lunge at half glutes and half quads. This rule does not create a second accounting.

And a dumbbell in each hand is bilateral. A movement is unilateral because one limb is doing the work, not because the load is in one hand.

Terms on this page

unilateral: One limb doing the work, as in a split squat or a single-arm row. Where the load is held does not decide it.

bilateral: Both limbs working together, as in a two-footed squat or a standard Romanian deadlift.
