Hold a muscle at about a third of its growing volume

When one muscle is held so another can be specialised, its maintenance dose is a ratio, not a fixed number. Hold it at roughly a third of the volume she was growing it on. The holding-phase figure in The phases is for a whole-body holding phase at maintenance calories, and it does not transfer here.

Key figure

value: A third

unit: of the volume she was growing that muscle on

note: Fewer sets, not fewer positions. The range floor from Choosing exercises still applies to a held muscle.

Why the holding-phase number is the wrong one. That range covers every muscle at once, in a phase where nothing is being grown. This is one muscle held while another grows, usually in a surplus (surplus), with the rest of her training unchanged. Reading that number across is the error, and it is expensive. It spends most of what the specialisation was supposed to free.

Why a ratio is the right shape. It scales with the client. A muscle she was growing on a large volume keeps proportionally more than one she was growing on a small volume. That is what maintenance means for her rather than in general. A fixed number cannot do that.

How to use it

Volume decides whether you specialise at all. Nothing below applies until it says yes.

Take the muscle's current growing volume from her own program, not from a table.

Hold it at roughly a third of that. The range floor still applies to a held muscle. This is fewer sets rather than fewer positions.

Spend what you freed on the target, delivered as more exposures (exposures) at moderate per-session volume, under the per-session ceiling.

A worked example. Her glutes are the target. Her quads were growing on 15 sets a week. Hold the quads at 5 sets, a third, and the 10 sets you freed go to the glutes. Deliver them as more exposures at moderate per-session volume, under the six to eight set session ceiling.

You may go lower than a third. The phases shows how small a dose can hold tissue for a long stretch. It carries its own warning with it. A number that merely holds tissue is not the same as a number that keeps her training properly. Read it there.

When you may deviate. A muscle that is her limiting factor for the specialised lift is not a candidate for holding at all. An upper back held at a third while she specialises a squat pattern will show up in the squat. The priority rule in Building a session and the stall order in Volume decide what she cannot afford to reduce.

Watch for

Presenting a third as a measured threshold, to a client or in a program note. It is a planning ratio. Never give her a number for how long one muscle can be held. Never promise that her strength and her size on it hold at the same fraction.

Terms on this page

surplus: Eating more calories than she burns, so that she has the energy to build muscle.

exposures: The number of separate sessions in a week in which a muscle is trained.
