When to use it

Reach for a hold when she cannot keep her ribs over her pelvis inside a lift, and cueing inside the lift has not worked. The hold lets her own the position on its own before the lift has to supply it. If somebody cannot do a side plank, she probably should be doing a side plank. That is the indication, and it is also the limit.

Reach for it when

# Her ribs flare or her trunk folds inside a compound

sub: Lunge, squat, hinge, press or pull, and cueing inside the lift has not held

Confirm it: Film the lift. The rib cage lifts away from the pelvis, or the trunk folds, as the load goes up.

What it gets her: The position owned on its own before the lift has to supply it.

# It is the front of a squat session and you want a read before the bar gives you one

sub: A systems check, asked in advance

Confirm it: Ask one question: can she hold a pelvic position that lets her run force through her body? The weighted lunge hold, the Copenhagen hold and the side plank with an adductor squeeze are the check.

What it gets her: An answer to one question before the squat asks it with a bar on her back.

# She cannot feel the position at all

sub: Untrained, and describing the position has not worked

Confirm it: You have described "ribs down" three ways and nothing changes.

What it gets her: A sensation she keeps. Put her in the position and let her feel it (forced awareness). The feeling, not the time, is the product.

# She leans or flares the ribs the moment an arm goes overhead

sub: The wide, flat-ribbed lifter who reaches sideways to get there

Confirm it: Watch an overhead press from the front. The trunk tips or the ribs lift as the arm rises.

What it gets her: The rib cage compressed from the sides before the press, not during it. The obliques and serratus (serratus anterior) are already holding the trunk when the arm arrives.

# Her back is the complaint and the risk you are managing is fatigue, not weakness

sub: Short holds that end before position can degrade

Confirm it: Her position is good fresh and goes as she tires.

What it gets her: A hold that ends before the position breaks down. The back-pain figure of 5 to 10 seconds exists so that fatigue is not what is being trained. This protocol, not a different exercise, is the answer for the client with back pain.

The ceiling is half of the indication

Once she holds thirty seconds to a minute, the answer is not more time. Nobody should progress to holding a plank for 10 minutes.

The two ways out are a loadable flexion or extension exercise (Trunk flexion) or a loaded carry (Loaded carry).

Planks are for the very regressed client, and for a warm-up. A trained client who can already organise her trunk rarely needs one. Reaching for a plank for her is reaching for the wrong tool.

None of these reasons is about growing the muscle. A hold teaches position and awareness. It does not build size.

Terms on this page

forced awareness: Putting her into a position and letting her sit in it until she can feel it. The feeling she keeps is the product, not the time she held it.

serratus anterior: The muscle along the side of the rib cage that pulls the shoulder blade forward and holds it flat to the ribs. It also helps compress the rib cage from the sides.
