How to read an exercise page · T-19.6

Two to four cues, first one first, each marked internal or external

A Cues section holds two to four cues, in the order you try them, each marked internal or external. The page holds two to four. You say one. A coach under time pressure reads the first cue, so the first cue is the one that works most often.

Why the order. The list is ranked by what to try first. Try the first. Only move on when it fails. Four cues on a page are four candidates, not four instructions to give at once.

Why the mark. Internal and external cues do different jobs. An internal cue points her at her own body ("squeeze your glutes"). An external cue points her at the world ("push the floor away"). Cueing owns which wins when. The mark on the page lets you apply that rule without re-deriving it.

One cue per correction. Two is the absolute ceiling. The second should generally not be verbal: a hand, a target, a change to the setup. Cueing holds the rule; the page only supplies the candidates.

A cue is printed word for word. A paraphrased cue is a new cue nobody has tried on a client. Say it as the page prints it.

Some movements have one good cue. Padding the list to two would be an invention. When a page prints one cue, that is the whole list.

Test a cue before you trust it

Do not judge a cue by performing it correctly. Try every way it can be done wrongly. If she can do what the cue says without the target muscle working, the cue has failed.
