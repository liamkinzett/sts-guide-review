Assessing a new client · T-06.9

Holding phases only bridge hypertrophy and fat loss

A holding phase is used only when moving between hypertrophy and fat loss. It is a maintenance block between phases (holding phase), and it runs in either direction. The same phase type repeating, hypertrophy after hypertrophy, gets no holding phase. Use a deload when fatigue signals warrant one.

One named exception. A very large cut is sectioned rather than run as one continuous deficit. The maintenance stretches between those sections are holding phases. That is fat loss to fat loss. It breaks the rule above deliberately, and it is the only case that does. The Nutrition Manual owns when a cut gets sectioned.

How long it runs. The phases sets the length.

Why it works. Three reasons.

Fatigue clears. A hypertrophy block leaves accumulated fatigue that sits on top of her adaptations and hides them. Training at maintenance lets it dissipate, so what she actually built becomes visible in the logbook.

Her performance on any day is her fitness minus her fatigue (fitness-fatigue model). Both fade after training, and fatigue fades faster. Gains she has already made only show once it has gone. That carries one design instruction: cut volume, not load. Keeping heavy exposure through the reduced period preserves the neural side of what she built. So a holding phase keeps the top sets and drops the count. It never turns into a fortnight of light work.

Do not tell her a holding phase resensitises her muscle to training. Fatigue hiding her gains is the reason a holding phase works. Resensitisation is a claim we do not make.

Muscle is held on far less work than it took to build. One set per exercise, once a week, above 80% of her one rep max, can be enough to hold it. That has held muscle for over 32 weeks. Progressing it in a trained lifter takes two to three sessions a week. So the holding phase costs her almost nothing in muscle while buying the recovery. It costs so little because maintenance is genuinely cheap, not because the phase is short.

One condition, and it is the one that matters to us. The minimal maintenance dose only holds when she is eating enough (energy availability). A holding phase sits at maintenance calories, so it is not a problem there. It changes the reading for a client already in a deficit.

Minimum effective volume is a maintenance-calorie idea. You cannot carry it into a cut and assume it survives the trip. Underfeed her and the small dose stops holding the tissue.

That is why the retention volume for a cutting client, on the previous page, sits well above the tiny maintenance dose.

Her maintenance has moved. She finishes a build eating more than she started. A holding phase sits at maintenance calories, which is where you find her new number from real data rather than a formula. The nutrition side belongs to the Nutrition Manual.

The result is that she starts the cut recovered instead of carrying a block of fatigue into a deficit. That matters because a deficit is exactly when fatigue is hardest to clear and muscle is most at risk.

Watch for

Using a holding phase between two hypertrophy blocks. If she needs a break there, what she needs is a deload, and that takes a week. A holding phase there costs her weeks of training she never needed to lose.

Terms on this page

holding phase: A block at maintenance calories between a build and a cut, in either direction. Volume drops, load stays, her fatigue clears and her new maintenance shows itself.

fitness-fatigue model: Her performance on any day is her fitness minus her fatigue. Both fade after training, but fatigue fades faster, so gains show once fatigue is gone.

energy availability: The energy left for her body to run on after training has been paid for. When it is low, recovery, hormones and muscle retention all suffer.
