How to do it

The rep, in order

Separate the rib cage and upper back from the shoulder blade. This is the whole point of the movement. The traps and rhomboids run from the spine to the blade. If the upper back rounds and arches with the rep, both ends move together and neither is loaded.

Coach how far back the elbows drive and how much the blades pull in, not how far the handle travels.

Run the full travel. Let the blades slide out around the ribs at the front of the rep (protraction). Then drive the elbows back into full retraction at the finish (retraction). This is the upper-back instruction and it is deliberate here.

Stay on the pad. The chest does not leave it at either end of the rep.

Expect a smaller-looking rep. Range at the working joint goes up while handle travel goes down.

Squeeze hard at end range, and do not chase end range with the spine. The retraction that counts is bought by the blade on a still rib cage. Extra travel bought by arching the spine or coming off the pad is not part of it. Two limits, one rep.

Terms on this page

protraction: The shoulder blade sliding forward and out around the rib cage, away from the spine.

retraction: The shoulder blade sliding back toward the spine, so the two blades pinch together.
