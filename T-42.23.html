Answering a question you do not know the answer to

Tell her what you know. Then tell her what you do not. The admission is what buys the authority. It runs the other way to instinct.

The script has two halves. State the certain part. Flag the uncertain part openly.

The two halves

Look, this is what I know. I don't know how it's going to correlate to what you're feeling in this specific situation.

The mechanism. The admitted gap is not what erodes her trust. The confident promise is. Promise someone results in six weeks and fail to deliver them in six weeks, and she is gone. Say what you do not know, and you keep the chance to be right later.

The skill, named plainly. Letting people know what you do not know, and recognising when you do not have the answer.

Declare the scope line before you hit it. This is the referral version of "I do not know".

Naming the line early

There are times where I'll say: here's my line for scope of practice. If it's past that, I'm going to refer out, and I'll always be transparent about that.

Where it connects. For an uncertain outcome, pair the admission with the next step. Pain rules and when to refer owns anything medical the referral touches. Where the question is not a training one and nothing here covers it, escalate rather than improvise.

Watch for

A promise with a date on it. Six weeks is the example. Any confident timeline is the same trap.
