Teach the pattern with external stability, then load it

Teach her the pattern with something holding her position (externally stabilised), then load it. The loaded compound is the stability work.

Why it works. Big multi-joint lifts that send force through her mid-section work that musculature harder than isolated or unstable core drills do. Isolated trunk work does not build the trunk for heavy lifting the way the loaded lift itself does. So the sequence is: remove the stability demand to teach the pattern, then load. External stabilisation is how she learns the movement without also fighting to hold it. The loading that follows is what develops the mid-section, better than the isolated alternatives would.

Stabiliser work first is for a specific fault, not a stage. Building a session sends a client who cannot hold position to stabiliser work before the loaded compound. That rule is conditional: when she cannot hold position. It was never a stage every client passes through. Do not read it as anything larger.

One note on her body, and it points the same way. A woman's vertebrae are smaller in cross-section, and her spine tolerates less compressive load in absolute terms. At the same relative load, the compressive stress is therefore higher. What she does about it is the useful part.

In a deep squat her lower back (lumbar) flexes less and the base of her spine, the sacrum, tilts further forward. The lower-back segments also move more relative to each other. That configuration lets her develop superior muscular stabilisation of the spine and hold a protective arch under load. The women we coach stabilise the loaded squat well, and they do it through the squat. The female body has the detail.

Watch for

The program that builds toward the compound forever. If the stability work is not being loaded, it is not the stability work.

Terms on this page

externally stabilised: The machine, bench or support holds her position so she does not have to.

lumbar: The lower back: the part of the spine between the ribs and the pelvis.
