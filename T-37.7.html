Returning to training · T-37.7

Week-one loads: come back sooner, at a lower percentage

Come back to the movement sooner and at a lower percentage. Never at the load she finished on.

The layoff page says what she comes back to: compounds, few of them. The detraining page says which adaptations left and on what clock. This page picks the weight for the first session, which is the question you are actually holding when you write the program.

The error is anchoring on where she stopped. The number she last hit is the number she will offer you. It is the one number in the conversation with no bearing on what she can do now. Reproducibility rep to rep is the qualification, not her memory of a session.

The trade runs the other way to instinct. Come back to the barbell movements earlier, at a lower percentage, and spend longer climbing. Coming in lower gives you plenty of time to progress slowly. It also gives you room for the options and variations you want to introduce, instead of chasing the load she just finished at.

Why it works. A barbell asks for two things. The load is one. Carrying it, unracking it and the subtleties of the pattern are the other, and those are skill rather than strength. Coming back early at a low percentage buys the pattern back while the load is still light enough to practise. Practising a lift is that subject. Waiting until she can lift the old number spends the whole gap rehearsing nothing.

Watch for

Borrowing a number from Her first program. Its start-below-what-you-think rule is written for a brand-new client, not a return. Do not read a percentage across from it.

A fixed percentage. None is set. "Lower than she finished on" is the instruction, and you set the number low enough to leave weeks of climbing.

Say it like this

We're starting lighter than where you left off, on purpose. It gives us room to climb, and it gets the movement back while the weight is easy.
