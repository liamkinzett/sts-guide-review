Can a beginner recruit all their motor units under a heavy load?

At 80% of max and above, a beginner is already recruiting essentially the whole available motor unit pool. So is a trained lifter. Training age changes how much of that force reaches the bar.

A motor unit is one nerve and the bundle of muscle fibres it switches on (motor units). The pool is all of them. Under heavy loads, both trained and untrained lifters recruit essentially the whole available pool. The important difference is not whether recruitment happens. It is how effectively the system turns that recruitment into force on the bar.

"Untrained lifters can't access all their motor units yet" is widely repeated. It is wrong. At heavy loads the nervous system recruits essentially everything, whatever the training age.

What training age does change. How efficiently the movement is performed, and how well the body stabilises under load. A trained lifter wastes less force fighting their own opposing muscles (antagonist co-activation). They also stabilise better under heavy effort, so more of the recruited force goes into the lift.

Why it matters. A coach who explains a beginner's fast progress with "they can't access all their motor units yet" is naming the wrong mechanism. Recruitment near failure is not the limitation. Force wasted fighting their own stabilisers is. That points you at pattern, which is where Her first program puts a beginner.

Watch for

Quoting a percentage for how much of an untrained lifter's motor units training "unlocks". No such figure stands behind what STS teaches. The previous page carries the same caution on the neural-versus-muscular split.

Terms on this page

antagonist co-activation: The muscle opposite the working one tensing at the same time, so part of the force is spent fighting itself.

motor units: One nerve cell and the bundle of muscle fibres it switches on together. A muscle is made of many of them, from small to large.
