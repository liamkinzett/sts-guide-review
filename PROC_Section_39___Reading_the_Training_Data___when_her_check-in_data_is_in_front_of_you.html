Reading a check-in, start to finish

This is the topic's rules in the order you use them at a check-in. The posture is the topic's opening line: most of what is in the data should change nothing.

Route three things out before you read anything. Each one looks like a data question and is not.

A pattern of missed sessions rather than a performance signalAdherence as a signal

From what she logged to what you change

? Can the logbook be trusted: loads against every set, reps filled in, numbers that were not rounded up?

no: First time, raise it, take her word and write the block with the assumption line. Second time, stop progressing the program.

? Has one movement been flat for two exposures in a row, inside the same block?

no: Nothing changes. A single bad session, soreness, a missed session, a pump or one week of bodyweight is noise on its own.

? Is she training that movement hard enough?

no: Raise her effort first. It is the most common answer and the one most often skipped.

? Has her technique held, and is she recovering: sleep, stress and food?

no: Fix that first. Never add sets to an under-fuelled client.

1 · Test. First, can the logbook be trusted? Sessions with no loads against them, missing reps, numbers rounded up to what she meant to do. None of this topic's rules work on a logbook you cannot trust. First time: raise it, take her word, write the block you would have written with the same loads, and preface it with the assumption line. The lack of data is a service problem, not a physiological one. A second patchy check-in is step five.

Then read per exercise, not across the program. The unit of judgement is one movement over time. Two flat exposures on one movement means go and look. It does not yet mean change anything.

2 · Read. Sort what you found into the two columns. On the signal side: two flat exposures on one movement. A decline once read rather than reacted to. Technique degrading within a set. An effort rating the repeat-set test contradicts. A reported change in where she feels a movement. Sleep that has genuinely changed.

On the noise side, on its own: a single bad session. Soreness in either direction. A missed session. A pump or the absence of one. Bodyweight moving in one week. A request for variety.

The right-hand column is the harder half. Every item in it produces a feeling that something should be done. A week is not a trend. Do not react to the most recent session because it is the most recent.

3 · Do. Diagnose before you change anything. Volume owns the order, and the first question is whether she is training hard enough. Volume is fourth on that list.

Then change one thing, the smallest thing that could work. In order: a cue, a setup or constraint, load, range, volume, the exercise, the split. Each rung down costs more to reverse and takes longer to read.

4 · Change. What explicitly does not change: everything in the noise column, on its own. A missed session, soreness, a pump, one week of bodyweight, a variety request. Nothing changes in pairs either. Two changes at once cannot be read, and half the time neither one worked. Deviate only where two changes are genuinely coupled, such as reducing load while lengthening a range, and say so in the notes.

5 · Escalate. Where this stops being a data read.

It stops being this topic whenWhere it goes
Pain appears anywhere in the dataPain rules and when to refer
The report is where she feels a movementFaults and what you can actually see has the questions, then the exercise page. It is data, not chat
An effort rating fails the repeat-set testThe repeat-set protocol in Faults and what you can actually see, then Effort and intensity. Effort calibration
The pattern is adherence rather than performanceAdherence as a signal
Logging is patchy a second time, after the raiseStop progressing the program. No new block until there is data to write it from
It is not a training question at allThe coaching relationship. You escalate, you do not improvise

The stopping point is one raise, then the stop. There is no count of patchy check-ins to reach. She is told once, the block goes out with the assumption line, and a second patchy check-in ends progression until the data returns.

Terms on this page

repeat-set test: She rests sixty seconds and repeats a set she called one rep short, at the same weight. If she comfortably does, the set was not as hard as she logged.
