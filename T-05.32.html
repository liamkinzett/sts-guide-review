A scale jump after starting or switching contraception

A scale jump right after starting or switching a hormonal method is read as a trend question. It is not a fuelling question and it is not a training question.

A client will sometimes see the number on the scale move within days of a pill change. She assumes it reflects her training or her eating. It usually reflects neither.

What the pill does not do. It does not impair strength adaptation and it does not change protein needs, as the last page covered. A single overnight weight change is a different thing again. No source puts a figure on how much of it is water or how long it takes to settle.

What you apply instead. You track her symptoms rather than the calendar, the way the last section already teaches. A single number is not a symptom trend. One reading off a scale answers nothing on its own.

What you do

Do not diagnose water retention. Do not deny the number is real either.

Ask her to watch the trend over one to two weeks rather than react to the single reading.

Keep training exactly as the plan already has it. Nothing about her strength adaptation changes because the scale moved.

Say it like this

One number the morning after a change like that doesn't tell us much on its own. Let's watch it over the next couple of weeks rather than react to today's reading. Nothing about your training changes either way.

Watch for

Attaching a number or a settling timeframe to what you tell her. Do not say how much is water or how long it will take to settle.
