Tempo and pauses · T-15.6

Nothing goes past four seconds

The eccentric never goes past four seconds. Five is allowed for an exceptionally tall client, whose limbs make the same range take longer to travel.

Key figure

value: 4 seconds

unit: the eccentric ceiling

note: 5 for a very tall client. Beyond that the fatigue buys nothing.

Why it works. Beyond the 4 second ceiling the cost is fatigue in her nervous system and in the muscle's fuel supply, and it buys nothing. It comes directly out of the repetitions near failure, where the stimulus actually is.

A seven second hamstring curl eccentric is the classic example: she does less work, finds it harder, and grows no more for it.

The growth cliff sits much further out, at ten seconds. Our ceiling is well short of that, which tells you the ceiling is not about growth. It is about what the fatigue costs the rest of the set.

Where the ceiling gets tested. Some coaches run a 5 second eccentric on specific movements, a seated hamstring curl or a deficit snatch grip deadlift in a high volume block. Treat those as specialist applications rather than the rule. Four seconds is the STS number, 5 seconds for the very tall client, and that is where it stays.

Watch for

The client who has decided that slower is better and is running fives on everything. She is doing less work and finding it harder, which feels like progress. It is not.

Terms on this page

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.
