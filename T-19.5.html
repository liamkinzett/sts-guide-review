How to read an exercise page · T-19.5

The page says where it's hardest; Tempo and pauses sets the pause

An exercise page states where in the range the tension peaks, and why. It never prints a pause duration. Where a movement is hardest is a fact about the movement. How long she pauses there is a prescription, and Tempo and pauses owns every prescription of that kind.

Why the split. Where tension peaks comes from how heavy the movement feels through the rep (resistance profile). That is set by the lever the load acts through, the length of the muscle and the position of a machine's cam. That does not change from client to client, so it belongs on the exercise page.

A pause length changes with her phase, her lift and her fatigue, so it lives in one place. A page that printed a pause would soon disagree with the topic that governs it.

How to use the two together. Read Where it's hardest on the page. Then open Tempo and pauses to decide whether she pauses there, and for how long. The Romanian deadlift is a worked example: the page says the bottom, and the tempo topic says what to do at the bottom.

Watch for

A tension peak with no reason behind it. Where it peaks is a mechanical claim, and the page should say why. Look for the lever (moment arm), the muscle length or the cam. If none is given, treat the peak as a guess.

Terms on this page

resistance profile: Where in the range an exercise is hardest and where it is easiest. It is set by the line of the load against the joint, so a dumbbell and a cable can load the same muscle very differently. On a machine the cam sets it.

moment arm: The perpendicular distance between the joint axis and the line of force.
