Low back: the emergency comes first

Verdict

Rule out the emergency before you read anything else about her back.

The low back presentations look ordinary. One of them is not.

Pain rules and when to refer owns the emergency signs. They are the signs of pressure on the nerves at the base of the spine (cauda equina syndrome). Do not read the rest of the low back pages until you know them. She will mention them in passing, putting her shoes on. You have to be listening for them.

Red flags

title: Emergency department the same day if she has back or leg pain plus any of these, starting in the last two weeks

New difficulty starting to wee, or she cannot feel the flow

New numbness or altered feeling around the genitals, between the legs or around the back passage (saddle numbness)

She can no longer feel when her bowel is full

New sexual dysfunction, or loss of vaginal sensation

Severe or worsening weakness or loss of feeling in the legs

emergency: One flag already sends her. Two never make you wait and see. Refer on the symptom, not on a sign. You are not the one who rules it out.

Red flags

title: Also a warning sign. Refer urgently, though not as an emergency

Sudden pain in both legs

Pain in one leg that becomes both legs, or swaps sides

A red flag specific to hinging

She becomes lightheaded on a hinge. Do not treat it as a technique fault. It is a health sign. The plausible causes are the balance system of the inner ear (vestibular) or her blood pressure. Stop the movement and refer.

Say it like this

I need you to get that looked at today, at an emergency department rather than a GP appointment. It's probably nothing, and the one thing it might be is time-sensitive, so I'd rather you went and it was nothing.

Terms on this page

cauda equina syndrome: The bundle of nerves at the base of the spine is being squashed. Early signs are bladder or bowel changes, numbness around the saddle area, new sexual dysfunction or worsening leg weakness.

saddle numbness: Numbness or altered feeling in the area that would touch a saddle: the genitals, the inner thighs, between the legs and around the back passage. A red flag for cauda equina syndrome.

vestibular: The balance system in the inner ear. When it misfires she feels dizzy or lightheaded on a change of head position. A hinge is one such change.
