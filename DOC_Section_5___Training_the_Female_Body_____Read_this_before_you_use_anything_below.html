How reliable is popular advice on training women?

Much of what is said about training women sounds far better supported than it is. Treat a confident female-specific claim as a question, not a fact.

An app, a book or a podcast can state a rule about women's training with total confidence. That confident tone is not the same thing as the rule being correct. Some of these claims are unsafe as written. Some ask a coach to examine her, which a coach must never do. Everything taught in this topic has been checked for safety.

What this means for you. If a popular rule is not taught here, do not run it on the strength of the claim. Check it against this topic first. If it is not here, leave it out of her program and program from what she reports, the same way you would for any client.

Watch for

A rule that arrives with the client. An app, a podcast or a book has told her what women must do differently. She is not wrong to care about it. Check the rule against this topic before you act on it.

Anything that asks you to examine her. No page in this topic does. If a claim needs an internal examination, feeling for something with your hands (palpation), or a grading to act on, it is not yours.

Say it like this

A lot of what's written about women's training sounds more certain than it is. I work off what holds up, and off what you tell me week to week.

Terms on this page

palpation: Feeling a part of the body with the hands to assess it. It is a clinical examination, and a coach never performs one.
