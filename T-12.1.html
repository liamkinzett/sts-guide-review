Building a session · T-12.1

Priority goes where she is freshest

Whatever matters most in this session is performed first. Not the biggest lift, the important one. If her priority is glutes, glutes open the session. If it is delts, delts open it, even though a lateral raise is an isolation and there is a press on the same page. Priority beats pattern.

One exception. A movement paused in the stretch (stretch-paused), or one biased toward the stretched position, is never the first working set. Cold connective tissue is rigid and vulnerable in a loaded stretch. This bites on most STS clients. Glutes are the priority, and the glute stretched-position slot is the B-stance RDL (b-stance), a stretch-paused movement. Read alone, this rule would open leg day with exactly the movement that is barred from the first slot.

So give her priority the first slot it can legally hold, not the first slot. The stretch-paused movement moves down the order. Her warm-up runs regardless of where it lands. Warming up and Tempo and pauses hold that rule. The common practice of putting arms and delts at the start of a lower body day follows the same logic. On a lower day they compete with nothing.

Why it works. Performance falls across a session. Muscle recruitment drops, force production drops, and the last exercise is performed by a more tired lifter than the first. That decline is real whatever the exercise is. The question is not which movement deserves the front of the session. It is which muscle you are willing to train with her worst sets.

Watch for

A program that names a priority in the notes and contradicts it in the running order. What comes first is what you prioritised. The notes are just commentary.

Terms on this page

stretch-paused: A movement with a deliberate pause at the point where the working muscle is longest under load.

b-stance: A split stance where the back foot sits behind on its toe as a kickstand and the front leg does the work.
