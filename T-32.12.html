Faults and what you can actually see · T-32.12

The film has to cover the whole set before you can call a fault absent

A fault is only absent if the film was long enough to show it. First rep to last, one continuous take. Not a clip, not "a few reps", not the best set. The set she actually did.

The camera angle is half of what decides whether a fault could be seen. This is the other half: time. Two faults in the lookup table are set-wide by definition. Position degrading across the set, and range cut short in its late-set form. Neither exists inside one rep. A coach sent three good reps is not screening them and cannot report them absent.

The one thing that makes this cheap. She is filming anyway. The whole set over three reps costs a few seconds of video and nothing else. This is the rare rule with no trade-off attached, which is why it is a rule rather than a default.

How she films a set

The whole set. First rep to last, one continuous take. Not the best set, the set she actually did.

From the angle the fault needs. On a squat, if you get one angle, take the front. Behind for a lean away from the working side. Side for the shoulder blade.

Leave it running through the hard reps. The last two are the ones you need.

If any of that is missing, the note says "not screened". Never "clean".

Watch for

The client who stops recording when the reps get hard. That is precisely where position degrading across the set lives. It is also the most natural thing in the world for her to do.

Say when she sends a clip

Leave it running to the end of the set. The last two reps are the ones I actually need.
