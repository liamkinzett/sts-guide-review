What each block does, and the conjugate program

Accumulation builds the raw material, transmutation turns it into strength, and realisation shows it. The last block does not make her stronger. It reveals the strength the first two built.

The three blocks

# Accumulation

sub: Build the raw material

What she is doing: She gets bigger and more able to do work. Weights feel easy on purpose. The volume is the stimulus rather than the load.

# Transmutation

sub: Turn the new tissue into strength

What she is doing: Volume comes down, intensity goes up, and she switches to the exact lifts she is testing.

# Realisation

sub: Express it

What she is doing: Almost no volume, very heavy singles and doubles, an aggressive taper. This block reveals the strength the first two blocks built.

Use the block peak for anyone working back from a fixed test date. It is the most complete peak of the three when there is a date on the calendar.

The conjugate peak, in one line: near-maximal work every week on a rotating variation, with bar speed trained separately on its own day. Nothing ever goes stale.

Four days: ME Lower / ME Upper / DE Lower / DE Upper. ME is max effort (max effort), working up to a top single or triple. DE is dynamic effort (dynamic effort), light and fast.

Reading the table. "→ 3RM" means she works up to the heaviest triple she can do that day (3RM). "→ 1RM" is the heaviest single (1RM). Week 11 rehearses her first attempt (opener). Week 12 is a full taper (taper) into the test late in the week.

The lifts in it. A board press stops the bar on boards laid on her chest, so the top of the lift is trained (board press). A floor press is a bench press done lying on the floor, which cuts the bottom of the range. Chains on the squat add load as she stands and take it off as she descends.

Bands and chains are accommodating resistance (accommodating resistance). They add load through the rising part of the lift, where she is strongest. The strong top is overloaded without the weak bottom becoming impossible. Levers and moment arms has the mechanism.

The box squat, the board presses, the floor press and the block pull are all lifts cut short of their full range. They are partials (partials). Here they are heavy rotating variations for a competitive powerlifter. They let her go heavy every week without wearing out the tested lift, and each rotates out within two weeks. That is a different job from a partial used to fix a fault, which The strength system rejects. Squat variations and Bench variations hold each lift's own page.

Terms on this page

3RM: The heaviest weight she can lift three times with good form.

1RM: The heaviest weight she can lift once with good form.

opener: Her first attempt on the platform. Light enough to be certain, heavy enough to set up the attempts that follow.

taper: Cutting the training load right down before a test, so fatigue clears and strength shows. It is the final days before the test.

partials: A lift done through part of its range rather than all of it, cut short on purpose by a box, a board, blocks or pins.

max effort: Working up to the heaviest weight she can lift that day on the chosen lift, for a single or a triple.

dynamic effort: Light, fast sets, usually 40% to 70% of her max, that train her to produce force quickly.

board press: A bench press where boards on her chest stop the bar a few inches short of touching, so the top of the lift is trained.

accommodating resistance: Bands or chains added to a bar so the load rises as she moves through the part of the lift where she is strongest. It matches the lift's strength curve so the top of the range is overloaded too.
