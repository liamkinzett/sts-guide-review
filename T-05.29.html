Relaxin, breastfeeding and joint laxity

The joint-loosening hormone relaxin stays elevated for 12 months after birth, and longer while she breastfeeds. Whether it explains what she feels is not something you tell her either way.

What holds. Relaxin (relaxin) remains elevated for 12 months postpartum, and longer if breastfeeding. Joint laxity does increase during pregnancy.

What you do not claim. One view is that relaxin directly loosens the pelvic joints. That would predispose her to pain at the front of the pelvis (pubic symphysis) and at the back (SI joint). Joint looseness does rise after birth, but relaxin levels do not explain it on their own. It has several causes. Do not tell her relaxin explains what she is feeling. Do not tell her it does not.

What this means for loading her. The load model does not change for it. The postpartum return runs on the same 2 to 3 RIR, reps in reserve (RIR), from week one, whatever the mechanism. A client reporting joint looseness is read the ordinary way, by how each movement responds. Pain rules and when to refer holds that sort. There is no relaxin-specific protocol.

Say it like this

There's a hormone that stays elevated while you're breastfeeding. On its own it doesn't explain joints feeling loose, and I can't tell you either way that it's what you're feeling. Either way we load off how the joint responds, not off a theory.

The fuelling half of this population is the breastfeeding page. This page is the joint-mechanics half and stops where that one starts.

Terms on this page

relaxin: A hormone of pregnancy that loosens ligaments and joints. It stays elevated after birth and for longer while she breastfeeds.

pubic symphysis: The joint at the very front of the pelvis where the two pubic bones meet.

SI joint: The sacroiliac joint, where the base of the spine meets the pelvis on each side, near the dimples of the low back.

RIR: Reps in reserve. How many more reps she could have done when the set stopped. 2 RIR means she stopped two reps short of failure.
