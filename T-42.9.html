The coaching relationship · T-42.9

When you get it wrong, lead with the impact, never your intention

When you get something wrong, lead with the impact. Never with your intention. The failure mode is the one that feels most natural.

The wrong shape. "I got this wrong, but know that my intention was good." That sentence is about you. It asks her to manage your feelings about the mistake.

The right shape.

The apology that works

No. I got this wrong. Here's what happened because I got it wrong. And this is what I'm going to do to put it right.

It is really the only thing you can do in an apology.

The same instruction, twice more. If you get it wrong, own up to it, then look at yourself and rewire your approach. Do not double down. And the sequence afterwards: okay, I got it wrong. Now find the problem. Problem-solve. Then put things in place so it does not come up again.

After the apology

Name the impact. What happened to her because of it.

Name the fix. What you are doing about it, specifically.

Find the cause. What in your process let it happen.

Close the hole. So the same thing cannot arrive twice.

Why it works. Explaining your intention asks her to reassure you. Naming the impact and the fix leaves her with something she can use.

What is not this page. Changing your mind on new information is not a mistake and does not get this apology. Her apology to you runs in reverse: accept it in a sentence and move to the next step.

Watch for

"But I meant well." It may be true. It is still about you. Leave it out.
