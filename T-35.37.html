Anaphylaxis: lay her flat, injector, triple zero

Verdict

Lay her flat, give her adrenaline injector, call triple zero (000). In that order.

You are not deciding whether it is anaphylaxis. If in doubt, give the adrenaline injector.

Follow her own action plan, in its order, and lying flat comes first. A severe, fast allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) is handled by the plan she carries. The standard plan across Australia and New Zealand is the ASCIA Action Plan for Anaphylaxis. Watch for any one of the signs below.

Red flags

title: Watch for any one of these signs

Difficult or noisy breathing

Swelling of tongue

Swelling or tightness in throat

Wheeze or persistent cough

Difficulty talking or hoarse voice

Persistent dizziness or collapse

emergency: Any one sign: lay her flat, give the adrenaline injector, phone an ambulance on 000 (AU) or 111 (NZ).

The actions, in the plan's own order and words

LAY PERSON FLAT - do NOT allow them to stand or walk

GIVE ADRENALINE INJECTOR

Phone ambulance - 000 (AU) or 111 (NZ)

Phone family/emergency contact

Laying her flat is first and it is not a formality. Standing or walking a person in anaphylaxis is the part that kills. The plan puts it above the injector for that reason.

You are not deciding whether it is anaphylaxis, which is exactly why this is inside your scope. The plan settles the judgement itself: IF IN DOUBT GIVE ADRENALINE INJECTOR. You never name a condition, and nothing here asks you to name one.

What comes from her plan and your first-aid training, not from here

Injector technique. It comes from her device's own instructions and from your current first-aid training. Keep that training current.

The mild-to-moderate column, antihistamine dosing and the observation period. Those belong to her plan and her clinician.

You never advise on a drug, and that is unchanged. Following a client's own action plan in an emergency is first aid, not prescribing.

Ask at intake. A client with a known anaphylaxis risk should carry her own ASCIA Action Plan and her own injector. The sixth question of the pre-exercise screen, other conditions needing special consideration, is where that surfaces.

Terms on this page

anaphylaxis: A severe, fast allergic reaction that can close the airway or drop blood pressure. It is treated by lying her flat, then the adrenaline injector, then an ambulance.
