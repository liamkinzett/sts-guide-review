Recognising under-fuelling and where it goes

Chronic under-fuelling drives a syndrome that winds the whole body down. Body composition does not screen it, so never conclude she is fine from how she looks.

What under-fuelling is. She is not eating enough for her training and the rest of what her body needs (low energy availability). Chronic under-fuelling drives relative energy deficiency in sport (RED-S). That shuts down resting metabolic rate, immune function, thyroid output and bone formation.

The most important line here. A client can look exactly as you expect a healthy athlete to look and be deep in it. Never conclude she is fine from how she presents.

What you can actually see

# Training response

sub: An inability to gain or maintain muscle despite progressive lifting

Why it matters most to you: You are the only person in her life watching a progressive lifting record. A client who is training well and not adapting is information nobody else has.

# Physical

sub: Persistent fatigue, disturbed sleep, gut distress and bloating, chronic low iron, low libido, mood changes, repeated illness

# Performance

sub: Reduced coordination, impaired judgement, reduced muscular endurance, longer recovery

# Behavioural

sub: Dietary restriction, tight mental control around eating, a drive for thinness, body image distortion

Note: Tight mental control around eating is called cognitive restraint (cognitive restraint).

# Menstrual

sub: Absent periods for three months or more, or infrequent ones

Void in a pill user: A bleed on the pill is a withdrawal bleed. It tells you nothing here.

Not one of these is diagnostic. Together they are a reason to ask.

Where this fires. Not while you are reading this topic. It fires when a client stalls. Volume gives you a four-step list to work in order, and under-fuelling sits at the recovery step.

Red flags

title: Refer. The destination is a team, not one practitioner

A diagnosed eating disorder

Any sign of an unstable heart or circulation (haemodynamic instability)

A suspected bone stress injury

Pain rules and when to refer owns the routing. The destination is multidisciplinary rather than a single practitioner.

Watch for

Trying to measure energy availability yourself. The measurement is error-prone even for specialists. It depends on self-reported food and exercise diaries. No blood test catches it early. You are noticing, not quantifying.

Scoring her against a questionnaire you found online. STS does not run one. Do not use its item counts, cut-offs or referral thresholds.

Terms on this page

low energy availability: Not enough food coming in to cover both her training and the rest of what her body needs to run.

RED-S: Relative energy deficiency in sport. She is eating too little for what she does, and her body starts shutting things down. Metabolism, immunity, thyroid, bone and the cycle all wind down.

cognitive restraint: Deliberately and continually limiting what she eats by rule, regardless of hunger.

haemodynamic instability: Her heart and circulation are not holding up: fainting, a very slow or racing pulse, or blood pressure that drops when she stands.
