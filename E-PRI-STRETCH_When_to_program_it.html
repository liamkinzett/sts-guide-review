When to use it

The problem you notice. There is a range she needs in today's session and cannot reach walking in. Or she reports tight hip flexors and you are about to load hip extension. Or she is very strong, very stiff and short of movement options. A stretch is the right first move in the first two. In most other cases a coach reaches for one, it is the wrong move.

Warming up owns whether this runs at all, and it owns the durations. These rows say when a stretch is the right first move.

Reach for it when

# There is a range she needs in today's session and cannot reach walking in

sub: The working set is going to ask for a position she does not have cold

Confirm it: Watch her first rehearsal of the main lift. The range she is short of is the one to open.

What it gets her: Access to that range inside the warm-up, so the working set can reinforce it. A stretch in the warm-up lets her access ranges she cannot access walking in. A better movement pattern and some load can then reinforce them.

# She reports tight hip flexors and you are about to load hip extension

sub: The hinge or the thrust is coming

Confirm it: She reports the front of the hip as tight (hip flexors), and the session loads hip extension.

What it gets her: Step one of the three-step framework STS uses identically for the hip, the ankle and the shoulder. Couch stretch at roughly 3 × 30 seconds, then the drill, then loaded hip extension. Step three is the point. Step one exists to make itself redundant.

# She is very strong, very stiff, and short of movement options

sub: The powerlifter case

Confirm it: Her lifts are heavy, her movement menu is three barbell lifts, and every position looks the same.

What it gets her: Mobility delivered as loaded variety rather than as remedial drills. Instead of a pinned-down bench press, a pull-up and a landmine press. Instead of sumo deadlifts and squats, something on one leg. That variety, done correctly, improves her mobility.

Three reasons coaches reach for a stretch, and none of them is a reason

She feels tight. A pelvis tipped forward (anterior pelvic tilt) leaves the hamstrings already lengthened and still reported as tight. The length is being perceived as tight. Stretching those hamstrings will not provide any beneficial result. The correction is teaching a better relationship at the pelvis. Ask what the tissue's actual length is before you stretch it.

She is weak. Weak people need to get strong. Being strong is not better for everybody, but part of her immobility may be that she is not strong. The third row above is the carve-out, and it is scoped to the already-strong.

It fills the slot. You have her twice a week for an hour. Is the best return on those 2 hours going to be 30 minutes of drills turning the hip in (internal rotation) and out (external rotation)? Probably not. Warming up owns the decision.

The pigeon and the knee

With a torn cartilage pad or other knee pathology, a wide foot on the pigeon is contraindicated (meniscus). Pull the foot in toward the groin, or use the seated figure-four. Pain rules and when to refer owns the triage.

Pick the PNF lat stretch and the pigeon the same way: for a range today's session needs. Do not prescribe them because the lat or the glute is the day's muscle.

Setup. Stack the torso, do not stretch a cold muscle, and read Setup for each stretch.

Terms on this page

hip flexors: The muscles at the front of the hip that lift the thigh. Kneeling with the hip pushed forward stretches them.

anterior pelvic tilt: The pelvis tipped forward, so the lower back arches more.

meniscus: The cartilage pad inside the knee. Twisting the knee under load is what injures it.

internal rotation: Turning a limb inward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face back or down, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more toward the middle. It is the hip range most often short and most often neglected.

external rotation: Turning a limb outward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face forward or up, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more to the side. It is the direction the pigeon takes the hip.
