Ankle pain, continued: return to impact, and escalate

Step 4. Change

Do not return her to impact on how she feels. The gate is a calf that can move at least her own bodyweight on one leg. This is the protocol behind it.

How the heel raise is tested

Seated single-leg heel raise, six-rep max. Knee bent to 100 degrees, in a Smith machine, bar across the thigh with padding. Starting weight 80% of bodyweight. Warm up with 10 double-leg reps at that weight. Record the highest weight moved for six reps at a 1-second lift, 1-second lower, to full height.

Count the bar. The published values include the Smith bar at 9.3 kg. Leave it out and your number is wrong.

Standing single-leg heel raise, endurance. Warm up with 10 double-leg raises. Then full-range single-leg raises, knee straight, to a metronome. Stop at voluntary fatigue, or when she fails to hold height and tempo for three consecutive raises.

Read that table the right way round

The gate that moved people up a level was pain: 3 out of 10 or less on a five-rep single-leg hop. That is the impact ceiling Pain rules and when to refer already holds. The strength numbers describe what capacity looked like at each stage.

The bodyweight floor comes from one injured man. Treat it as a floor to clear, not a threshold that clears her.

Fifteen male recreational runners. Nothing here was measured on women, let alone perimenopausal, postmenopausal or postpartum women. Keep that beside every number above.

Then re-run the heel-elevation test and the foot-shape check to see whether anything moved. Read progress off her day rather than off the bar. She will feel good before she is good.

Step 5. Escalate

Red flags

title: Refer, and change the plan

Anything acute. If something went really wrong, really fast, she gets checked out

Suspected Achilles rupture or calf tear: a sudden pop in the back of the leg, or she suddenly cannot push off. Same day

Suspected tendon pain at the heel attachment

Everything in the general stop-and-refer list, plus seven out of ten reported consistently, plus pain still consistent between three days and one week

emergency: None of this is softened by anything above it.
