The coaching relationship · T-42.36

A retraction the next morning is not the end of it

A retraction the next morning is not the end of the conversation. It is the same two-sided silence, wearing different words. This is the usual choice.

Two meanings again. "Ignore that, I was being dramatic" could mean exactly what it says. It could also mean she said something true at midnight and is now frightened of what saying it cost her. You cannot tell which from outside.

Do not take the retraction at face value. Do not relitigate the midnight message either. Say what you noticed. Ask what is going on now, not what was going on then. The question is about the retraction itself, not an interrogation of the original complaint.

About the retraction, not the message

No need to take it back. I'd rather know how you're feeling about it this morning than pretend last night didn't happen.

Why it works. Accepting the retraction closes a door she may want left open. Reopening the midnight message makes her defend it. Asking about now does neither.

Watch for

Relief. The retraction lets you off a hard conversation. That is exactly why it should not end one.
