Writing program instructions · T-40.4

Name the substitution before she needs it

If a slot has a likely equipment problem, the substitution goes in the notes, not in a message on the day.

The exercise pages own what the substitution is. Every page from Glutes through to Priming lifts carries a substitution ladder: the movements that stand in for that exercise, in order. This rule is only that the substitution must be written down in advance.

Know what you will find when you look. All 44 exercise pages carry a substitution ladder, and 42 of them include at least one rung STS does not program. Read down the ladder to the first rung that is both available in her gym and one STS programs. That is the substitution you write.

Where no rung on the ladder fits, fall back on Choosing exercises' selection logic. Ask what quality the movement was buying and choose a movement that buys the same thing. The note then says what that quality was, so the next coach can see the reasoning.

A worked case. Her program has a leg press in it and her gym has one, busy at the hour she trains. That is a likely equipment problem. Open the Quads page and read down the leg press ladder. Stop at the first rung her gym has and STS programs, and write it against the slot. She never has to message you from the gym floor.

How the note reads

If the leg press is taken, use the swap written under it. Same reps, same effort.

Why it works. A substitution decided in the moment, in a busy gym, by a client, is a different exercise chosen on appearance. Strength and skill names that error: specificity is about the adaptation, not the appearance. A movement that looks like the original and buys something else is not a substitution.
