Tempo and pauses · T-15.2

The notation, and what the second number means

Four digits, always in this order: eccentric, pause at the end of the eccentric, concentric, pause at the end of the concentric. Positions 2 and 4 are defined by the phase, not by where the weight is in space.

PositionWhat it is
1The lowering phase (eccentric), in seconds
2The pause at the end of the eccentric
3The lifting phase (concentric), in seconds
4The pause at the end of the concentric

`X` in any position means maximal intent rather than a count.

Why the second number is the trap. Take a seated hamstring curl at `3110`. The pause is at the end of the eccentric, which on that machine is knees extended and the pad up. That is where the hamstring is at its longest (lengthened). Written as "a pause at the bottom" it is wrong, because the bottom of that machine's arc is the shortened position. One coach describes the same pause as "at the top of the range". Two competent coaches, one pause, opposite words.

The same trap sits on a lat pulldown, a pec deck and a reverse hyperextension. It sits on every cable movement where the muscle's stretch is not the low point of the arc. Keep two ideas apart. The exercise has a bottom, where the weight is lowest. The muscle has a lengthened point, where it is longest under load. The code is written to the muscle, never to the weight.

Why it works. A tempo code is written by one person and executed by another, often months apart and usually without a conversation. Any digit that requires the reader to work out which end of the machine you meant is a digit that will be guessed.

Watch for

"Pause at the bottom" written into a program note. It undoes the rule. Write "pause in the stretch" or write the number.

Terms on this page

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.

concentric: The lifting half of a rep, where the muscle shortens under load.

lengthened: The point in the rep where the working muscle is at its longest while it carries the load. It describes the muscle's length, not whether she feels a stretch. It is not the same as the low point of the exercise.

tempo: The four-digit code that sets how many seconds each phase of a rep takes: lowering, pause, lifting, pause.
