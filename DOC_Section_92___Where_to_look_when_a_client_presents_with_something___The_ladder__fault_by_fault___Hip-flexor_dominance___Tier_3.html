Hip-flexor dominance

You ask about this; you do not watch for it.

If she feels a pinch in the hip crease rather than the lower abs, ask what the set was for before you change anything. On some prescriptions the hip flexors are the target, not a fault. Only if they are not the target do you reduce the range or change the variation.

Where it shows, and the fix

# Leg raise

sub: You ask about this, you do not watch for it. She reports a pinch in the hip crease rather than work in the lower abs.

Do this: Ask what the set was for first. On some prescriptions the hip flexors are the target. Only then reduce range or change the variation

There is no set cue written for this fault. Say the fix in plain words, one thing this set.

Other cues on those exercises. Not about this fault. Use them when the same set needs them.

Cues

When: Leg raise

Say: Body hollow.

Why: Set it before the first rep. It is the setup and the anti-sway measure in two words.

When: Leg raise

Say: Flex the spine.

Why: The rep-defining cue. She pulls the knees up with a sit-up sensation and finishes beyond 90 degrees of hip flexion.

When: Leg raise

Say: Lower your legs back to the starting position under control. Using an eccentric tempo (lowering slowly) is a fantastic way to maintain tension on the target muscles (your core and quads).

Why: Tempo and pauses owns the tempo itself. All three cues STS carries for this movement are internal.

Terms on this page

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.
