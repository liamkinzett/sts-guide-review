What she will ask you · T-47.10

"Can I change the shape of a muscle?"

Some of shape is trainable and some is not. Where the line falls is not sharp, and the effects are small. This is a judgement call, and the answer has three parts.

What can change without a fibre getting thicker. A muscle can grow in length, by adding contractile units in series (sarcomerogenesis), which alters its physical orientation. Training at longer muscle lengths produces more growth overall than training at shorter ones. Muscle and growth owns that mechanism.

On whether you can bias where along a muscle it grows. Longer-length training grows the muscle more as a whole. Growth measured at the top, the middle and the bottom of a muscle barely differs between sites. So the answer to "can I fill the gap near my elbow" is: possibly a little, and the effect is small.

Say all three parts

Possibly a little. Training the muscle through its longest range gives it the best chance. And the effect is small, so we don't build the program around it.

What is fixed. Where a muscle attaches and how long its belly is are the part of her shape that training does not move. Nothing here explains those, and you should not try to.

Why it works. Three parts is the whole answer. "Yes" over-promises. "No" is wrong. "A little, and here is how" is true and gives her something to do.

Watch for

Building a program around a peak or a gap. The effect is small. Build for the muscle and let the shape be what it is.

Terms on this page

sarcomerogenesis: A muscle growing longer by adding new contractile units end to end, rather than growing thicker.
