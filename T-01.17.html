Muscle and growth · T-01.17

Do later reps, and later sets, give more growth?

Two different questions hide inside this: are later reps within a set the better stimulus, and are later sets within a session the better stimulus. They don't share an answer.

Within a set, the later reps carry a disproportionate share of the growth stimulus, not the whole of it. Across sets, the first set gives the most growth for its fatigue. Each set after it gives less. Do not tell her set three or four is where the growth happens.

Within a set. On a light load, the big motor units only join in as the small ones tire. You can see it on a bench press. The lift slows from about 1.2 seconds on rep one to 3.3 seconds on rep five as the big units come online. The electrical activity in the muscle rises across the set to match. The early reps of a set are not its best stimulus.

Across sets. Two things are true at once, and they point different ways. Fatigue left over from set one brings the big units in earlier in set two. So a later set holds more tension-bearing reps for the same rep count. Myo-reps and rest-pause sets (rest-pause) are built on this, and they match or beat straight sets for growth.

At the same time, set one gives the most growth for the fatigue it costs (stimulus-to-fatigue ratio). Each set after it adds real but shrinking stimulus while the fatigue cost keeps climbing. Each extra weekly set adds roughly 0.37% more muscle size.

What it means: More sets means more total stimulus | It does not mean set four beats set one

Where STS lands. More sets means more total stimulus in a session. It does not mean set four is individually a better stimulus than set one. The ratio runs first set best and falling from there, which is the reverse of the usual gym premise. Choosing exercises defines the stimulus-to-fatigue ratio. This page supplies the direction it runs.

A worked case. A client does four sets of leg press. Set one gives her the most growth for its fatigue. Set four adds a little more growth and a lot more fatigue. That is why we cap sets per exercise instead of chasing volume. Volume has the caps.

What this means for coaching. Later sets add real extra stimulus at a fatigue cost that grows faster than the benefit.

Do not tell her later reps within a set are junk either. On a light load, they are the reps that finally reach the big fibres. The earlier reps simply bought the fatigue that made that possible.

Say the mechanism, not the percentages

These figures came from single-joint holds, sub-maximal bench presses and pooled multi-week volume programs. Not from four consecutive sets of a barbell squat. Say the mechanism. Do not present the numbers as measured on a squat.

Terms on this page

stimulus-to-fatigue ratio: How much growth signal a set or exercise gives for the fatigue it costs. It is a way of thinking about an exercise's price, not a number anyone measures. A high ratio means a lot of stimulus for little tiredness.

rest-pause: One hard set broken into pieces by very short rests, so more reps are done near failure with a given load. The big motor units are already working when each piece restarts.
