Steps and cardio · T-17.10

Every client gets a step goal. Nutrition sets the number

A step target goes in the program regardless of goal, phase or pathway. The number comes from the Nutrition Manual, not from this topic.

Why it works. Steps are the cheapest tool available. They raise expenditure substantially, they cost almost nothing in recovery, and unlike cardio they do not compete with the lifting. That is exactly why the Nutrition Manual spends steps before it spends cardio.

Why the number is not ours. It is set against her intake, her rate of loss and her life. The Nutrition Manual owns all three. A training guide that also set step targets would produce two numbers that disagree the first time either side changed.

When you may deviate. Two cases.

  • Her work already puts her well past any target you would set. She has a step count rather than a step goal. Setting one changes nothing except how much of her day she spends looking at a watch.
  • Her walking is limited by a joint, an injury or a condition. The expenditure has to be found somewhere else. Steps are the wrong lever there rather than a low one.
    • Watch for

    Steps quietly becoming cardio. A client told to add 4,000 steps who achieves it on a treadmill at pace has been given cardio. Count it as cardio against her dose.
