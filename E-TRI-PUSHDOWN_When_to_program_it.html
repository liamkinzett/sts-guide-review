When to use it

The problem you notice. Her shoulder will not tolerate overhead work at all and you need a way back into loading it. Or her elbows flare, or complain, on every triceps extension. Or she wants to get stronger at dips. The pushdown answers all three, and the setup, not the load, is usually the fix.

Reach for it when

# Her shoulder will not tolerate overhead work at all

sub: A way back into loading the shoulder, at the bottom of a stated ladder

Confirm it: Every overhead position provokes her. The ladder runs single-arm cable pulldown, then single-arm cable row, then arm training.

What it gets her: Movement through the shoulder again. A rope pushdown is the bottom rung: "arm training is a pretty basic introduction to doing things with the stabilizer shoulder. Getting movement through the shoulder a little bit... we're training muscles across the shoulder joint".

# She reports neck or upper-trap symptoms and you want the accessory slot to do two jobs

sub: Shoulder extension bought inside the triceps slot

Confirm it: She reports the symptoms, and her program has a triceps slot to spend.

What it gets her: The cross-body version (cross-body extension) puts the shoulder into extension while the elbow works. It is named as the swap for a generic pushdown: "something to get some secondary shoulder stuff going on".

# She is trying to get stronger at dips

sub: Carry-over from joint-position exposure, not from the triceps

Confirm it: Dips are in her program and stalling.

What it gets her: The cross-body version is the exposure drill. "just get as much exposure to those joint positions that you can". "and you're going to see a good carry over to actually getting stronger and better at your dips".

# You are choosing between a dip and a pushdown for triceps volume

sub: One buys cheap volume. The other buys a compound's fatigue bill

Confirm it: Ask whether this session, or this week, can afford a loadable compound's fatigue cost right now.

What it gets her: Where fatigue is the constrained resource, the pushdown delivers isolated triceps volume without touching that budget. That means priming, a cut, or a week already carrying heavy compounds elsewhere. It can be pushed closer to failure than the dip can. Where there is fatigue budget to spend, an assisted dip buys chest and shoulder work as well. That can be the better buy when those muscles also need the volume. The two are not a straight swap for each other.

# Her elbows flare, or the elbow complains, on any triceps extension

sub: The line of force running straight through the joint

Confirm it: Film from the front. The cable is offset sideways from the elbow, or the elbows drift out.

What it gets her: Single-arm cable or neutral-grip handles, so the line of force passes through the elbow rather than across it. Flare is a joint-fit signal, not a discipline problem, and the flared version leads to chronic elbow pain. This is a setup requirement, not a coaching correction.

What it will not do, and the selection error to avoid

The shoulder stays neutral, so the long head is not loaded at length here (long head). Two pushdowns are not two triceps slots.

If the long head at length is what is missing, that is Overhead cable extension. Nothing on this page substitutes for it.

The cross-body version has two uses. Name which one you are programming. It is a shoulder-function exercise that also trains the triceps. And it is a shortened-position triceps exercise that is effective but "would not rank at the top". Which one governs its slot is your call to make out loud.

Setup. A high pulley, shoulder blades back and down, upper arm pinned at the side, the cable lined up through the elbow. Setup has the full list.

Terms on this page

cross-body extension: A single-arm cable triceps extension where the elbow extends across the front of the body, with the shoulder pulled back into extension.

long head: The head of a muscle that crosses the shoulder joint as well as the elbow: on the triceps it only stretches with the arm raised, on the biceps only with the arm behind the body.
