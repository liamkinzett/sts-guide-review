The coaching relationship · T-42.15

Lay the hard part out at the start

Lay the hard part out at the start. Then you never have to walk it back. It is a lot easier if you lay it all out up front than to unwind it later.

The conversation this is for. Her goal does not work the way she thinks it does. It takes longer. It is more uncomfortable. It needs things she did not expect to have to do. Say so before she starts, not in week eight.

Name the behaviour plainly when it is the behaviour. You're not paying attention. You don't prioritise this. You don't have the time, or you can't make the time. Said plainly, licensed by the intake agreement, these are observations. Dressed up, they are hints she can ignore.

Where the outcome is genuinely uncertain, say so and give her the next step. The pairing is the point.

An uncertain outcome, with the next step attached

I don't know what the outcome is going to be. I've had people in similar scenarios who had favourable outcomes, and people in similar scenarios who had less favourable ones. But there is something that we can do.

Softening the delivery for a sensitive client. The message is fixed first. The wording is keyed to her. Writing to a client carries a worked before-and-after of the same hard message delivered bluntly and then landably. How sensitive she is remains your calibration. What must not move while you calibrate is the message.

Why it works. Told at the start, the hard part is the terms of the deal and she chose them. Told later, the same truth is a walk-back, and it costs you authority every time.

Watch for

Saying "it depends" and stopping. Uncertainty on its own is not a conversation. Uncertainty plus the next step is.
