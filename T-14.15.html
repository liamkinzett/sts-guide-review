Blood flow restriction is for when heavy loading is off the table

Default to standard loading. Reach for blood flow restriction (BFR) only where heavy loading is off the table, never as an upgrade on it.

What happens under the cuff. Two things. The cuff traps blood in the limb, which drives local oxygen shortage, metabolite build-up and cell swelling, and that signals growth directly. And the cuff makes light loads fatiguing very fast. That forces early recruitment of the biggest, fastest fibres (high-threshold motor units) at loads that would not normally reach them. As bar speed slows under that fatigue, those fibres express high mechanical tension. Both happen. Both are why a light load can build muscle under a cuff.

Where it earns a place. Post-surgical rehabilitation is the strongest case. After a knee reconstruction, low-load BFR restores quad size where heavy loading is not yet possible. At 30% of her one rep max (1RM) it beats heavy loading at 70% on pain and function. It also holds muscle during cast immobilisation. In older adults it matches heavy training's strength and size gains at low load. BFR walking gives low-impact conditioning.

Where it is oversold. For a healthy client who can load heavy. BFR does not improve on ordinary heavier training. When sets are taken to failure either way, BFR and heavy loading produce equal muscle growth. Its edge is training around a joint that cannot take load. It is not raw superiority.

Why it matters. A client who has heard "BFR builds muscle at low load" without the rehab context asks for it as a shortcut. For someone who can load heavy, it is not one.

Cuff pressure. The general range is 40 to 80% of the pressure that fully stops blood flow to the limb (limb occlusion pressure). Use that range for her as well. Pregnancy and the postpartum period belong to The female body. This page does not extend into them.

Watch for

Reaching for BFR because it sounds advanced rather than because she genuinely cannot tolerate the load that would otherwise be prescribed. That confuses a workaround for load intolerance with an upgrade on load.

Say it like this

That's a tool for when we can't load the joint properly. You can load it properly, so we're not reaching for it here.

Terms on this page

BFR: Blood flow restriction. A cuff on the upper part of the limb restricts blood flow out of the muscle while she trains with a light load.

high-threshold motor units: The large motor units that drive the biggest, fastest fibres.

1RM: The heaviest weight she can lift once with good form.

limb occlusion pressure: The cuff pressure that fully stops blood flow to the limb. BFR is set as a percentage of it.
