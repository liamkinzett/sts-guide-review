Practising a lift · T-48.6

How identical her reps should look depends on where she is

A beginner's reps vary so much that adding variety is pointless. A skilled lifter's reps look the same from first to last. Which standard you hold her to depends on where she is.

Early on, her execution is highly variable. Her first squat, her second and her third differ so much they may as well be three different exercises. Her brain wants a deliberate path: one movement, one setup, small steps toward the thing you want her to learn. Give it that.

Skilled lifters do not miss. Each rep, set and session looks the same at the beginning as at the end. That consistency is the destination. It is not the entry requirement.

Consistency is the end state, not the starting standard. So a beginner needs a deliberate, narrow path. An advanced lifter is judged on whether her reps match. Hold each to the standard for where she is, and do not fail a beginner for looking like a beginner.

What the path looks like in a session. Learning runs in stages: thinking every rep through, then linking the pieces together, then doing it without thinking. A session can be built the same way. Isolated joint drills in the warm-up, the pieces put back into the pattern, then load.

Watch for

Rotating exercises on a beginner "for variety". Her reps already vary more than any exercise swap would. She needs repetition on one path.

Accepting rep-to-rep variation from an intermediate. By then her reps should match. Variation that persists is a sign to go and look.
