Motivation and adherence · T-44.13

Ask the questions. Do not supply the answer

Ask the questions. Do not supply the answer. If you want somebody to do something, lead the horse to water by asking the right questions. Then she feels she has some autonomy in the decision.

The method has a name. Helping someone change through questions (motivational interviewing) has four components:

The four components

Open-ended questions. Questions she cannot answer with yes or no. What got in the way this week?

Affirmations. Naming what she did, specifically.

Reflective listening. Saying back what she said, so she hears it.

Summarising. Pulling the conversation together so she leaves with her own conclusion.

This is also the answer to a client who asks you to motivate her. There is no speech. The method is questions. Ask her what she wants, what is in the way, and what she would do about it if she were coaching herself.

Why it works. A decision she reaches herself is one she owns. A decision you hand her is one she can hand back. The coaching relationship makes the same point about open-ended questions over verdicts: an open-ended question makes her supply the observation. A verdict makes her defend herself.

Watch for

A question with the answer already in it. "Don't you think you should sleep more?" is a verdict with a question mark. Ask what she has noticed about her sleep.

Terms on this page

motivational interviewing: A way of helping someone change through questions rather than instructions. You ask open-ended questions, affirm what she did, reflect back what she said and summarise, so the decision stays hers.
