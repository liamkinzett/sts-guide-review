Fatigue and recovery · T-03.17

Growth signalling and strength under sleep loss

A single bad night costs the growth signal quickly. Strength on the day barely moves. The two run on different clocks, so do not judge one night's sleep by one session's numbers.

On the night. The signalling cost of one bad night is real and fast. The figures are in Assessing a new client. The strength cost from the same night is small. For a short night rather than a sleepless one, it barely shows. The previous page carries that.

So the bar lags the biology. One bad night hits the growth signal before anything shows up in the logbook. A good session after a bad night does not mean the night was free. A rough session does not mean the night caused it.

Over weeks, the picture inverts. Take six weeks of real training under mild habitual short sleep, about 1.5 hours of nightly debt. Strength and muscle gains came out no different from a well-slept group. Training in the background, at adequate protein, absorbs a moderate chronic debt. Tell an anxious client this about a bad week.

The question you are answering. Is this an isolated bad night, or a pattern of chronic short sleep against otherwise consistent training and eating? The first is a signalling cost that will not show in this week's numbers. The second, at a moderate level, has not cost the outcome, provided the rest of the program is intact.

Say it like this

One bad week of sleep isn't going to undo your training. Keep eating and keep showing up, and we'll fix the sleep.

Watch for

Stretching the six-week result too far. It came from previously untrained middle-aged adults on an elastic band program. Do not promise an advanced lifter on heavy free weights the same protection without saying so.
