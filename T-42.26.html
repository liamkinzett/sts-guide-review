The coaching relationship · T-42.26

She asks why you will not just give her what she asked for

She asks why you will not just give her what she asked for. Explain the why first. Keep her want as the goal. Release her only if she still wants it sold.

The want is never argued with. It is accepted as the goal, and the needs are built around it.

Client line, then coach line

"I want a 300 pound bench."

"Okay. Well, let me figure out what you need."

The frame that answers her question, said to her openly. You filtered in what you wanted, and we filled in the gaps with what we need. Said out loud, it keeps you on the right side of telling her what you know.

When she keeps pressing, the escalation is scripted, and it is not a capitulation.

After the explanation, if she still does not agree

I always explain thoroughly why we do the things that we do. If you want to keep critiquing what I'm doing and don't believe in it, then here's a coach who will give you what you want. If you still don't agree with the why, don't hire me as your coach.

Read the order. The thorough explanation comes first, every time. The release comes only after it. What never happens is the program quietly converting into what she asked for. Where the release is the ending, ending it is a legitimate decision and refunding is the named option.

A blunter register exists, for an athlete. A requested metric is declined on its value rather than negotiated with. You do 15, I think you're less cool than the guy who did 10. That register is athlete-blunt. Writing to a client keys the wording to the client while the message does not move. What survives the translation is the move: the metric she is demanding is declined on its value.

The frame under all of it, said about a client and never to her. She comes to you because she thinks that is what she wants to hear. Your job is to tell her what she needs to hear. That is the stance. The lines above are the only wording.

Why it works. Arguing with the want makes her defend it. Accepting it as the goal and filling the needs in around it, out loud, leaves her nothing to defend. The intake agreement is what licenses the harder second conversation when it comes.

Watch for

Releasing her before you have explained. The release is the last step, not the first. Skipped explanation is the failure, not her disagreement.
