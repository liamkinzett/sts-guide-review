Which phase she is in, and when it ends

Every phase has its own end condition, and that condition is what you test. The calendar tells you where to start looking, never when to stop.

This page is the rules of this topic in the order you use them. It starts with a pathway already chosen. It tells you when the phase she is in finishes. It does not place a client from scratch; that is Assessing a new client.

Two questions that only look like this one

# Which phases she runs, and in what order

sub: That is her pathway, not her phase

Where it goes: Assessing a new client. It got her to a pathway and this topic is what sits inside it. It also owns when a holding phase is used at all.

# Her nutrition phase

sub: A training block is not her nutrition phase

Where it goes: The Nutrition Manual. A build runs twelve weeks minimum and holds several hypertrophy blocks. The rate of loss in a cut is the Nutrition Manual's figure too.

Step one. Test the end condition, never the calendar.

What ends each phase

# Priming

sub: Can she demonstrate all five exit criteria

Look at: Session completion, technique to standard, mobility clearance, food tracking, steps.

Do this: Run it until all five clear. A mobility failure does not stop her training; it puts targeted work into her warm-ups and extends priming. If she clears it in four weeks, move on.

# Hypertrophy

sub: Is overload still happening

Look at: Load or reps still climbing cleanly with technique holding.

Do this: Extend while she is still adding. End early on a stall across consecutive sessions or on fatigue signals. Never cut below three weeks. Extend to line up with the next phase rather than cutting this one short.

# Cut

sub: Where she sits against the projection, and whether she is happy

Look at: Her actual bodyweight against the projection, recalculated each week.

Do this: Project the whole cut before it starts, with the rate taken from the Nutrition Manual. It ends at the projection or it ends with her.

# Holding

sub: Weight stable and rested, physically and mentally

Look at: Both, not either.

Do this: Three weeks is the floor and twelve the ceiling. Past twelve it has become a maintenance block and she should be in a phase with a goal attached.

# Deload

sub: Did one of the three triggers fire at all

Look at: Regressing weeks, life or illness, or the week after a test day.

Do this: One week, occasionally two. Sets come down and nothing else moves. Load, effort, selection and frequency all hold.

# Strength

sub: Which of the three four-week phases she is in

Look at: Accumulation, transmutation or realisation.

Do this: Twelve weeks in three four-week phases, and only the last four are the peak. Below twelve, take weeks out of accumulation and transmutation, never realisation. If the calendar will not give you eight, run a hypertrophy phase instead. It ends in a test, pitched at the rep max her technique can carry.

Step two. Read it. The number is where you start and the condition is what decides.

The two reads that go wrong most often

A block that is still working is not a block to end on schedule. Extension is the normal answer to a phase doing its job.

A deload is reactive. No trigger, no deload. A client who needs only a week of ease needs a deload, not a shortened holding phase.

Step three. Change what belongs to the boundary.

What moves around a phase boundary

# Effort belongs to the phase

sub: RIR is set by the block she is in

What changes: RIR runs 3 to 0 across a hypertrophy block. It floors at 1 in a deficit. It sits at 2-3 through a holding phase with no progression to zero.

# After a deload, resume exactly where you left off

sub: No reset and no ramp back in

What changes: Nothing. Loads pick up from before the deload, at the block's ongoing RIR.

# After a test, deload, then build again

sub: The tested numbers are her benchmark

What changes: Record the tested numbers. They are what the next strength block is measured against.

# Realisation phases are capped, not strength blocks

sub: Two realisation phases a year for most clients

What changes: A surplus strength block finishes at the end of transmutation and skips the bill.

Effort by phase, in reps in reserve ((RIR))

3-0 | Hypertrophy block: 3 in week one, falling to one week at 0 on isolation work

3-1 | Cut: 3 down to 1, and 1 is the floor

3-2 | Holding phase: 2 to 3 throughout, no progression to zero

What never changes is the realisation four weeks. Every other length flexes on its own rule. Those four are the four you never take from.
