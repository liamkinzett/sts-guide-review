Muscle and growth · T-01.25

Does EMG activity show muscle growth?

A rising electrical signal from a muscle across a set shows the nervous system recruiting harder. It does not show the muscle growing. Never read a growth ranking off an EMG chart (EMG).

Electromyography, EMG for short, measures electrical activity linked to muscle activation. Surface EMG uses electrodes on the skin, picking up the electrical signals the nervous system sends as it switches on the motor units beneath them. It is useful for studying what the nervous system is doing during a movement. It does not directly measure hypertrophy.

During a set to failure, fibres tire. The nervous system covers for them by switching on bigger and bigger bundles of fibres, each run by its own nerve (motor units). The electrical activity read from the skin rises across the set to match. This is the same recruitment order as the previous pages, seen through a different instrument.

If that signal drops or flattens while she is trying as hard as possible, the electrical pattern has changed. That on its own does not tell you whether the limit sits in the nervous system or in the muscle.

EMG is a snapshot of one moment. Muscle growth is a change that takes months to show. The signal is a stand-in for neural drive and recruitment within a set. It is not a direct read of the tension in the fibres, the force they produce, or the building signal that follows.

What it means for an EMG-ranked exercise list. A chart that ranks exercises by EMG is reporting recruitment and fatigue state. It is not a growth ranking, whatever it calls itself. Treat it as exactly that and no further. Choosing exercises gives you the reasons that do decide selection.

The deeper version, if you want it. Lifters were told to focus on the muscle, or on the outside world, during a shoulder raise. Their electrical reading did not differ, even though that reading was the exact thing measured. The tool reads what the nervous system is doing in the moment, not what the muscle will become.

Say it like this

That chart shows which exercise made the nerves fire hardest on the day. It doesn't show which one grows the muscle. We pick exercises for other reasons.

Terms on this page

EMG: Surface electromyography: electrodes on the skin that read the electrical activity in a muscle as the nervous system drives it.

motor units: One nerve cell and the bundle of muscle fibres it switches on together. A muscle is made of many of them, from small to large.
