Motivation and adherence · T-44.8

One habit at a time. Gate the next on a month of the last

One habit at a time. Gate the next habit on a month of the last one done well. Overhauls are avoided because they do not last.

A coach describes his sequence. He does not give a new client a training program to start. Diet first. Then steps. Once she does that well for about a month, then the training program.

Why overhauls are avoided. A huge lifestyle shift is exciting. It is sustainable for about three days.

The smallest version of the same idea. Start with breakfast. Start with eating the same breakfast every day. One thing, done until it is boring, then the next.

One habit gates the next

Habit one | 4 | Done well for about a month before anything is added

Habit two | 4 | Added only once the first runs on its own

Habit three | 4 | The same gate again

Where you deviate. Withholding a training program from a client who came to you for training is a commercial decision as much as a coaching one. Where she is paying for a program, give her the program. The sequencing still applies to everything you add after it. Anything she eats belongs to the Nutrition Manual.

Why it works. Each habit costs attention until it is automatic. Two new habits at once cost twice the attention and neither becomes automatic. A month is about how long one takes to stop costing anything.

Watch for

Five changes in week one because she is keen. Keen lasts three days. Sequence lasts.
