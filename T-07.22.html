The phases · T-07.22

A strength phase is twelve weeks; only the last four are the peak

A strength phase runs about twelve weeks, in three four-week phases. Only the last four weeks are the peak.

Each phase trains a different quality, and exercise selection changes with it.

A twelve-week strength phase

Accumulation | 4 | Build the raw material. Volume is the stimulus. Variations, higher reps, low joint stress

Transmutation | 4 | Convert new tissue into force. Volume down, intensity up. The tested lifts become the main movement

Realisation | 4 | Shed fatigue and express what is there. Tested lifts only, performed as she will on the day

What each phase looks like in practice. Take a deadlift block run this way. Accumulation (accumulation) spends four weeks on high-volume variation work, climbing in five percent steps. Transmutation (transmutation) drops to three to six reps on paused and deficit work. Realisation (realisation) cuts the high-fatigue accessories. A top set is worked down from four reps to three, two and a single, while the back-down volume holds.

Only the last four weeks are the peak, and that distinction matters. A peak makes nobody bigger or stronger. It is practice at displaying strength she has already built. A lifter who spends half the year peaking spends half the year not adapting. So weeks 1 to 8 are hypertrophy and strength work by another name. Only weeks 9 to 12 are the peak.

How long a true peak is. For a general powerlifter, which is who we coach, the true peak is the last three to four weeks. An elite lifter needs only two to three. The three to four weeks before that can arguably be counted as well. Treat the boundary as soft. Our weeks 9 to 12 sit inside it either way.

Why it works. Cutting the load in the final weeks (taper) raises the strength she can show, because fatigue clears faster than fitness fades. Twelve weeks is a coherent structure, and it is a default rather than a requirement.

When you may deviate. Eight weeks is the floor and sixteen the ceiling. Below twelve you take weeks out of accumulation and transmutation, never out of realisation. If the calendar will not give you eight, do not compress the block. Run a hypertrophy phase and start the peak when there is room. Realisation is where the fatigue comes off. Take the weeks out of accumulation if you have to take them from anywhere.

The eight-week minimum

Accumulation and transmutation | 4 | Share four weeks between them

Realisation | 4 | Never shortened. This is where the fatigue comes off

Watch for

Treating this as twelve weeks of heavy lifting. The last four weeks are mostly about getting fresh. A coach who keeps loading through them wastes the eight that came before.

Terms on this page

accumulation: The first four weeks of a strength phase, where volume is high and the load is moderate. The job is to build the raw material.

transmutation: The middle four weeks of a strength phase. Volume comes down, intensity goes up, and the tested lifts become the main movements.

realisation: The last four weeks of a strength phase. Fatigue is shed so that the strength she has built can show up on test day.

taper: Cutting the training load right down before a test, so fatigue clears and strength shows. Fatigue clears faster than fitness fades, so she is fresh.
