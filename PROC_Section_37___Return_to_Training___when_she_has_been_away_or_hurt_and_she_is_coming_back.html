What to do when she has been away or hurt and she is coming back

This page adds nothing. It is this topic's rules in the order you use them. The precondition comes first: Pain rules and when to refer. If she was referred and has not been cleared, this topic has not started.

Which return is this?

? Was she referred, and is her clearance still in doubt?

yes: Pain rules and when to refer. This topic has not started.

? Was the reason illness?

yes: Training stopped while she was sick. Her doctor decides she is well. Then the illness ramp: two to three weeks, weight down first.

? Was a specific tissue injured?

yes: The ladder, from rung 1. Compensation work starts early.

? Two weeks off or less?

yes: The program picks up unchanged. Warn her the load will feel heavier.

= A layoff. Compound work, few movements, at a lower percentage than she finished on.

Route two look-alikes first

Two weeks off, no injury. Pick up unchanged. No easing back in. The boundary is deliberate: the layoff return owns anything longer, and the two do not blur. Give one warning beforehand: the same load genuinely feels heavier after a layoff.

A compressed week rather than a return. Training around life.

Step 1. Test. Establish which of two returns this is, because they run on different rules. A layoff, where the whole person is detrained, runs the layoff return. A specific tissue coming back after an injury runs the ladder. Then ask what she can demonstrate rather than whether it still hurts. Pain-free is not ready, and the absence of a symptom is not a qualification. The one quantified criterion is the calf: before running, hopping or plyometrics, it moves her own bodyweight.

Step 2. Read. Clearance ended the medical question, not the loading one.

She will tell you she is ready and she will be telling the truth about what she can feel. She has no access to what her tissue can currently tolerate, and neither do you. That is why the ladder exists rather than a judgement call.

The qualification to advance is reproducibility of the stimulus rep to rep, never one successful repetition.

And at three weeks, what she sees in the mirror is mostly fluid and glycogen rather than lost muscle.

Step 3. Do.

Do, by type of return

# A layoff: compound work with minimal isolation

sub: Not the old program

Why: It was calibrated to the training age she had, not the one she has now. A detrained client improves on almost anything. The scarce resource is her sessions.

Do this: Come back to the movement sooner, at a lower percentage. Never at the load she finished on.

# An injured tissue: the ladder, from rung 1

sub: Isometrics, then tempo, then regular reps, then velocity, then bounding

Do this: Tempo and volume never advance together. Reproducibility rep to rep is the qualification to move up.

# Keep her on the command side

sub: No chaos implements

Why: The moment she leaves the ground, control passes to the demand side. The reverse-band jump is the named worst offender. Unpredictability multiplies an asymmetry rather than training it away.

# Start the compensation work early

sub: Wherever the injury changed how she moves for more than a fortnight

Do this: Train the off side: the oblique slings, the opposite hip, trunk and lats. The compensations are not protected by pain and become her perceived neutral.

Step 4. Change. What moves in the program:

  • Rehabilitation runs two jobs at once, and the second is nearly always skipped. Managing the injured region is only the first.
  • Plyometrics arrive only as the end phase. Starting at rung 5 because she looks fine is the instinct this topic exists to interrupt.
  • A personal best does not close the case. A number going up is not proof the problem went away. The load split can encode the compensation while the squat climbs.
  • A post-illness spike is not a new baseline. Keep loading conservative through the two to three week window.

    • What does not change: the two-week client's program. That rule exists to stop a coach deloading a client who does not need it. This page sets none of her volume or effort once she is back. Those belong to Volume and to Effort and intensity.
