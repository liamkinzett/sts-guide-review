What she will ask you · T-47.18

"Aren't free weights better than machines?"

Machines against free weights is a goal question. For building muscle the machine often wins. Tool choice follows the goal.

The mechanism. A machine puts the target muscle to work sooner. On a free weight, a less-skilled lifter does not start working the muscle until halfway through rep five. The first reps go to finding the position. If the goal is strength, the barbell. If the goal is size, you may want the working reps to start earlier, on a machine.

Which tool for which goal

Barbell | Strength expression. The skill is the point.

Dumbbells | Between the two

Machine | Muscle-building stimulus. The target muscle works from rep one.

External support preserves intensity when she is tired. Free weight rows become machine rows. Externally supported, she can still drive intensity and hold the volume she was doing. When systemic fatigue is high, the machine is the higher-quality choice, not the compromise.

The "functional and unstable" half of the myth is inverted. Stability is the ability to resist force. Why train it on an unstable surface, where she inherently cannot produce as much force? Unstable surfaces cap force production. They train stability worse. Real transfer comes from loaded single-leg patterns: the split squat, the single-leg RDL, the step down. Choosing exercises owns selection.

The stabiliser-muscles trope argues nothing. Use the three mechanisms above.

Say it like this

Machines aren't the easy option. They let the muscle we're targeting work from the first rep instead of the fifth. Free weights are for when the skill of the lift is the point.

Watch for

Bosu-ball anything for stability. You have capped the one thing stability is made of.
