Body image and the referral line · T-43.11

Model non-moral food language, unasked

Model non-moral food language. Never wait to be asked about it. Earned, deserved, punished, clean, dirty, cheat. None of it is your vocabulary.

Two coaches corrected each other mid-sentence. One said "good" foods. The other cut in.

The correction, as it happened

Fun foods. Not good or bad foods. Fun foods.

The correction was applied to a coach, not to a client. That is the point. You fix your own language first, and you fix it before she has ever used the words.

Why it works. She learns what food means partly from how you talk about it. A coach who says "earned" has taught her that food is a wage. A coach who says "cheat" has taught her that eating is a rule she can break. Model the language you want her to have and most clients pick it up without a word said about it.

When her language has already darkened. Model rather than correct. Where post-diet hunger is driving the guilt, the reframe is physical. Dieting is a voluntary famine, and elevated hunger hormones can persist for up to a year after a diet. It is not on her and it is not her fault. Anything about what she eats belongs to the Nutrition Manual.

Watch for

Correcting her words. Telling her not to say "cheat" is a rule she did not agree to. Say it your way, every time, and let her hear the difference.

Earned, deserve, punish, from her, repeatedly. That is language darkening. Test for the three referral behaviours.
