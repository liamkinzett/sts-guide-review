Ankle: what you do where we give no protocol

Several things a coach reaches for on an ankle have no STS protocol. Here is what you do instead of inventing one.

What we do not give you, and what you do

# Balance work first

sub: Pain trumps balance work every time

Do this: Balance and wobble drills are a late stage, not the first thing. The starting point is standing on one leg, not a wobble board. Reduce pain and restore load first.

# A rolled ankle on the day

sub: Plyometrics make it worse. A hold does not

Do this: If she has rolled it, no jumping that day. She can hold a calf position against load to get load into the foot, calf and ankle and rebuild confidence. That is one substitution, not a ladder. She is still referred.

# A return from a sprain

sub: A sequence with no numbers

Do this: Range of motion, then active assisted, then active, then active resisted, then dynamic stability. Pick one stimulus at a time. There is no bodyweight floor for a sprain; the calf figure is an Achilles number.

# Ankle bracing, taping or high-top shoes

sub: No guidance

Do this: Do not invent a rule and do not read the wrist's wrap material across. Her physio decides.

# A minimum ankle range

sub: No threshold exists

Do this: Never measure her ankle range to judge it normal. Run the heel-elevation test instead and see whether depth appears.

# The heel wedge

sub: Use it. Do not explain it as a range gain

Do this: It is a centre-of-mass tool. Say so, and say nothing about injury prevention.

# Achilles in pregnancy or after birth

sub: Outside this topic

Do this: The female body owns pregnancy and postpartum. Refer to a pelvic health physiotherapist for anything in that window.

Do not import these from general knowledge

A rules-based test for whether an ankle needs an X-ray, and a knee-to-wall lunge test of ankle range. Neither is STS practice. The second is a range measured to judge normality, which is barred for a coach.

A high ankle sprain, or one on the inside of the ankle, has no read here. Refer.
