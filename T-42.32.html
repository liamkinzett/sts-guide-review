The coaching relationship · T-42.32

Three moments tempt a verdict. Ask, in every one

Three moments tempt a verdict instead of a question. Ask, in every one of them. This is a habit of asking rather than telling, and it is a judgement call each time.

The moments, and the question instead

# She already knows the news before the check-in opens

sub: A regain, a bad week she has seen coming

The verdict would be: Confirming what you both already know

Ask instead: What she thinks happened. Let her supply it first.

# She insists a partial number is fine

sub: Roughly-there adherence, defended

The verdict would be: Correcting the number for her

Ask instead: Whether she is happy with where the number has her

# She is half right and half wrong in one complaint

sub: The accurate half and the inaccurate half, tangled

The verdict would be: Litigating one half against the other

Ask instead: Concede the accurate half plainly. Ask about the rest rather than arguing it.

# She measures you against a previous coach

sub: "My old coach used to..."

The verdict would be: Defending your own method

Ask instead: What she liked about how the old coach worked. Or whether she is happy with where she is now.

One mechanism under all four. An open-ended question makes her supply the observation. A verdict makes her defend herself. Every row is that.

What this is not. Nothing here is an apology. Where a genuine mistake of yours put you in the room, that is a different page, and you lead with the impact. None of these four rows is that case.

What licenses it. The intake agreement. She agreed to have exactly these conversations, so asking is not intrusion.

Watch for

A question with the verdict inside it. "Don't you think roughly-there isn't really the plan?" is a verdict with a question mark. Ask whether she is happy with where it has her.
