Using HRV to decide whether to train

Heart rate variability does not decide whether she trains. Her own report does. The number is an input.

Heart rate variability (HRV) is the small change in time between heartbeats that a wearable reads as a recovery score. It is a real signal. It is not a gate.

Why a coach is tempted to gate on it. HRV can stay low for a day or two after the soreness has gone. Repeatedly pushing through a low reading can seem to cost something. That is one body's experience, not a rule.

What watching the number does to her. Every low reading becomes a reason to worry. She becomes fixated on her own physiology and a slave to the data rather than a reader of her own body.

Your call. Cancelling or cutting sessions on a low HRV has not been shown to improve her results. So the rule stands. Ask how she feels first, and treat the score as one input. Where a client is already anxious about her numbers, the case against weighs more.

Say it like this

Your HRV is one piece of information about last night. It doesn't decide today. Tell me how you feel, and we'll look at the number after.
