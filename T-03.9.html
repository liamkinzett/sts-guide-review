Fatigue and recovery · T-03.9

What the recovery industry actually delivers

Recovery is not one event. It is the gradual return to a state where she can train productively again. That return includes:

  • restoring the muscle's force capacity;
  • letting local fatigue clear;
  • repairing and remodelling tissue;
  • refilling fuel stores;
  • and settling any soreness enough that she can move well.

    • How long that takes depends on the session, her training status, her sleep and her life stress. There is no single universal recovery clock to hand her. Judge recovery by whether she is ready to do useful work again, not by whether every sensation has gone.

    Cold plunges, sauna, compression boots, red light and massage guns together, by one direct estimate, deliver under 5% of that.

    It is a working estimate from being asked the question directly, not a lab-measured percentage. For 99% of people, by that same estimate, recovery is the unpriced things: sleep, food and training that can actually be absorbed. Say so plainly when she asks.

    Why the market says otherwise. A clinic can profit from a membership to use its boots, sauna, plunge and lights. It cannot profit from telling her to go home and sleep. Commercial visibility is not evidence of a physiological effect.

    The adjacent tools do no better. Stretching and foam rolling after a session do not improve strength, range of motion or soreness.

    The redirect is the usable part. When she asks about a recovery tool, answer the question, then move the conversation to sleep. The next pages take the tools one at a time. For any of them, ask two separate questions. Does it help her feel or function better today? Does it support, do nothing to, or work against the adaptation she is actually training for? Those are different questions, and a tool can pass the first while failing the second.

    Say it like this

    Nearly all of your recovery is sleep, food and training you can recover from. The gadgets are the small extra, not the foundation. Use them if you enjoy them, not because they're doing the work.
