Three ways to peak in twelve weeks

A peak is 12 weeks that turn her training into one tested number on one day. There are three shapes it can take: linear, block and conjugate. All three assume a four-day split, Lower / Upper / Lower / Upper. That is the strength standard, and it gives every foundational pattern equal exposure.

Before you write any of them, fix her working max. Every percentage below is a share of a current, true one-rep max (1RM). Where she has no recent single you trust, use a training max (training max) of 90-95% of her best recent lift. Run the numbers off that.

Under-shooting and hitting every session beats over-shooting and missing reps in week 6.

If she does miss a rep at the written effort, the load was wrong, not her. The next session undershoots slightly rather than repeating the miss. Effort and intensity has the rule.

Everywhere else STS writes effort as reps in reserve (RIR). These plates are written as percentages of a tested max instead, which is why the max comes first.

A worked example. Her best squat single is 100 kg, but it is months old and you did not see it. Her training max is 90 to 95 kg. Take 90 kg. Week 1 of the linear peak is 5×5 @ 70%, so she squats five sets of five at 63 kg. If every rep moves well, the shape holds and week 2 climbs to 72.5%.

Read the percentages as a starting shape rather than a prescription. They are what these programs look like when nothing gets in the way. Her actual weekly performance moves them.

The linear peak, in one line: reps go down, weight goes up, every single week. One long ramp from volume to a single.

The deadlift always carries one set fewer than the squat and the bench, because it costs more to recover from. A back-off set (back-off set) is the lighter work after the day's heaviest set. Week 12 is a taper (taper): the work drops right off so fatigue clears before the test.

Terms on this page

back-off set: A lighter set done after the heaviest set of the day.

taper: Cutting the training load right down before a test, so fatigue clears and strength shows. It is the final days before the test.

1RM: The heaviest weight she can lift once with good form.

training max: A working figure of 90-95% of her best recent lift, used in place of a true 1RM when she has no recent single you trust.

RIR: Reps in reserve. How many more reps she could have done when the set stopped.
