Translating a clinician's clearance into load

When a clinician clears her for "light" or "moderate" work, you never turn that word into a percentage. Run it through the effort model instead.

Whose word it is. Grading and clearing a diagnosed condition belongs to the diagnosing clinician. That includes what "light" means clinically. A coach does not translate it into a number on her own authority. Anything that needs clarifying goes back through Pain rules and when to refer.

What replaces the guess. STS never sets load as a percentage of a one-rep max in the first place. Load is an output of a rep target and an effort target, set in reps in reserve (RIR). Applied here, a clinician's "light" becomes a conservative RIR anchor, well clear of failure, with the load set to whatever satisfies it. That is not a workaround. It is how STS already sets load for everyone.

Why it works. A coach who converts "light" into "60%" has manufactured a number nobody gave her. She cannot defend it if asked where it came from. Ask the client to name her own clearance in her own terms. An exertion level, a rating of how hard it felt (RPE), an activity she can already do. That keeps the number inside the clinician's actual instruction.

A worked example. Her physio has cleared her for "light lower body work". You do not write 60%. You ask her what light felt like in the clinic and which movements she was shown. Then you set a conservative RIR anchor, well clear of failure, and let the load be whatever satisfies it.

Verdict

What "light" means for her body is the clinician's judgement, not a number you supply.

Send anything that needs clarifying back to the clinician.

Say it like this

Your physio's the one who gets to define light for your body right now. Tell me what that feels like to you and I'll build around it, rather than me guessing a percentage.

Terms on this page

RIR: Reps in reserve. How many more reps she could have done when the set stopped. 2 RIR means she stopped two reps short of failure.

RPE: Rating of perceived exertion. How hard a set felt to her, on a scale out of 10, where 10 is nothing left.
