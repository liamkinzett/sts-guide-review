Practising a lift · T-48.8

Arousal is a resource with a cost, so teach her to come back down

Getting fired up for a lift is a resource, and it costs something. The skill you want is the ability to come back down. The strength it produces is real.

What too much looks like. She gets far too aggressive through her squats and her bench, with too many stimulants and too much adrenaline in the mix. Then on the deadlift her grip goes. Grip failing on the deadlift late in a session is the tell that she spent her nervous system earlier.

The trainable quality is controlled aggression (arousal). A lifter who can heighten herself to the state a heavy lift needs, and then come down again. Coming back down is the half that is usually missing.

Music, caffeine and smelling salts are conditional, not useful in themselves. They amplify a focus she already has. They do not supply one. If she has the skill of holding her focus and shutting out distractions, they can help. If she does not, they add noise.

Why it is rationed. Going heavy requires a level of arousal she does not want to live in all the time. It is a dark place to have to go to. That cost is why heavy singles are rare and why the arousal that goes with them is saved for the day that needs it.

Watch for

The client who is "up" for every set of every session. Arousal that never comes down is being spent on work that does not need it.

Reaching for stimulants to fix a focus problem. They amplify focus. They cannot create it.

Say to the lifter who is too fired up for a light day

Save that for the heavy day. Today we practise, and I want you calm enough to feel what you're doing.

Terms on this page

arousal: How switched-on and fired up her nervous system is before a lift. High arousal raises the force she can express and costs her afterwards.
