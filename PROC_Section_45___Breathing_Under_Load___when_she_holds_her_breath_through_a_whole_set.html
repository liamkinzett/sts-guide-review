What to do when she holds her breath through a whole set

This page adds nothing. It is this topic's rules in the order you use them, for the client who holds her breath through a whole set.

Red flags

title: Stop and refer before you coach the breath

She goes lightheaded, dizzy or grey during or after a set. This stops being a technique conversation. Pain rules and when to refer governs.

Anything that persists after the breathing pattern is retrained is medical.

emergency: Any actual loss of consciousness, even brief, is an ambulance call. Ring 000. It is never a breathing fault.

Step 1. Test. Count the reps in the set. One heavy rep with a held breath is correct. Anything that spans a breath cycle with the breath held is not.

Step 2. Read. Watch the bar on the video rather than her face. A chest breath moves the bar.

Step 3. Do

Re-teach to one breath cycle per rep. In on the way down, out on the way up. Never one breath per set.

Give her the brace and the breath as two things. She stays tight through her middle and keeps breathing behind it.

Teach the belt breath. Deep into the belly, pushed out against the belt. Never into the chest.

Tell her a breath every rep at the end of a hard set is the plan, not a failure.

Step 4. Change. Drop the load while she re-learns it. A breathing pattern is a movement pattern like any other, and she can only hold so many new things at once. Cueing covers how many.

Step 5. Escalate. Anything that persists after the pattern is retrained is medical. Any actual loss of consciousness, even brief, is an ambulance call. Pain rules and when to refer covers where it goes.

Say when she has been holding it all set

You've been doing the whole set on one breath. That's a one-rep tool. Every rep gets its own breath from now on, and we'll take some weight off while you learn it.
