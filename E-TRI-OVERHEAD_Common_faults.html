Common faults

Seven common faults

# Range cut short

sub: A tiny range of motion, which forfeits the only thing this movement buys

Why it happens: The load is too heavy to take into the stretch, or the rope is in the way of her head.

Do this: Reduce load and reach the stretch. Let the rope split.

# Rib flare

sub: Over-arching the low back to get the arm overhead

Why it happens: Restricted overhead shoulder range. Compare the overhead press: this is usually a shoulder-flexion problem, not a bracing one.

Do this: Ribs to hips. If it does not hold, regress the variation rather than cue harder.

# Body English

sub: Swinging to move the load

Why it happens: The load is more than the triceps can move on their own.

Do this: Reduce load.

# Load past the position she owns

sub: Too heavy to stabilise overhead

Why it happens: She chose a weight for the pushdown, not for an overhead position.

Do this: Reduce load. Start light and work up.

# Elbow off its line

sub: Fixed hands on a straight bar or dumbbell forcing an uncomfortable path

Why it happens: This one is in your prescription, not her rep (Tier P). A fixed implement dictates the elbow path.

Do this: Change the implement, not the cue. The rope lets the hands split.

# Bar off the midline

sub: Lowering behind the head badly. Forcing extreme shoulder flexion on a vertical cable path

Why it happens: The line of pull runs straight up and down instead of angled slightly back.

Do this: Correct the path. Angle the line of pull slightly backward.

# Coach-side faults

sub: The cable too taut at end range, so tension is lost where it matters

Why it happens: This one is in your setup, not her rep (Tier P). The pulley is too low.

Do this: Pulley height is a setup decision. Raise it slightly off the bottom.

The elbow row is an implement problem, not a coaching one. The answer is to change the implement rather than to coach harder. Cueing explains why a setup change beats a long cue list.
