Assessing a new client · T-06.14

Classify per movement pattern, not per person

She can be advanced on a hinge and a beginner overhead. That is normal, and it is useful. Give her one headline level for phase planning, then decide exercise selection and progression pattern by pattern (per-pattern classification).

A pattern you cannot classify gets marked unknown. She may never have trained it. You may not have seen enough to call it. Write unknown rather than guessing, then program it as a beginner would be programmed until you know better. A gap you can see in the record is worth more than a confident invention.

This is where you end up, not where you start. At intake you have never seen her move, so you carry one assumed level taken from the form. You write the per-pattern version at the end of priming, once you have watched her train and reviewed footage. Do not pretend to a precision you cannot have on day one.

The six patterns. Squat, hinge, horizontal press, vertical press, horizontal pull, vertical pull. A level against each.

Terms on this page

per-pattern classification: A training level written against each of the six movement patterns rather than one level for the whole person. The six are squat, hinge, horizontal press, vertical press, horizontal pull and vertical pull.
