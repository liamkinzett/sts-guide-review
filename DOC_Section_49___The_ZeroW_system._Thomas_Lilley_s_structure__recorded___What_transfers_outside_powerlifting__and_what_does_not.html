What transfers outside powerlifting, and what does not

Half of the strength system is structure, and it travels into any program. The other half is tied to the three lifts being the point, and it does not.

You have now read the whole system. Take the first list into any client's program. Leave the second with the competitive powerlifter.

Travels: it is structural

# Frequency as three independent numbers

sub: The priority lift, its pattern, the muscle

Why it travels: Each level is set on its own, so the idea works whatever the priority lift is.

# The category ladder

sub: Main, then second pattern, then unilateral, then isolation, then trunk

Why it travels: It is an order of categories, not a list of exercises.

# Intensity held at one to two RIR

sub: Rather than waved wildly

Why it travels: Steady effort close to failure is what drives adaptation in any program (RIR).

# Volume deloads

sub: Drop sets, hold intensity

Why it travels: It is the same shape as the STS deload (D-volume deload).

# Whatever goes last is shafted

sub: Plain order bias

Why it travels: The last exercise in any session returns less than it should. Building a session covers it.

Does not travel: it is tied to the lifts being the point

# Two to three sets a week on the main lift

sub: Only adequate because the accessories exist to feed it

Why it stays: Carry that number into general programming and the movement is under-trained.

# A fixed five-week block with a planned deload

sub: Four weeks hard, one week deload

Why it stays: A general client deloads only when a trigger fires. The phases lists them.

# How often a competitor peaks

sub: A competition-calendar decision

Why it stays: It is about how often a competitor should peak. It says nothing about how a physique client trains.

# One competition-variation exposure a week

sub: Meaningless where there is no lift being judged

Why it stays: The exact lift only needs practising when it is the thing being tested (competition variation).

# Rep waving 8/6/6, then 6/5/4, then 5/5/4/3

sub: Pinned to percentages of a tested one-rep max

Why it stays: Without a tested max the percentages have nothing to hang on.

# The beginner SBD circuit

sub: Teaching the competition lifts, not general training

Why it stays: It exists to teach three specific lifts from day one.

# No SBD days

sub: Only a question where all three are trained as competition lifts

Why it stays: If she is not training squat, bench and deadlift as competition lifts, the question does not arise.

Terms on this page

RIR: Reps in reserve. How many more reps she could have done when the set stopped. 2 RIR means she stopped two reps short of failure.

D-volume deload: A deload where sets come down to one or two, and the weight and the effort stay high.

competition variation: The exact lift as it is performed on the platform, with her competition stance, grip and bar position.
