Pain rules and when to refer · T-35.26

A hand on her body is a cue, not therapy

Guiding her body with your hands during a set is cueing, provided it stays a cue and stays consented. It is not the hands-on assessment you are barred from.

What is barred is clinical assessment. Palpating to find out how bad something is, measuring a range to see whether it is normal, grading or staging what you find. A movement screen you watch is allowed, and so is naming your guess. Grading it, or letting it delay a referral, is not.

What is not barred is coaching a movement with a hand on the body. Region by region's knee section has a coach press a finger on a toe, to close a system a foot cue cannot close verbally. That is cueing. A physio guiding a pelvis into position during a hip thrust, as part of a rehabilitation protocol, is a different act. It has a different purpose and sits under a different scope.

The line is purpose, not contact. Ask what the touch is for. Teaching her where a rep should start and finish is coaching. Correcting a position because you believe it will fix something you have identified in her is assessment wearing a cueing costume. It puts you back inside the barred territory.

Consent is not optional. Ask before the first touch, not only once. Expect a client to say no without treating it as a rejection of the coaching itself.

Before any hands-on cue

Is it okay if I touch you?

Instead of copying the physio's hands

Cue verbally first.

Where a hand teaches what words cannot, use it, with consent, for the movement pattern only.

Never adopt a hands-on technique because it is what her physio does. That technique belongs to an assessment and treatment relationship you are not in.

Asked what you think of her physio's, chiropractor's or massage therapist's treatment, judge the tool and never the practitioner. Massage, cupping, adjusting and dry needling are short-term tools that push her into the next phase, and the next phase is the gym. The coaching relationship holds the full answer.

Watch for

A client asking for the exact hands-on correction her physio uses, as reassurance. Explain the difference plainly rather than obliging her by imitation.
