Muscle and growth · T-01.19

Do the burn, the pump or soreness build muscle?

Mechanical tension is what builds muscle. The burn, the pump, soreness and hormone spikes have not been shown to add anything on top of it.

A hard set can leave her with a burning feeling, a big pump, soreness the next day, or a sense the muscle has been "destroyed". Those feelings are real. The mistake is assuming they are the reason the muscle grew. The useful distinction is between what a productive set can feel like and what actually provides the growth stimulus.

The burn.

The burning feeling during a hard set comes from a build-up of by-products in the working muscle (metabolic stress): lactate, hydrogen ions and phosphate. A strong burn can go alongside productive training. But making the burn bigger on its own does not add growth once the training variables that matter are matched. Drop sets push the burn to its maximum.

At equal volume they grow a muscle no more than ordinary sets. The burn can accompany hard work. It is not a separate target to chase.

Muscle damage and soreness. Training can damage muscle, and that damage can produce soreness. It is most likely when someone is new to an exercise, or returns to a movement after time away. More damage does not mean more growth.

Early in a block, some of the extra protein-building goes into repairing that damage rather than adding new size. As the body gets used to the exercise, damage falls even while productive training keeps going. Damage and soreness track poorly with growth. A client can grow without being sore, and get very sore without having had a better growth session.

Hormone spikes. Testosterone and growth hormone rise for a short time after training. That short rise was once treated as a major reason some sessions build more muscle than others.

Neither the training load nor the size of that hormone spike decided who grew more or got stronger. The local signalling happening inside the trained muscle mattered more than the size of the temporary spike.

The pump. The pump is the temporary size increase from fluid moving into the working muscle during a set (cell swelling). It can feel satisfying and tell you a muscle has been working. It has not earned a place as its own driver of growth. "Sarcoplasmic" growth, an increase from fluid and glycogen rather than contractile tissue, is not something STS programs for either. Its own page in this topic covers why.

What she has heard, and what it does

# The burn (metabolic stress)

sub: Lactate, hydrogen ions and phosphate building up

What it does: Drop sets push the burn to the maximum. At equal volume they grow a muscle no more than ordinary sets. The burn is a feeling, not a driver.

# Muscle damage and soreness

sub: Once taught as a main driver

What it does: Damage tracks poorly with growth. Early in a block, the protein building goes into repairing damage rather than adding size, until the damage settles. Soreness tells you nothing about growth.

# Hormone spikes from a session

sub: Testosterone and growth hormone in the hour after training

What it does: Neither the training load nor the hormone response after it decided who grew or got stronger. Signalling inside the muscle did.

# The pump (cell swelling)

sub: The muscle filling with fluid during a set

What it does: Part of the burn picture. It has not earned a place as a driver on its own.

# "Sarcoplasmic" growth

sub: Size from fluid and glycogen rather than contractile protein

What it does: Not a kind of growth you can program for. Its own page in this topic covers it.

What this means for coaching. Do not judge a hypertrophy session by how painful, pumped or sore it made her. Those sensations can go alongside productive training, but they are not the outcome. Judge the program by what matters over time.

Is the target muscle trained effectively? Is the work progressing? Is recovery adequate? Is she actually adapting? Everything on this page has been checked directly against tension, and none of it adds a shown effect on top of it. Fatigue and recovery covers soreness in full.

Say it like this

The burn and the pump feel like progress, and they're fine. They're not what grows the muscle. The load you moved is.

Terms on this page

metabolic stress: The build-up of by-products such as lactate, hydrogen ions and phosphate in a working muscle. It is felt as the burn.

cell swelling: The muscle filling with fluid during a set, felt as the pump.
