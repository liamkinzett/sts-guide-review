What changes because she is a woman, and what does not

Most of what you have been told changes for a woman does not. What does change is narrower, and it is pulled together here.

The basic principles do not change because she is a woman. Hypertrophy, strength adaptation, exercise selection, progressive overload and the general shape of good coaching all still apply. She does not need an entirely separate training philosophy.

What you watch is her, week by week. Her own reported fatigue and symptoms. That is the instrument, and it is deliberately not a survey. And watch the one signal only you hold: an inability to gain or maintain muscle despite progressive lifting. Body composition does not screen under-fuelling. Never conclude she is fine from how she presents.

What you do not do. You do not raise her contraception as a topic with her. Her intake already tells you her status, which is what lets you read her bleed answer correctly. You ask about fuelling directly, the way you would with any client.

What stays the same

# Percentage loading across the month

sub: Nothing measured shows a reliable strength swing across the cycle

# The program, against any cycle calendar

sub: Cycle-syncing has been ruled out

Why: Muscle protein synthesis does not differ across the cycle, and a generic calendar cannot map onto her.

# Everything, for a pill user

sub: Hormonal contraception does not impair strength adaptation or change protein needs

# Volume and effort prescriptions

sub: Neither is adjusted on sex

Why: Women do not get more volume or shorter rests by default, and a deload is not timed to the cycle. Volume owns volume and The phases owns deloads.

# Her macros across the cycle

sub: They do not change substantially

What changes

# Reps to failure on a back squat, at one submaximal load

sub: Higher before ovulation, lower in the first days of her period. The upper body is flat

Do this: Do not generalise it. Leave the percentages alone.

# A bleed on the pill

sub: It is a withdrawal bleed, so the most visible under-fuelling sign is switched off for over half the roster

Do this: Screen for under-fuelling regardless of whether she is bleeding. Notice, do not quantify.

# After menopause

sub: Heavy loads stop being optional, and the scale hides the remodelling underneath a stable weight

Do this: Keep genuinely heavy compound work in, in the region of six to eight reps, by the same effort and volume rules as anyone else. Change what she measures.

# Pregnant or postpartum

sub: The whole model becomes symptom-led

Do this: Anchor the effort at 2 to 3 RIR, reps in reserve (RIR), and let the load be the output. The calendar is a watch-list, not a prescription.

# Breastfeeding and asking to lean out

sub: Fuel her and train her strong

When the under-fuelling signs appear, the conversation moves, not the program. The route in is the stall list in Volume, at the recovery step. The locked bone rule in Mobility and stretching stands, and nothing here loosens it.

Red flags

title: Refer these, every time

Suspected under-fuelling: a referral conversation, not a programming adjustment. The destination is a team, not one practitioner

A diagnosed condition whose management is phase-specific: clinical

A pregnancy stop sign: stop the session and contact the obstetrician, GP or midwife

Pelvic floor symptoms: stop the session, refer to a pelvic health physio, keep training everything else. Never say leaking is normal

A suspected bone condition: refer rather than diagnose

PCOS (PCOS): you train the client, not the condition. Everything in this Library applies to her. The condition belongs to her clinician. The under-fuelling screen still applies, because you cannot tell a PCOS absent period from an under-fuelling one

Pain rules and when to refer holds every one of those routes.

Terms on this page

RIR: Reps in reserve. How many more reps she could have done when the set stopped. 2 RIR means she stopped two reps short of failure.

PCOS: Polycystic ovary syndrome. A hormonal condition that can disturb her periods. It is diagnosed and managed by her clinician, never by a coach.
