Watch for

  • High whole-body fatigue cost (central fatigue). Chest-supported rows are expensive. Many more sets of single-arm cable pulldowns are tolerable than of chest-supported rows. Use the shift in working feel toward rear delts and biceps as the audit signal, not a set count. Volume owns the count.
  • Neck pain from rowing has the same chain as elsewhere in this topic. The shoulder blades stop managing the force and the muscles from blade to skull absorb it. Stacked rib cage first, before anything is done to the neck. Pain itself is Pain rules and when to refer.
  • What external stability costs. After machine rows she feels the target muscle. After barbell rows the core and the low back are taxed too. Neither is superior. They are different tools with separate uses. Removing constraints also removes the diagnostic signal, so treating stability as a route to output is the wrong reason to remove them.
  • Who to modify for. Tall lifters need the foot plate and the arm-path reach checked on fixed machines. Anyone whose low back ends a free-weight row before her back is worked belongs in this family. Advanced lifters with set compensations may have the fault hidden by the pad rather than fixed by it. Anything about her being a woman is The female body.
  • Prerequisite. None. This family sits low enough in STS's ladders to be a starting point rather than a destination.
    • Terms on this page

    central fatigue: Tiredness in the nervous system and the whole body, not in one muscle. It is why some exercises cost far more than their set count suggests.
