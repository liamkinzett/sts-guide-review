Pain rules and when to refer · T-35.2

Cauda equina: emergency department the same day

Verdict

Back or leg pain plus any one of these flags, starting in the last two weeks: emergency department today.

Refer on the symptom. Do not wait for a sign. You are not the one who rules it out.

The early signs of this surgical emergency are things a client mentions in passing. The condition is a squashing of the bundle of nerves at the base of the spine (cauda equina syndrome). If she reports back or leg pain plus any flag below, starting in the last two weeks, she goes to emergency care the same day.

Red flags

title: Emergency department the same day: back or leg pain plus any of these, starting in the last two weeks

New difficulty starting to wee, or she cannot feel the flow

New numbness or altered feeling around the genitals, between the legs or around the back passage (saddle numbness)

She can no longer feel when her bowel is full

New sexual dysfunction, or loss of vaginal sensation

Severe or worsening weakness or loss of feeling in the legs

emergency: One flag already sends her. Two never make you wait and see. Refer on the symptom, not on a sign. You are not the one who rules it out.

Red flags

title: Also a warning sign. Refer urgently, though not as an emergency

Sudden pain in both legs

Pain in one leg that becomes both legs, or swaps sides

Two flags together are worse than one. More than one alerting feature means a greater likelihood of something serious. One flag already sends her. Two never make you wait and see.

Refer on the symptom. Do not wait for a sign. These symptoms mandate an emergency referral even when there is nothing to find on examination. A face-to-face examination is not required first. You are not the one who rules it out.

Why the same day. Outcomes are better when this is treated within a day or two of onset. The damage it prevents is permanent. A referral that turns out to be nothing costs an afternoon.

Watch for

The way she will tell you. This does not arrive as an emergency. It arrives as "my leg's been a bit weird", or a comment about the toilet while she is putting her shoes on. You have to be listening for it rather than waiting to be alarmed.

Say it like this

I need you to get that looked at today, at an emergency department rather than a GP appointment. It's probably nothing, and the one thing it might be is time-sensitive, so I'd rather you went and it was nothing.

This page is cauda equina only. Chest pain, breathlessness, collapse, fainting, a seizure, a head injury and an allergic reaction each have their own page in this topic. When to call triple zero is the threshold for an ambulance, and the two are separate decisions on separate clocks. A coach who believes a client needs emergency care acts on that judgement. She is not looking for permission here.

Terms on this page

cauda equina syndrome: The bundle of nerves at the base of the spine is being squashed. Early signs are bladder or bowel changes, numbness around the saddle area, new sexual dysfunction or worsening leg weakness.

saddle numbness: Numbness or altered feeling in the area that would touch a saddle: the genitals, the inner thighs, between the legs and around the back passage. A red flag for cauda equina syndrome.
