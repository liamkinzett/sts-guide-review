Cueing · T-34.6

Suspect the cue before the athlete

She did what you said. If the movement did not change, the first hypothesis is that you said the wrong thing.

A cue that works on one person can be actively bad for another. The lifter who rolls onto the outside of her foot when told "knees out" did exactly what she was told. The cue was wrong for her, not the other way round.

Why it works. The opposite assumption ends the investigation. A coach who concludes the client cannot do it stops looking, and the cue that would have worked never gets tried.

Watch for

"She just doesn't feel it." Sometimes true. It is never the first thing to conclude, and it is certainly not concludable from one cue.
