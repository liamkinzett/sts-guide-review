Pain rules and when to refer · T-35.1

Name your best guess, call it a guess, never grade it

You may name your best guess by its proper clinical name. You must call it a guess, a physio confirms it, you never grade it, and it never delays a referral. That is the whole of your scope on an injury.

What a coach may do. Run the movement screens she can watch. Reach a working read of what is most likely going on. Name it by its standard clinical term. Use it to choose what to train and what to stop.

Say it like this

My best guess from what I'm seeing, and it is a guess, not a diagnosis, is X. A physio is the one who confirms it. Here is what I'd change in the meantime.

Name it properly or it is useless. A pinching hip (FAIS), not "something at the front of the joint". The (FADIR test), not "an impingement test". Her next move, and yours, is to search the name, so a vague term costs the lookup. Region by region carries the regional tests and the names to use.

What is still barred, and none of this has moved.

BarredWhy
Grading it. Mild, moderate, grade 2, "a small tear"A grade is a clinical measurement and you are not insured to make one
Palpating to find out how bad it is. Measuring a range to judge it "normal"Hands-on assessment of severity is a clinician's job, and severity is what a grade is
Any test needing hands on the joint. Anterior drawer, Lachman, laxity testingThese read a structure's integrity through your hands. Movement screens she can watch are not this: FADIR, a loaded split squat compared side to side, a step-down, a passive-then-active leg lift are all yours to run
Letting the guess delay or replace a referralThe referral numbers and the red flags are absolute. A working read never downgrades them
Saying it with certainty, or without naming it a guessThe label is what keeps it a guess. Without it you have diagnosed
Pregnancy and postpartumThe female body. Outside this topic entirely
Verdict

A guess is not a reason to keep her in the gym.

Seven out of ten reported consistently, or still consistent between three days and one week: she is referred, whatever your read says. Cauda equina flags go the same day, always.

What you do alongside the guess. You describe what you saw and what she reported, in plain language, to her and to whoever you refer her to. That description is genuinely valuable and nobody else has it: you are the only person watching her train. The working read adds to it. It never replaces it.

A scan she brings you, or is frightened of, is the same. You do not interpret it. Explaining why lifting is not the cause is inside your scope. Telling her what her joint can take is not. Region by region's low back pages explain what a scan does and does not tell her.

These still stand. A coach who suspects a spine condition refers rather than diagnoses, and may still say her guess (Mobility and stretching). You observe a visual sign, and you never assess, palpate, measure or grade it (Faults and what you can actually see). Pregnancy and postpartum belong to The female body.

Terms on this page

FAIS: Femoroacetabular impingement syndrome. Pinching at the front of the hip caused by the shape of the bone itself, either a bump on the ball or an over-deep socket, so the ball catches on the front rim of the socket. She feels it as a deep pinch at the front of the hip in deep flexion.

FADIR test: Flexion, adduction, internal rotation. A movement screen for the pinching hip that a coach can watch: knee to chest, across the body, then turned inward.
