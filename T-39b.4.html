Adherence as a signal · T-39b.4

She is not skipping. She is substituting her own workouts

A client who trains hard and trains something else is not on the four-pattern list. Her adherence numbers can look fine while she is doing none of what you wrote.

The four patterns are all about work not happening. This is work happening, and it is the wrong work. It is a different pattern with a different answer.

Name the cost before the behaviour. The cost is real and it is not obvious to her. Going off program, and on a good day jumping significantly up in weight, limits her long-term progression. It takes away from the peak.

The damage is to the phase, not to the session. She had a good day and spent it. The phases owns what each phase is buying, and that is what tells you how much a substituted week actually cost. A week swapped inside an accumulation block is not the same event as the same week swapped near a peak.

The standard for a good day is stated, and it is not more load. A good day means performing what needs to be performed, just better. It does not mean suddenly adding 10 or 20 kilograms to what her working sets were supposed to be.

The conversation. Getting mad does not help anything, as a coach. Two halves, and the order matters: here is what happened, then here is how we go forward from here. No verdict on her. The coaching relationship owns the objectivity, and Motivation and adherence owns the vocabulary. Where her logged adherence and what she actually did disagree, get a third reading rather than picking a side.

The two halves, in order

Here's what happened this week. And here's how we're going to go forward from here.

Then close the reason rather than the behaviour. The remedy is telling her what the program is for. Explaining the direction is what buys the adherence. A client who knows what the phase is building toward has a reason not to spend it. Writing program instructions owns the notes that carry that. The coaching relationship owns the intake agreement that made the plan hers in the first place.

Where it is boredom rather than disagreement, the answer is variation inside the prescription rather than a different session. Motivation and adherence says to engineer the novelty inside the plan before she goes looking for it outside.

Where it has become a question of whether the arrangement works at all, The coaching relationship owns the escalation. You do not improvise that conversation.

Watch for

Treating this as low adherence. The numbers may be excellent.

Treating it as defiance on the first occurrence. A hidden constraint, or a gym she cannot access, is at least as likely. Motivation and adherence owns the hidden constraint.
