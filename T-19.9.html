How to read an exercise page · T-19.9

Watch for points; it does not explain

The Watch for section names an injury caution, a population to modify for, or a prerequisite, and points to the topic that owns it. It does not explain the physiology. The female body owns what changes because she is a woman. Pain rules and when to refer owns pain and referral.

Why it works. A subject explained in two places will disagree within a year. Suppose an exercise page explained thinning spinal bone (vertebral osteoporosis) in its own words, and The female body explained it in others. A coach would soon be reading two versions. One home per subject keeps the Library saying one thing.

When a page says more. A flag that changes what you do with this movement belongs on the page in full. A contraindication that rules the movement out is the page's business. The test is whether the information alters the coaching of this exercise. Whether it is interesting does not count.

Two examples

# Points elsewhere

sub: Pregnant or postpartum clients on the Romanian deadlift page

What the page does: Names the population and sends you to The female body. The page does not decide what a pregnant client may lift.

# Written in full

sub: Never let her lock the knees out under load on a seated hamstring curl

What the page does: Gives the caution and the reason, because it changes how you coach every rep of that exercise.

Terms on this page

vertebral osteoporosis: The bones of the spine have lost density and can fracture under loads a healthy spine would tolerate.
