The knee is rarely the problem

The knee is where missing hip rotation, missing pelvic stability and missing ankle motion land. It is rarely where the problem started.

Why. The knee is a hinge. When the joint above it will not rotate properly, the hinge adopts the force of that rotation. It has very little capacity to compensate. A hip that will not rotate lands in it. So does a pelvis that will not hold still, or a foot that will not roll in and out.

The second half of the claim. Pain is a symptom of dysfunction, not the injury. The dysfunction sits below a threshold, and time multiplied by repetition walks her across it. So the dysfunction was there before the pain, and it persists after the pain goes. Not being in pain does not mean she has a high level of function.

Knee-directed work is the symptom-treating option

Terminal knee extensions, IT band rolling, anything aimed at the joint itself. It is no different from taking a couple of ibuprofen and then hitting a squat session.

So when she points at her knee, look at her hip, her pelvis and her ankle.
