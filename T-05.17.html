Training after a caesarean

After a caesarean, any deep core or abs work comes out for 12 weeks. Adjust techniques so nothing pulls on the scar.

Everything else in the postpartum return applies unchanged. Movement choice is what adjusts, not the model. Full body, low load, 2 to 3 reps in reserve, climbing week by week.

Where the twelve weeks comes from. It is not a number from a published guideline. It is clinical guidance from a physiotherapy practice, relayed directly to STS, and STS follows it as the conservative standard.

Why twelve is not arbitrary. A caesarean cuts through the tough connective sheet of the abdominal wall (abdominal fascia). Six weeks after the operation it is still healing and sits at roughly half its tensile strength. It reaches most of it between six and seven months.

Watch for

Pain at the scar or stitches. That is a stop sign on the stop-the-session page. End the session and refer.

Movements that pull on the scar indirectly. A heavy brace, a long lever or a twist can load the scar without being "core work". Adjust the technique.

Terms on this page

abdominal fascia: The tough connective sheet that holds the abdominal wall together. A caesarean cuts through it, and it heals slowly.
