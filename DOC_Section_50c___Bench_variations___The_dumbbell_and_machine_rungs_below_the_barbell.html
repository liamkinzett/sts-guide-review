The dumbbell and machine rungs below the barbell

Below the barbell sits a substitution ladder, not a set of variations. Each rung is less absolute load and more freedom of motion. The Barbell bench press page in Chest holds the ladder in full.

Walk the load down inside the pattern

Barbell. The most absolute load, the fixed path.

Dumbbells. Less load, a free arm path.

Machine chest press.

Cable chest press.

Single-arm dumbbell chest press. The least absolute load and the most freedom.

Two levers sit above the ladder. Slow the tempo. Or tire the chest first with a pec fly (pre-exhaustion), so the press runs at a lower absolute load.

A large deliberate arch that shortens the pressing range gets a neutral-grip dumbbell press across flat, incline and upright as the accessory. It gives back the range and the shoulder-blade freedom the barbell takes away.

A decline angle. Where she wants the lower-chest bias of a decline, take a high-to-low cable fly rather than a decline press. The decline arm path on cables is comfortable and safer at the shoulder than a decline press.

Terms on this page

pre-exhaustion: Tiring one muscle with an isolation exercise first, so that it is the limiter in the compound lift that follows.
