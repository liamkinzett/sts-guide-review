Breathing under load · T-45.5

Breathe into the belly and against the belt, never into the chest

The breath goes into the deepest part of her belly and pushes out against the belt. Never into the chest.

The mechanics. Pull in the deepest belly breath she can, and push it out against the belt. If instead she sucks her belly in, she folds under the load.

The visible tell on a squat. Chest breathing is the biggest breathing problem people have when they squat. Her lungs are not strong enough to start pushing 400 pounds off her back. Her brace is.

Watch the bar, not her face. A chest breath moves the bar. That is something you can see on a video without asking her anything, which makes it a diagnosis and not only a cue. Diagnosing from video covers reading the footage.

Belly breathing in every direction is the same skill as bracing a heavy lift. On a plank she breathes into the sides and back of her trunk under the brace. That is one reason the hold is trained at all.

When the belt is on. A belt is for the heavy, low-rep work, the heavy doubles and triples. It is not for the volume work. Submaximal sets are where her bracing is trained, and belting them removes that training effect. Heavy and few, belted. High volume at a lower percentage, unbelted. Effort and intensity holds the belt rule.

Watch for

The bar wobbling at the top of a squat on every breath. She is breathing into her chest. Re-teach the belly breath before anything else.

"Suck your stomach in" as a bracing cue. It does the opposite of a brace.

Say when you teach the belt breath

Big breath into your belly, right down low. Push it out into the belt, then go.
