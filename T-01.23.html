Muscle and growth · T-01.23

Why can some people build muscle in a deficit while others cannot?

Building in a deficit depends on headroom on both sides: room to grow, and fat to draw on.

Training status, available energy, the deficit's size and sleep all move it. Which matters most changes with the client.

The previous page gives the population answer: likely if new or returning, not promised if trained. This page is the mechanism under it, for the coach who wants to explain rather than assert.

Adaptive headroom matters. A beginner has a lot of room to improve. The stimulus is new, the nervous system is learning fast, and muscle can respond strongly to fairly modest training. A highly trained lifter has already captured much of that easy adaptation. That difference in training status can matter more than small differences in the size of the deficit.

Energy can come from stored body fat. A calorie deficit (calorie deficit) means she is not getting enough energy from food to cover total expenditure. That does not mean no energy is available: stored body fat can supply it. This is part of why someone with real stored energy and a strong training response can lose fat and build muscle at the same time. A very lean, highly trained athlete has less room on both sides of that equation.

The competition is in the signalling, not just the calories. Lifting switches on the building pathway (mTORC1). Hard endurance work switches on a fuel-sensing pathway (AMPK) that directly holds the building pathway back. Whether both jobs can run at once depends on whether her signalling and fat stores can cover both.

Protocol choices move the line. Six to twenty-four hours between a lifting session and a hard cardio session moves the interference away from the lifting. So does doing the lifting first. Steps and cardio covers the cardio dose.

Sleep belongs in the same conversation as food. One night without sleep cut protein building by 18%. It cut testosterone by 24% and raised cortisol by 21%. Over fourteen days on an equal deficit, people sleeping 5.5 hours lost more muscle and less fat than people sleeping 8.5 hours. Mild sleep restriction did not undo the training effect. Severe restriction did.

A worked case. Two clients on the same deficit. One is trained, lean and sleeping five and a half hours. The other is a beginner sleeping eight. The beginner has headroom on both sides and may build muscle while dieting. The trained client is holding what she has, and sleep is the first thing to fix.

What this means for coaching. Her place on the ledger depends on training status, available energy and sleep as much as on the deficit's size. Everything here was worked out without regard to her cycle or her hormones, so hold it loosely for her. The female body covers what changes because she is a woman.

Terms on this page

mTORC1: The switch inside a muscle fibre that turns mechanical loading into a higher rate of protein building.

AMPK: An energy-sensing switch inside the muscle. It turns on when fuel runs low, as it does in hard cardio, and holds the muscle-building switch back.

calorie deficit: Eating fewer calories than the body burns, so body weight falls.
