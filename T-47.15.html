What she will ask you · T-47.15

"I can't feel it working"

Feeling the muscle is not proof it worked. Her perception still matters. Two-sided on purpose.

The first half. Female physique competitors will say they feel their glutes work better in a sumo stance. Sensation does not correlate to stimulation. Feeling it does not mean it is working better.

The second half is half the rule. Sensation does not dictate stimulation, but it does play a role. As coaches we do not ignore our clients' perception of their own performance. Dismissing what she feels is a coaching failure, even when the feeling is not the finding.

What mind-muscle connection actually is. Motor learning. Practising a movement over and over and getting better at executing it. She has got cognitively better at moving through a movement, and better at calling in the groups of muscle fibres the movement needs (motor units). That is all it is.

What you want instead of feeling is position.

When she says she does not feel it

I don't need you to feel this. I need your body to get into the right position. If the position is right, the muscle is doing the work whether you feel it or not.

Feeling something can mean she did it wrong. Unexpected sensation in a squat usually means she shifted. A new feeling is a cue to check the position, not a sign the set was better.

Compared

columns: What she feels | What you check

Tells you: How the set felt to her. Real, and worth hearing. | Whether the position and the load did the job

Do this: Listen to it. Never dismiss it. | Judge the set on it

Watch for

"If you can't feel it, it's not working." Wrong, and it teaches her to chase a burn over a position.

"Doesn't matter what you feel." Also wrong. Her perception is data, and ignoring it is a coaching failure.

Terms on this page

motor units: One nerve cell and the bundle of muscle fibres it switches on together. A muscle is made of many of them, from small to large. Practice makes her better at calling in the right ones.
