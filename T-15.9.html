Tempo and pauses · T-15.9

No slow eccentric where tendon holds the end position

Wherever the loaded end of the range is held by tendon and ligament rather than by a contracting muscle, the eccentric stays controlled but brisk. A three to five second eccentric there is contraindicated: never program it.

The worked example is the dumbbell lateral raise. With the arm out to the side the shoulder is supported largely by tendon and ligament. A long loaded descent loads that connective tissue rather than the muscle. It produces joint irritation and tendon pain, and it prepares nothing.

Read it as a shape rather than a movement. Deep loaded overhead positions and a cable flye taken to full stretch are the same shape. In each, the end of the range is held passively. Ask where the load is sitting at the end of the rep. If the answer is the joint rather than the muscle, the eccentric is brisk.

Watch for

This one hides inside the standard eccentric. A blanket two to three second eccentric is already a slow eccentric on a lateral raise. The fault does not look like an exception being made. It looks like the standard being applied.

Terms on this page

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.
