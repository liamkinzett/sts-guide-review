Assessing a new client · T-06.18

Record your conclusions before you write the program

Before you open the program, record three things. Her assumed training age, the pathway, and one line of reasoning. Three lines and about ninety seconds of work.

Everything up to this point has been you reading and deciding. None of it is written down anywhere yet.

The three things

Her assumed training age. One overall read, taken from the form. You have not seen her move yet, so this is a working assumption rather than a classification. Note anything the form makes you suspect is a gap.

The pathway. It settles at intake and there is no second branch to close. Her stated goal is the only input. Nothing about her composition changes it. Record it and the reasoning.

One line of reasoning. Why this pathway and not another. This is the part everyone skips and the part that matters most later.

What it looks like.

> Training age. Assumed intermediate. Three years consistent, logs her sessions, can name what has stalled. Never mentions pressing so overhead may be a gap.

>

> Pathway. Priming first, then hypertrophy. Her stated goal is the only input. No cut conversation. She did not raise one.

>

> Reason. Named glutes specifically and trains consistently with a logbook. Overhead starts light and technical until I have seen it.

Where it goes. With the program, in her file. The next person to open it finds your reasoning attached to your decisions. These same three fields are the beginning of her weekly record. What you write here is not paperwork. It is the first entry in the running story of her coaching.

Why it works. Look at what already exists. The onboard form was written by her. The call summary was written by whoever sold to her. Neither document contains a single word of your thinking. Without these three lines, the logic joining her intake to her program exists only in your head.

That matters in two situations, and both of them will happen. In six months you will not remember why you chose this route. You will be trying to work out whether a plateau means the plan was wrong or the execution was. And another coach may have to cover for you, even for a week. They inherit her form, her program and no explanation of how one became the other.

Watch for

Writing the reason for the file rather than for a reader. "Client wants glutes" is not reasoning. It is a restatement of her goal. The line has to capture the decision you made and what tipped it.
