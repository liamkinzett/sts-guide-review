Common faults

Five common faults

# Range cut short

sub: Quarter and half reps

Why it happens: The sled is loaded past what she can move through a full range.

Do this: Reduce the load. Complete the range.

# Base too wide or too long, with weight into the heels

sub: High and wide foot placement

Why it happens: High feet feel stronger because the hips take over. Wide feet feel stable. Neither is the mid-foot stance STS uses.

Do this: One label, two faults. The high half drives the shin backwards (negative shin angle), which is the heels fault. The wide half is the base fault. Name whichever you see, and reset her feet to the middle of the platform.

# Butt wink (butt wink)

sub: The sled lowered past the pelvis. The tailbone peels off the pad

Why it happens: She has gone past the depth limit you recorded, or it was never recorded.

Do this: Stop the sled above it.

# Load past the position she owns

sub: Heavy loading with the heels off the plate

Why it happens: The load has outrun her ability to keep the whole foot down.

Do this: Reduce the load.

# Lumbar finish (lumbar finish)

sub: Hip press: using the lower back to open the hips

Why it happens: The hips have run out of range or the glutes have tired, so the lower back supplies the finish.

Do this: Finish at the hip.

Terms on this page

negative shin angle: Her front shin leans back, so the knee sits behind the ankle rather than out over the toes.

butt wink: At the bottom of the squat her pelvis tucks under and her lower back rounds. It usually shows up when she runs out of hip range or loses her brace.

lumbar finish: Finishing a hip movement by arching the lower back instead of extending the hip.
