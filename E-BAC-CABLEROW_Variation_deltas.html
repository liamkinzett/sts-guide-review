Variations

STS programs three versions: the palms-down seated cable row, the palms-facing seated cable row, and the single arm cable high row. The grip is not the governing choice on the two-arm versions. The bias is. What it's for carries both biases.

The three versions

# Pronated grip seated cable row

sub: Palms down (pronated grip)

What changes: Nothing about the bias. Declare it, lat or upper back, and coach that. The grip follows the handle you chose. On an upper-back row a palms-down grip with the arm path angled up reaches traps and rhomboids.

# Neutral grip seated cable row

sub: Palms facing (neutral grip)

What changes: The same. Declare the bias and coach that. On an upper-back row a palms-facing grip with the elbows flaring wider reaches traps.

# Single arm cable high row

sub: High to low, bench-supported or half-kneeling, upper-back-biased

What changes: The pulley height. It is the group's one stretched row and the chest-supported row's next version. What it's for has the setup, and the warning that it is a teaching tool first.

The one rule about the handle

The close-grip V-bar is banned for back rows. It forces the upper arms to turn hard inward (internal rotation). The elbows flare, and the line of pull leaves the lats and the upper back. The biceps becomes the main mover.

Prefer two separate handles to one rigid bar.

Terms on this page

pronated grip: Palms facing down or away from her, knuckles up. On a row it is the overhand grip.

neutral grip: Palms facing each other, or facing in toward her body. On a row it is the grip on two parallel handles.

internal rotation: Turning a limb inward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face back or down, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more toward the middle. An arm held that way is internally rotated.
