Muscle and growth · T-01.13

Are there different 'kinds' of muscle growth to train for?

Never pick a rep range to chase one "kind" of muscle growth over another.

You may hear muscle growth split into two categories. Myofibrillar hypertrophy (myofibrillar hypertrophy) is an increase in contractile protein. Sarcoplasmic hypertrophy (sarcoplasmic hypertrophy) is an increase in fluid, glycogen and other non-contractile material. This split is often used to justify different rep ranges, with low reps supposedly producing one "type" of growth and high reps producing the other. STS does not use this model for programming.

Why the split is less useful than it sounds.

A growing muscle can change in several ways inside. Contractile structures can increase, and the amount of non-contractile material can change too. The problem comes when these are presented as two clean, independently selectable adaptations that a coach can choose between by changing rep range. That programming rule has no basis. Even the accounts that describe the split concede the two rarely happen independently of each other. It is not a distinction you can program for.

What this means for coaching. Nothing in the program changes. No STS rule picks a rep range to bias one kind of growth over the other. Volume, Effort and intensity and Tempo and pauses are unaffected. Choose rep ranges for the reasons taught elsewhere in the library: loading, fatigue, exercise suitability, progression and closeness to failure.

What changes is what you say. "Higher reps for sarcoplasmic, lower for myofibrillar" is a sentence with no basis. Do not use it. What we do hold about changing a muscle's shape is small, and it lives in What she will ask you.

Say it like this

There aren't two kinds of muscle growth you can pick between with a rep range. Muscle grows. We pick reps for other reasons.

Terms on this page

myofibrillar hypertrophy: Growth by adding contractile protein inside the fibre. In the popular split, the kind that adds strength.

sarcoplasmic hypertrophy: Growth by adding fluid and glycogen around the contractile protein. In the popular split, size without strength. STS does not program for it.
