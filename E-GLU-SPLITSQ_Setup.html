Setup

Set her up in this order

Stance width at femur-head width. That is inside hip width, not outside it. A wider base is itself the compensation. Where she can't move sideways at the hip (frontal plane) she will choose a wider base to avoid having to.

Stance length found by procedure, not by eye. Have her perform a reverse lunge to a square front knee. Step back until the rear knee no longer reaches the floor. Then shift forward to the last spot where the back knee can touch. Cross-check from the side: torso angle and shin angle should match.

Rear foot on a box at roughly knee height, used as a pivot point. The working foot stays flat.

Front-leg dominant weight. The front leg is the working leg. The back leg is not there to carry the lift.

Load side chosen from her error, not by default. STS programs the opposite-hand dumbbell (contralateral load) or the goblet hold. Never a same-side dumbbell (ipsilateral load).

On step 5. The opposite-hand load is the answer when she drives the knee out and can't find the frontal plane. It is also the fix when her pelvis drifts to the back leg.

A same-side dumbbell is the textbook answer when she dumps inside her base of support, but STS doesn't program it. Coach that error with the cues rather than by moving the dumbbell. The goblet hold is a loading choice for a quad bias (see When to use it), not a fix for an error.

Terms on this page

frontal plane: Side-to-side movement, like a lateral lunge or a side raise.

contralateral load: The weight is held in the hand opposite the working leg.

ipsilateral load: The dumbbell held in the hand on the same side as the working leg. It suits a client who dumps inside her base of support, and STS doesn't program it.
