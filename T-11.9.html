Split design · T-11.9

Delts ahead of chest, and often no direct chest work

Deltoid work takes priority over pressing for the chest. A program with no direct chest work in it is a normal program here, not an oversight.

Off the strength path only. On the powerlifting and powerbuilding paths, where a tested lift is the goal, the bench is not negotiable. There she needs equal exposure across the foundational patterns, and the second pressing slot stays. This page is for the muscle-building path.

The reason is recovery, not preference. Her capacity to recover is finite and every set spends some of it. For a client whose goals are lower-body dominant, a standard pressing day spends that capacity on a muscle that does not serve her goal. It is not that chest work is bad. It is that it is expensive and it is buying something she did not ask for.

Two ways to make the room, both used. Drop a second dedicated pressing slot. Or convert an upper day into a day for the back of her body (posterior chain): back work combined with glutes and hamstrings.

A session that does press still pulls. Any session with a barbell bench needs an overhead press or a pulldown later in it. Building a session has the rule.

Watch for

Reading "bias" as "less of". A coach who leaves in two chest exercises and adds a lateral raise has not applied this rule. They have added work to a week that was already full.

Say it like this

Your program is built around what you've told me you want. Chest pressing isn't in there much because the recovery it costs is better spent on the stuff that's actually going to change how you look. It's not that it's bad for you. It's that it isn't the priority.

Terms on this page

posterior chain: The muscles down the back of the body that extend the hip and support the spine: glutes, hamstrings and the lower back muscles.
