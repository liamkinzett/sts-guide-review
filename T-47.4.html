What she will ask you · T-47.4

"Should I brace before every lift?"

She does not need to brace before every lift. The brace scales with the task. When most people brace, they tighten everything, and that is inefficient.

The mechanism. Brace and breath are separable, and the proportion belongs to the task. A heavy squat gets a full brace. A cable curl does not. Breathing under load owns the mechanism. This page is the client-facing half of it.

Belly breathing is rejected by name. She does not have lungs in her stomach. The cue misdescribes the anatomy, and Breathing under load carries what to teach instead.

The draw-in and pull-your-navel-to-your-spine instruction a previous trainer probably gave her is endorsed nowhere. Not once. That absence is the answer: do not teach it.

Say it like this

You don't need to lock everything down for every lift. The brace matches the job. Heavy bar, full brace. Light cable work, breathe and move.

Why it works. A brace that matches the task saves her effort for the lifts that need it. It also stops her holding her breath through a set of curls.

Watch for

"Brace your core" on every exercise. Tightening everything is the inefficiency you are trying to remove.
