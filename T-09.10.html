Effort and intensity · T-09.10

Rep ranges are always specific, never broad

A rep range is two or three reps wide. Write 8 to 10. Or 10 to 12. Or 12 to 15. Never 8 to 15.

A band of two or three reps is a prescription. A band of seven is a coach declining to choose. The client will resolve it for you in whichever direction is easier that day.

Why it works. The range is what makes progression readable. A client working in 8 to 10 who reaches ten on every set has earned more load, and both of you can see it. A client working in 8 to 15 has an enormous amount of room to drift inside. Nothing ever looks like a plateau or like progress. The wide band does not give her freedom. It removes the signal. Progression has the detail.

A worked example. Two clients both lift 20 kg on the same movement for six weeks. One is written 8 to 10 and moves from eight reps to ten. She has earned a jump. The other is written 8 to 15 and moves from nine to eleven. Nobody can say whether that was progress, a good day, or drift.

Watch for

A broad range appearing because you were unsure which one fits. Pick the narrow band you think is right and correct it at the next check-in. A clear wrong answer is fixable. A vague one is not.
