Pain rules and when to refer · T-35.31

Chronic fatigue and post-exertional malaise: refer

Verdict

A crash that runs for days after exertion is a referral, not a pacing problem.

Send her to a clinician experienced in ME/CFS before programming around it.

A multi-day crash after exertion is not a bigger version of ordinary pain. It is a different physiology, and you refer it. The pattern has a name (post-exertional malaise), and it is the defining feature of chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS).

Why the usual rules do not transfer. Fibromyalgia pain-monitoring rules and tendon pacing frameworks allow symptoms up to 3 to 5 out of 10, provided they resolve by the next morning. They run on 24 to 72 hour recovery windows. A client whose crash runs a full week is describing something else.

The risk runs the opposite way from ordinary pacing advice. Pushing through exertion on a pain scale is exactly the mechanism that can trigger a severe relapse. A pain-scale rule built for a tendon assumes the cost of overshooting is more pain. Here the cost is a multi-day systemic crash. That is a different failure entirely, not a bigger one.

What you do. Refer. This sits squarely inside your scope. Do not build a graded-exposure program from fibromyalgia or tendon material and call it safe for her. Send her to a clinician experienced in ME/CFS management before programming around a described crash pattern.

Say it like this

What you're describing doesn't match anything I can safely program around. I want you seen by someone who treats this specifically before we plan anything.

Terms on this page

post-exertional malaise: A crash in energy and function that arrives hours or a day after exertion and lasts days, out of proportion to the effort. The defining feature of chronic fatigue syndrome.

ME/CFS: Myalgic encephalomyelitis, also called chronic fatigue syndrome. Its defining feature is a multi-day crash after exertion. A clinician manages it; a coach does not program around it.
