Muscle and growth · T-01.9

Why do two people on the same program get different results?

Two lifters on the same program will not get the same result. Tell her that at the start, before she compares herself to anyone.

Training gives a stimulus. The size of the adaptation to that stimulus differs from person to person. Every squat program does not give every lifter the same squat.

Many inherited and biological traits can drive that difference. Muscle insertions and structure, fibre characteristics, and limb lengths and leverage all play a part. So do starting muscle size and training history, along with other factors that do not reduce to one simple explanation.

Asked which single factor matters most, the answer is not the one clients usually reach for. It is how strongly muscle responds to a lifter's own hormones (androgen receptivity). That ranks above the three she will name: where the muscles attach (insertions), fibre type, and limb length (levers).

This page is for the conversation, not the program. Nothing changes in what you write. What changes is that she was told at the start, rather than discovering it at week eight while watching someone else. When comparison itself becomes the problem, The coaching relationship covers it. This page is the physiology under that conversation.

Say it before she compares

Two people can run the same program and get different results. That's normal, and it's mostly things neither of you chose. We judge your program against you, not against anyone else.

Terms on this page

androgen receptivity: How strongly a person's muscle responds to hormones such as testosterone. It varies from person to person and is a large part of why results differ.

insertions: Where a muscle attaches to bone. It shapes how the muscle looks and its leverage, and it is set before training starts.

levers: Limb and torso lengths. They change how a movement loads the body.
