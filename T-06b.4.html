Her first program · T-06b.4

Load: start below what you think she can do

Pick the weight you believe fits, then go under it. Week one is where you find out what the weight should have been.

Why it works. The usual way to choose load is to land the target reps at the target effort. That needs a logbook and a client who can rate effort. On day one you have neither. The rule cannot be applied and something has to stand in for it. Erring light is that something.

The asymmetry is what makes it correct rather than timid. Too light costs one week of a block whose purpose was measurement anyway. Too heavy costs technique on patterns she is still learning. Technique is the one thing this block exists to protect.

There is a mechanism behind the asymmetry. A beginner cannot generate much whole-body fatigue. She cannot yet express strength efficiently enough to produce it. The usual penalty for under-loading barely applies to her.

The reference point, if you want one. A beginner's first loaded work sits around 45% of an estimated maximum where she could probably manage 50%. That is the size of the aim-off, not a number to prescribe from.

Watch for

Letting her talk you up on day one. She will finish a set that felt easy and ask for more weight. She is not wrong that she could lift it. She is wrong that lifting it this week is worth anything.
