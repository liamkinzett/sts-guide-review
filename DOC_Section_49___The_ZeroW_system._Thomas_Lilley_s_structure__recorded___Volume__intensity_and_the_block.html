Volume, intensity and the block

The strength system runs low volume on the competition lift and moderate volume on the pattern. Effort is held high at one to two RIR all the time. These are a competitive powerlifter's numbers.

The numbers

# Main lift volume

sub: Two to three sets a week per competition lift

Detail: For an established competitive powerlifter. That is adequate, not low, because the accessories feed the lift.

# Pattern volume

sub: Six to twelve sets per movement pattern per week

Detail: For strength (movement pattern).

# Intensity

sub: One to two RIR, held high all the time

Detail: Intensity is what drives the adaptations (RIR).

# The four-week wave

sub: 2 RIR, then 2 or 1, then 2, then 2 or 1

Detail: Week 1 two RIR. Week 2 two or one. Week 3 two. Week 4 two or one. It is never swung wildly.

# Reps across blocks

sub: 8/6/6, then 6/5/4, then 5/5/4/3

Detail: Block 1 runs 8/6/6. Block 2 runs 6/5/4. Block 3 runs 5/5/4/3. These are competition-lift schemes, pinned to percentages of a tested max.

# Block length

sub: Fixed in advance for a competitive powerlifter

Detail: Four weeks hard, one week deload. A general client's block has no fixed deload week; The phases sets it.

# Why a lighter block exists

sub: Above 80% a technical bottleneck folds

Detail: Above 80% a weak link, a weak upper back say, folds because the load is too heavy to control. Waving down under 80% lets her build the position without the load folding her.

One strength-system block, for a competitive powerlifter

Weeks 1 to 4 | 4 | Hard. Effort waves 2 RIR, then 2 or 1, then 2, then 2 or 1

Week 5 | 1 | Deload. Sets drop to one or two, effort stays high

What the system rejects. Leaving reps in the tank. A five-by-five at 70% drives neither hypertrophy nor skill expression, so it buys nothing.

Two to three sets a week is not a muscle-volume prescription

It does not contradict the 15 to 20 sets an intermediate gets. The two numbers count different things. The strength system's counts exposures to the competition lift. STS counts hard sets per muscle per week, which in this system the accessories supply.

The week still holds full muscle volume. Only the barbell exposure is low, and it is low on purpose so the lift stays sharp instead of fatigued.

A coach who reads the competition-lift number as a muscle-volume prescription will badly under-train her client.

Terms on this page

movement pattern: The family a lift belongs to, whatever the implement. Squat, hinge, push, pull, lunge.

RIR: Reps in reserve. How many more reps she could have done when the set stopped. 2 RIR means she stopped two reps short of failure.
