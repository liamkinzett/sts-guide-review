Warming up · T-16.11

Prep work is dynamic and done with intent

A movement earns a place in the warm-up if it is dynamic and performed with real intent rather than held or drifted through. That is the inclusion test, and it is written as a test rather than a list so you can judge a movement nobody has named.

What "real intent" means. Dynamic movement done above roughly 20% of maximal effort is what prepares her to lift. Twenty percent is a low bar. It rules out drifting through a movement, not effort. A hip airplane done with an active foot and a controlled reach passes. The same shape wobbled through while she chats does not. A passive hold fails the test at any duration, because nothing in it is dynamic.

The menu, indexed by what the session opens with.

OverheadThe pressing menu, plus deliberately rotating the shoulder blade through its full range
PullingThoracic extension. Trap 3 raises

The pressing menu runs in an order. Ribcage first, then the shoulder blade, then rotation. Each one frees the next. The movements themselves are taught in Priming lifts.

Terms on this page

prep work: The movements inside the warm-up slot that get the first loaded movement ready. Chosen for the session in front of you.

thoracic extension: Straightening the upper back so the chest comes up and the rounding between the shoulder blades disappears. Over a foam roller it is a standard prep movement for a hinge or a pull.
