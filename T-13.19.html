Choosing exercises · T-13.19

A Smith machine preference for glute work is not the wrong call

A client who prefers the Smith machine for her glute work is not automatically making a wrong choice. Earned stability and exercise preference are two separate questions.

A Smith machine holds her position for her (externally stabilised). The bar path is fixed. It does not demand the stability a free barbell squat requires her own trunk to supply. A client who has not yet earned a free barbell squat is not making a wrong choice by preferring the Smith. She is choosing the tool that matches where she actually is.

Where it becomes a genuine trade rather than a free pass. For a client who has earned the free-weight version, the muscle ends her set and her position no longer does. Her Smith preference then stops being about readiness. It becomes a choice about where the movement is hardest (resistance profile).

A fixed bar path changes the balance demand. It can shift emphasis toward or away from the glutes, depending on where her feet sit relative to the bar.

That is a real biomechanical difference from a free barbell squat. Not a downgrade.

What to do

Run the earned-stability test first. What ends her set: the muscle or her position?

She has not earned the free-weight version. The Smith is not a compromise. It is the correct tool, and nothing needs defending to her.

She has earned it and still prefers the Smith. Judge the choice on its resistance-profile merits, the way the three screens already ask. Never on the assumption that the barbell version is owed to her because of time served.

Terms on this page

externally stabilised: The machine, bench or support holds her position so she does not have to.

resistance profile: Where in the range an exercise is hardest and where it is easiest. It is set by the line of the load against the joint, so a dumbbell and a cable can load the same muscle very differently.
