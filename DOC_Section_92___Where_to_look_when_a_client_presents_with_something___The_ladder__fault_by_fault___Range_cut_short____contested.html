Range cut short

She stops short of the full range the movement is built for.

Reduce the load and complete the rep.

Where it shows, and the fix

# Standing curl

sub: Cutting the top range

Do this: Complete the rep

# Shortened-position curl

sub: Chasing a pump in the shortened range. Long squeezes, short range, load falling away across the set

Do this: Your judgement call, not a settled fault. A long squeeze in the short range is fine when it still costs her nothing. Correct it when the range stops being the rep.

# Calf raise

sub: Cutting the range. Also load not matched to range: one load across a steep curve truncates the end it does not suit

Do this: Two loads, per above

# Romanian deadlift

sub: B-stance: she starts already flexed at the hip

Do this: Reset the start position

# Hip thrust

sub: Never reaches full hip extension

Do this: Reduce load, finish the rep

# Leg press and hip press

sub: Quarter and half reps

Do this: Reduce load, complete the range

# Overhead cable extension

sub: A tiny range of motion, which forfeits the only thing this movement buys

Do this: Reduce load, reach the stretch

There is no set cue written for this fault. Say the fix in plain words, one thing this set.

Other cues on those exercises. Not about this fault. Use them when the same set needs them.

Cues

When: Standing curl

Say: Lock your upper arm and shoulder blades in stone.

Why: The first cue. It closes the two biggest leaks in one instruction: elbow drift and scapular shrug.

When: Standing curl

Say: Smash your forearms directly into your biceps

Why: For the top of the rep.

When: Standing curl

Say: Forget about hands, focus on elbow flexion.

Why: For a lifter who is lifting the handle rather than flexing the elbow.

When: Standing curl

Say: Rotate your torso outward so your palm can face straight up.

Why: A setup-level fix for the diagonal wrist path. Pair it with "Grip a post with your opposite hand to keep your torso angle locked."

When: Calf raise

Say: An inch lost is 25% of calf range.

Why: It is a range-of-motion descriptor rather than a dose.

When: Calf raise

Say: Drive through your big toe

Why: STS gives this as a step-up correction for a heel-dominant client, referencing standing-calf-raise foot pressure as the reference sensation. It describes calf-raise foot pressure accurately.

Terms on this page
