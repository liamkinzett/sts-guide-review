Cues

Try them in this order. The first two are the standard. The next two are correctives for one error each, and the last is an internal cue to pair with a sensation check.

Cues

When: Every rep, on every version

Say: Big toe, knee, sternum in line.

Why: External cue. The primary organising cue. Try it first on every variation.

When: The bottom of the rep

Say: Hip outside of knee.

Why: External cue. The frontal-plane check (frontal plane). Where this isn't happening the movement isn't doing its job, however good the rest looks.

When: She is too far forward over her toes and the heel lifts

Say: Heel pressure.

Why: External cue. A corrective for this one error. Full foot pressure is still the default on every lift, so don't cue heels as a habit.

When: She is too heel-dominant and the toes lift

Say: Drive through the big toe.

Why: External cue. The opposite corrective. Which of the two you use depends on her error.

When: Her torso drifts, or she can't feel the hip

Say: Stack your ribcage over your pelvis.

Why: An internal cue, so pair it with a sensation check. If she can't feel the hip she is too far into the quad. Have her sit back and chase depth until there is a stretch across the side of the glute.

On the heel cue. Elsewhere STS bans "push through your heels" as a default and replaces it with full foot pressure (see Romanian deadlift). Here it is a corrective for one named error, used only when her heel is lifting. It never becomes the way she does every rep.

Terms on this page

frontal plane: Side-to-side movement, like a lateral lunge or a side raise.
