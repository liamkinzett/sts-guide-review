What it's for

The machine and chest-supported row is a horizontal pull with the stability supplied by a pad or a seat (externally stabilised).

Every machine row STS coaches is upper-back-biased: wide elbows, the shoulder blades travelling their full range, and full retraction demanded at the finish. The target is the traps and the rhomboids.

STS never sets a machine row up lat-biased. If the lat is the target, she goes to a cable or a free-weight setup instead. See Seated cable row and Free-weight row.

The versions, each declared

# Pronated grip chest supported row

sub: Palms down (pronated grip). The rhomboid and trap setting

Why: An overhand grip biases the traps and rhomboids rather than the lats. Pair it with the family's upward-angled arm path.

Setup: Seat set so the arms fully extend comfortably in front.

# Neutral grip chest supported row

sub: Palms facing (neutral grip). The shoulder-extension setting

Why: Neutral or rotating handles let the arm travel truly behind the body. Taking the arm behind the body that way stops the load landing on the joint at the top of the shoulder (AC joint). Named targets are mid-back, mid-lats and lats.

Do this: Choose it where the arm needs to travel behind the body. Choose it where the AC joint is the thing you are protecting.

# Machine low row

sub: Follows the seated cable row's upper-back standards

Why: Seat height is the trap-to-rhomboid dial. A higher seat pulling low to high biases the rhomboids further.

# Chest-supported T-bar row

sub: You will see it in gyms. STS doesn't program it

Why: Feet on the platform, chest into the pad. The support protects the shoulder blades. Retract and squeeze to finish each rep. Coach it to the family's standards if she meets one.

# Chest supported dumbbell row on an incline bench

sub: Also not programmed. The equipment-missing swap

Why: Backrest at roughly 45 degrees, seated backwards leaning into it, arms hanging. Swaps has it.

Why the slot exists, stated three ways.

  • External stability removes the stabiliser bottleneck. Chest support takes the low back and the hips out of the row and isolates mid-back muscles that are otherwise hard to grow. What is left is the shoulder blade moving around the rib cage.
  • It removes the limit that ends the set early. A free-weight row demands a brutal hinge held still for the whole set (isometric). The low back or the core almost always fatigues first and ends the set before the back reaches failure.
  • A lifted chest changes what is loaded. Rounding over the pad stretches and loads the spinal muscles (erectors). A lifted chest puts the load onto the rhomboids and mid-traps and builds mid-back thickness.

    • Resistance holds tension across the range. The coaching attention is on the full travel. That means the stretched position with the blades out at the front, and the shortened position with the blades in at the finish. There is a boundary on how much shortening is worth having; How to do it sets it.

    The slot it fills. A send-it slot in a session, one of the loadable exercises placed after a high-skill, low-load opener (see Building a session). It is also the shoulder-extension assessment when a dip is too much. A chest-supported row shows whether the shoulder blade dumps over the top.

    Terms on this page

    externally stabilised: The machine, bench or support holds her position so she does not have to.

    pronated grip: Palms facing down or away from her, knuckles up. On a row it is the overhand grip.

    neutral grip: Palms facing each other, or facing in toward her body. On a row it is the grip on two parallel or rotating handles.

    AC joint: The acromioclavicular joint, where the collarbone meets the top of the shoulder blade. You can feel it as the bony bump on top of the shoulder. Taking the arm behind the body with a neutral grip keeps load off it.

    isometric: A hold. The muscle works against the load but the joint does not move.

    erectors: The long muscles either side of the spine that hold the back straight and extend it. Rounding over a pad stretches and loads them instead of the mid-back.
