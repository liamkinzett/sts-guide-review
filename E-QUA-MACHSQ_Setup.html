Setup

Set her up in this order

Back flat against the pad. It stays there for every rep. The pelvis must not round off it at any point.

Foot placement decides whether this is a quad exercise at all. On a hack squat, a moderate-to-wide stance with the toes turned out. A narrow stance restricts range and loses the outer sweep of the thigh. On a pendulum, wider with the toes out suits most clients.

Feet low on the plate. Set high, they drive the shin backwards (negative shin angle) and turn the movement into a poor glute exercise. The gym already has a better tool for that job.

Pendulum only: take the ankle out of the equation. A squat wedge or a lifting shoe gets her past ankle mobility so the knee can reach full bend.

Smith machine only: upright torso with the feet set slightly forward. That maximises knee bend and reduces both hip bend and twisting load on the spine.

On the hack squat stance. Moderate-to-wide with the toes out is the stance to start from, because it keeps the range and the outer sweep. A narrow stance with the heels elevated is the maximum quad-bias option. Start wide, and treat narrow-and-elevated as a deliberate choice for a client who owns full depth there.

Terms on this page

negative shin angle: Her front shin leans back, so the knee sits behind the ankle rather than out over the toes.
