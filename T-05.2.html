Programming around the cycle

Cycle-syncing a program to a calendar does not work. Never program from one.

Cycle-syncing means changing her training or her food by the predicted phase of her cycle. It is popular, and it does not hold up.

Nothing measured shows her strength moving by phase, so there is no basis for raising or lowering her percentages by phase. The same goes for her food. Protein and energy needs do not change substantially across the cycle. The small shift in the second half of the cycle (luteal phase) is trivial in size.

The mechanism is not there. The process that builds new muscle protein after training (muscle protein synthesis) does not differ across the cycle. Most cycle-syncing rests on the idea that it does.

A generic calendar cannot work even in principle. Cycle length and ovulation timing vary between women. A standard template cannot map onto any one of them.

Why it matters. Cycle-syncing presents as taking women's physiology seriously. Prescribing from a calendar that does not match her cycle is the opposite. Programming around the cycle in practice means dropping the calendar entirely and reading her, week to week, the way the next page describes.

Watch for

A client arriving with an app that has synced her training to a predicted phase. She is not wrong to care. The calendar is wrong.

Say it like this

I'm not going to program from a cycle calendar. It doesn't hold up, and your cycle isn't the average one anyway. I'd rather work off what you actually report week to week.

Terms on this page

luteal phase: The second half of the cycle, from ovulation to the next period, when progesterone is higher.

muscle protein synthesis: The process that builds new muscle protein after training. It is the mechanism behind muscle growth.
