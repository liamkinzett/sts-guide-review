Common faults

Nine common faults

# The range appears at the knee

sub: Hips travel back and the knees travel back with them. Shin vertical or angled back

Why it happens: She does not yet separate hip from knee, so both joints share the movement. The load ends up over her ankles.

Do this: Cue "forward shin angle".

# Weight into the heels

sub: Toes lift, weight sits back

Why it happens: "Push through the heels" is what most lifters have been told. Sitting back feels safe, and it stops the hips travelling.

Do this: Cue "forward shin angle", with the head slightly forward as a counterbalance.

# The grip escapes the demand

sub: Palms-down dumbbell hold, shoulders float. She reports an achy upper back

Why it happens: A palms-down hold (pronated grip) makes it hard to switch the lats on. The shoulders drift forward and the upper back takes the load.

Do this: Switch to a neutral grip. Add straps when grip is what ends the set.

# Bar off the midline

sub: Bar drifts forward, sometimes scraping the shins

Why it happens: The lats are off, so nothing holds the bar to her.

Do this: Brace the lats and track the bar over the midfoot. Cue "shoulder blades down" before the rep.

# Chasing range she does not own

sub: Hips stop, torso keeps folding

Why it happens: She thinks depth is the bar reaching the floor. Once the hips run out of range the back supplies the rest.

Do this: Stop where the hips stop. Set her end range by watching the hips, not the bar.

# Rib flare

sub: Big lower-back arch, chin up. Hip extension replaced by lower-back extension

Why it happens: The ribs were never brought down before rep one, so "chest up" becomes an arch in the lower back.

Do this: Ribs to hips before the rep. Cue "ribs down".

# Base too wide or too long

sub: Stance wide, toes splayed. Knees track out and the hip's inward turn is lost

Why it happens: A wide stance feels stable, but it blocks the inward turn of the hip (hip internal rotation) the hinge needs to load the glute.

Do this: Narrow the stance to hip width, toes straight.

# The pelvis escapes to the back leg

sub: B-stance. Back heel drops, weight shifts back. It has quietly become a two-legged lift

Why it happens: The front leg fatigues or the load is too heavy, and the back leg takes over.

Do this: Kickstand contact only, back foot on its toe. Drop the load if the heel keeps landing.

# Range cut short

sub: B-stance. She starts already bent at the hip

Why it happens: Setting up over a dumbbell tips her forward before the set begins.

Do this: Reset the start position. Stand tall before every rep one.

Lower-back pump is a clue, not a verdict. A pumped lower back after RDLs does not by itself mean she is doing them wrong. Check four causes in this order: heel-heavy pressure, hinging through the knee, going past her active hip range, and a lost brace. Fix the first one you find and re-film. Pain, as opposed to pump, is Pain rules and when to refer.

Terms on this page

pronated grip: Palms facing down or away from her, knuckles up. On a dumbbell RDL it lets the shoulders roll forward and the lats switch off.

hip internal rotation: The thigh turning inward at the hip. A hinge needs a little of it for the glute to load; a wide, toes-out stance takes it away.
