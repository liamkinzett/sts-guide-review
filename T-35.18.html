Pain rules and when to refer · T-35.18

A click she has always had is not a referral

New against a baseline is read by what changed. A longstanding noise without pain is not a referral on its own.

The joint row already separates a painful click, lock or pop from a painless one. The painful one is a referral trigger. The painless one is not, on that row. What it does not spell out is how to tell a change from a habit.

The intake questions are the tool. Was there an incident, did it creep in, did one rep feel different from the one before it? A noise she has "always had" without pain sits on the painless side and needs nothing done to it. A noise that appeared after a specific tweak is a change. The onset question tells you whether to treat it as new information.

Where pain has entered alongside the change, it is no longer a noise question. It is the joint row: refer. The first sort still runs first: is this a warning or the stimulus, before how bad. A region-specific mechanism, where one exists for the joint, belongs to Region by region.
