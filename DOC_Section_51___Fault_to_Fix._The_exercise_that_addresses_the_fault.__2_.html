Three kinds of fix

Every fix on the pages that follow is one of three kinds: a regression, an accessory or a setup change. Know which one you are giving her, because each changes the program in a different way.

The three kinds of fix

# Regression

sub: Move her to an easier or more constrained movement

What it means: She stops doing the thing. The squat comes out and a goblet squat, a box squat or a leg press goes in, until the position holds.

When to use it: The fault is present at the load she is on and a cue has not moved it.

# Accessory

sub: Add a movement elsewhere in the session or the week

What it means: She keeps doing the thing. The lift stays in the program and a second movement builds what the lift is exposing.

When to use it: The lift itself is worth keeping and the gap is strength or control somewhere specific.

# Setup

sub: Change how the lift is set up

What it means: The lift itself changes shape. Bar height, stance, grip, touch point, rack height, tempo or the order of the session.

When to use it: The fault comes from where she starts or how the lift is arranged, so a cue cannot hold it.

This is not the cue list. What to say when I see a fault answers what you say when you see a fault. This topic answers what you program. Where a fault has only a cue and no exercise, it appears here as cue-only. It is sent to the cue pages rather than given a row.

A fault with no exercise-level fix is listed as cue-only at the end of its lift. Never guess an accessory for it.

Fault names are the names from Faults and what you can actually see. An exercise page never creates a rule, and this table never creates a fault.
