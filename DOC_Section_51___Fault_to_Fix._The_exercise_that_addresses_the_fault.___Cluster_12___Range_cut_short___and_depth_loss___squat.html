Range cut short on the squat: box at her depth

Set a box at the depth she actually has, then lower the box before you add load. Going lighter and squatting deeper is not the answer on its own. Depth has to arrive in the desired position, without deviating from it.

Range cut short on the squat

# She squats halfway

sub: Regression: a box at her depth

What you see: She stops well above parallel.

Do this: Find a box that height and start with a box squat. Regress along the ladder while keeping range the same: counterbalance box squat, then goblet box squat, then no box. Bring the centre of mass closer to her before you take the box away. The first move is always to lower the box.

Exercises: Goblet squat

# She has never squatted deep under load

sub: Accessory: build the depth where a machine holds her

What you see: Depth appears on a machine and vanishes under a bar.

Do this: Build depth in patterns where the machine or bench holds her position (externally stabilised). Leg press, hack squat, belt squat, machine squat, and a single-leg rear-foot-elevated split squat. Film every set to confirm she reaches depth without rounding or tucking.

Exercises: Leg press; Hack squat; Smith machine squat; DB Bulgarian split squat

# One side is weaker than the other

sub: Accessory: scale to one leg

Do this: Leg press and hack squat for the hips. Where there is a left-to-right difference, scale the same movement down to its single-leg version.

Exercises: Single leg press

The go-lighter-and-squat-deeper trap

If she cannot squat to depth, the solution is not just to go lighter and squat deeper. That is part of it. What is missing is squatting deeper in the desired position without deviating from it. Otherwise she either breaks, or she starts squatting high again on her own.

Terms on this page

externally stabilised: The machine, bench or support holds her position so she does not have to.
