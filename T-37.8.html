Returning to training · T-37.8

Returning after illness: her doctor decides, not you

Returning after illness is not the same decision as returning after a layoff. She does not pick up where she left off, and you are not the one who decides she is well.

The two-week rule on the detraining page is for a client who simply stopped training. Illness is a different process. The criteria below are the national health service's own.

The general instruction, for any illness the criteria do not name. Anyone recovering from a moderate or severe illness speaks with her healthcare provider before returning to exercise.

Verdict

Her doctor decides when she is well. You do not.

You are not grading how sick she was. Your scope is a guess, never a grade, exactly as everywhere else.

Where she has had COVID-19 specifically, the published criteria say people can start exerting themselves more if all three are true.

After COVID-19, all three before she loads again

10 days of rest since symptoms started.

At least seven days with no symptoms.

No longer taking any medications such as paracetamol.

Read the scope

Those three are published for COVID-19 only. Do not apply the ten days and the seven days to a head cold or to a fever of unknown cause. For anything else, the general instruction above is what you have.

Training with a fever or a cold. While she is unwell, training stops, and a head cold gets the same advice: the illness protocol page says so. When she is well enough to come back is her doctor's call. Never decide that yourself. The answer to "can I train with a fever" is no, and her GP says when she is ready.

Red flags

title: Stop the session and send her to a clinician if she has any of these

Chest pain or palpitations

Unexpected breathlessness

Signs of blood clotting, such as swollen calves

emergency: Severe central crushing chest pain lasting more than 10 minutes: call 000.

Pain rules and when to refer carries the triple zero threshold in full. This is the same call arriving through a different door.

Say it like this

You've been unwell, so we're not testing anything this week. Ask your GP before we go back to normal loading.

You are not refusing to train her. You are refusing to be the person who decided she was ready.

Why it works. The person who knows how sick she was is her doctor. Everything a coach can see is downstream of that. A client who feels fine on the day is the exact case the guidance exists for.

Watch for

"I sweated it out", "it was only a cold", "I feel fine now". All three are self-assessments, and none of them is the clearance the guidance asks for.

Once she is genuinely cleared, run the layoff return. Illness is treated as a longer layoff than the calendar shows. That means compound work with minimal isolation, calibrated to the training age she has now rather than the one she had before she was ill.

The first session's load comes back sooner and lower than she finished on, never anchored to her last number. A detrained client improves on almost anything. Warn her in advance that the same load will feel heavier.

The illness protocol page sets the shape of the ramp: two to three weeks, weight down first, her normal number of sessions.
