Mobility and stretching · T-16b.1

Training through full range is the mobility work

Full range resistance training gets her the same range a stretching routine would, and it changes the tissue on the way. Stretching does not.

The equivalence is on measured range. The mechanism is not equal. Both expose the muscle and its tendon to tension at length. That tension is what makes the muscle add length by laying down new contractile units in series (sarcomeres).

Resistance training has a second route that passive stretching does not. When a muscle contracted at length lets go under load, elastic recoil lengthens the fibres while they are still carrying it. Loaded contraction at long length grows series length far more than passive stretching over the same time.

Why the second half matters. Most of what stretching buys is neural rather than structural, which the next pages explain. So "training is as effective as stretching" is the weakest true statement available here. Training reaches the same range and it changes the tissue. That is the claim, and it is not the same claim.

What it looks like. Picture a client who squats to depth, pulls from the floor and presses overhead three times a week. She is loading every major joint through its full range under control. Her hips, ankles and shoulders are getting a mobility dose bigger than any routine she would do at home.

Watch for

Reading "as effective as stretching" as the whole finding. The mechanism is better, not just equal. Teach it that way.

Say it like this

Squatting to depth and pulling from the floor is your mobility work. You are already doing it, three times a week, under load.

Terms on this page

sarcomeres: The tiny contracting units a muscle fibre is built from, laid end to end in a long chain. A muscle gets longer by adding more of them in series.
