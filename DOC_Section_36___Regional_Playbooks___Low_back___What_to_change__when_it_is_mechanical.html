Low back: what to change when it is mechanical

Red flags clear? Then three mechanical presentations have a known first lever.

Mechanical means the pain comes from how she moves and loads, and changes when that changes.

Three presentations with a known first lever

# Low-back pump on an RDL

sub: Her lower back fills up and burns during Romanian deadlifts (RDL)

Why it happens: It is a clue, not a verdict. Four causes, checked in this order: pressure heavy in her heels, hinging through the knee, going past her active hip range, and losing her brace.

Do this: Work down the four causes in order. Fix the first one you find, then look again.

# "Feeling it in the low back" on a hip extension or kickback

sub: She reports it. You will not see it

Why it happens: Her lower back is supplying range that her hip should be supplying (lumbar compensation).

Do this: Ask her after the set, every time. This is a fault you find by asking, never by watching.

# Position degrading across a set

sub: Rep one looks nothing like rep eight

Why it happens: She has run out of the strength or the brace to hold the position under that load.

Do this: Reduce the load.

Why the order of the four RDL causes matters. Each one earlier in the list can produce the ones after it. Heel-heavy pressure tips her into hinging through the knee. Hinging through the knee sends her past her hip range. Past her hip range, the brace goes. Fix the earliest and the rest often disappear.

Terms on this page

RDL: The Romanian deadlift. A hinge from standing with a slight knee bend, pushing the hips back, that loads the hamstrings and glutes.

lumbar compensation: The lower back moves or arches to supply range that another joint has run out of. She feels it in her back on a hip exercise, and you cannot see it from outside.
