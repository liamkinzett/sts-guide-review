Safety bar squat

The safety squat bar is an upper-back and erector movement rather than a quad one. The handles sitting in front, plus gravity, put her in a position she can hold without much body awareness. The bar encourages spinal stabilisation rather than demanding it first, and it is a really good exercise for building upper-back strength.

Selection. The test is whether her upper back is a weak point. Where it is not, that is a reason to leave the bar on the rack rather than a default. Its second role is insurance. It trains the rounded-over position, so a bad rep on a competition bar meets a position she has already trained.

Watch for

Reaching for it as a shoulder workaround. Use it only where her upper back is the actual target, never as a default for a low bar she cannot hold. A shoulder that will not tolerate low bar gets closer hands first, then high bar.

Pushing up on the handles to start the rep. That removes the demand on her upper back to resist folding (anti-flexion moment). She arrives at the bottom with the muscles beside her spine (erectors) unloaded, which is the opposite of the reason the bar was chosen.

Where it sits. When equipment stops a barbell back squat, the swaps run in order. The safety bar squat where the rack has one, then the front squat, then the goblet squat. It counts as the squat pattern when you count how often the pattern is trained.

Terms on this page

anti-flexion moment: The load trying to fold her forward at the trunk, and the work her back does to resist it.

erectors: The long muscles either side of the spine that hold the back straight and extend it. They hold her back from rounding under a load.
