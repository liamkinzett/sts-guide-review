What to do when she presents with wrist pain

The wrist is rarely where it began. Three rules do the work. Look at the hand first. Keep the wrist neutral at the bottom of a press. Remember that wrist flexor load is felt at the elbow.

Work through it in this order

Test. The hand first, because it reports on the shoulder. Then the sort.

Read. A wrist complaint may not be a wrist problem, and may not even present at the wrist.

Do. Neutral in the sideways plane at the bottom of a press. Closer is not better.

Change. Re-check the hand position before you change load or exercise.

Escalate. Numbness, pins and needles, night pain or grip giving way go out immediately.

Step 1. Test

The hand is where you look first. It gives neither false negatives nor false positives, so intervening there changes the whole system. Then sort every movement into helps, no effect, hurts.

Step 2. Read

A wrist complaint may not be a wrist problem. Wrist flexor load is felt higher up, and can arrive as pain on the inside of the elbow. If she reports elbow pain, read the elbow pages in this topic as well.

Step 3. Do

Neutral in the sideways plane at the bottom of a press. Closer is not better, and it is not dose dependent. Once she is neutral, do not coach her further in.

Your working read. Name it, label it a guess

The governing claim. Tendon pain at the wrist and elbow is not an overuse injury. It is a misuse injury: some dysfunction or instability is irritating the place where the tendon attaches. So the question is never "how much has she done". It is "in what position".

Tendon or attachment? Where the muscle meets the tendon, or where the tendon meets the bone. It does not matter to what you do. Either way it is the mechanical light on the dashboard. Name it if she asks. Do not let naming it change what you do next.

It is not load dependent. It is range-of-motion specific

The single most useful line in this region. It hurts at the elbow on skull crushers, and not on rope extensions. Same muscle, same load, different position. Finding the position that provokes it is the job, and scaling the position does not mean scaling the load.

What you see, what you might guess, what changes on Monday

# Pain on the inside of the elbow, into the wrist flexors. Desk worker, mouse all day

sub: Working read: medial epicondylitis (golfer's elbow)

Why it happens: A day of claw hands on a keyboard, then a lift asking for full elbow and wrist extension overhead. It is not what she did in the gym.

Do this: Ask what positions she is in most of the day. Put something between the desk and the bar, and bridge the position before you load it.

# Pain on the outside of the elbow, worst on pushdowns

sub: Working read: lateral epicondylitis (tennis elbow)

Do this: Change the position, not the weight. Find the variation that does not provoke it and train there.

# Wrist pain that appeared with close-grip pressing, diamond push-ups, plate presses or crossover flies

sub: Working read: the wrist bending sideways to compensate

Why it happens: Bring the hands in past the line of the AC joint and the wrist has to bend toward the thumb or little finger to cope.

Do this: The AC joint plane rule, the same one the shoulder runs on. Never load the hands inside it. Widen to her own shoulder width. If she wants the arms-in path, hands drawn in toward the midline (adducted), go single arm.

# Wrist pain at the bottom of a bench press

sub: The wrist has left neutral in the sideways plane

Why it happens: Little fingers trying to touch the forearm, bent either way, leaves her in a world of hurt.

Do this: Neutral wrist at the bottom. Note the implement: a fixed machine holds the wrist neutral whatever the shoulder does. A free weight lets her express wrist position from the shoulder, which is the point and also the risk.

# Biceps or forearm complaining on curls, wrist held palm-down

sub: Position, not tissue

Why it happens: The biceps is disadvantaged with the palm down. Something else holds the position.

Do this: Turn the palm up, or go neutral. The wrist is upstream of the complaint.

# Pain arriving after a change in training, up or down

sub: Working read: a load-change trigger

Why it happens: A change of load over 15% is the usual figure for a jump in injury likelihood. A 15% decrease sets the stage too.

Do this: Look at what changed in the last fortnight before you look at the joint.

Check the whole chain, every time. If this starts to hurt, look at the elbow, look at the wrist, look at the shoulder. The wrist is rarely where it began.

Red flags

title: Refer, and do not sort first

Numbness, pins and needles, night pain, or grip giving way. These are nerve symptoms, and the only nerve condition we can name here is one a clinician managed

Everything in the general stop-and-refer list, plus seven out of ten reported consistently, plus pain still consistent between three days and one week

emergency: Wrist injuries are unforgiving. Escalate earlier here than elsewhere.

How a working read is said

My best guess, and it is a guess, not a diagnosis, is that your wrist is bending sideways to cope with a narrow grip. Let's widen it and see. If it's still there, you see a physio.

Step 4. Change

Re-check the hand position first, before you change load or exercise. Read progress off her day rather than off the bar.

We give no guidance on supports

Wrist wraps are discussed. Their tightness, duration and when to remove them are not. Do not invent a protocol and do not read one across from another region.

Step 5. Escalate

Everything in the general stop-and-refer list and the two numbers. If the question turns out to be about medication rather than training, it goes to her prescriber. Pain rules and when to refer covers that route.

Terms on this page
