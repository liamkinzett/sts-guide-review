Core · E-COR-FLEX#Role

What it's for

Trunk flexion shortens the front abdominal wall by bending the spine on purpose (rectus abdominis). Flexion is the movement here, not an error inside it.

What it is for

# Resistance profile

sub: Heaviest in the shortened-to-mid range

What it does: The load is greatest as she curls in. The top of the crunch is where it is hardest.

# Length position

sub: Crunches load short, the GHD sit up loads long

What it does: The crunch family loads the muscle from the middle of its range to fully shortened (shortened position). The GHD sit up is the stretched-range member of the family (lengthened position). The trunk extends backwards under control before it flexes.

# Continuous tension

sub: Only the cable version loads progressively without changing the exercise

What it does: On a cable the stack never rests, so the tension never leaves. The kneeling cable crunch has no page of its own at STS. Do not read the cable's continuous-tension claim across to the machine.

# Where it sits

sub: Direct core work, or first on a push day

What it does: General direct core work. It is also a legitimate front-of-session warm-up on a push day where rib-cage awareness is the limiter. Loading the sternum directly is what teaches her to control it.

# Why flexion is trained at all

sub: A spine she never leaves in extension

What it does: She cannot extend a spine she never leaves in extension. Flexion work keeps the two directions in balance. It is also the forward-and-back read (sagittal plane) in a three-plane core screen. The other two reads are a side bend for the side-to-side plane (frontal plane) and a chop for the twisting plane (transverse plane). STS has no page for the side bend or the cable chop.

Terms on this page

rectus abdominis: The long muscle down the front of the abdomen, the "six-pack". It bends the spine forward by pulling the ribs toward the pelvis.

shortened position: The muscle is working while it is bunched short.

lengthened position: The point in the rep where the working muscle is at its longest while it carries the load. It describes the muscle's length, not whether she feels a stretch.

sagittal plane: Forward-and-back movement, like a squat, a hinge or a curl.

frontal plane: Side-to-side movement, like a lateral lunge or a side raise.

transverse plane: Twisting movement around the long axis of the body, like a chop, a throw or a rotational lunge.
