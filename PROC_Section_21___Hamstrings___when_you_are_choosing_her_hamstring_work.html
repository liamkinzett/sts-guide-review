Choosing her hamstring work

You are filling a hamstring slot and the machines all look like the same exercise. They are not. This page is the Hamstrings topic in the order you use it, and nothing here is new doctrine.

Two questions decide the selection.

Ask these before you look at which machine is free

Which half of the muscle does the slot need? The hip half is the hinge, and that is the Romanian deadlift in Glutes. The knee half is the hamstring curl, this topic's one exercise.

Which end of the range does the program need loaded? The seated and lying curls are different exercises with different jobs. Neither substitutes for the other.

The difference is the hip. Seated keeps the hip bent, which pulls the hamstring long before the knee has done anything. It loads the stretched-to-middle part of the range (lengthened position). Lying keeps the hip open, so the hamstring stays short. It loads the squeezed end (shortened position) and misses the stretch the seated version reaches.

If the program needs the long position and the seated machine is taken, the lying machine is not the same movement. That is a selection decision, and Choosing exercises owns it, along with the recovery-cost reasoning on the curl's Watch for page.

The standing single leg curl reads differently again. It is deliberately not very loadable. It belongs with the low-load, short-range work chosen for joint awareness rather than output.

Cramping in the hamstring during curls is something you ask about. It does not show on video. Ask after the set.

Do the standards in order.

Coach these, in this order

Knee joint lined up with the machine's pivot. True of all three machines.

Find the pad before she curls. Thighs pressed up into the pad, then contract.

A still joint position. The hips do not lift or slide to steal leverage.

A deliberately slow lowering phase (eccentric). Stop just short of a fully straight knee. That keeps tension on the muscle and stays out of the bunched-up position where it cannot produce force (active insufficiency).

Lying version: thighs into the pad, not the hips**. Do not chase a glute squeeze.

Cue the pad press first. Then correct off the fault list: the bounced turnaround, the hips lifting or sliding, the knee over-straightening at the bottom.

Before you decide anything about tempo, state which machine. Tempo and pauses uses the seated curl against the lying curl as its worked example of this trap. The two are hardest at opposite ends.

What does not change. STS doesn't program the Nordic hamstring curl, so it has no page here. There is no home version either, because STS coaches clients who train in equipped gyms. This page gives the curl no rep range of its own. Her sets and reps come from Volume and Effort and intensity, like every other exercise.

Watch for

Never let her lock the knees out fully under load on a seated or preacher-style setup. That is the one hard caution on the movement, and the reason is on the curl's Watch for page.

Pain is Pain rules and when to refer. The ladder for pain at the front of the knee, which uses the standing single leg curl, is in Region by region.

Terms on this page

lengthened position: The point in the rep where the working muscle is at its longest while it carries the load. It describes the muscle's length, not whether she feels a stretch.

shortened position: The muscle is working while it is bunched short.

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.

active insufficiency: A muscle that crosses two joints and is shortened at both is too bunched up to produce much force. Bending the knee does this to the hamstrings in a hip thrust.
