The coaching relationship · T-42.27

Comparison has four targets. Route by target first

Comparison has four targets and they are not one problem. Ask what she is comparing herself to before you say anything. Most of the time the answer she needs is a fact you already have, not a reframe.

The order is fact first, then the three moves. Where a fact exists, give her the fact. The expectation reframe, the circumstance question and the metric swap come after. Where no fact exists, they are what you do instead. Leading with the reframe when she asked a factual question reads as deflection, which is what she was comparing against in the first place.

Ask what she is comparing herself to

# Someone whose training looks different

sub: More days, longer sessions

The fact you have: Building a session on session length. Volume on why more is a weaker lever than it is sold as, and on the minimum that works.

Then: The three moves, if the fact does not settle it.

# Someone whose body looks different

sub: The most common one

The fact you have: The same program does not produce the same result. Muscle and growth says it before she compares. What she will ask you holds the answers on calves and on the blocky-waist fear.

Then: The three moves.

# Another of your clients

sub: A problem you are part of

The fact you have: None applies. This is a boundary question, not a training one.

Then: Take it to the head coach before you answer. Whether to answer in a group or privately, and what your own posting of client results does to her, are decisions for him.

# Her own younger self

sub: A number from years ago

The fact you have: The third move directly. Assign a new performance metric so there is a different target.

Then: The other two moves as needed.

Pre-empt rather than answer, where you still can. "The same program does not produce the same result" exists to be said early. A comparison pre-framed in week one is a conversation you have already had, and it costs far less than the same words in week six.

This page supplies no wording. No script exists for the comparison moment and none may be improvised. What it does is stop you reaching for a script when what she actually needs is a fact you already hold.

Watch for

Treating all four as the same conversation. Four coaches in a test had the facts in their hands and could not find them, because they heard "comparison" and reached for the feeling.
