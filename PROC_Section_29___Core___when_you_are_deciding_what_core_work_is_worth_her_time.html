Deciding what core work is worth her time

Before any core work goes in, know what the slot is for and what the movement can give her. This page is the Core topic in the order you use it. Nothing here is new.

1 · Ask what the slot is for

Training, a systems check before a squat session, or a warm-up ahead of overhead work. The plank is a hold, where the muscle works but nothing moves (isometric). Its two hold lengths answer different questions, so pick by the question you are asking and record which one you used. Isometric hold has both.

On a leg raise, ask where she feels it. A pinch in the hip crease rather than work in the lower abs is something she reports, not something you see. Ask her rather than measuring an angle from footage. Leg raise has why.

2 · Know what each tool is worth

What each tool can and cannot give her

# Holds, carries and the Pallof press

sub: Coordination and load tolerance

What they give: A position owned, a load accepted, a twist resisted. Nothing in them stretches, shortens or grows, so they do not build capacity.

# Trunk flexion

sub: Capacity

What it gives: The trunk loaded through a range. It is the only page here that builds capacity.

# A heavy single-arm row

sub: Anti-rotation under real load

What it gives: It challenges the trunk against rotation harder than a Pallof press does.

# A static plank

sub: Low level, hard to scale, easy to over-use

What it gives: Position and awareness for the client who cannot organise her trunk yet. Once she holds thirty seconds to a minute, move her on.

Low-stimulus work is always stacked with something that carries a real stimulus. No page here programs core work for size.

3 · Pick by the job

Pick by the job

# Resist rotation

sub: The Pallof press

Do this: Progress it by complexity, never by hanging weight on it. Anti-rotation has the ladder.

# Flex

sub: The crunch family

Do this: Flexion is the rep standard and a flat back is the fault. Screen it that way, the opposite of a hinge.

# Hold

sub: The plank pair

Do this: Progress by duration and leverage rather than by hunting a sticking point. Once she holds thirty seconds to a minute, move her on.

# Raise the legs

sub: The hanging or captain's chair knee raise

Do this: The pelvis tucks, or the rep is a hip-flexor rep. Progression runs knees to straight legs. Load is not on that ladder.

# Every drill with intent

sub: All of them

Do this: A drill performed passively trains nothing. Add the hold, add the tension, or take it out.

4 · Place it in the session

  • Grip-burning work goes at the end of the session, where it cannot tire the hands before a pull. Building a session has the order.
  • A hold has no hardest point, so Tempo and pauses has nothing to act on there. Progress it by duration and leverage.
  • Rest periods depend on the phase. In a hypertrophy or strength block, never pair core into the rest of a compound set. A tired trunk leaks force on the next set. In a fat-loss or conditioning block, pairing is a legitimate tool, especially for a beginner. Rest and intensity techniques has the rule.
  • In a warm-up, unloaded stability drills are fine. The bird dog, the Pallof press and the RKC plank. Never add load to them.
  • Nothing here is a gate. Nobody has to pass a plank before she is allowed to lift.
  • A carry is not yet written into a client's program. Loaded carry teaches you to coach one when it appears in her training.
    • When she wants to do her core work between heavy sets

    We're not pairing your core work into the rest here. I need your brace fresh for the next set. In a fat-loss block it's a different call and I'll tell you.

    5 · When to send it up

    Red flags

    title: Refer before you load her spine in flexion if she has either of these

    Spine bones that have lost density (vertebral osteoporosis)

    A fixed forward rounding of the upper back (kyphosis)

    Pain rules and when to refer decides. A client with back pain gets the ordered ladder on the trunk flexion page, with her direction bias found before rung one. Region by region covers the low back.

    Pregnancy is a referral, and The female body governs. This topic carries no pregnancy program. The return after a baby belongs to Returning to training. After a caesarean, any deep core or abs work comes out for 12 weeks.

    Terms on this page

    vertebral osteoporosis: The bones of the spine have lost density and can fracture under loads a healthy spine would tolerate.

    kyphosis: An exaggerated forward rounding of the upper back. Loaded spinal flexion is contraindicated with it.

    isometric: A hold. The muscle works against the load but the joint does not move.
