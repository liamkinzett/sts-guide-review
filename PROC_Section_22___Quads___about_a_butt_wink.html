What to do about a butt wink

Her pelvis tucks under at the bottom of the squat and her lower back rounds (butt wink). You are not deciding whether butt wink is a fault. You are finding out what this client's wink responds to. A wink that a heel wedge removes and a wink that survives bodyweight are different things with different programs. That needs no diagnosis, which is exactly why it is available to you.

Test: her squat four ways, in one session, in the same warm-up state

Run the passes in this order

Her squat as normal, at a comfortable working weight. The baseline. Note the depth at which the pelvis moves, not the depth she reaches.

Same load, heels elevated. This shifts her balance point (centre of mass) and takes the ankle out of the question.

Heels elevated and the load in front. A goblet squat. This moves the load in front of her as well.

A counterbalance. A light plate held at arm's length. Only if the first three all wink. It strips the load almost entirely.

Film it from the front

This inverts the usual default. The side view gives joint angles and spinal position and nothing else. The hips opening and turning out on the way down are only visible from the front. A lifter can have a clean side view with a genuinely bad hip position. Take both angles if you can. If you get one, front wins.

Record three things per pass. Does the pelvis move. Where in the descent it moves, since early and gradual is a different thing from late and sudden. Whether anything hurts, asked rather than inferred.

Read: the bucket is the answer

Which bucket she is in

# Constraint-resolved

sub: Pass two or three removed it

The read: Balance point, not structure. It is a setup problem whose setup you have already found.

Do this: The wedge or the front load that worked becomes her warm-up, scheduled for removal rather than kept forever.

# Load-dependent

sub: Clean when light or early in the set, present when heavy or late

The read: Capacity she cannot yet express under that load. Split design separates skill from fatigue from absolute load.

Do this: Work the fixes below in order, and read the wink against the load she was under.

# Constraint-resistant

sub: Still there at pass four

The read: The one that gets real work. Also the one nearest the escalation line.

Do this: Work the whole list below, mobility last. Pain is Pain rules and when to refer.

Before you accept the first bucket, check whether you built it

A lifter who needs more hinge in her high-bar squat, cued to stay vertical, winks because she is being vertical. You do not merely fail to help her. You generate the fault you then correct. If she was recently cued to sit more upright, undo that before anything else. Diagnosing from video carries this.

Do: in this order. Mobility is last, not first.

Fix it in this order

Slow it down so she can see it. Tempo makes the moment visible to both of you.

Fix where she starts, not where she finishes. A pelvis tipped forward at the unrack (anterior pelvic tilt) becomes a wink at depth.

Change the constraint. The wedge or the front load that worked in the test becomes her warm-up, scheduled for removal rather than kept forever.

Regress down the ladder if the constraint is not enough. The ladder is on the barbell squat's Swaps page.

Build capacity in the bottom position.

Mobility. Only what is left after the five above.

Consider she needs more hinge, not less.

The guardrail, and it is the most important line here

Neutralising a pelvis that is tipped forward is not the same as tucking a neutral one under. Step two removes excess forward tilt from a client who has it. It does not install a tuck in a client who does not. Coaches frightened of arching sometimes cue a tuck under maximal load, and that is the wrong answer. If her pelvis is neutral at the top, that step is finished.

Change: the constraint is warm-up, not furniture. Depth is the deepest point at which she holds a neutral pelvis. It is a position she owns, not a target you set. Choosing exercises holds that standard. Do not buy depth by dropping load.

Escalate: a fault that is not costing her anything is not a fault. A constraint-resistant wink in a client with no pain and no performance cost is a note in her file, not a program. Pain is Pain rules and when to refer.

Terms on this page

butt wink: At the bottom of the squat her pelvis tucks under and her lower back rounds. It usually shows up when she runs out of hip range or loses her brace.

centre of mass: The single point her bodyweight and the load balance around. The further it sits from her feet, the harder she has to work to stay balanced.

anterior pelvic tilt: The pelvis tipped forward, so the lower back arches more.
