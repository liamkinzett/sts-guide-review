Filling a slot, and deciding when a movement changes

This page is the whole topic in the order you use it. Nothing here is new. A coach brings two questions to it: what fills this slot, and does this movement stay or go.

The five moves, in order

Test. Look at what ends her set, her footage, her phase and, for a swap, the reason.

Read. Turn what you saw into a working read, never a verdict delivered to her.

Do. Fill the slot in order: coverage, screens, price, earned, substitutes.

Change. Know what a swap costs and what holds still.

Escalate. Know what this topic does not cover.

Test. Start with what you can observe and what the phase has already decided.

Four things to check first

# What ends her set: the muscle or her position?

sub: The test that decides what she has earned

Why: If her position fails before the muscle does, she has not yet earned the complex, heavily loadable work. That work is held by her own trunk (internally stabilised).

# The footage of the exercises she already has

sub: The name in the program is a claim

Why: Her execution decides whether the movement trains the muscle you assigned it. You already watch video for mobility and for effort. This is the third thing to look for.

# What phase is she in?

sub: Loadability equals fatigue

Why: Priming and a cut limit highly loadable movements. A build can afford them.

# For a swap: which of the three reasons applies?

sub: Loadability, joint strain or variety

Why: Time served is not on the list.

Read. A working read, never a verdict.

What you find, and what it means

# She cannot feel the muscle at all

sub: The one case for shortened range first

Read: Shortened-range work is a teaching tool here, and a temporary one. Once she can find the muscle, she goes back to mid and lengthened work.

# Her position ends the set before the muscle does

sub: She has not yet earned the demanding version

Read: She does not get the complex, heavily loadable, internally stabilised movement yet. The answer is not the same movement lighter.

# The program looks right on paper and the muscle is not responding

sub: Watch before you add

Read: Watch her perform the sets she has before you add any. Performed differently, the volume landed on another muscle.

# A plateau

sub: Diagnosis first

Read: Rotation is a good reason only once the plateau has been diagnosed. Progression owns that diagnosis.

# She is bored of a movement

sub: A real rotation reason

Read: A program she does not want to run is a program she runs badly.

# A movement still producing progress

sub: It stays

Read: None of the three rotation reasons is about the exercise having stopped working.

Do. Fill the slot in this order.

Filling a slot

Coverage first. Every muscle she trains gets at least one mid-range movement and, wherever the muscle allows it, one lengthened. Shortened is optional.

Screen the candidate three ways. Alignment, resistance profile, bracing. Before the choice is made. A filter applied after deciding is decoration.

Price it. The more load it can carry, the more fatigue it generates and the more recovery it demands.

Match it to what she has earned. On the squat that follows training age. The intermediate or advanced client skips most of the progression.

On glutes, check the lengthened slot reaches the lateral hip shift. A two-footed Romanian deadlift loads the glute at length and still does not fill that slot.

On a strength path, pick accessories by where the lift fails and what tissue would fix it. On a hypertrophy path, accessories follow the coverage rule and the second-movement rules in Building a session instead.

Write named substitutes on the movements likely to need one. Her swap comes from your list, or she messages you.

Taking one out. A reason from the list of three, never a quota. Then tag it, with the reason recorded at the moment of decision: KEPT, ROTATED IN or EVOLVED.

Stimulus-to-fatigue ratio is not a step

It is a way of thinking, not a measurement. Use it to explain a selection you already have reasons for. Never as the reason.

Change. What a swap costs, and what holds still.

  • A ROTATED IN movement starts a fresh logbook entry from zero. Her nervous system pays a relearning cost while the pattern is new.
  • A swap re-runs the volume arithmetic. The volume audit in Volume assumed every exercise lands where the plan put it. Count again.
  • The mid and lengthened floor stands, whatever the phase does to the ratio.
  • An exercise stays the same within a block. The comparison window in Progression depends on it.
  • Never judge a new movement by the calendar. Judge it by whether one of the three rotation reasons has appeared.
