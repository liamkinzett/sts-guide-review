Variations

STS programs six versions: the dumbbell, the chest-supported dumbbell, the machine standing and machine seated raises, and the cable with the pulley at the knee or at the ankle. The equipment decides the resistance profile, and the dumbbell and the cable are opposites (resistance profile). Where it's hardest states it.

The two cable heights

# Cable lateral raise, pulley at knee

sub: Hardest from the stretched bottom to mid-range

Why: The opposite end from the dumbbell. The line is most horizontal here, so the bottom is loaded most.

# Cable lateral raise, pulley at ankle

sub: The same profile, lower anchor

Why: The criterion for choosing between the two heights is written down and it is not preference. Set the pulley so the cable line is perpendicular to the forearm at the bottom of the rep. That is what makes it a cable profile rather than a worse dumbbell. Set it per lifter. Beyond that criterion there is no other difference to coach between the two heights.

Leaning away on a cable lateral raise. A standing dumbbell raise has almost no resistance at the bottom, because gravity pulls straight down through almost no lever at the shoulder (moment arm). The load peaks only at 90 degrees, where the delt is already short. Leaning away from the stack, or angling the cable low instead, creates a real lever from the very start of the rep. The hardest point moves earlier, into the stretched position (lengthened).

Use the lean-away for exactly that: to move where the rep is hardest. Do not tell her it grows more muscle than the dumbbell version. Program the cable for the position it loads, not for a growth promise.

Read the shruggie fault as a fix-priority rule. Trap dominance on this movement is an arm-path problem before it is a cueing problem. Fix the arm path from Setup step 2 first. Reach for a shoulder-blade-down cue only as a second lever if the path fix does not hold. That is why "avoid elevating your shoulders" is not one of the four cues.

Terms on this page

resistance profile: Where in the range an exercise is hardest and where it is easiest. It is set by the line of the load against the joint, so a dumbbell and a cable can load the same muscle very differently.

moment arm: The perpendicular distance between the joint axis and the line of force.

lengthened: The point in the rep where the working muscle is at its longest while it carries the load. It describes the muscle's length, not whether she feels a stretch.
