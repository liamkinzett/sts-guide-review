Why the lower back feels the deadlift more than the squat

When her back feels the deadlift more than the squat, check the wedge before you blame the exercise. A mistimed deadlift asks the lower back to cover for a lost wedge in a way a squat's slower entry rarely allows.

Say the mechanism, not "the deadlift loads the back more".

Most lifts, including the squat, begin with a lowering phase (eccentric). That gives her time to organise her position before the load peaks. A deadlift begins with a lifting phase (concentric). She has to create tension on the bar off the floor and be exactly where she needs to be the moment she starts producing force.

Where it goes wrong is timing, not muscles. Before the pull she needs to take the wedge (wedge).

She pulls the slack out of the bar with her shoulders and lats. At the same time she pushes into the floor. That pre-tensions her so she does not shoot forward.

When no wedge is taken, the hips extend before the knees instead of with them. Her weight and the bar's line of force fall out of sync at exactly the moment spinal load peaks. The lower back (lumbar spine) is what covers the gap.

The practical answer for her. A mistimed deadlift asks the lower back to cover for a lost wedge. A squat's slower entry rarely lets that happen in the first place. That is the mechanism behind a felt difference, not proof that the deadlift is simply harder on the back by nature. Faults and what you can actually see names the visible signs: hips shooting up, no wedge, the bar off the midline. This page is the mechanism under them.

Say it like this

If you're feeling the deadlift much more in your lower back, the first thing I want to check is the wedge off the floor. Losing it shifts more of the job onto your lower back. That's something we can test and fix.

Terms on this page

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.

concentric: The lifting half of a rep, where the muscle shortens under load.

wedge: The braced position she takes before the bar leaves the floor: slack pulled out of the bar, lats on, feet pushing into the ground, so the whole body is tensioned before the pull.

lumbar spine: The lower back, the part of the spine between the ribs and the pelvis.
