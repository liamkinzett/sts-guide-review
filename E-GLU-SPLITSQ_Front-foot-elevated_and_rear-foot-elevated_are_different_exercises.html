Bulgarian and FFE are different exercises

Rear-foot-elevated (the Bulgarian) and front-foot-elevated (the FFE) look alike and are not the same exercise. They load different things and they are judged by different checks.

The FFE split squat is unilateral. Both of her feet are on the ground, and one leg is still the working leg. The front leg does the working-leg job. The back leg contributes hip extension: it opens at the hip (hip extension) and does not share the working-leg job.

That is exactly what makes the FFE a single-leg exercise with both feet down.

It sits with the reverse lunge and the stationary lunge as one of the three ways this family loads back-leg hip extension, not opposite them. One leg or two governs what counts as single-leg work, and the FFE counts.

Say it the right way

Say: the front leg does the working-leg job; the back leg contributes hip extension.**

Don't describe the FFE as bilateral. Both feet touching the ground does not make it bilateral. One leg is still doing the working-leg job.

Compared

columns: Bulgarian, rear foot elevated | FFE, front foot elevated

What it trains: Front-hip function, the front glute and the front quad. | The front leg's hip and glute, plus active hip extension of the back leg.

Where her weight sits: Almost all the way on the front leg. | Front-leg dominant, with the back leg actively extending. She must not load under the front leg too much.

The success check: The front hip travels outside the front knee. | The back leg extends, rather than the torso folding.

The FFE standard follows from that. She is not folding over, and she is not loading under the front leg too much. An FFE split squat that has become front-leg dominant has become a Bulgarian with a box in the wrong place. This exercise uses a negative shin angle (negative shin angle) on purpose, to bias her posterior chain (posterior chain). That is the same setting read from the other side.

One consequence for programming. The FFE split squat, the reverse lunge and the stationary lunge all load back-leg hip extension. They are interchangeable for that job. Choosing exercises owns the choice between them.

Terms on this page

hip extension: Opening the hip so the thigh travels behind the body line. It is the glute's main job, and in the FFE the back leg does it actively under load.

negative shin angle: Her front shin leans back, so the knee sits behind the ankle rather than out over the toes. It shifts the work away from the front quad and towards the hips and hamstrings.

posterior chain: The muscles down the back of the body that extend the hip and support the spine: glutes, hamstrings and the lower back muscles.
