Diagnosing from video · T-33.5

Three things break technique, so sort which one first

Technique breaks for one of three reasons: the absolute load, how close she is to failure, or general movement skill. Each has a different lever, and the levers do not substitute for one another. Sort which one before you change anything.

The three buckets

# Absolute load

sub: Form degrades when the weight is heavy, however far from failure she is

What you see: Position is fine at lighter loads. Past a certain weight it goes on the first rep, however far from failure she is.

What moves: Cap the load. Work in a range where the position holds.

# Relative intensity

sub: The weight is irrelevant. Position goes as she nears failure, every time

What you see: Clean at any load until the last two or three reps, then it breaks.

What moves: Rep range and set count. Stop the set earlier.

# General movement skill

sub: It looks like this everywhere, at every load, fresh or tired

What you see: The same fault on a light warm-up and a heavy set.

What moves: Skill work. A longer job than the other two. Practising a lift covers it.

Sometimes all three. These are not exclusive categories and a client can be two of them at once.

Where a shortcut sends you wrong. Split design sorts a breakdown into skill and fatigue by when it appears: skill shows on the first rep, fatigue arrives later. The absolute-load client shows on the first rep too. Read against that shortcut she looks like a skill problem, and gets more frequency. More frequency does not cap load. Sort her here first.

Why it works. Prescribing skill work to a load-sensitive lifter is a year of not fixing it. Capping load on a skill problem hides it. Each bucket has its own lever.

Watch for

Bucket three as a verdict on the client. Naming it a skill problem does not absolve you of any responsibility to make her move better.
