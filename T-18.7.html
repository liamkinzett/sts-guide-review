Function is the test, not symmetry

Address an imbalance where you can. Do not make it the mission, and do not treat it as a diagnosis. Nobody is symmetrical and nobody needs to be.

What a healthy lifter looks like. Uninjured athletes are fairly symmetrical. Major left-to-right differences generally do not exist in healthy legs. The threshold for possible concern is three degrees or more of left-to-right difference in joint range of motion. At or under three degrees the difference cannot be seen and matters little. Side-to-side knee travel of up to about three centimetres is entirely normal in a heavy squat. Correcting it from a visual screen is unjustified.

Three things follow, and the third is the one coaches get wrong.

  • Perfect symmetry is not achievable. Humans are not built symmetrically and never have been. A program aimed at eliminating asymmetry is aimed at something that does not exist. It will keep finding work to do forever.
  • Asymmetry is not a diagnosis. It does not mean she is injured. It does not mean she is going to be. Elite athletes compete at the highest level with scoliosis. Structure and performance are not the same question.
  • So the test is function. Is it changing how she moves, what she can load, what hurts? Then it is worth attention. Is it a difference you noticed on a video that is not affecting any of those? Then it is information, not a problem. Unilateral work prescribed to fix it is a solution looking for one.

    • When you do program the pair deliberately. Start every set on the weaker or non-dominant side and let that side set the standard for both. Progress the pair by what the weaker side can do that session, never by what the stronger side can do. That stops the gap widening under a coach unconsciously chasing the stronger side's numbers. The cable kickback and the side plank both carry this convention.

    Watch for

    The difference you believe you are seeing. At or under three degrees nobody can see it. A knee travelling a couple of centimetres in a heavy squat is doing what knees do.

    Say it like this

    You're not symmetrical. Nobody is. I'm watching whether it changes how you move, not whether it's even.
