Shoulder dumping forward

Her shoulder rolls forward at the bottom or finish.

Control the finish and hold the shoulder back.

Where it shows, and the fix

# Lat pulldown

sub: The shoulder dumps forward at the bottom

Do this: Control the finish

# Dip

sub: The shoulder rolls forward at the bottom

Do this: Coach the part that is agreed. The shoulders must not ride up into an early shrug, and the position must survive to the end of the set.

# Triceps pushdown

sub: Scapula (scapula) rolling forward, getting shruggy

Do this: Hold the shoulder back

There is no set cue written for this fault. Say the fix in plain words, one thing this set.

Other cues on those exercises. Not about this fault. Use them when the same set needs them.

Cues

When: Lat pulldown

Say: Tucking the pelvis, keeping a nice tall spine, not overdoing it and almost getting flexed. Finding that nice neutral position so my lats can actually wrap around the rib cage.

Why: It goes first because rib cage flare rather than arm path is the headline fault.

When: Lat pulldown

Say: can you basically do a crunch with the amount of weight that you're pulling?

Why: A test disguised as a cue.

When: Lat pulldown

Say: Depress your scapulae

Why: In either of its image forms: "Pull your armpits to your pelvis"

When: Lat pulldown

Say: Get the head into a still position.

Why: Use it when the compensation is being sourced from the neck.

When: Dip

Say: Shoulders away from ears.

Why: The first cue. It is the one with a documented outcome behind it. It targets delayed scapular elevation, which is the fault both sources agree on.

When: Dip

Say: Don't get too shruggy, don't let the shoulders roll forward, full hand pressure, get your feet kind of underneath you.

Why: A mixed cue, internal then external. A full form reset rather than a mid-set correction.

Terms on this page

scapula: The shoulder blade. The flat bone on the back of the rib cage that the arm hangs from.

:

scapulae: The two shoulder blades. One is a scapula.

scapular: To do with the shoulder blade, the scapula.
