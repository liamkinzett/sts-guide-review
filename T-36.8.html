Assessment starts with the twenty-three hours she is not training

Look at the positions she holds all day before you look at the lift. The gym is a few hours a week. The rest of it is the load.

The worked example is desk work. A client at a keyboard and mouse for most of her waking hours holds her wrists bent and turned down. Claw hands. She then arrives and is asked for full elbow and wrist extension overhead. The tissue was never prepared for the position it is now being loaded in. The load is not the reason it hurt. That sequence is the named mechanism for pain on the inside of the elbow.

The question you ask. What are you doing between leaving the desk and putting that bar overhead? If the answer is nothing, that is the finding.

Why it works. It explains the client whose wrist hurts on a weight she has handled for months. Nothing changed in the program, because the change was not in the program. Pain rules and when to refer tells you to ask what changed. This rule tells you what to ask about.

Watch for

Assuming the program caused it, because the program is what you can see.

Turning exposure into a diagnosis. You are establishing what her tissue is used to, not naming a pathology. A working read, labelled a guess, is fine. Grading or staging it is not.

Say it like this

What does a normal day look like before you get here? Desk, driving, on your feet? I want to know what your body has been doing for the twenty-three hours before it lifts.
