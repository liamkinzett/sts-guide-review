Glutes · E-GLU-THRUST#Substitutions

Swaps

STS's first move is to change the exercise, not to load this one harder. The reason is the direction the load pulls (loading vector). The thrust's own load pulls her into a forward tilt of the pelvis (anterior pelvic tilt), which is the position this movement needs least. When she cannot hold position, more weight makes it worse.

The route back to loading the shortened position

45 degree hip extension. The default swap. It trains the same shortened position and is easier to execute well. See Hip extension, 45 degree and GHD.

Pelvic control demonstrated under load. She knows where her pelvis is in space and holds it there through a set. This is the gate on advancing, not time served.

Glute drive machine. The externally stabilised version specifically. The machine steers the load, which makes it easier to reach muscular failure before her technique fails.

When the thrust is not available

# Equipment missing

sub: No glute drive machine or Smith machine

Swap to: 45 degree hip extension

Why: The primary swap. Inside the pattern itself the glute drive machine and the Smith machine hip thrust are the two setups STS uses. STS doesn't program the Kas variations.

# A joint says no

sub: The hip crease or the pelvis will not take the bar

Swap to: 45 degree hip extension

Why: The same swap, on execution grounds. Inside the thrust, keep the bar off bony landmarks and reduce load. Pain is Pain rules and when to refer.

# Home

sub: No gym access

Why: STS doesn't coach home training, so there is no home swap to give.

Terms on this page

loading vector: The direction the load pulls on her. On a hip thrust it pulls the hips down toward the floor, which drags the pelvis into a forward tilt.

anterior pelvic tilt: The pelvis tipped forward, so the lower back arches more.
