Reading her training data · T-39.8

A logbook and a set of photos answer different questions

A climbing logbook and a set of photos are not two readings of the same question. Trusting one over the other is a category error. Neither overrides the other.

The signal-and-noise table carries no photos on either side, because this topic's decision runs on training signals, not composition ones. A climbing logbook is read entirely by this topic's own rules, whatever the photos show.

What the photos show is read by a separate method, in Assessing a new client. It is read in bands, from her check-in photos, never as a trigger for a recommendation on its own.

They are not competing instruments answering one question. One tells you whether the training stimulus is working. The other tells you what shape she is currently in. A gap between the two across twelve weeks is worth naming to her using both readings side by side. It is never resolved by picking a side.

Naming the gap with both readings

Your lifts have moved every week for twelve weeks, so the training is working. The photos are changing more slowly than the logbook. Both of those are true, and neither one cancels the other.

Where the photos flatten long enough to read as a genuine composition stall, that judgement belongs to the composition read in Assessing a new client. The ordinary lag between a strength gain and a visible change is not a stall. It is a coach's own read in bands, not a trigger this topic's table supplies.
