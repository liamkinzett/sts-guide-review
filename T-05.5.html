What a bleed on the pill does not tell you

A bleed on the pill is a withdrawal bleed, not a period. Screen every pill user for under-fuelling as if she had no period to read.

What a withdrawal bleed is. In the break between active pills, the pill's hormones drop and she bleeds (withdrawal bleed). Her own cycle did not cause it. So the bleed tells you nothing about whether her own cycle is working.

Why this is a safety rule. A missing period is the most visible sign that a client is under-fuelled. Under-fuelled means she is not eating enough for what she trains (energy availability). On the pill that sign is switched off. Her cycle can have shut down from under-fuelling (functional hypothalamic amenorrhoea) and you would never see it.

Screen regardless of whether she is bleeding. The absence of a missed period is not evidence of anything in a pill user.

Why it works. The usual field shortcut is that a regular period means she is eating enough. That shortcut is void for over half the roster. It fails silently, which is the worst way for a screen to fail.

What you say. Nothing about her contraception. Ask about fuelling directly, the way you would with any client.

Verdict

Suspected under-fuelling is a referral conversation, not a programming change.

What happens next is not yours. Pain rules and when to refer holds the routing.

Watch for

Reassurance drawn from a regular bleed. It is the exact thing this rule says you cannot draw.

Terms on this page

withdrawal bleed: The bleed a pill user gets in the break between active pills. It is caused by the drop in the pill's hormones, not by her own cycle.

functional hypothalamic amenorrhoea: Her periods stop because the brain has switched off the cycle in response to stress, usually under-fuelling or overtraining. Nothing is structurally wrong.

energy availability: The energy left for her body to run on after training has been paid for. Low energy availability means she is not eating enough for what she trains.
