Shoulder: pulling the blades back is not stability

Pulling her shoulder blades back does not stabilise them. It lifts them off the rib cage. Stability at the shoulder blade means contact with the rib cage, and retraction reduces it.

Why. The shoulder blade has no ligament holding it on. It rests on the rib cage like two dinner plates stacked. As she retracts, the blade lifts clear. You could put your hand under its edge and feel the underside.

What it overturns. The standard rehabilitation answer for a forward-rolled shoulder is retractions plus external rotation drills, turning the upper arm outward in its socket (external rotation). That is the exact opposite of what we are going for. On a pressing set-up, depression matters more than retraction.

Where the rule stops

It changes "retract" as a general cue. It does not touch the bench press set-up, which keeps its own locked shoulder position. Chest owns that.

Say it like this

I don't want you pulling your shoulder blades back. I want them flat against your ribs, like they're glued on. Think "wide and down", not "back".

Terms on this page

external rotation: Turning a limb outward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face forward or up, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more to the side. A muscle that does it is an external rotator.
