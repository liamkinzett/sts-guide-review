She grows for six weeks then stalls: read what happens next

A client who reliably grows for about six weeks and then flatlines is not presenting a mystery. She is presenting the six-week default doing exactly what it says.

Six weeks is where a hypertrophy block is expected to run out of overload if nothing else changes (overload). The pattern is the phase system, not a client-specific ceiling, until you have ruled out the three things that actually explain "then it dies."

Rule these out first

The block genuinely ended and nothing replaced it. Running the same exercises, volume and effort wave past week six with no deload and no rotation stalls by design. A block that was never formally ended is not evidence of a ceiling. It is evidence the phase never changed.

The stall list was never run. Effort, technique and recovery come before volume or a fresh block. Check whether her RIR ratings have drifted or her technique has shortened before you assume anything about her. Volume has the list.

The exercises are due for rotation. Loadability and joint strain, not novelty, are the reasons to rotate. A pattern that reliably flattens at the same point in every block is one of the signals to rotate on. The fix costs nothing extra in volume. Choosing exercises has the rule.

All three ruled out and it still happens. Now it is a real observation about her recovery or adaptation rate, worth recording.

What would make this a real finding. The six-week ceiling only becomes informative once two things have happened. A genuine deload, triggered by exactly this regression, and the stall list have both been run, and the pattern still holds. Before that it is the phase system doing what it is designed to do.

Watch for the reverse error

Treating "she always stalls at six weeks" as proof the block length itself is wrong for her. A client whose blocks land near the default is the default working. She is not a client who needs a different number.

Terms on this page

overload: Doing more than last time: more load, more reps or better reps at the same load. It is the signal that tells the muscle to adapt.
