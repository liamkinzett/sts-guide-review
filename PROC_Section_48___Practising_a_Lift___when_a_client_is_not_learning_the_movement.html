What to do when a client is not learning the movement

This page adds nothing. It is this topic's rules in the order you use them, for the client who is not picking up a movement.

Step 1. Test. Is this a technique problem or a practice problem? Name what you see using Faults and what you can actually see. Then ask the question this topic adds: has she had enough exposures to have learned it at all? A movement she has only done a handful of times is not yet learned, and a fault in it is not yet a fault.

Step 2. Read. Where is she? A beginner's reps vary so much they are barely the same exercise. Consistency is the destination, not the entry requirement. Judge her against where she is.

Step 3. Do

Call the session what it is. Tell her today is practice, and that none of it should feel hard.

Put the skill work early and fresh. First working movement after the warm-up, before anything tiring.

Take the mirror away and film it instead. She watches the set afterwards, not her reflection during it.

Where the movement is too big to hold, break it into parts. Then reassemble it in the same session.

Step 4. Change. Nothing here changes load or volume on its own. Volume and Effort and intensity still govern how much and how hard. What changes is what the session is for.

Step 5. Escalate. Where it is a bad day rather than a bad pattern, warm up into it. Where it persists across weeks, Progression covers the stall. Where she has simply not had enough exposures, the lever is more of the pattern at low fatigue, and Split design covers raising its frequency. Where it hurts, Pain rules and when to refer comes first.

Watch for

Solving a practice problem with load. Adding weight to a movement she has not learned loads past the thing that is improving.

Solving a practice problem with cues. Past two cues, the exercise or the setup is the question. Cueing covers the stop rule.
