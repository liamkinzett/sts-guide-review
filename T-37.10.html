Returning to training · T-37.10

Training stops while she is sick, and the way back is a ramp

While she is unwell, training stops. That is the instruction, not a decision she makes on how she feels on the day.

Her doctor decides when she is well enough to train again. Until then, this is what we advise.

What we advise

# A head cold

sub: Congestion, sore throat, no fever

Do this: Generally advise her not to train.

# Anything below the neck

sub: Chest symptoms, body aches

Do this: Stop.

# A fever

sub: Any fever

Do this: Stop.

Watch for

She may tell you that a head cold is fine to train through. Our position on a head cold is deliberately the stricter one. Say the stricter thing.

The way back is a ramp over two to three weeks. It is a ramp on intensity, not on volume. She comes back to her normal number of sessions. They are lighter sessions.

The levers, in order

# 1 · Weight

sub: The first preference

Do this: Start her back lighter than she was before she got sick.

# 2 · Sets

sub: Sometimes

Do this: Bring sets down where she needs it. It is not the default lever.

# 3 · Session count

sub: Very rarely

Do this: Leave the number of sessions alone unless there is a reason.

A short illness needs no ramp at all. If she feels fine after two days, she goes straight back. The ramp is for an illness that actually took something out of her.

Two weeks ill, and the plan itself gets looked at. Not just the loads. That is the same call you make for any change in her circumstances: temporary, or permanent.

Say it like this

You're not behind. We start back lighter for a couple of weeks and build the weight up, same sessions as always.
