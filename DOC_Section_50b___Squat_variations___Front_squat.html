Front squat

The front squat is the squat for a lower back that limits her, quads that need priority, or a torso that folds. It is also the step between the goblet squat and the back squat.

Front squat: use it when

# Her lower back is what limits her squat

sub: She feels squats in her lower back, or her back tires before her legs

What it gets her: The squat keeps going with less load through her lower back, because the torso stays upright.

# Her quads are the priority, or they lag

sub: The upright torso lets the knees travel forward

What it gets her: More of the work on the front of her thighs: the upright torso lets the knees travel forward.

# She folds forward or sits back in a back squat

sub: The load in front physically won't let her

What it gets her: The load in front physically won't let her, so it teaches the upright pattern.

# She is newer to barbell squatting

sub: A barbell squat before a bar goes on her back

What it gets her: The step between the goblet squat and the back squat.

Watch for

Her upper back gives out before her legs. Then the set is not training her quads. Lighten the load or build upper-back strength first.

What it changes. Front rack or crossed arms, with the stance slightly wider than a goblet squat. Ribs tucked, elbows driven up. She starts the rep by bending the knees, and keeps the sternum high out of the bottom of the squat (the hole). The bar sits behind the shelf of the front delts rather than on the muscle belly.

The load in front is a constraint rather than a cue. She cannot sit back and load her lower back with forward slide (shear) under it. The upper-back extension that is passive with her hands on a back-squat bar becomes active (thoracic extension).

Upper-back burn with quiet quads means her position ended the set, not her legs. A front squat keeps its quad-dominant, upright demand only while the upper back can hold the bar still (isometric). When that hold fails first, the set ends there and the quads never reach the load the program assumed. Three ways out:

  • Build upper-back capacity so she can hold the position longer.
  • Reduce the load until the rack hold outlasts the set.
  • Pick a quad-dominant movement that does not load the upper back isometrically while that capacity catches up.

    • Before you reach for a heel wedge, front squat her. She usually hits depth, which shows that ankle range was never the limiter. Move the load forward, low bar to high bar to front to goblet, and staying upright gets easier. The point her weight balances around (centre of mass) sits further forward.

    The front rack gets the unloaded position test. Can she get her elbow inside her wrist with the arm raised in front of her? If not, do not load it. Elbow pain in the rack position is not tight shoulders. It is an unstable shoulder. Do not stretch it, and do not avoid the position. Double down and improve it, because when it hurts there it will hurt in high bar and then in low bar.

    The pain-control variation. In the week she feels good and wants load back, prescribe something so difficult that loading it hard is impossible. That is a five-second lowering (eccentric), two-count pause front squat to a box. She can still chase effort. The exercise caps the load rather than her intent. The general ceiling for an eccentric is four seconds. This is a prescription for that one week in a pain return, not a general tempo.

    Tempo. `2110`, with the pause in the hole.

    Terms on this page

    the hole: The bottom of the squat, where she turns around and drives up.

    shear: A force that slides one part of a joint sideways across the other. In the lower back it is one vertebra sliding forward on the one below.

    thoracic extension: Straightening the upper back so the chest comes up and the rounding between the shoulder blades disappears.

    isometric: A hold. The muscle works against the load but the joint does not move.

    centre of mass: The single point her bodyweight and the load balance around. Where it sits decides how upright she can stay.

    eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.
