One leg or two · T-18.8

Post-injury asymmetry is real, large and lasting. Correct that one

The picture changes completely after an injury or surgery. This is the asymmetry you do correct.

What it looks like. Months after a knee reconstruction, a lifter squatting on two legs still drives markedly less through the surgical knee. Her hip takes up the difference. The force profile is severely imbalanced. The movement is not. Both legs look highly symmetrical on camera, with under three degrees of difference in joint range at ankle, knee and hip.

So this is not something you detect by watching. A coach watching those squats sees two symmetrical legs. It comes from her history. A client who has had a reconstruction is carrying this whether or not anything looks wrong. That is the mirror image of the healthy case, where what looks wrong usually is not.

It is a compensation, not a preference, and it persists for years. Left uncorrected, the healthy limb and the surrounding joints absorb a disproportionate load. That is a route to the next injury rather than the last one.

When you may deviate. This is a principle, not a protocol. Pain rules and when to refer owns rehabilitation loading. A specific presentation is its business, not this topic's.
