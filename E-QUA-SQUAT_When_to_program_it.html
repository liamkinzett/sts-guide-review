When to use it

The problem you notice. She holds position under a bar and the block's job is loaded lower-body growth. Or her warm-up has quietly shrunk to nothing. Or she feels squats in her lower back and you have to decide which squat she does. The barbell squat is STS's primary loaded knee-and-hip pattern, and the bar position changes the exercise. Take the two decisions in turn: front or back, and whether a barbell squat is the answer at all.

Front squat or back squat

Compared

columns: Front squat | Back squat

Use it when: Her lower back is what limits her squat: she feels squats there, or her back tires before her legs. Or her quads are the priority, or they lag. | The goal is the most load and strength, including every strength-block squat session.

Also when: She folds forward or sits back in a back squat: the load in front will not let her, so it teaches the upright pattern. Or she is newer to barbell squatting: it is the step between the goblet squat and the back squat. | You want her glutes working alongside her quads in one lift, because her hips travel back and the glutes share the work. And only once she owns the goblet and front squat positions: no barbell on her back before that.

What it gets her: The squat keeps going with less load through her lower back, because the torso stays upright. The upright torso lets the knees travel forward, so more of the work lands on the front of her thighs. | The most loadable squat she owns.

Watch for: Her upper back giving out before her legs. Then the set is not training her quads: lighten the load or build upper-back strength first. | Her lower back starting to complain. Move her to the front squat.

Hamstrings do not grow from squatting

Never list hamstrings as a muscle a squat builds, and never count squat sets towards hamstring volume. The hamstrings do not work in a meaningful way when she squats. Hamstrings are built by hinges and curls.

Reach for the barbell squat when

Reach for it when

# She already holds position under a bar and the block's job is loaded lower-body growth

sub: The squat carries her a long way, and it has a limit

Confirm it: She keeps a neutral pelvis and a braced trunk through a working set on video.

What it gets her: A long way through load in one movement. She reaches technical failure before her muscles fail, so the squat takes her most of the way. The rest of the stimulus comes from the machines that hold her in place (externally stabilised): the leg press and the leg extension. Do not expect the squat to finish the job on its own.

# Her preparation has quietly disappeared

sub: She walks in, trains and leaves, and the warm-up keeps shrinking

Confirm it: Look at what she does before her first working set. A leg press, a leg extension, even a Bulgarian, she can jump straight into. A squat she cannot.

What it gets her: A lift that forces the preparation back. Putting a squat in her program for no other reason than to hold her accountable is a legitimate reason. The lift is justified by the warm-up it forces, not only by the stimulus it delivers.

# She is a female athlete and the goal is a durable knee

sub: Not a number on the bar

Confirm it: Her sport is the goal and knee robustness is what she is asking for.

What it gets her: Load as the protection. Deep squatting with correct technique has a protective effect on the knee joint. Female athletes squatting above 1.6 times their bodyweight show markedly lower injury risk. Depth is not the hazard. Loaded depth without position is.

# Her back is what hurts and the day still needs a squat pattern

sub: A win out of the gate instead of a provocation

Confirm it: She reports the back, not the legs, as the problem. Pain itself is Pain rules and when to refer.

What it gets her: A squat that moves her pelvis vertically rather than horizontally. Take the front-loaded end of the ladder on the Swaps page: a counterbalance or a goblet squat, heels elevated if needed. Elevating the heels is not cheating.

Two situations where the answer is not this page

A beginner who is weaker than gravity does not get a barbell on her back. Under a load running down her spine (axial load) she goes hypervigilant and recruits her lower back and calves simply to stay upright. If she cannot stand on one leg, she does not squat with two. Start her on the leg press.

A lifter who is simply not skilled at the back squat gets a pendulum or hack squat instead. See the Machine squat page.

Terms on this page

externally stabilised: The machine, bench or support holds her position so she does not have to.

axial load: Load running down the length of the spine, as with a barbell on the back or overhead.
