Watch for

The external-rotation screen. Run it before prescribing any two-arm dumbbell curl.

Check

She stands straight with the arms straight forward and turns them outward as far as she can (external rotation). Does the elbow stay in line with the wrist and the shoulder?

yes: Bilateral curling is fine.

no: Bilateral curling is contraindicated. She goes single-arm, permanently rather than as a temporary measure.

Forcing it puts the hand on a diagonal while the load falls straight down. That creates sideways twisting at the elbow, on both the inner and the outer side. It is the named cause of medial elbow pain in curls. Larger lifters flare more. The taller and wider she is, the likelier single-arm is required.

Curling elbow pain traces to a misaligned shoulder, elbow and wrist stack rather than to a tissue problem. Treat the stack first.

Pain on the outside of the elbow is often the brachialis, not the wrist extensors (brachialis). With a static elbow angle and a palm-down wrist, the biceps is disadvantaged and the brachialis holds the position. Reverse curls, lateral raises and clean pulls are the named culprits. Change the grip, not the load. Turn the palm up (supinate), or go neutral. Region by region has the elbow playbook, and Pain rules and when to refer owns the triage.

Wrist extension, the arm work almost nobody does

This is the page where the mechanism lives. Every other arm page points here rather than repeating it.

Lifters accumulate large amounts of forearm flexion fatigue. It comes from holding curls, from back work and from gripping on hinges. That accumulation is a primary driver of elbow pain, on the outside and the inside of the joint. Targeted extension work restores the balance. STS recommends it specifically to manage or prevent lifting-related elbow pain.

STS has no wrist-extension exercise on its programmed list, so you will not find it on an exercise page. This is what the work looks like when it is done.

  • Setup. She kneels beside a bench with the forearms flat on it and the hands over the edge, palms down. Pad the forearm so only the wrist moves.
  • Execution. Extend the wrists as high as possible, then lower under strict control. Torso, knees and forearms stay supported and still.
  • Cue. Internal: "Squeeze your hands as hard as possible throughout your wrist extension exercise. You cannot lift the weight if it slips out of your grip!"
  • Fault. The grip relaxes or slips, which ruins leverage and wrist control.
  • The governing principle is to train both sides. Only ever training palm-up and neglecting the extensors is the named fault.

    • Placement. Forearm work put into a session can compromise grip on the main lift. The test is that she can always complete the set and rep goal of each pairing. Late in a session, prefer machines or cables. There is less risk of dropping a weight under fatigue.

    Watch for

    The hammer curl paired with a wrist curl is a deliberate pairing of muscles that work together. Do not run that pairing before anything that depends on grip. The wrist curl is not on STS's programmed list either.

    Pregnancy, the return after a baby and under-fuelling are The female body's. This page adds no modification of its own for them.

    The screen above is the only prerequisite.

    Terms on this page

    external rotation: Turning a limb outward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face forward or up, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more to the side. On the screen it is what she does with the arms held straight out in front.

    brachialis: The muscle under the biceps that bends the elbow. It carries arm thickness and the separation between biceps and triceps.

    supinate: Turn the forearm so the palm faces up. Of the foot, roll it onto its outside edge so the arch lifts. On a curl it puts the work back on the biceps and takes the twist off the elbow.
