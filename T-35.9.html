Pain rules and when to refer · T-35.9

Read progress off her day, not off the bar

Load is the last thing to improve. It is the first thing coaches look at. Read her progress off her daily life, and re-ask every week, because she will not volunteer it.

ReadNot
Stairs up but not downLoad on the leg press
Getting in and out of a carHer session RPE
Waking when she rolls over in bedWhether she "felt it" today
Putting shoes and socks on

These move in tenths. The gym numbers move in whole units, and they move after the daily-life signals do. A coach watching only the bar sees nothing for weeks, then concludes nothing is working.

You have to re-ask

Clients notice these signs arriving. They do not notice them leaving.

She will not volunteer that stairs stopped hurting. The absence of a symptom is not an event.

If you do not ask, the best measurement you have goes unrecorded.

Where the movement itself is the marker, the marker is still not load. In a position she cannot yet control she will shake. The progress is the shaking stopping, which happens at the same load.

Why it works. It gives you something to measure weekly that actually moves. That keeps both of you out of the trap of testing the painful movement to see whether it still hurts.

Watch for

A pain score out of ten as your only instrument. It is not the human experience. It will sit on "about a four" for a month while she quietly starts sleeping through the night again.
