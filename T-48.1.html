Practising a lift · T-48.1

Her first months of progress are learning, not tissue

In her first months, most of her progress is her nervous system learning the movement, not new muscle. Program those months as practice, and never load them as though the tissue had already changed.

A new lifter adds weight fast. It is tempting to read that as muscle arriving. It is mostly coordination arriving: her brain organising the movement so more of her existing strength reaches the bar. Coaches call this early run of progress newbie gains (newbie gains), and the thing improving during it is the learning (motor learning).

Loading it as though it were tissue loads past the thing that is actually improving. If you push the numbers because the numbers are moving, you outrun the skill that produced them. Push the quality of the movement first. The load follows.

Technique and learning are two different things. Technique is what a rep looks like. Learning is the process that gets her there. For a long time coaches said "motor learning" when they meant technique. Keep them apart: Strength and skill covers what a good rep is, and this topic covers how she gets to it.

Never give her a week count for it. Newbie gains have no timetable. A number implies a date on which something changes, and a coach who has the date will blame a stall on it. When a lift genuinely stalls, Progression covers how to read it, and it does not run on a calendar.

Watch for

"She has good numbers so the technique must be fine". In the first months the numbers climb because the coordination is improving. They say nothing about whether the pattern is right.

"She just doesn't feel it". What she calls the mind-muscle connection is this same learning. She practises a movement over and over until she recruits it well. Feeling arrives with position. Ask for the position first.

Say when her numbers jump early

Your body is learning the movement right now, and that is where most of this progress is coming from. We keep the quality up and the weight will follow.

Terms on this page

newbie gains: The fast progress a new lifter makes in her first months. Most of it is her nervous system learning to coordinate the movement rather than new muscle.

motor learning: The process by which her brain and body learn to produce a movement reliably. It is what improves during practice.
