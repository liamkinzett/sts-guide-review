Rib flare

Her ribs splay and her lower back arches to find range.

Ribs to hips, set before the rep. Check first that she is not the opposite, stuck-down case.

Where it shows, and the fix

# Hip adduction (adduction)

sub: Lumbar (lumbar) arch, hips tipped forward, back off the pad

Do this: Ribs to hips. If the pelvis will not change seated, change the torso angle

# Free-weight row

sub: Ribs flaring, collapsing, shifting laterally

Do this: Ribs to hips

# Lat pulldown

sub: The headline fault here. Arching the back to reach overhead. Also stability found in structure rather than function

Do this: Ribs to hips. Check she is not the stuck-down case

# Barbell bench press

sub: Ribs splayed, excessive arch. Check she is not the stuck-down case first.

Do this: Ribs to hips

# Dumbbell bench press

sub: Ribs splayed

Do this: Ribs to hips

# Machine and cable chest press

sub: Rib cage flared

Do this: Ribs to hips

# Romanian deadlift

sub: Big lumbar arch, chin up. Hip extension replaced by lumbar extension

Do this: Ribs to hips before the rep

# Activation drill

sub: Leaning back so the shoulder shoots up

Do this: Ribs to hips

# Stretch

sub: Arching the back to lean further on a couch or half-kneeling stretch

Do this: Ribs to hips. The extra range is coming from the spine

# Barbell squat

sub: Excessive spinal extension, anterior pelvic tilt (anterior pelvic tilt)

Do this: Ribs to hips before the rep. Check she is not the opposite case first

# Lateral raise

sub: *"Tits to the ceiling."* *"Flappy bird wing dog"*

Do this: Ribs to hips

# Overhead press

sub: Leaning back so the shoulder shoots up. Leaning forward

Do this: Ribs to hips

# Overhead cable extension

sub: Over-arching the low back to get the arm overhead

Do this: Ribs to hips. Compare the overhead press: this may be a shoulder-flexion problem, not a bracing one.

Cues written for this fault.

Cues

When: Free-weight row · internal cue

Say: separate the rib cage and the pelvis

Why: For the contralateral single-arm row specifically. It names the actual task, which is the trunk resisting rotation while the arm moves.

When: Dumbbell bench press · external cue

Say: Top of the head to the ceiling, like you're pulling yourself up with a string.

Why: For the sternum-stuck-down client who cannot find a stacked rib cage lying back.

When: Machine and cable chest press · external cue

Say: Top of the head to the ceiling, like you're pulling yourself up with a string.

Why: For the sternum-stuck-down client who cannot find a stacked rib cage lying back.

Other cues on those exercises. Not about this fault. Use them when the same set needs them.

Cues

When: Hip adduction

Say: Drive from the adductors.

When: Hip adduction

Say: Keep your back pressed against the pad.

Why: It enforces the setup detail that decides the set.

When: Hip adduction

Say: Don't let the pads crash.

Why: It enforces continuous tension and stops the stack being rested. Correct the concept before you correct the rep.

When: Free-weight row

Say: chest over your knees

Why: Sets the parallel torso the whole exercise depends on. It is checkable from the side in a single frame.

When: Free-weight row

Say: toes on the ground like grip in the ground

Why: The direct fix for the heel fault. It produces whole-foot pressure rather than a conscious weight shift.

When: Lat pulldown

Say: Tucking the pelvis, keeping a nice tall spine, not overdoing it and almost getting flexed. Finding that nice neutral position so my lats can actually wrap around the rib cage.

Why: It goes first because rib cage flare rather than arm path is the headline fault.

Terms on this page

adduction: A limb travelling in toward the midline of the body: the leg pulled in by the inner-thigh muscles, or the arm brought in to the side.

lumbar: The lower back: the part of the spine between the ribs and the pelvis.

anterior pelvic tilt: The pelvis tipped forward, so the lower back arches more.
