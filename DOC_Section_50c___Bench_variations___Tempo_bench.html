Tempo bench

A tempo bench slows the rep down, and it carries the first four weeks of a block peak. It sits alongside the pause squat and the deficit deadlift.

Where the tempo bench sits in a 12-week block peak

Weeks 1 to 4 | 4 | Tempo bench, pause squat and deficit deadlift. Mostly variations, higher reps, plenty of tissue built, low joint stress

Weeks 5 to 8 | 4 | The tested bench becomes the main movement. The variation drops back to the second exercise

Weeks 9 to 12 | 4 | The tempo bench is gone. Tested bench only

How long the descent takes

Standard | 2-3

Learning the movement, or a joint needs caution | 3-4

The ceiling | 4 | Nothing goes past four seconds

A very tall client | 5 | The one exception to the ceiling

The counts. The lowering (eccentric) is two to three seconds as standard, three to four while she is learning or where a joint needs caution. Nothing past four seconds, five for a very tall client. Relearning the movement after a layoff or a corrected fault is `3-4/1/1/1`. A long descent, a pause at the chest, a controlled press, a pause at the top.

The strength-system rule for the secondary-day variation. For a competitive powerlifter, the tempo goes on the lowering and on the lifting (concentric), with the focus on position. A tempo eccentric followed by exploding up is rejected, because lifters lose position on the way up or fall back on the technique being fixed.

Terms on this page

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.

concentric: The lifting half of a rep, where the muscle shortens under load.
