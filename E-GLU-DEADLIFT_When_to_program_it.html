When to use it

The problem you notice. The goal on her page is a number, not a muscle. She wants to pull more off the floor, or she is on the powerlifting path and the deadlift is one of her three lifts. When the goal is glute or hamstring size, this is the wrong page. The Romanian deadlift is the hinge for a muscle.

Reach for it when

# The goal on the page is a number, not a muscle

sub: She wants to move the most weight off the floor

Confirm it: Ask what she is training for. "A bigger deadlift" or "a total" is this lift. "Bigger glutes" is the RDL.

What it gets her: The most weight she can move off the floor. The deadlift does not try to bias glutes, hamstrings or quads. It organises her to move the most weight.

# She is learning the deadlift, or it keeps breaking down

sub: Build the pattern on the trap bar first

Confirm it: She is new to the deadlift, or her conventional pull loses position set after set. Or she is returning from an injury and her back should carry less tension.

What it gets her: The hinge pattern learned or rebuilt with less tension on her back. Program the trap bar deadlift, then move her to the conventional pull when the pattern holds and the injury allows.

# You want one lift that puts the most muscle against meaningful load

sub: Strength first, with the size arriving behind it

Confirm it: Her program needs a single heavy hinge and she can already hinge well.

What it gets her: A whole-body strength stimulus. Per rep it is not the most potent muscle-builder on the page. The strength it demands is the point.

# She is on the strength path and sumo is on the table

sub: Programmed for a bigger number, and nothing else

Confirm it: She is on the powerlifting-strength emphasis and a number is the goal. Assessing a new client explains how her goal sets the emphasis.

What it gets her: A bigger competition pull. Sumo pre-shortens the glutes, so it is a poor glute builder. Never prescribe it to build glutes.

Reach for something else when she is hesitant about technique

Teach the position on the bent-over barbell row first. It puts her in the same hinge position, where the load tries to slide the spine forward (shear), under far less weight. It is deliberately less loadable out of the gate, and that is the feature.

What this page does not decide. Sets, reps and frequency for the deadlift live in The phases and Building a session. This page tells you when the lift earns a slot and how to coach it. Deadlift programming for a meet is Peaking for a meet.

Setup. Jump stance, bar over the midfoot, shoulders just in front of the bar, lats on, big breath. Setup has the nine points in order.

Terms on this page

shear: A force that slides one part of a joint sideways across the other. In the lower back it is one vertebra sliding forward on the one below. Any bent-over position under load creates it; the lats and the brace resist it.
