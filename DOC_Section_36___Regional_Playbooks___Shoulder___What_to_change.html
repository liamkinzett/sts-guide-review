Shoulder: what to change first, by presentation

Where the shoulder presentation is one we already know, the first lever is written down. Change the position before you change the load, and check the chain before you treat the joint.

First lever by presentation

# Pain overhead

sub: Do not add overhead correctives

Do this: Start below shoulder height. Reach low in order to access positions high. If she could already get into the position the corrective needs, she would not need the corrective.

# Lacking internal or external rotation

sub: Do not treat it at the shoulder

Do this: Rotation is the upper arm turning outward (external rotation) or inward in its socket. Peel back to side-to-side (frontal plane) and forward-and-back (sagittal plane) movement first. The rotation limit usually sits on a simpler one.

# Poor overhead position generally

sub: Check rib cage on pelvis before anything else

Do this: If that fails, it is the whole intervention.

# Shoulder blade tipping forward, or riding up early

sub: Invisible from the front

Do this: Film it from the side. Faults and what you can actually see says the same.

# A shrug on pressing or flyes

sub: A range signal

Do this: She has run out of range where tension is being added. End the set.

# Shoulder pain on a front raise

sub: Reported rather than always visible

Do this: Ask about it after the set. Then run the pain rules.

# Wrist or little-finger-side pain on pressing

sub: Check the AC joint plane before the wrist

Do this: Widen the hands to her own shoulder width. The wrist is usually paying for the grip.

Terms on this page

external rotation: Turning a limb outward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face forward or up, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more to the side. A muscle that does it is an external rotator.

frontal plane: Side-to-side movement, like a lateral lunge or a side raise.

sagittal plane: Forward-and-back movement, like a squat, a hinge or a curl.
