Diagnosing from video · T-33.3

Constraint as test: add the fix and see whether the fault survives

Give her the thing that should fix it, and watch whether the fault survives. If it disappears, you have found what it was. If it stays, that is the finding.

The most useful method in this topic. Give her the heel wedge. Give her a load held in front. Narrow the stance. If she still squats like a hinge with the wedge under her heels, you have learned something no amount of watching would have told you.

This is how you tell a fault from a structure remotely. No measuring device, no hands on her, none of the passive range measurement a coach cannot do from a video.

A test, not a fix. Cueing also reaches for a constraint, as a fix: you are solving the problem. Here you are applying the same constraint to learn from whether it works. The same logic as a cue that fails having told you something, applied to constraints instead of words.

Why it works. It converts a question you cannot answer by looking into an experiment with two outcomes. Both outcomes are informative. Nothing else in the topic does that.

Three constraints and what each one tests

# A wedge under her heels

Tests: Whether her ankles are what stops her sitting into the squat.

If the fault goes: Setup. Keep the heel lift as her warm-up while you build the range.

If it stays: Not the ankle. Test the next one.

# A load held in front

Tests: Whether her centre of mass, not her structure, is tipping her forward.

If the fault goes: Position, not structure. The front load holds it for now.

If it stays: Not her balance. Keep going.

# A narrower stance

Tests: Whether the hip position, not the width, is producing the fault.

If the fault goes: Stance was the lever.

If it stays: You have ruled out three setup causes. It is time to look at capacity or structure.

Watch for

Applying the constraint and not recording the result. The test is worthless if next week's coach cannot see what it ruled out.

Say when you set up the test

Try it with a plate under your heels. I'm not saying that's the fix yet. I want to see what changes.
