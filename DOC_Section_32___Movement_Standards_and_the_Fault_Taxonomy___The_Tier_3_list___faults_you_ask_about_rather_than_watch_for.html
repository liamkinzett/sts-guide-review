The Tier 3 list: faults you ask about rather than watch for

These faults are real and they do not appear on video. They appear when you ask a question, or when a protocol surfaces them.

The ten Tier 3 faults and how you find each one

# Feeling it in the low back

sub: 45-degree hip extension, kickback

How you find it: She tells you. It signals movement through the lower back rather than the hip.

# Hip-flexor dominance on a leg raise

sub: A pinch in the hip crease, not work in the lower abs

How you find it: She reports the pinch. On some prescriptions the hip flexors are the target, so ask what the set was for.

# Upper-back achiness on an RDL

sub: A symptom, not a shape

How you find it: She reports it. With the load in front the lats cannot engage, and the traps and rhomboids take the job.

# Shoulder pain on a front raise

sub: Reported, not always visible

How you find it: She reports it. It is pain, so Pain rules and when to refer comes first. The exercise page carries its own escalation ladder.

# Elbow pain mid-set on a skull crusher

sub: Visible only in second-order signs

How you find it: She regrips repeatedly. She sets the bar down early. Then you ask. It is pain, so Pain rules and when to refer comes first.

# Tension migrating mid-session on a machine row

sub: The working feel moves to rear delts or biceps

How you find it: She reports it. This is the junk-volume audit: the set stopped training the muscle it was written for.

# Lower-back pump on an RDL

sub: A diagnostic, not a verdict

How you find it: She reports it. Four causes to check in order: heel-heavy pressure, hinging through the knee, going past active hip range, bracing failure. Glutes holds the detail.

# Where a single-leg RDL is felt

sub: Glute reads one way, entirely hamstring reads another

How you find it: She tells you. Felt in the glute reads as good pelvic stability. Felt entirely in the hamstring reads differently. STS programs the B-stance RDL rather than the single-leg version; the read still applies if you see one.

# A pump in a secondary or tertiary muscle

sub: The work landed next door

How you find it: Self-report, then adjust the setup.

# A set logged as near-failure that was not

sub: A protocol, not an observation

How you find it: The repeat-set protocol. If she can repeat the weight and reps after a short rest, the set was not where she logged it.

Two of these are worth more than the rest. The migrating tension is the junk-volume audit. The repeat-set protocol is the only way to check a logged effort, and everything in Effort and intensity rests on logged efforts being roughly honest.

Watch for

Watching for any of these. You will miss them, because you watched instead of asking.
