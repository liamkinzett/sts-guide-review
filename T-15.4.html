The pause table, movement by movement

Look the movement up here and take the code from its row. A movement that is not listed is matched to the nearest row by asking where the muscle is actually loaded.

Case 1. Pause at the end of the eccentric. That is the end of the lowering half of the rep (eccentric). A real loaded stretch. A bounce risk. Or both.

Case 3. Pause at the end of the concentric. That is the end of the lifting half (concentric). Only where the resistance profile peaks in the shortened position, which is a short list. Peak torque, the greatest turning force on the joint (torque), is the point in the rep where the muscle has to work hardest.

MovementTempoPeak torque is
Hip thrust, glute drive`2011`Lockout
Leg extension`2011`Full knee extension
45 degree hip extension`2011`Top

Case 3 is narrow on purpose. A peak-contraction hold on most movements is a squeeze where tension has already fallen away, or a workaround for equipment with a poor profile. Neither describes a hip thrust, whose peak torque genuinely is at lockout. If you cannot say why the profile peaks shortened, the movement is not in case 3.

The reverse pec deck looks like case 3 and is not

Peak tension on every rear delt variation is mid-range. At the shortened end of the sweep the extra range comes from arching the spine and pulling the shoulder blades together (scapular retraction). That is trap and rhomboid work rather than rear delt. Pausing there holds a position where the target muscle is not the one working. No terminal pause on it.

The exercise pages decide where tension peaks. This table follows them. When an exercise page resolves where a movement's tension peaks, that decides its row here.

This table is a worked list, not the reasoning. Levers and moment arms teaches how to read a resistance profile. Until you can do that, use the list and match new movements to the nearest row by asking where the muscle is actually loaded.

Terms on this page

resistance profile: Where in the range an exercise is hardest and where it is easiest. It is set by the line of the load against the joint, so a dumbbell and a cable can load the same muscle very differently.

cammed machine: A machine whose pulley is shaped so the load she feels changes through the rep. A well-designed cam matches how strong she is at each joint angle, so tension stays on the muscle throughout.

shortened position: The muscle is working while it is bunched short.

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.

concentric: The lifting half of a rep, where the muscle shortens under load.

torque: The turning force on a joint. The further the load sits from the joint, the more of it there is.

scapular retraction: Pulling the shoulder blades together and back towards the spine.
