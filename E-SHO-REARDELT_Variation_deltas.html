Shoulders · E-SHO-REARDELT#Variation deltas

Variations

STS programs three versions: the single arm cable rear delt fly, the dual cable rear delt fly and the reverse pec deck. There is no difference to coach between the single-arm and the dual cable versions. What governs one governs the other: the arm path, not a fixed machine angle, decides what gets loaded. Rear delt fibre direction changes with where the upper arm is placed, so cable height is set per lifter and never fixed.

The failure every version shares

If the shoulder blade travels with the arm, origin and insertion move together and the muscle is not loaded at all. Hold the blade slightly forward and move only the arm.

Cables crossed or uncrossed. The rear delt has no single fixed line of pull. Its origin fans broadly across the ridge of the shoulder blade while sharing one attachment on the upper arm. For the line of pull to be right, three things line up in opposition: the cable's plane, the upper arm's plane and the fibre direction.

Crossing the cables, or crossing the hands, is a practical fix rather than a mechanical upgrade. It stops the carabiners clashing at the start of the rep. And it lets the hands travel further across the body. That extends the stretch on the rear delt before the pull out to the side begins (horizontal abduction). A high-to-low pulling plane of roughly 45 degrees is what stops the upper traps taking over.

Never load the hands inside the plane of her AC joint

The AC joint is where the collarbone meets the shoulder blade, at the top of the shoulder. Crossing the hands inside that line forces the wrist to bend sideways to compensate. The named injury is to the cartilage on the little-finger side of the wrist (TFCC).

It buys nothing for the rear delt. The extra length is not delivered to the target muscle.

If you want the hands to travel across the body, do it single arm. The shoulder blade can then move around the rib cage instead of both AC joints locking.

How much of this matters, by client. For a general client, fine-tuning the cable angle is close to marginal. Pulling back through horizontal abduction is enough. The angle detail is for advanced physique competitors chasing complete development.

Do this

# Cross the cables where the setup calls for it

Do this: Cross them so the carabiners clear and the hands can travel further across. Do not present the crossing as the thing driving results for a general client.

# Coach the high-to-low plane for everyone

Do this: Roughly 45 degrees, high to low. It keeps the traps out of it, and that detail is worth coaching regardless of client level.

Where the lightest plate is still too heavy. This isolation movement is light by nature, and on some stacks the smallest increment overshoots what she can move well. STS doesn't program a dumbbell reverse fly or a band pull-apart as a swap here, so do not write one in. Progression covers an equipment increment that overshoots her band. Send the question up rather than invent a swap.

Terms on this page

horizontal abduction: The arm, held out in front at shoulder height, travelling back and out toward the side, as in a rear delt fly.
