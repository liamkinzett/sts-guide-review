Pain rules and when to refer · T-35.34

Cracking her own neck between sets

A client cracking her own neck or upper back between sets is not automatically your business. Twisting to crack her lower back is a different case.

The line is drawn by region. Rotating the neck to crack it, or leaning over a chair to crack the upper back, is treated as harmless habit. Winding up, kicking a leg out and rotating to crack the lower back (lumbar spine) is where the line sits. The reason is how the joints of the lower back are built (facet orientation).

Read that as an attitude, not a script to repeat to her. Neck and upper back self-cracking between sets: leave it alone. Lumbar rotation self-manipulation: not endorsed.

Your scope still governs what you say. Do not tell her it is definitely fine; that is a clinical judgement. Do watch for whether it is symptom-driven: pain, a felt need to crack it, or any of the joint referral signs. Any of those moves it out of "her business". A neck she cracks reflexively between sets, with no pain and no compulsion, gives you no grounds to intervene.

Two calls are yours to make. Whether repeated lumbar self-manipulation itself earns a referral, and when neck cracking has become compulsive rather than habitual. Where you see either, watch for pain and the joint referral signs, and say what you saw.

Terms on this page

facet orientation: The small joints at the back of each vertebra face different directions in the neck, upper back and lower back. The lower back's face front to back, so it is built to bend, not to twist under force.

lumbar spine: The lower back, the part of the spine between the ribs and the pelvis.
