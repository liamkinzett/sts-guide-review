The screen is an alignment check, not a range measurement

Run the screen. It is an active alignment check, not a measurement of passive range. The two are different, and the difference is why a coach is allowed to make this call.

The rule in Faults and what you can actually see is that a coach never calls a fault from a passive range measurement. External rotation measured with a goniometer while a coach holds the joint is exactly that (external rotation). It stays off limits.

The screen on this page is not that. She moves herself, actively, as far as she can. You judge one thing you can see: whether the elbow stays in line with the wrist and the shoulder. No angle is measured, and nobody holds the joint.

So the screen and the rule agree. Where it fails, curling with both arms is contraindicated and she goes single-arm, permanently. Watch for has the full screen.

Terms on this page

external rotation: Turning a limb outward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face forward or up, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more to the side. On the screen it is what she does with the arms held straight out in front.
