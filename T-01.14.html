What do you give her when she asks how long until she'll see a change?

When she asks how long until she sees a change, give her a measurement and a horizon. Never a gain figure. The measurement is one she watches being built. The horizon is scaled to her training age.

Be clear with yourself about what this page can and cannot supply. There is no timetable for when she will look different, and this page does not invent one. What it gives you is progress she can watch being measured, and a horizon for reviewing it.

There is no single date when she will suddenly "look different". Visible change depends on too many variables for a coach to responsibly promise a universal timeline. A better answer uses two things instead: a measurable sign of progress, and a time horizon that fits her.

Start with her own data. Before predicting what happens over the next several weeks, find out what is happening now. Look at her body weight, training log, performance and any other agreed measures. Build expectations from her actual response, not from a generic table.

Training age changes the clock. Beginners can often make fast, visible performance gains, because they have large nervous-system and skill adaptations still on the table. Advanced lifters generally progress slower, because much of that easy adaptation has already happened. The same question can deserve a different answer depending on who is asking. Do not give a beginner and an advanced lifter the same expectation just because they are both on an equally good program.

Answer it in this order

Turn the question into one week of her own data. Have her weigh herself today and through the week. Talk on Monday about what moved. Then make predictions for the next few weeks from her own numbers, not from a table.

Buy the stall in advance. In week one or two, tell her things stall around week five and six. When it comes, she was ready for it. Reading her training data owns the stall conversation itself. This page only attaches it to the timeline answer from day one.

Price the horizon off her training age. A beginner wants 12 to 15 pounds on her bench. Tell her "we'll talk in two weeks", and she will have it. An advanced lifter with a 500 pound bench wanting 20 pounds gets "give me eight to 10 weeks". Same question, different clock.

Both horizons are stated in performance terms: pounds on a bar and weeks to get them. Neither is a date for looking different. What you give her is a measurement she watched being made and a horizon that matches her training age.

Why it works. A predicted figure is a promise the physiology cannot underwrite. A prediction built from her own week of data is one she watched being made, and it moves when she does. The coaching relationship covers what a confident promise costs when the weeks arrive and the result does not.

Watch for

Reaching for the annual gain figure because she pressed hard for a number. She gets the year-against-twelve-weeks mismatch, never the pounds.

Say it like this

Weigh yourself today and a few times this week. We'll look at it together on Monday and make some predictions from your own numbers. And so you know now: things usually stall around week five or six. That's normal, and we'll be ready for it.
