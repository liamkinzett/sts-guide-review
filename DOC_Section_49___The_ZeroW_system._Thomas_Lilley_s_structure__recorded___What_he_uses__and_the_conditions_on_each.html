The movements, and the conditions on each

The strength system runs a short list of movements, and several of them carry a condition. Use the conditional ones only where the condition is met.

The movements

# Squat, bench, deadlift

sub: The competition lifts

Use: Once a week each, two to three sets (SBD).

# Hack squat, leg press, belt squat

sub: The second lower-body pattern exposure

Use: Machine-based, on the second lower day.

# RDL

sub: The hinge slot, far above the good morning

Use: Safer and higher return than the good morning, which beginners almost always butcher.

# Stiff-leg deadlift

sub: A distinct hinge option

Use: Named separately from the RDL.

# Lunge, split squat

sub: The unilateral slot

Use: Given its own place in the session (unilateral).

# Leg extension, hamstring curl

sub: The two isolation slots

Use: One each, two sets.

# Suitcase carry

sub: The trunk slot

Use: A stated favourite. STS does not yet program it, so the trunk slot takes core work. The Loaded carry page in Core teaches you to coach one where a client already carries.

# RKC plank

sub: Rigid torso, big brace

Use: A plank held with everything squeezed hard, so the torso stays rigid (RKC plank). Trunk work built on bracing (bracing).

# Close-grip bench

sub: The primary secondary-day bench variation

Use: The usual second bench exposure.

# Overhead press, chest press machine

sub: Day two of the push pattern for larger lifters

Use: Overhead press first, chest press machine second.

# Spoto press

sub: Only where she loses tightness on the way down

Use: Useless if the fault is that she relaxes on the chest, which is a technique error rather than a control error (Spoto press). Execute with a feather touch, or a paused hover an inch off the chest.

# Board press

sub: Only long-limbed lifters, and lifters who bench in a supportive shirt

Use: Be wary that she does not turn into a good board presser and a poor bench presser (board press).

# Sumo block pull

sub: Sumo pullers only

Use: It trains holding pelvic tension to break the floor, and the knee snap at lockout (block pull).

Terms on this page

RKC plank: A plank held with the whole body braced as hard as it can be, so the torso stays rigid rather than simply held up.

SBD: Squat, bench and deadlift. The three competition lifts in powerlifting.

unilateral: One limb doing the work, as in a split squat or a single-arm row. A lunge or a split squat is unilateral; a back squat is not.

bracing: Filling the trunk with air and tensing the whole midsection so the spine stays rigid under load.

Spoto press: A bench press where the bar pauses just above the chest and never touches it. She has to stay tight at the bottom.

board press: A bench press where boards on her chest stop the bar a few inches short of touching, so the top of the lift is trained.

block pull: A deadlift pulled from blocks or pins, so the bar starts above the floor.
