Biceps · E-BIC-LENGTH#Variation deltas

Variations

STS programs six versions in this slot. Three are preachers, one is the incline curl, and two are face-away cable curls. They are long in different places, and that is the delta that matters.

The six versions

# Single arm DB preacher curl

sub: Loaded long, not stretched

What changes: Nothing between this and the other preacher rows beyond the single-arm setup, which is the default anyway. The label is the point. Long at the elbow, short at the shoulder. The long head crosses the shoulder (long head). Full length needs the elbow straight and the arm behind the body together (shoulder extension). A preacher pad fixes the upper arm in front of the body, which is the opposite of shoulder extension. So the preacher loads the biceps long at the elbow and never stretches the long head.

# EZ bar preacher curl

sub: Same label. Two arms on one bar

What changes: The bar fixes both hands, so it suits the smaller lifter who can jam the upper arms straight up and down. On our steep pad it loses tension at full extension and peaks mid-range.

# Machine preacher curl

sub: Same label. The cam sets the profile

What changes: Where it is hardest is set by the machine, not the movement. Read the machine in front of her. Where it's hardest explains.

# Incline DB curl

sub: The shoulder is extended, the load is mid-range

What changes: The shoulder is behind the body, so the biceps is long at both ends. The dumbbell still peaks with the forearm at a right angle. It is the entry point for a cranky shoulder.

# Face away cable bicep curl

sub: The one that loads the fully lengthened long head

What changes: The cable keeps tension into the stretch, where a dumbbell gives nothing. Elbow straight and arm behind the body together. Both alignment checks gate the load.

# Single arm face away cable bicep curl

sub: The same, one arm at a time

What changes: Same setup, same checks, one arm.

Terms on this page

long head: The head of a muscle that crosses the shoulder joint as well as the elbow: on the triceps it only stretches with the arm raised, on the biceps only with the arm behind the body.

shoulder extension: The upper arm travelling back behind the line of the body.
