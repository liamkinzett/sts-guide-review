Pain rules and when to refer · T-35.13

Medication is not yours

You never advise on a drug: not the dose, not the timing, not what training does around it. The licence to name a guess about a movement problem does not extend to medication. The boundary sits in a different place here.

It includes every question that sounds like scheduling rather than medicine. Whether she should train on a day she forgot a dose. Whether to take it before or after a session. Whether a session should be lighter while she adjusts to a new one.

The tell that this is not a training question. The answer depends on how the drug behaves in her body. You do not know that. The disclaimer does not change what you did, any more than it does for a diagnosis.

Compared

columns: Do | Not

What you say: Describe what you observed. Which sessions, what changed, what she reported | Explain why it happened

Who answers it: Send her to the prescriber, the only person who can answer it | Send her to a physio, who cannot answer it either

Her training: Keep coaching everything else. A medication question is not a reason to stop training her | Pause her program while she waits for an appointment

Verdict

Refer to the prescriber, named specifically.

Not "a doctor" and not her physio. Keep coaching everything else.

Where a referral would take her away from training, collaborate instead. If the only available referral is to someone who does not train people, work with them rather than hand the client over. The classic case is blood pressure. The coach refers for the medication and keeps the training conversation her own. The aim is an intensity nobody is worried about.

Say it like this

That one's for whoever prescribed it, not me. What I can do is tell you exactly what I've seen in your sessions and when, so you've got something concrete to take to them. And we keep training in the meantime.

Watch for

Inferring what a drug does to training. Beta blockers, SSRIs, metformin, statins, the pill, antidepressants, corticosteroids, thyroid medication: you will hear the names. Do not infer what any of them does to her recovery, heart rate or temperature. This page is a boundary, not a pharmacology reference.
