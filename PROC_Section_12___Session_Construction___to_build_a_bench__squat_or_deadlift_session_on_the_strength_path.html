A bench, squat or deadlift session on the strength path

When she names a competition lift (competition lift), this is the strength path. Build the session to serve the lift. If she asked to build a muscle instead, use the muscle-building route in the rest of this topic. Nothing below is a new rule. It is this topic's rules run in order for one worked case.

Build it in this order

Check the path. "A bench session", "a squat session", "a deadlift day" or "SBD", squat, bench and deadlift, all name the strength path. "Build my chest" or "an upper day" does not.

The main lift. One to two top sets (top set). Volume on the competition lift is two to three sets a week per lift for an established lifter. That is adequate, not low. It counts barbell exposures, not muscle volume. The accessories supply the rest, and Volume still owns hard sets per muscle per week. Read the barbell figure as a muscle-volume prescription and you will badly under-train her.

Order the session. The shape is below. The highest-skill, least-supported movement first, then supported pressing, then the required pull, then isolations, then delts. The pull gives the shoulder blade back its turn upward, the turn that lets the arm go overhead (upward rotation).

Accessory dose. Two sets per accessory, each within one to two reps of failure (RIR). Intensity stays high, because intensity is what drives the adaptation.

Do not stack the competition lifts. Squat, bench and deadlift do not share one day. Whatever goes last is shafted, the whole-body fatigue is too high, and putting the whole week in one session is bad for her head. Exceptions: a mock meet, which is a practice competition, or temporarily to build trust.

Pick the accessories on the load axis. Slots 2 and 4 must not repeat slot 1's profile. Pairing has already judged every pair. Read the verdict.

The week

# 1 · Bench day

Competition bench press | Main lift | The highest-skill, most powerful movement goes first, while she is freshest | 1 to 2 top sets

Supported press, such as a machine chest press | Secondary press | Externally supported so she can push a little more output. A different job, not the same press twice | 2 sets, 1 to 2 RIR

Overhead press or pulldown | Required pull | The bench pins the shoulder blade. This restores upward rotation. Not optional | 2 sets, 1 to 2 RIR

Pec fly, tricep pushdown or dip | Isolation | One or two, not five. This is where arm work sits on this path | 2 sets, 1 to 2 RIR

Delt isolation | Delts | The push day closes here | 2 sets, 1 to 2 RIR

Where the chest movements sit on the load axis. Slot 1 is hardest in the stretch. Slots 2 and 4 come from a different row.

Watch for

The pec deck and the cable fly are opposite ends. They are not swaps for each other.

Heavy lat rowing immediately before the press. It drops pressing performance. The pull goes after the press.

Reading two to three barbell sets a week as her chest volume. It is her competition-lift exposure count. Her muscles still get their weekly hard sets through the accessories.

Who this is for. The strength system's specifics, the set counts and the fixed order above, are written for a competitive powerlifter. A general client whose goal is muscle keeps the STS defaults in the rest of this topic.

Terms on this page

competition lift: One of the three lifts tested at a powerlifting meet: the squat, the bench press and the deadlift.

top set: The heaviest set of the exercise that day. Back-off sets follow it at a lighter load.

RIR: Reps in reserve. How many more reps she could have done when the set stopped. 1 RIR means she stopped one rep short of failure.

lengthened position: The point in the rep where the working muscle is at its longest while it carries the load. It describes the muscle's length, not whether she feels a stretch.

shortened position: The muscle is working while it is bunched short.

upward rotation: The shoulder blade turning so its outer corner rises and the socket tilts upward. It is what lets the arm travel overhead. A muscle that does it is an upward rotator.
