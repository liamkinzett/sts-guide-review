Glutes · E-GLU-RDL#When to program it

When to use it

The problem you notice. Her glute day reads hip thrust, 45 degree hip extension, cable kickback. Three movements in a row that all work the glute short (shortened position). Or she has told you she wants glutes and, when you watch her hinge, the hinge itself is the problem.

Most women are already good at squeezing the glute short. Very few are good at loading it long (lengthened position). The RDL is the mid-to-lengthened slot the rest of her week never touches.

Reach for it when

# Her glute day is three short-position movements in a row

sub: Read it straight off the program sheet

Confirm it: List her glute exercises and ask where each one loads the glute. If every answer is "at the top", the long position is untrained.

What it gets her: Load put where she is actually weak. The hinge is the mid-to-lengthened slot the rest of her week never touches.

# She wants glutes and the hinge itself is the problem

sub: Watch one set of RDLs before you add anything clever

Confirm it: Film from the side. Knees travelling back with the hips, weight in the heels, torso folding after the hips stop.

What it gets her: The pattern fixed rather than nuance stacked on a broken one. Two-legged hinging done properly is one of the best glute builders there is. It earns that rank precisely because most clients cannot yet do it.

# The day's main lift was a squat and the second slot is open

sub: The second movement attacks what the main lift neglected

Confirm it: Check the session. A squat first, and nothing hip-dominant yet.

What it gets her: The hip-dominant pattern the squat left on the table. The second movement never works the same muscle the same way as the main lift. The named second-slot hinge is the RDL or the stiff-leg deadlift.

# Her low back or her glutes keep finishing the rep

sub: The hamstrings never become the limiter

Confirm it: She reports the back or the glutes fatiguing first, set after set, with her position holding.

What it gets her: The hamstrings made the true bottleneck. Put a seated hamstring curl first, so the hamstrings are already tired (pre-exhaustion) and nothing else can take the lift over. This is an advanced exception, not the default order.

Three ways to over-program it

A split-stance RDL and a barbell RDL on the same day. Redundant. Move them across days.

Heavy split-squat work plus a barbell RDL on one day. Rejected outright. Both drain the same energy reserve, and she will do neither well.

A "glute day" that adds up to four hinges. That is the caricature. Count the hinges.

It is a main lift, not a free accessory

High fatigue cost. The lowering phase (eccentric) causes heavy muscle damage, and the lift leaves deep structural fatigue behind. Treat it as a main lift when you count her volume.

Remove it entirely in peak week and the final weeks of contest prep. A depleted athlete has no business absorbing that eccentric load. This applies to contest preparation only. It says nothing about an off-season client.

Setup. Hip-width stance, toes straight, bar an inch from the shins, ribs down before rep one. Setup has the full list.

Terms on this page

shortened position: The muscle is working while it is bunched short. For the glute that is full hip extension, standing tall or locked out on a thrust.

lengthened position: The point in the rep where the working muscle is at its longest while it carries the load. It describes the muscle's length, not whether she feels a stretch.

pre-exhaustion: Tiring one muscle with an isolation exercise first, so that it is the limiter in the compound lift that follows.

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.
