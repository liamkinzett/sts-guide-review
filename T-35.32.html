Fibromyalgia, already diagnosed: the first four weeks

Where a client already carries a clinician's fibromyalgia diagnosis, the first four weeks have a structure. Twice-weekly supervised sessions, a light start, and progression checked every 3 to 4 weeks rather than weekly.

The structure. Twice weekly, supervised, in groups of 5 to 7. A 60-minute session built from 10 minutes of warm-up and 50 minutes of resistance work across all four limbs and the trunk. Starting load 40% of her one-rep max (1RM). Progression evaluated only every 3 to 4 weeks, not weekly, climbing towards 80% 1RM by week 15 over the full 15 weeks.

Compared

columns: Set by the structure | Not set, so you build it and say so

Frequency: Twice weekly, supervised | Specific exercises or equipment

Load: 40% 1RM to start | Sets, reps or a reps-in-reserve target

Progression: Checked every 3 to 4 weeks, not weekly | Rest intervals

Coverage: Large muscle groups, all four limbs and trunk | An objective rule for regressing mid-session on a flare

The drop-outs are the warning, not a footnote. Of the women this structure comes from, 25% stopped altogether and 7% stopped because a pain flare-up ended it. Fixed, non-autoregulated progression is what those pain-driven exits are tied to. The lesson is about the progression method, not about which exercises to avoid.

Where the structure stops, you are building, not sourcing. Below block level, the set and rep scheme, the rest intervals and any flare-regression rule are yours to build. Say so to the client rather than presenting them as established.

Who it applies to. Adult women aged 20 to 65 with a fibromyalgia diagnosis and no other named conditions. Do not extend it to anyone else without saying so.

A diagnosis, not a screening call. This applies once a clinician has diagnosed fibromyalgia. You never decide whether unexplained widespread pain is fibromyalgia.

Terms on this page

1RM: The heaviest weight she can lift once with good form.
