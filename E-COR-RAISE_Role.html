Core · E-COR-RAISE#Role

What it's for

The leg raise is trunk flexion driven from the bottom end. The pelvis is flexed toward the rib cage rather than the rib cage toward the pelvis.

What it is for

# The defining standard

sub: Pelvic flexion, not hip flexion held rigid

What it does: The front abdominal wall (rectus abdominis) flexes the spine by tucking the pelvis. A rep that only raises the legs trains the hip flexors instead.

# Hanging version

sub: Trunk work plus a shoulder and grip demand

What it does: The shoulder sits in an unstable environment under load, and the hands have to hold her up for the whole set.

# Captain's chair

sub: The same trunk work with the grip demand removed

What it does: Hip-flexion work without grip fatigue.

# Straight-leg version

sub: The long-lever member and the progression route

What it does: Progression runs from bent knees to straight legs. Bending the knees to a right angle is the regression. STS has no page yet for the straight-leg version on either the bar or the chair; it is the direction you progress her in.

# Resistance profile

sub: None stated

What it does: What is stated instead is a range requirement, on Where it's hardest.

# Where it sits

sub: Direct core work, at the end of the session

What it does: It is also the rung immediately below a toes-to-bar, which STS doesn't program. It burns the grip, so it goes at the end of a session where it cannot tire the hands before a pull. Building a session has the order.

Terms on this page

rectus abdominis: The long muscle down the front of the abdomen, the "six-pack". It bends the spine forward by pulling the ribs toward the pelvis.
