What she will ask you · T-47.13

"Does fat turn into muscle?"

Fat does not turn into muscle. Losing fat and gaining muscle are two processes running in parallel, never a conversion. This is a judgement call, and the second half of the question, what happens after a break, has a narrower answer.

The partition, in a coach's words. Not all calories turn into muscle. She will not get stronger just by getting fatter. A surplus is needed to build, but new fat tissue does not make her stronger. The calories have to be converted into muscle, and that is literally what muscle growth (hypertrophy) means.

The mechanism. Body recomposition, losing fat and adding muscle at the same time, is two separate processes. Muscle protein synthesis (muscle protein synthesis) can exceed breakdown even in a deficit (deficit), which is what allows fat loss and muscle gain together. Two processes. Not one tissue becoming another.

Compared

columns: Fat loss | Muscle gain

What drives it: Eating less than she burns | Training hard and building more protein than she breaks down

Where it happens: Everywhere | In the muscles she trains

Can they run together: Yes, and it is two processes | Yes, and it is not a conversion

Say it like this

Fat and muscle are different tissues and one never becomes the other. What can happen is losing one while you build the other at the same time. That's two jobs, and we set up the training and the food to do both.

What happens after a break. Keep the answer to what you can see. The plan rebuilds from where she is. Do not tell her she kept anything at the fibre level through the break. Do not promise that muscle memory brings it back on a timetable. Muscle and growth owns what is known about time off.

Food is the Nutrition Manual's. The mechanism is yours to understand. The surplus and the deficit are not yours to set.

Watch for

"Your muscle turned to fat while you were off." It did not. Muscle was lost and fat was gained, separately. Say that.

Terms on this page

hypertrophy: An increase in the size of a muscle, brought about by its existing fibres growing larger rather than by the muscle gaining new fibres. The fibres thicken in response to training, and it only happens in the muscles she trains.

muscle protein synthesis: The process that builds new muscle protein after training.

deficit: Eating fewer calories than she burns, so that she loses body fat.
