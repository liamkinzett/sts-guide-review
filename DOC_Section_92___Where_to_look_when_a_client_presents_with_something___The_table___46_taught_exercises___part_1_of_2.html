The table: hip adduction to front raise

One row for each of the 46 exercises STS teaches, with the facts that live on its page.

Sets, reps, reps in reserve, rest and tempo have one home each and are not repeated here. See The numbers.

Every exercise

# Hip adduction (adduction)

sub: Direct adductor loading

Where tension peaks: At peak contraction, in the shortened squeezed position. The pause goes there.

Check before you prescribe it: The Copenhagen needs a partner.

Swap to: Copenhagen hold. No equipment needed. It does need a partner

On its page: 3 cues · 7 faults

# Seated cable row

sub: Horizontal pull on continuous cable tension

Where tension peaks: It depends on the bias. The two are in different places.

Check before you prescribe it: Neck pain from rowing has a named causal chain and it is not a neck problem.

Swap to: Low-to-high band row (not an STS movement). Band on a low anchor at roughly 45 degrees, stepped back far enough for tension

On its page: 4 cues · 6 faults

# Free-weight row

sub: Horizontal pull performed from an unsupported hinge

Where tension peaks: Mid-range and the lengthened bottom

Check before you prescribe it: The low back is the designed-in limiter.

Swap to: The family substitutes within itself. Barbell, then dumbbells, then landmine row (not an STS movement). The single arm dumbbell row

On its page: 3 cues · 4 faults

# Machine and chest-supported row

sub: Horizontal pull with external stability supplied by a pad or a seat

Where tension peaks: At maximal scapular (scapular) retraction, the finish.

Check before you prescribe it: High central fatigue cost.

Swap to: Prone on an incline bench set at roughly 45 degrees, arms hanging. The chest supported dumbbell row (not an STS movement)

On its page: 4 cues · 4 faults

# Lat pulldown

sub: Vertical pull

Where tension peaks: At the lengthened overhead position.

Check before you prescribe it: Elbow pain here is a downstream symptom rather than an elbow problem.

Swap to: A cable column and a handle is the floor. Purpose-built pulldown machines are a preference rather than a requirement

On its page: 4 cues · 4 faults

# Pull-up

sub: The library's vertical pull, lat-biased, loaded heaviest in the lengthened hang

Where tension peaks: At the bottom, not the top.

Check before you prescribe it: The chin-up's shorter bottom range is not a fault.

Swap to: The four rungs on its page, in that order

On its page: 7 cues · 4 faults

# Straight-arm pulldown

sub: Single-joint shoulder extension through a long arc with the elbow held static

Where tension peaks: At the top of the arc, arms overhead and behind the head, in the lengthened position.

Check before you prescribe it: The shoulder over a long range is the named vulnerability.

Swap to: Cable and dumbbell are interchangeable implements for this pattern. If the high pulley is occupied, use a dumbbell

On its page: 4 cues · 2 faults

# Standing curl

sub: The default mid-range mass builder for the elbow flexors

Where tension peaks: The pause goes at the top, in the fully contracted position.

Check before you prescribe it: Run the external-rotation screen before you choose between bilateral and single-arm. Where it fails, bilateral curling is contraindicated and she goes single-arm, permanently.

Swap to: The EZ bar, named for wrists that complain on a straight bar. A cable is the other implement

On its page: 4 cues · 4 faults

# Lengthened-position curl

sub: The long head of the biceps crosses the shoulder

Where tension peaks: Peak contraction on the preacher, not the bottom.

Check before you prescribe it: Never program preacher curls first in a session. The movement is heaviest near the fully lengthened stretch, which is a tear risk under high load.

Swap to: Put something under one side of an incline bench to steepen the pad and hang the arm over

On its page: 4 cues · 2 faults

# Shortened-position curl

sub: The shortened end of elbow flexion, with the shoulder at or in front of the torso line

Where tension peaks: At the top, in the peak shortened contraction.

Check before you prescribe it: Placement is the one hard rule here.

Swap to: Any one of the three for any other. The spider curl needs an incline bench. The concentration curl needs a seat

On its page: none cues · 2 faults

# Calf raise

sub: Plantarflexion under load

Where tension peaks: No single tension peak is named.

Check before you prescribe it: Calves before squats is an option, on the grounds of ankle preparation rather than pre-exhaustion.

Swap to: Machine standing and machine seated calf raise are the only two programmed. Knee angle picks between them

On its page: 2 cues · 3 faults

# Barbell bench press

sub: The library's heaviest loadable horizontal press and the competition lift

Where tension peaks: At the chest, in the lengthened position.

Check before you prescribe it: Injury caution: scapular protraction or elevation under a heavy barbell jams the shoulder.

Swap to: Rungs 1 and 2 of its ladder

On its page: 4 cues · 4 faults

# Dumbbell bench press

sub: The horizontal press with a free converging arm path

Where tension peaks: In the lengthened position, half an inch off the chest.

Check before you prescribe it: The dumbbell version's advantage is the lengthened position it reaches.

Swap to: Rungs 1 and 2 of its ladder

On its page: 4 cues · 3 faults

# Dip

sub: The closed-chain press into shoulder hyperextension

Where tension peaks: No pause position is prescribed for the dip.

Check before you prescribe it: The dip appears twice in the library, under chest and under triceps.

Swap to: The first ladder on its page

On its page: 4 cues · 2 faults

# Pec fly

sub: The single-joint chest movement

Where tension peaks: Opposite ends, by machine. The pec deck is a lengthened-range machine. The cable fly is hardest in the shortened, most contracted position.

Check before you prescribe it: Anterior shoulder and biceps caution.

Swap to: Pec deck for cable fly and back again. No third option is named

On its page: 4 cues · 4 faults

# Machine and cable chest press

sub: The braced horizontal press

Where tension peaks: Nowhere in particular. Tension is continuous through the range, which is the point.

Check before you prescribe it: Seat height decides what this machine trains.

Swap to: Rungs 1 to 3 of its ladder

On its page: 4 cues · 7 faults

# Push-up

sub: The closed-chain horizontal press

Where tension peaks: No pause position is prescribed for the push-up.

Check before you prescribe it: Trunk sag or piking is the fault to watch. Brace, or regress the lever.

Swap to: Not applicable. The push-up needs nothing, which is part of why it is the foundation of the pattern

On its page: 3 cues · 4 faults

# Anti-rotation

sub: Anti-transverse-plane work

Where tension peaks: Pallof press: the demand rises as the hands travel further from the chest. No length position is loaded.

Check before you prescribe it: A client can satisfy an alignment check by rotating the pelvis to buy the position. Alignment passing is not the standard passing.

Swap to: Single arm dumbbell row. STS's stronger position is not to substitute the Pallof press but to replace it

On its page: 3 cues · 2 faults

# Loaded carry

sub: A coordination and load-tolerance tool. It teaches a trunk to accept load. It is not a core-capacity exercise

Where tension peaks: Not stated. Do not infer it from the plank.

Check before you prescribe it: There is no dose. Prescribe by effort, not by numbers.

Swap to: Plank or side plank

On its page: 1 cue · none faults

# Trunk flexion

sub: Dynamic spinal flexion to shorten the rectus abdominis

Where tension peaks: At the top, in the contracted position.

Check before you prescribe it: Hard contraindication for loaded spinal flexion: vertebral osteoporosis and kyphosis (kyphosis).

Swap to: Kneeling cable crunch (not an STS movement): adjustable resistance in a simple position

On its page: 4 cues · 4 faults

# Isometric (isometric) hold

sub: An isometric

Where tension peaks: Nowhere. That is the point of the movement, not a gap in the entry.

Check before you prescribe it: Not a gate.

Swap to: Nothing to swap. A mat is the whole equipment list

On its page: 4 cues · 1 fault

# Leg raise

sub: Trunk flexion driven from the bottom end

Where tension peaks: Not stated. No pause is prescribed.

Check before you prescribe it: Prerequisites, stated as the reasons this movement gets substituted.

Swap to: Captain's chair knee raise: hip-flexion work without grip fatigue. Its straight-leg version is the progression

On its page: 3 cues · 2 faults

# Deadlift

sub: A deadlift is not an RDL and is not coached like one

Where tension peaks: At the floor, in the position you set before force started.

Check before you prescribe it: Spinal flexion under load: Mobility and stretching holds what is known, Faults and what you can actually see holds what you may screen.

Swap to: Romanian deadlift. This page is for the lift

On its page: none cues · 13 faults

# Hip extension (45 degree and GHD)

sub: The library's default loaded shortened-position glute movement

Where tension peaks: At the top, in the fully shortened position

Check before you prescribe it: Feeling it in the low back is something you ask about, not watch for.

Swap to: Glute-ham raise (not an STS movement), where a GHD is available and she can take the added knee-flexion demand

On its page: 3 cues · 5 faults

# Cable glute kickback

sub: An isolation movement

Where tension peaks: Mid-to-shortened

Check before you prescribe it: Feeling it in the low back is something you ask about, not watch for.

Swap to: Donkey kick and machine kickback (neither an STS movement). The 45 degree cable setup of this same movement

On its page: 4 cues · 4 faults

# Lunge

sub: Genuinely unilateral, which separates it from the split squat: the other leg actually unloads

Where tension peaks: At the lowest point of the stride, mid-to-lengthened.

Check before you prescribe it: A wobbly lunge may be a strength problem wearing a coordination costume.

Swap to: A single goblet-held weight in place of two dumbbells

On its page: 3 cues · 4 faults

# Romanian deadlift

sub: The library's primary loadable posterior-chain pattern

Where tension peaks: At the bottom, in the fully lengthened position.

Check before you prescribe it: High fatigue cost.

Swap to: Dumbbell, trap bar, shrug machine, Smith

On its page: 4 cues · 9 faults

# Split squat

sub: It is a frontal-plane exercise, not a one-legged squat

Where tension peaks: At the bottom, mid-to-lengthened.

Check before you prescribe it: One leg or two decides whether a single-leg movement is the right choice at all.

Swap to: Stationary lunge

On its page: 4 cues · 4 faults

# Step-up and step-down

sub: Step-up: hardest at the transition off the floor, tension peaking in the midrange of hip extension

Where tension peaks: Step-up: midrange of hip extension.

Check before you prescribe it: This is the movement that exposes what a two-footed variation hides.

Swap to: None named. The only equipment is an adjustable box or bench. The height must be adjustable to her

On its page: 4 cues · 5 faults

# Hip thrust

sub: The library's loaded shortened-position glute movement

Where tension peaks: At the top, in the fully shortened glute position

Check before you prescribe it: Bar padding and hip-crease comfort decide whether she can load this at all.

Swap to: 45 degree hip extension is the primary swap. Inside the pattern itself, the glute drive machine and the Smith machine

On its page: 3 cues · 4 faults

# Hamstring curl

sub: The library's knee-flexion isolation

Where tension peaks: It depends on the variation. The two peak at opposite ends.

Check before you prescribe it: The named injury caution.

Swap to: Seated for seated. A lying curl does not substitute for a seated one

On its page: 3 cues · 5 faults

# Activation drill

sub: Not warmth

Where tension peaks: Hold the peak protraction on the serratus press.

Check before you prescribe it: A half-kneeling position needs three camera angles to read properly.

Swap to: Shorten the lever: go standing or split stance instead of half-kneeling

On its page: 4 cues · 3 faults

# Stretch

sub: Step one of three

Where tension peaks: These are held positions rather than loaded ranges

Check before you prescribe it: Meniscus or knee pathology with the foot set wide on the pigeon.

Swap to: None named. There is nothing to substitute for a stretch

On its page: 4 cues · 4 faults

# Soft tissue work

sub: A short-term sensory tool

Where tension peaks: Not applicable. It is not a loaded range.

Check before you prescribe it: Warming up holds STS's position on foam rolling and self-myofascial work generally.

Swap to: The lacrosse ball, in the same tier. The foam roller is the named implement

On its page: 2 cues · 2 faults

# Leg extension

sub: The library's open-chain knee-extension isolation

Where tension peaks: At the top, at full extension

Check before you prescribe it: Knee pain on extensions: ask about it, then Pain rules and when to refer.

Swap to: The leg extension is a single-purpose commercial machine

On its page: 3 cues · 3 faults

# Machine squat

sub: The externally stabilised squat

Where tension peaks: At the bottom, fully stretched, hamstrings on the calves.

Check before you prescribe it: A machine is not automatically the braced case.

Swap to: The ladder on its page

On its page: 3 cues · 6 faults

# Leg press and hip press

sub: The library's externally stabilised bilateral press

Where tension peaks: At the deepest point of knee flexion.

Check before you prescribe it: The sled and the pad hide the pelvis from a front-on camera.

Swap to: None for the press itself. It is the swap rather than the thing swapped

On its page: 3 cues · 5 faults

# Barbell squat

sub: The library's primary loaded knee-and-hip pattern

Where tension peaks: At the bottom.

Check before you prescribe it: Spinal flexion under load: Mobility and stretching holds what is known, Faults and what you can actually see holds what you may screen.

Swap to: SSB squat first, where the rack has one. Then barbell front squat. Then goblet squat. Read the SSB note on its page first

# Front raise

sub: The least loadable rung on the vertical press continuum

Where tension peaks: Shortened, as for any free-weight raise.

Check before you prescribe it: This is not a strength movement.

Swap to: None named. The plate and incline variations are not covered

On its page: 3 cues · 4 faults

Terms on this page

adduction: A limb travelling in toward the midline of the body: the leg pulled in by the inner-thigh muscles, or the arm brought in to the side.

scapular: To do with the shoulder blade, the scapula.

kyphosis: An exaggerated forward rounding of the upper back.

isometric: A hold. The muscle works against the load but the joint does not move.

:

plantarflexion: Pointing the foot down, as when she rises onto her toes.
