What a squat variation is for

Where the bar sits changes the exercise. It is never decoration. High bar, low bar and a load held in front each ask her hips for a different amount of hinge. Copy the standard from one to another and you manufacture faults.

So a variation is chosen for a job. There are six.

The six jobs a squat variation does

# Learning the pattern

sub: Counterbalance, then goblet, then front, then high bar, then low bar

What it looks like: A complete beginner starts at the first rung. She builds at one level before she advances.

# A constraint that removes a fault

sub: A heel wedge, or a load held in front

What it looks like: If the fault disappears, that was the fault. The constraint becomes her warm-up, scheduled for removal rather than kept forever.

# A joint or pain lever

sub: The lever follows the mechanism, not the movement's name

What it looks like: Low bar for a lower back that hurts from forward slide (shear) is the wrong lever. A goblet box squat for a cranky knee. A hack or pendulum squat where the lower back is what gives out first.

# A second exposure to the pattern

sub: The category repeats, not the exercise

What it looks like: Competition squat on squat day, then a machine squat on the second lower day. That is the second exposure (exposure) to the pattern.

# Building tissue early in a peak

sub: Variations carry the first weeks

What it looks like: Pause squat in weeks 1 to 4 of the block plate. Box squat, then chain squat, as the max-effort rotation in the conjugate plate.

# Capping load in rehabilitation

sub: A variation so hard she cannot go heavy on it

What it looks like: The exercise caps the load rather than her intent.

Hold every squat to its own standard. The Barbell squat page in Quads holds the setup and the faults. The pages that follow hold what each variation changes and the job it does.

Terms on this page

shear: A force that slides one part of a joint sideways across the other. In the lower back it is one vertebra sliding forward on the one below. A forward-leaning squat loads it.

exposure: One session in the week where a lift, a pattern or a muscle is trained.

block plate: One of the three 12-week peaking programs in Peaking for a meet, built as three four-week blocks.

conjugate plate: The 12-week peaking program in Peaking for a meet that rotates a heavy max-effort variation every one to two weeks. It needs bands, chains and boards.
