Tendon injury risk in women

Some tendon problems do run more common in women, but that does not establish a weaker female tendon. Under-fuelling is one important factor that impairs tendon adaptation, and it does so in anyone. Never repeat "women have fragile tendons" as fact.

Neither a plain fact nor a complete myth. Some sites really are female-predominant. Tendon pain at the side of the hip (gluteal tendinopathy) runs at 20.2% adult prevalence, with 1.8 new cases per 1,000 each year. Achilles tendinopathy was historically recorded near 89% male. Recent groups run 14% to 55% female depending on who was counted. The old "male tendon" framing came from who was counted, not from biology.

The actual driver. Not eating enough for what she trains (low energy availability) suppresses two things. The hormone chain that runs her cycle (HPG axis), and the growth factor tendon cells depend on (IGF-1). Suppressed IGF-1 directly impairs collagen synthesis in tendon cells and blunts the response to load. This applies to anyone training in an energy deficit. It is not a female-specific fragility. The under-fuelling pages already own how you notice and route it.

Training status may buffer a low-oestrogen state, though the direct evidence here is about muscle, not tendon.

Postmenopausal trained women showed muscle-damage markers similar to premenopausal women after a lengthening-heavy protocol. That is evidence about muscle recovery, not tendon tissue. It is offered as a reason trained tissue in general may hold up regardless of hormone status, not as tendon-specific proof.

Stress fractures run 1.5 to 3 times higher in female athletes, driven heavily by energy availability rather than sex alone. That is bone rather than tendon, but it is the same pathway and the same referral.

Relaxin. Do not tell her relaxin makes her joints loose or injury-prone, and do not tell her it doesn't. The breastfeeding-and-joints page has the detail.

Say it like this

Some tendons genuinely show up more in women, but it's usually not about your tendons being weaker. It's usually about whether you're fuelling enough for what you're training.

Terms on this page

gluteal tendinopathy: Pain and thickening in the tendons where the glute muscles attach at the side of the hip. It hurts to lie on that side and to climb stairs.

low energy availability: Not enough food coming in to cover both her training and the rest of what her body needs to run.

HPG axis: The hormone chain from the brain to the pituitary to the ovaries that runs her cycle. Under-fuelling turns it down.

IGF-1: Insulin-like growth factor 1. A growth hormone the body needs to build and repair tissue, including tendon.
