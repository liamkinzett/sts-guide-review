Training through pain: the five and the three

Where a client has a diagnosed condition and clearance to train, loading may continue if the pain stays under a threshold and settles. The ceilings are 5 out of 10 for resistance training and 3 out of 10 for impact work.

SituationThreshold
Resistance trainingPain during or immediately after does not exceed 5 out of 10
High-velocity or impact work: hopping, jumping, runningStrictly 3 out of 10 or less
Ceilings for a diagnosed, cleared client

Resistance training | 5 | During or immediately after

High-velocity or impact work | 3 | Hopping, jumping, running

Two rules that matter more than the number.

Pain provoked in a session must return to its pre-session baseline by the following morning. Assess it by asking about morning stiffness. A simple submaximal test also works.

Pain and stiffness must not trend upward week over week. Reduce volume or load in any of three cases. It fails to settle overnight. It does not ease immediately after the session. It climbs across weeks.

You may deviate. But know what you are deviating from

These thresholds come from one condition: long-standing pain in the middle of the Achilles tendon (chronic midportion Achilles tendinopathy). They were set for diagnosed patients, inside a defined protocol, with weekly physiotherapist contact for the first six weeks.

They are not a general pain licence.

A client without a diagnosis and without clearance is not in the population these numbers describe. Her case runs on the buckets.

Terms on this page

chronic midportion Achilles tendinopathy: Long-standing pain and thickening in the middle of the Achilles tendon, a few centimetres above the heel. Not at its attachment to the bone.
