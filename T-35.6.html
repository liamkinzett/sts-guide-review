Pain rules and when to refer · T-35.6

Pain or discomfort: the first question is never how bad

The first question is never how bad it is. It is which of two things it is: pain or discomfort. Every other decision in this topic depends on this one sort.

Compared

columns: Pain | Discomfort

What it is: A negative sensation that is a warning | A negative sensation that is the set working

If she continues: A worse outcome. It resolves into damage | An adaptation. It resolves into growth or strength

Example: A knee that reports a sharp catch at the bottom, however mild | A Bulgarian split squat that starts to burn in the fifth rep

A Bulgarian split squat that starts to burn in the fifth rep is discomfort. It is the stimulus. A knee that reports a sharp catch at the bottom is not, however mild it is.

Intensity does not separate them. Direction does. Discomfort can be brutal. Pain can be a four out of ten. Discomfort resolves into an adaptation. Pain resolves into damage.

Why it matters. Get it wrong towards pain and you detrain a client who needed to push. Get it wrong towards discomfort and you load an injury. It is also the distinction clients cannot make on their own. Both feel bad, and gym culture teaches them to ignore both equally.

This question runs first on a referral question too. "Should I send her to a physio", "her hamstring has been sore for two weeks": sort it before you rate it. A severity number does not do the sorting.

The order on any pain or referral question

? Is it discomfort, the set working, rather than pain, a warning?

yes: The set continues. Nothing else in this topic applies.

yes: Refer regardless of severity. Same day.

= Then, and only then, the duration and intensity triggers. A number is the last input, never the first.

A number is the last input, never the first. An answer that opens with a pain score has skipped the only step the client cannot do for herself.

Ordinary soreness a day or two after a session is a different thing again. That delayed soreness (DOMS) and the burn during the set are not the same thing, and neither is a warning. Fatigue and recovery explains it.

Watch for

The client who has been taught that everything is discomfort. "No pain no gain" is a sorting failure, not a work ethic.

Say it like this

Is this the kind of sore that feels like the set is working, or the kind that feels like a warning? Don't rate it out of ten yet. Just tell me which one it feels like.

Terms on this page

DOMS: Delayed onset muscle soreness. The soreness that arrives a day or two after a session, from the repair of ordinary training damage, and fades on its own.
