Hamstring pain, continued: what to do and your working read

Step 3. Do

Sort every movement into helps, no effect and hurts, and build from what does not hurt. Where the presentation is one we already know, the first lever is written down.

First lever by presentation

# Pain at the top of the hamstring, near the sit bone

sub: Refer. Tendon pain at an attachment is a compression problem

Do this: Suspected tendon pain where compression at the attachment is the aggravator (insertional tendinopathy) is a referral.

# Pain in the belly, mid-thigh

sub: More likely muscular

Do this: The sort. Expect the hinge pattern in the provocative bucket.

# It only appears on deep hip flexion

sub: Reduce range before load

Do this: The lever order.

# It appeared after a volume jump

sub: The intervention is the volume, not the exercise

Do this: Take the history. Put the volume back.

# Low-back pump on an RDL

sub: A hamstring question wearing a back costume

Do this: The RDL's four causes, in order: heel-heavy pressure, hinging through the knee, going past her active hip range, losing the brace. The third is the hamstring one.

# "I feel it in my hamstrings, not my glutes, off the floor"

sub: A position finding, not a tissue one

Do this: She is not screwing her feet into the floor. She is not producing rotation into the ground. Fix the setup.

# A hamstring that feels permanently tight

sub: Do not chase length

Do this: Foam rolling it is misguided. Load it in a position instead. See the working read.

# Provoked by the seated curl but not the lying curl

sub: The hip is the variable, not the knee

Do this: Seated keeps the hip bent and loads the hamstring long. Lying keeps it short. Work the lying version while the seated one provokes.

# Pain at full knee lockout on a seated or preacher-style curl

sub: A setup fault, not a symptom to sort

Do this: Stop short of lockout. A two-joint muscle bunched at both ends (active insufficiency) at lockout raises the risk of a tendon tear. Hamstrings carries this as its one hard caution.

Your working read. Name it, label it a guess

The first fork ends the block for two of the four answers.

Red flags

title: The first fork

Blood where blood should not be: bruising, swelling, heat, after a moment she can name. Suspected tear. Refer the day it happens. Nothing below runs

Painful when the leg is moved for her, not when she moves it: the structure of the joint, not the muscle. Refer. This is not a hamstring plan

A deep dull ache from the buttock down the leg: probably not the hamstring at all. Anything below the knee, or numbness, pins and needles or weakness, refers today

emergency: The two numbers and the red flags are untouched by anything below. Painful when she moves it herself and quiet when it is moved for her is the only answer that continues.

Every level should have an escape rope to the nearest medical professional. Knowing when to sound the alarm is part of being an athlete. Say that to her.

Naming is permitted. Grading is not, and this is the region where that breaks. Hamstring injuries are conventionally graded one, two and three. You never repeat a grade. A grade is a clinical measurement and you are not insured to make one. Calling a tendon reactive rather than degenerative is grading it too.

The label this region gets wrong

High hamstring tendinopathy is the name that gets attached to a hip. One worked case was diagnosed with it and turned out to have a torn hip labrum. That is not an argument for staying silent. It is the reason the words "and it is a guess" are load-bearing here rather than decorative.

How a working read is said

My best guess, and it is a guess, not a diagnosis, is a hamstring strain in the belly of the muscle. A physio confirms it. Meanwhile we rest that muscle, not you, and everything else keeps going.

Terms on this page

insertional tendinopathy: A tendon problem where the tendon attaches to bone. It is made worse when that attachment is compressed, such as at the bottom of a deep stretch.

active insufficiency: A muscle that crosses two joints and is shortened at both is too bunched up to produce much force. Bending the knee does this to the hamstrings in a hip thrust.
