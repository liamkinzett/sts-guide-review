Inheriting a client · T-41b.5

Look backwards before you look forwards. Audit the program she arrives with

The first thing you do with an inherited client is view where she has been. Looking backwards to be able to look forwards is one of the most important parts of choosing a new direction.

Especially when she arrives from another coach, do your due diligence. Read her training history before you write a line.

What the audit is looking for is not errors. It is exposure. One of the foundational moves coaches miss is looking at overexposure of positions. Then you start to sprinkle in exposure to ranges she has not been in. General preparation work falls into this too.

Read what that reframes. The question is not whether her old program was good. It is which positions and ranges she has been living in, because that shows you what she has not been exposed to. A client who has spent a year in a narrow set of positions has a list of ranges waiting. That list is the start of your direction.

A worked read. Her last three blocks are all hip thrust, back squat and leg press, heavy, in the same rep bands. Her hips have been living in a mid-range, loaded position. Nothing has asked for depth, a long lever or a single-leg stance. The audit does not say the program was wrong. It says where she has not been, and that is where the next block can go once the first month of reading is done.

Why it works. A program you did not write can only be judged against what it did. What it did is in the logbook, in the positions she has repeated and the ones she has never met. Before you send a program owns what a complete program contains. This page owns reading one you did not write.
