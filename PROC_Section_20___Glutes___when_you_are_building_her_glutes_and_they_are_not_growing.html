When her glutes are not growing

You are building her glutes and the mirror and the log both say nothing is happening. This page is the Glutes topic's exercise pages in the order you use them. Nothing here is new doctrine.

Look at the program before you look at her.

Each glute movement loads the muscle at a different length. The hinge loads it long (lengthened position). The 45 degree hip extension loads it short (shortened position). Between them they cover both ends of the glute's range. The hinge is the single biggest driver of glute growth. So the first check is structural, and you can do it from the program sheet.

Check

Does her week hold a hinge?

Does anything in her week load the shortened position?

yes: The structure is sound. Move to her video.

no: Fix the structure first. Build around the hinge and pair it with the 45 degree hip extension.

Then ask for video and read it against the fault lists. Every exercise page names the ways its movement quietly stops training the glutes. The RDL that hinges through the knee. The hip extension that never ends. The rep that finishes in the lower back (lumbar finish) on the thrust and the kickback. The split-stance RDL (B-stance) whose back heel drops until it has become a two-legged lift.

Ask where she feels it too. Where she feels it is a clue, not proof. On the split squat a client who cannot feel her hip is too far into her quad.

No single look at her settles a stalled glute. The fault lists are your instrument; work through them.

Read the video

A rep that finishes in the lower back is not loading the glute at the top. That is the shared read behind the lumbar-finish faults on the hip thrust, the hip extension and the kickback. Feeling it in the low back on the hip extension or the kickback is a fault you ask about. You cannot see it on video, so never claim to.

Lower-back pump on the RDL is a clue, not a verdict. Check heel-heavy pressure, hinging through the knee, going past her active hip range, and a lost brace, in that order.

Read the chain from where the fault starts rather than where it shows. A regression has to address where the fault starts. One joint runs out of range, the next one along takes over, and the fault you see is two joints away (compensation cascade). Misreading that chain is a coach fault; the Lunge page works an example.

Do, in this order

Fix the program

# Build around the hinge

sub: The biggest growth driver in this topic

Why: It is the main loadable pattern for the back of her body and it puts load where most women are weak: the long glute.

Do this: Romanian deadlift as the main hinge of the lower-body day.

Exercises: Romanian deadlift

# Pair it with the loaded shortened position

sub: The 45 degree hip extension is STS's default there

Why: The hinge loads the glute long and the hip extension loads it short. Together they cover the muscle's whole range.

Do this: 45 degree hip extension. It is also the hip thrust's own first swap, because it is easier to execute well.

Exercises: 45 degree hip extension; Hip thrust

# Fill the lengthened glute slot with the B-stance RDL

sub: Never as the first exercise of the session

Why: A movement that loads and pauses her in the stretched position is never how a session opens. She has to be warm and primed first.

Do this: Place it after the warm-up and the priming work. See Warming up and Building a session.

Exercises: B-stance RDL

# Fix position with the equipment before abandoning the pattern

sub: The switch is triggered by a fault, not by her level

Why: Two ladders exist and they pull opposite ways. One adds external stability, barbell to Smith machine to shrug machine. One lowers the load ceiling, dumbbell to B-stance dumbbell.

Do this: Read Choosing the equipment on the Romanian deadlift page and pick the ladder that matches her fault.

# Work the fixes and cues in their printed order

sub: On the kickback, change the setup before you reach for a cue

Why: The order on each page is the order that works most often. Skipping to the clever cue wastes sessions.

Do this: Start at the top of the fault list and the top of the cues. See Cueing for the cue ladder.

# Fit the equipment to her

sub: Box height, trap bar fit and stance width are set to the lifter

Why: A box that is too high, a trap bar too wide for her frame or a copied stance width all create faults no cue removes.

Do this: Set the box height on the step-up, check the trap bar against her frame on the RDL, and let her find her own thrust stance.

Exercises: Step-up; Romanian deadlift; Hip thrust

What you do not do: prescribe a movement STS doesn't program. Several regressions are named on these pages so you know why we use something else. The single-leg RDL and the glute-ham raise are the ones you will meet here. Use the swap the page gives.

What changes and what does not

The hinge is a main lift with a high fatigue cost. Where it sits in the session is decided in Building a session; how many sets in Volume. Do not program a reverse lunge beside a Bulgarian split squat: they do the same primary job.

A stalled glute changes exercise selection and execution. It does not change her sets (Volume), her effort (Effort and intensity) or her tempo and pauses (Tempo and pauses). An exercise page never rewrites those numbers.

When to send it up

Whether a glute movement belongs in her program at all is Choosing exercises. Whether the slot should be single-leg is One leg or two. Pain anywhere in this is Pain rules and when to refer. Pregnancy and postpartum programming questions are referred out; see The female body. Where a page names a question as a head coach's call, coach the printed default and send the question up.

Terms on this page

lengthened position: The point in the rep where the working muscle is at its longest while it carries the load. It describes the muscle's length, not whether she feels a stretch.

shortened position: The muscle is working while it is bunched short. For the glute that is full hip extension, standing tall or locked out on a thrust.

B-stance: A split stance where the back foot sits behind on its toe as a kickstand and the front leg does the work.

compensation cascade: One joint runs out of range or strength, so the next joint along takes over. The fault you see is often two joints away from where it started.

lumbar finish: Finishing a hip movement by arching the lower back instead of extending the hip. Her back takes the load meant for her glutes.
