Volume · T-08.9

When she stalls, volume is the fourth thing to check

When her logbook stops moving, check her effort, her technique and her recovery before you touch volume. Volume is fourth. Work this list in order.

Work it in this order

Is she training hard enough? Less experienced lifters consistently believe they are closer to failure than they are. This is the most common answer and the one most often skipped.

Has her technique drifted? Range of motion shortens under load before anything else does. A rep that got easier because it got smaller is not progress.

Is she recovering? Sleep, stress and food. A program is not under-dosed if she is under-slept. Adding sets to that client makes it worse.

Then volume. Only once the first three are clear, and never before food has been cleared in step three.

"Food" is doing more work in step three than it looks. Take a lean client who trains hard, moves well and sleeps. If she stalls, that is a reason to ask about fuelling. No single sign of under-eating (low energy availability) is diagnostic on its own. Together they are a reason to ask. Do not read a stall as a finding.

You are the only person in her life watching a progressive lifting record. Nobody else can see it. Hormonal contraception hides the most visible sign for over half our clients, because a withdrawal bleed is not a period. The logbook may be the only signal there is. The female body covers this in full.

Why volume waits until food is cleared. Risk rises sharply where low energy availability is present. Adding sets to an under-fuelled client is therefore the worst-timed move on this list.

Two causes can look identical and need opposite answers. A stall from under-effort and a stall from under-fuelling look the same on paper. One needs harder sets. The other needs more food, and harder sets would hurt her. What you can see does not tell you what caused it. The first move is working out which cause you are looking at.

Why it works. Three of the four are free. Adding volume costs recovery she may not have. It is the only one of the four that can make the situation worse rather than merely failing to help.

Watch for

The reverse error. Working the list is not a reason never to add volume. If she trains hard, moves well, sleeps, eats and sits at the bottom of her band, she needs more sets. The list has told you so.

A beginner inverts this order. For her, execution comes first. Her effort ratings are the one signal you cannot read yet, so step one cannot be answered.

Terms on this page

low energy availability: Not enough food coming in to cover both her training and the rest of what her body needs to run. Growth stalls, recovery suffers and injury risk climbs.
