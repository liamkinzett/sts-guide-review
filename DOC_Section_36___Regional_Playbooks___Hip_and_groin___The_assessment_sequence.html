Hip: the assessment sequence

Watch the bodyweight squat first. If it is clean, test the joint through all six directions. Then sort.

In this order

Watch the bodyweight squat. How she moves her body before how she moves a barbell. Look for knees clanking together, thighs rotating inward, glutes not firing.

If the squat is clean, test the joint through all six directions. Not the range the squat happens to demand.

Sort every movement into helps, no effect, hurts.

The critique that matters most

Coaches use the range of the exercise as the diagnostic. "I can squat to depth, so I'm fine." The hip extends and rotates inward much further than a squat ever asks. A clean squat rules out very little.

The commonest restriction is rotation, specifically internal rotation. That is the thigh turning inward in the hip socket (internal rotation). People over-mobilise the outward turn (external rotation) with pigeon and figure-four work. They neglect the other direction entirely.

Terms on this page

internal rotation: Turning a limb inward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face back or down, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more toward the middle. The muscles that do it are the internal rotators.

external rotation: Turning a limb outward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face forward or up, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more to the side. A muscle that does it is an external rotator.
