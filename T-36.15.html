How long it takes depends on what is actually wrong

A positional problem can change inside a session. A torn tissue cannot. Resting a position for weeks does not fix it.

The model nearly everyone carries. Pain means tissue damage, and tissue damage takes time to heal. That chain is correct only when there is tissue damage. Where nothing is torn you are dealing with the nervous system, and the nervous system can change instantly. It does not take eight to twelve weeks of stretching to get someone into the overhead position.

Why it matters. It tells you when to expect a result. If you have changed her position and nothing changed, your hypothesis was wrong, and you can find that out today rather than in six weeks. It also stops a coach selling twelve weeks of work that one setup change would have done.

The failure it names. Looking for a structure that is not broken. Clients arrive after a scan that showed nothing, still symptomatic. A diagnosis is just a name for symptoms.

Resting a positional problem can make it worse

The habits that produced the position do not stop when training stops. Sleep posture, desk work and daily loading carry on. Remove the gym and they are the only stimulus acting on her. Doing nothing is never the answer.

This never loosens a referral. Where a red flag or a referral trigger is present, she is referred. This rule governs the middle state, where nothing is flagged and the instinct is to rest anyway.

Say it like this

This looks like a position problem rather than something torn. If I'm right, changing how you set up should change it today, not in six weeks. If it doesn't change, I've guessed wrong and we look again.
