Faults and what you can actually see · T-32.9

Coning or doming down the midline changes the set, and is not a referral on its own

Coning or doming down the midline of her abdomen under load changes the set. On its own it does not send her out.

Verdict

Change the set. Do not refer for doming alone.

Tempo first, then range, then how the movement is done. Abandon the movement only when the graded options have not resolved it.

What it is. The primary visual sign during and after pregnancy (coning). It runs down the midline of the abdomen (linea alba) and appears under load rather than at rest.

The response is graded. Change the tempo. Reduce the range. Change how the movement is done. Abandon the movement only when the graded options have not resolved it.

The concern is not that a rep does damage. It is that she is creating habits you do not want wired in and then have to retrain later.

You still do not assess it. No palpation, no measuring, no grading how many fingers wide anything is. Those are clinical examinations, some of them internal. A coach who suspects a condition refers rather than diagnoses. You observed a visual sign and you change the set. That is the whole of your role here.

Red flags

title: What does send her out, to a pelvic health physiotherapist. A separate list

Any pelvic floor symptom: leaking, heaviness, tightness

SI joint pain, pubic symphysis pain, back pain, sciatica

Postpartum: increased bleeding, or excessive heaviness in the pelvis

Doming is not on that list, and its absence is deliberate. Doming alone means you change the set. If doming arrives with anything on the list, the list decides, and she goes to a pelvic health physiotherapist.

Why it works. Doming under load is common and is not by itself a clinical event. Referring every client who domes would send out a large share of a female roster. It would cost them money and alarm, and teach them that a normal training observation is a medical one. Caution is not free and it is not automatically safe. A referral threshold set too low is its own failure.

Watch for

Screening for this only in clients who have told you they are postpartum. It is a visual tell and it is worth knowing on sight.

Say when you see it

I want to change how that one looks. Same exercise, slower, with the range shortened a little.

Terms on this page

coning: A ridge or dome rising along the midline of the belly when the abdominal wall is loaded. It is a sign the deep wall is not holding. It is seen during and after pregnancy.

linea alba: The line of connective tissue running down the middle of the abdomen between the two halves of the abdominal wall.

SI joint: The sacroiliac joint, where the base of the spine meets the pelvis on each side, near the dimples of the low back.
