Shoulder: the AC joint plane in practice

The most portable standard in the region: never load her hands inside the line of the AC joint. It governs several movements at once.

Applies to: the plate press, the diamond push-up and a bench grip narrower than her shoulders. Also crossover rear delt flies, and any two-handed row that locks both AC joints.

MechanismWhat happens
WristHands inside that plane force the wrist to bend sideways. The named injury is to the cartilage on the little-finger side of the wrist (TFCC)
Cost against benefitFor the rear delt the extra length is not delivered to the target muscle. For the pec, work in the shortened position grows little anyway. And the hand runs into the chest, so the elbow bends less

Move the hands out and the deviation disappears, the wrist normalises, and the shoulder gets more extension behind the body. That allows more elbow flexion, which gives the triceps more length.

The rowing exception. If you want the arms-in path, hands drawn in toward the midline (adducted), row single arm. The shoulder blade can then move around the body instead of locking both AC joints.

A stop-rule for the rear delt fly. When the upper arm comes in line with the AC joint, the rep ends. Do not chase length by letting the blade protract further. The rear delt does not attach to the spine. Everything past that point is rhomboid and mid-trap travel, bought by rolling the acromion over the collarbone.

Why it settles an old argument. "Start novices close" and "close grip is a fault" were never in conflict once the AC joint plane is the line. Close and wide are both defined relative to it.

Terms on this page

TFCC: The triangular fibrocartilage complex, the cartilage and ligaments on the little-finger side of the wrist. Loading it with the wrist bent sideways is what injures it.

adducted: The upper arm held in close to the side of the body. An adducted row finishes with the hands close in.
