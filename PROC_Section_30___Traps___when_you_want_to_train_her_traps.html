Training her traps

You want to train her traps and the only programmed movement is the machine shrug. This page is the Traps topic in the order you use it. Nothing here is new doctrine.

Count before you add

Before you write a shrug into her program

Count her trap exposure. Her traps work in every lateral raise, no matter what, assisting the shoulder blade as it rotates upward. They work in every row that finishes with the shoulder blades pulled back. Her weekly trap work is not confined to what you wrote down as trap work.

Is she in a maximal-strength block? Traps sit with the odds and ends: forearms, calves, rear delts, traps, abs. Those lagging groups are woven into a session, and that weaving is not done during maximal-strength work.

Do you want mid-trap and rhomboid work rather than the upper traps? Then a shrug that lifts the shoulders is the wrong target. That work is a retraction, and it comes from her rows.

Read the mechanism the right way round. The shrug grows the traps by the net force through the muscle, not by the direction it pulls. The work happens in the loaded hang. There the load pulls the shoulder blade down and away from the rib cage, and the muscle lengthens under load. It does not happen at the top of the shrug.

What follows heavy trap loading. The counterpart to heavy trap loading is a lengthened raise, not another compressive press. After heavy farmer's carries, program a lengthened cable lateral raise rather than a military press.

Do: the one mandatory coaching move is a sentence

Say before her first set of shrugs

This is the one movement where lifting your shoulders is the work, not the leak.

On five other movements she is coached out of lifting her shoulders (scapular elevation). Those are the lateral raise, the pulldown, the machine press, the cable row and the front raise. Here it is the movement. Say it out loud, or the cue she has heard on five other movements will make her perform this one badly.

Change. STS programs the machine shrug only. The standing barbell or dumbbell shrug and the prone incline (Kelso) shrug are not programmed. They are different actions, not two ways of doing one movement. The standing version lifts the shoulders diagonally along the line of the trap fibres. The Kelso pulls straight back into retraction. There is no home version, because STS coaches clients who train in equipped gyms.

Escalate. Pain is Pain rules and when to refer. Neck symptoms in any upper-body movement are read first as the shoulder blade not managing force well, not as a neck problem. The one caution near this movement is a deadlift fault: the shoulders shrugging or riding up under a heavy pull. That is a fault on the pull, not on the shrug.

Terms on this page

scapular elevation: The shoulder blades lifting towards the ears. On most upper-body lifts it is a fault; on the shrug it is the movement.
