The phases · T-07.4

Priming is externally stabilised by default

Machines, cables and supported movements carry the early load in priming. That is external stabilisation (externally stabilised). Simple free-weight patterns are fine alongside them: dumbbell curls, lateral raises, dumbbell RDLs.

Leave the complex loadable barbell work alone, unless strength is the goal. If it is, those lifts are the skill being built rather than a reward for later. A beginner on a strength path works from goblet squat to high bar to low bar inside priming itself.

Why it works. External stability lets her learn the pattern without also fighting to stabilise it. You get cleaner footage to assess. She gets a shorter route to competence.

The stronger reason is not convenience. Think about a client who is new, deconditioned or coming back from injury. She is close to the limit of what she can hold steady against gravity.

Under an unstable load her nervous system goes vigilant. It prioritises staying upright over performing the movement, and she recruits a different set of muscles to do it.

Two clients can perform a squat that looks identical on video. One loads her quads. The other braces with her hamstrings to keep from falling forward.

Watch for

An unstable set does not train what the program says it trains. That is the whole argument for external stability. A client who cannot yet hold her own position (internally stabilised) needs a supported machine. Not a lighter free weight.

Terms on this page

externally stabilised: The machine, bench or support holds her position so she does not have to. Leg press, hack squat, chest-supported row, cable work.

internally stabilised: Her own trunk and bracing hold the position under load. Back squat, deadlift, barbell bench, standing overhead press.
