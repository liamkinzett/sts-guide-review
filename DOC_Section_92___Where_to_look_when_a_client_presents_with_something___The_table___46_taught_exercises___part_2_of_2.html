The table: lateral raise to skull crusher

The table continues: the last six of the 46, lateral raise to the skull crusher and JM press.

Every exercise

# Lateral raise

sub: Isolation for the lateral delt and for the horizontal arc of shoulder abduction (abduction)

Where tension peaks: It follows the implement. The two peak at opposite ends.

Check before you prescribe it: Sternum stuck down is a fault here.

Swap to: A chest-supported constraint ladder, which removes the body English the dumbbell version invites

On its page: 4 cues · 6 faults

# Overhead press

sub: The library's vertical press and the front delt's main loaded pattern

Where tension peaks: At the bottom, in the lengthened position.

Check before you prescribe it: Flaring into full abduction jams the humeral head against the acromion.

Swap to: Seated DB overhead press or machine shoulder press. A Swiss bar and a seated barbell rack are also named

On its page: 3 cues · 4 faults

# Rear delt

sub: Isolation for the rear delt, which runs scapula (scapula) to humerus

Where tension peaks: In the middle.

Check before you prescribe it: The pad distinction, stated per entry.

Swap to: The three variations substitute for each other. Prefer a cable fly over the reverse pec deck. A cable lets you match the line of pull

On its page: 4 cues · 3 faults

# Shrug

sub: Traps grow by net force. The work happens in the loaded hang, where the load pulls the scapula down and away from the rib cage. Not at the top of the shrug

Where tension peaks: Not stated.

Check before you prescribe it: No fault list, no pause and no injury tier are written for it yet. Coach the loaded hang and the standard cues.

Swap to: None programmed. The standing shrug and the trap bar shrug are named alternatives, and neither is an STS movement

On its page: 1 cue · none faults

# Overhead cable extension

sub: The only tricep slot that loads the long head at length

Where tension peaks: In the deep stretch at the bottom.

Check before you prescribe it: Extreme overhead shoulder flexion is the risk position.

Swap to: None programmed. The French press is named, across EZ bar, kettlebells and dumbbells, and is not an STS movement

On its page: 3 cues · 7 faults

# Triceps pushdown

sub: Smooth continuous tension, peaking in the mid-to-shortened range of elbow extension

Where tension peaks: At the shortened end

Check before you prescribe it: The cable must line up directly through the elbow joint.

Swap to: Rope to a cable tricep extension (pronated grip), a cable tricep extension (neutral grip) or a single arm cable tricep extension

On its page: 4 cues · 6 faults

# Skull crusher and JM press

sub: Two movements in one entry, split by where they load

Where tension peaks: At the stretched bottom.

Check before you prescribe it: Chronic elbow tendinopathy cannot be seen on video. She has to tell you.

Swap to: EZ bar to dumbbell skull crusher

On its page: 2 cues · 4 faults

Terms on this page

abduction: A limb travelling out and away from the midline of the body: the arm in a lateral raise, or the thigh moving out to the side.

scapula: The shoulder blade. The flat bone on the back of the rib cage that the arm hangs from.

:

pronated: Turning the forearm so the palm faces down or away. Of the foot, rolling inward so the arch flattens and the ankle drops in.
