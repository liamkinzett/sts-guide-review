Pain rules and when to refer · T-35.10

She is afraid of the movement

A client hurt in a position keeps looking for the pain there, long after the injury and long after the rehabilitation. Pain is a learned response, and the position is often fine now.

It is easy to put someone in a position where they can be loaded without pain. It is not easy to remap someone's mindset around a position that has caused them pain.

The tell. She approaches a specific point in a specific movement and something changes: hesitation, a held breath, a shortened range with no mechanical explanation. She may say she is scared of it. She may say she avoids it. She may simply not trust it. She is not guarding tissue. She is expecting an outcome.

This has a name, and it is not a character problem. Avoiding a movement because she expects it to hurt (fear avoidance) is exactly how a nervous system should behave after being hurt.

The job is new evidence, not encouragement.

Terms on this page

fear avoidance: Avoiding or bracing against a movement because she expects it to hurt, after it once did. The nervous system is protecting her from an outcome, not from tissue damage.
