Warming up · T-16.7

No general warm-up, except on test day

She does not need 10 minutes on a bike or a treadmill before an ordinary session. Go to the prep work and then to the ramp.

That bike or treadmill time is a general warm-up. It raises her body temperature and heart rate without touching the movement she is about to do.

Why it works. A general warm-up does not prepare the pattern she is about to load. Ten minutes on a bike before 3 sets at 80% of her max, taken to failure, gave no benefit over walking in cold. Before power work it added nothing that a specific warm-up had not already done. The prep work and the ramp do the preparing, and they do it for the movement she is about to perform.

The exception is test day, and it is not arbitrary. The one place a general warm-up plus a specific one beat the specific one alone was a one rep max (1RM) test. That is test day. Every strength phase ends in one, which The phases covers. So test day keeps a general warm-up and nothing else does.

Watch for

A client who likes her walk. This is a rule about what we program, not a rule about what she is allowed to do. If a walk settles her before training, it is not costing her anything.

Terms on this page

prep work: The movements inside the warm-up slot that get the first loaded movement ready. Chosen for the session in front of you.

ramp: The run of feeder sets that climbs toward the first working set.

1RM: The heaviest weight she can lift once with good form.
