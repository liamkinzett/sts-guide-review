When to use it

The problem you notice. You want to take her biceps to failure and you want that to be safe. Or her form breaks down whenever the weight gets heavy, whatever her proximity to failure. Or the upper-body session has no warm-up and the first exercise has to be one. The shortened-position curl is the finisher, and the high cable curl has a second job at the front of a session.

Reach for it when

# You want her biceps taken to failure, safely

sub: The last slot of the session

Confirm it: The slot is at the end of the session, after the main work, and the plan calls for a set to failure.

What it gets her: Failure work in the one place STS sanctions it for the biceps. Spider curls go in the end-of-session slot because that is where the biceps can be taken to failure safely. A cable curl is stabilised work, so true failure is allowed on it. Effort and intensity has the test. Read it against the preacher rule on Lengthened-position curl. Biceps failure work is late-session work.

# Her form breaks down whenever the weight gets heavy, whatever her proximity to failure

sub: A high relative intensity at a light absolute load

Confirm it: Watch a heavy set and a light set taken equally close to failure. The heavy one swings and the light one does not.

What it gets her: Higher relative intensity with lower weight (relative intensity). The high cable curl trains to failure at a light absolute load (absolute load). This client is the worked example for it.

# The upper-body session has no warm-up and you want the first exercise to be one

sub: Warm-up by exercise

Confirm it: The session opens straight into upper-body work with no prep slot.

What it gets her: The shoulder blade turned up (upward rotation), the shoulder turned outward (external rotation) and a stacked rib cage (stacked rib cage), from the first movement. High cable curl first, paired with a single-arm cable pullover. STS doesn't program the cable pullover, so the curl carries the job on its own. That is a warm-up by exercise, not a biceps-development slot. Do not count it as both.

# She reports neck or upper-trap symptoms

sub: Buy a second adaptation inside the arm slot

Confirm it: She reports the neck or the upper traps, and an arm slot is already in the session.

What it gets her: Secondary shoulder work bought inside the curl. Swap a generic curl for a high cable curl, or a machine preacher with a low reach, to get some secondary shoulder work going on.

# The day is already full of overhead pulling and shoulder extension

sub: The other half of the arc rather than more of the same

Confirm it: Read the session. Pulldowns, pull-ups and rows already fill it.

What it gets her: Finish with a high cable curl. It uses the biceps at the shoulder as well, so across the week she gets the whole arc of shoulder flexion and shoulder extension.

Reach for this page for the reasons above, not as a mass builder

Ranked plainly, these are not the top of the list for growth. They are not bad for hypertrophy either. Mid-range and lengthened work are the main course. This group is the finisher.

Why the slot exists at all. Lengthened-range loading drives higher fatigue and higher injury risk than shortened work. That makes this the safer place for the sets taken close to failure.

Setup. Elbows pinned by a pad, a thigh or a locked shoulder, load light, blades set. Setup has the full list.

Terms on this page

relative intensity: How close to failure a set is taken, whatever the weight on the bar or the stack.

absolute load: The actual weight on the bar or the stack, whatever it feels like to her.

upward rotation: The shoulder blade turning so its outer corner rises and the socket tilts upward. It is what lets the arm travel overhead. A bench press holds it in the opposite position.

stacked rib cage: The rib cage sitting directly over the pelvis, ribs down, neither flared nor slumped.

external rotation: Turning a limb outward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face forward or up, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more to the side.
