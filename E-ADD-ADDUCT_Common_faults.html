Common faults

Seven common faults

# Load past the position she owns

sub: Reduced range, visible momentum, pads snapping rather than squeezing

Why it happens: The weight is too heavy for the position, so she throws the pads together and lets them fly back.

Do this: Reduce the load. The test is whether she can bring the pads together and control the return. If either half fails, it is too heavy.

# Base too wide or too long

sub: Legs set far outside a comfortable stretch before rep one

Why it happens: She, or the coach, treats the start width as a stretch to be won. A wide start overstretches the groin.

Do this: Set the start to a light stretch.

# Rib flare

sub: Lower back arched, hips tipped forward, back off the pad

Why it happens: The ribs were never brought down before the set. The arch tips the pelvis forward and puts the adductors under too much stretch.

Do this: Ribs to hips. If the pelvis will not change seated, change the torso angle.

# Body English

sub: Trunk swaying into the rep, one side leading

Why it happens: The load is too heavy, so the trunk joins in to move it.

Do this: Hands lightly on the handles, trunk still, load down.

# Momentum at the turnaround

sub: An audible clash as the pads meet

Why it happens: She drives the pads in fast instead of squeezing them together.

Do this: Control the entry into the squeeze.

# Tension dumped at an end range

sub: The stack touches down at the bottom

Why it happens: She lets the pads all the way out, so the weight rests and the adductors switch off.

Do this: Stop the lowering half before the stack bottoms out.

# Trunk sag on a braced hold

sub: Copenhagen hold. The trunk bends sideways and the hips sag toward the floor

Why it happens: The obliques and adductors give out before the set ends, and the hips drop to make it easier.

Do this: No sideways sag at any point. This is the single named fault for the Copenhagen hold.
