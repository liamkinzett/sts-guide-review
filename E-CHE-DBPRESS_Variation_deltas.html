Variations

STS programs the dumbbell bench press three ways: flat, 30 degree incline and 45 degree incline. Bench angle is a line-of-force variable, not a cosmetic one.

The three angles

# Flat DB bench press

sub: Biases the middle and lower chest

Why: The flat arm path loads the sternal and costal fibres.

# 30 degree incline DB bench press

sub: Biases the upper chest

Why: A low incline loads the clavicular fibres.

# 45 degree incline DB bench press

sub: The next rung up, not a different muscle

Why: No line is drawn between 30 and 45 for chest bias. The 45 is the rung after the 30 in the overhead progression on What it's for. Record the angle numerically so the exercise is repeatable. That is the instruction doing the work.

Getting a heavy dumbbell into position. Free dumbbells give her no external bracing. Before the pecs are ever loaded, her arm flexors and front delts manage a lot of twisting load (torque). They do it with nothing bracing her.

For an advanced lifter the change comes at about 120 lb (54.4 kg). There, getting the weights into position stops being simple positioning and becomes an athletic movement in and of itself. It is closer to a single-arm curl and hoist.

You will also hear 70 kg quoted. That is an elite powerlifter's figure, not a general one.

Judge the pickup by what you see

If getting into position is visibly harder than the set that follows, the pickup is its own movement. Coach it. Set the shoulder down and engaged before the weight moves, never after. Engage through the palm, drive through the pec, and shove the shoulder down so it is locked into a stable position. Then move the weight.

There is no set choreography for the pickup. Do not treat position as a non-event. Watch it.

Neutral grip against palms-forward: different exercises, not a hand position. Three mechanisms line up.

  • The shoulder space. A palms-forward grip tends to flare the elbows to 90 degrees. That closes the space under the top of the shoulder and can pinch the top cuff tendon (supraspinatus). Tucking toward neutral brings the lower traps and lats in to pull the ball of the shoulder down and open that space. Neutral-grip pressing across angles is the standard complementary movement for shoulder health.
  • The range. Wide palms-forward pressing caps the range at roughly 90 degrees. A tucked neutral grip lets the upper arm come closer to the torso. That extends the active range into the stretched position, by roughly 30 to 40 degrees judged by eye.
  • The rotation. Full palms-forward on a rigid bar turns the upper arm inward (internal rotation). That can force the small front-of-shoulder muscle (pec minor) to help. A neutral or palms-up grip turns the upper arm outward (external rotation). That puts the upper-back and rotator-cuff stabilisers back into the pattern.

    • The exception. Wide palms-forward barbell pressing is the most efficient position for powerlifting performance: the shortest range and the most load moved. The case for a neutral grip is a growth and joint-health case, not a strength-sport one. Do not sell it as a universal upgrade.

    Terms on this page

    supraspinatus: The rotator cuff muscle that runs over the top of the shoulder. Its tendon is the one pinched when the elbows flare to shoulder height.

    pec minor: The small chest muscle under the pec major, running from the ribs to the shoulder blade, that pulls the blade forward and down. It tips the shoulder blade forward when it takes over.

    torque: The turning force on a joint. The further the load sits from the joint, the more of it there is.

    internal rotation: Turning a limb inward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face back or down, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more toward the middle. An arm held that way is internally rotated.

    external rotation: Turning a limb outward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face forward or up, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more to the side. An arm held that way is externally rotated.
