ACL injury risk and sex differences

Never tell a client she is more likely to tear the main ligament in her knee because she is a woman. You would be stating a number you do not have.

That ligament is the anterior cruciate ligament (ACL). It is the one torn in the classic non-contact knee injury.

Why. What is known here does not carry a comparative injury rate between women and men: no relative risk, no rate per 1000 athlete-exposures. A gap like that means the number is missing, not that no such difference exists. Saying she is "more at risk" because she is female states a number you do not have.

The same goes for a cycle phase. A claimed injury spike in a specific phase holds up no better than the strength claim. It does not justify changing her program around it either. Programming around the cycle already covers why. Respect symptoms and history. Do not use a phase or her sex as a stand-in for a risk figure you do not have.

What you can say is about mechanism. In loaded back squats, male lifters show smaller hip and knee flexion with more ankle bend (dorsiflexion). Female lifters show less forward knee travel and compensate with more trunk lean. That describes how the movement is organised, not how often it fails. It proves no difference in injury rate.

Hip strength alone does not explain knee cave. Knees falling inwards (knee valgus) are only weakly linked to hip external-rotation strength in symptom-free women. Strengthening the hip abductors does not reliably prevent valgus collapse. What drives valgus is more than hip strength.

A strength target exists for lower-body injury generally. It is not ACL-specific. Squatting more than 2.2 times bodyweight for men, or 1.6 times for women, goes with lower lower-limb injury risk in collegiate athletes. Read it as a general strength target, not an ACL-specific claim.

Watch for

Repeating hormonal or anatomical explanations as fact. Oestrogen, relaxin, notch width and tibial slope are all offered as reasons. Do not repeat them to her as established.

Say it like this

Training builds the strength and control that protect a joint. I'm not going to turn being a woman into a risk prediction for your knee. Population findings don't tell me your individual risk, so we train what we can actually influence.

Terms on this page

ACL: The anterior cruciate ligament, the main ligament inside the knee that stops the shin bone sliding forward on the thigh bone. It is the one torn in a classic non-contact knee injury.

dorsiflexion: The ankle bending so the shin and the top of the foot move closer together, as when the toes pull up toward the shin or the shin travels forward over the foot. It is what lets the knee travel forward in a squat.

knee valgus: Her knees fall inwards, inside the line of her toes, as she squats or stands up.
