Frequency is three numbers, not one

The strength system sets frequency at three separate levels: the exact lift, its movement pattern, and the muscle. This is the single most useful idea in the system, and the one that transfers furthest outside powerlifting.

How often a lift is trained

The muscle | As often as her weekly sets require

The movement pattern | Twice a week for lower body, by any implement

The exact competition lift | Once a week

The three tiers

# Competition variation

sub: Once a week

What it is: The exact lift, for example the low-bar competition squat (competition variation).

# Movement pattern

sub: Twice a week for lower body, more where recovery allows

What it is: The pattern, by any implement (movement pattern). Lower body runs twice a week. Pushing runs twice, and up to five times where her recovery allows.

# Muscle

sub: As volume requires

What it is: Muscle-building accessory work. There is no skill in it and nothing for the nervous system to learn. Frequency is set by how many sets she needs.

How that looks in a week, for a competitive powerlifter. Each lower day pairs one competition lift with the other pattern's second exposure.

The week

# 1 · Squat day

Competition squat | Competition variation | The exact lift, once a week | Two to three sets a week

RDL, stiff-leg deadlift or good morning | Second hinge exposure | The hinge pattern by another implement, so the pattern is trained twice

# 2 · Deadlift day

Competition deadlift | Competition variation | The exact lift, once a week | Two to three sets a week

Hack squat, leg press or belt squat | Second squat exposure | The squat pattern by a machine, so the pattern is trained twice

The push pattern runs the same way. Competition bench on day one. For a larger lifter, day two is an overhead press and then a chest press machine.

The category is what repeats, not the exercise. Put simply, the deadlift exposure is not necessarily a deadlift (exposure).

Terms on this page

competition variation: The exact lift as it is performed on the platform, with her competition stance, grip and bar position.

movement pattern: The family a lift belongs to, whatever the implement. Squat, hinge, push, pull, lunge.

exposure: One session in the week where a lift, a pattern or a muscle is trained. Two exposures means it is trained on two different days.
