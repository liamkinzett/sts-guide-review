Assessing a new client · T-06.5

Read body composition in bands. It never triggers a recommendation

The body composition read happens at the end of priming, not at intake. It never triggers a recommendation.

By the end of priming her first check-in photos have arrived and you have a full block behind her. That is the first point at which you can actually read her body composition rather than guess at it.

There is no threshold. You do not recommend a cut off the back of this. You read her composition to interpret her own goal. And to answer her if she asks. No body fat number reopens the cut conversation later. This rule refuses to let one open it in the first place.

How you arrive at the number

Match her check-in photos against a body fat reference chart. Front, side and back, compared against images of women at known percentages. Take the closest match.

Use the Henselmans visual guide. Almost every body fat chart online is somebody's guess, recycled from another site. That one is different. Every photo in it was taken within a few days of a laboratory measurement. Each image is labelled with the figure and the method used to get it, most commonly a bone-density scan (DXA). Weight the DXA-labelled images most heavily. It runs from roughly 14% to 44% and has front and back compilations at the end.

Work in bands, not figures. "Somewhere around 20 to 24" is the right kind of answer. If she sits close enough to a line that you cannot call it, a second coach's read is worth having. Nothing turns on it either way, which is the point.

Expect real error, and expect it worst where we work. A photo method can be unbiased across a whole group. It can still be about five percentage points out on any single woman.

The error is largest at the extremes of the range. For our clients that means the lean end, which is exactly where most of our roster sits. And a coach eyeballing a photo will not beat software at it.

Treat five points as a floor on your own error rather than a ceiling. That is precisely why you work in bands.

Why a rough method is still the right one. Skinfolds need a trained hand and a client willing to be measured. A DXA scan costs money and means a trip she did not sign up for. Home scales and handheld impedance devices are inaccurate enough to be worse than nothing. They hand you a confident number that is wrong. A visual estimate at least knows it is an estimate.

The number never leaves your side of the conversation. It is a coaching input, not a result. You do not give her a percentage. It carries a precision the method cannot support, and she will hold onto the figure long after you have revised it.

If she asks why a cut might come first. The reasoning below is for answering her when she asks. It is not a recommendation script. Dieting down first means she spends the whole journey in a body composition she is comfortable living in. The cut only gets longer the further it is pushed out.

There is a physiological argument as well. Starting a build leaner means more of the extra food goes toward muscle and less toward fat. The body partitions a surplus better at lower body fat. Treat that as a supporting reason rather than the deciding one. The practical difference between starting at 22% and 28% is probably modest next to training quality. It also reverses at the bottom end, where being too lean costs her recovery and hormonal health.

How we coach the decision. Elsewhere, common practice is to assign the phase that fits the body in front of you. The coach's read is the deciding input there. We lay out the options, the pros and cons of each, and our recommendation when she has asked for one. Then she chooses. The read is an input to her decision rather than a substitute for it.

Why it works. People do not need decisions made for them. When a client cannot decide, it is almost never because she is incapable. It is because she does not have enough information yet. That is a problem we can actually fix.

We will not be coaching her for the rest of her life. At some point she is on her own, and what she takes with her matters more than any single block we wrote. A client who has been handed decisions for two years leaves knowing how to follow instructions. A client who has been shown the options and the trade-offs every time leaves knowing how to think.

So we teach the decision rather than making it. She ends up more invested, she takes pride in a path she chose, and she gets better at weighing outcomes for herself. A recommendation she agrees with is a plan she follows. A recommendation she merely complies with is one she stops following the moment it gets hard.

Terms on this page

DXA: A dual-energy X-ray scan that measures bone, fat and lean tissue. It is the laboratory reference most body fat figures are checked against.
