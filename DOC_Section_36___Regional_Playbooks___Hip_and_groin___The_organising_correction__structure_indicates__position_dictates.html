Hip: structure indicates function, position dictates it

Structure only indicates what a hip can do. Position dictates it. A hip you cannot change the structure of still has a position you can change.

Why it matters. Bone shape has become the standard excuse. A true anatomical limit is much rarer than believed. Soft tissue, imbalance and poor position explain most of what gets blamed on bone. The worked case is a lifter with exactly the hip geometry that supposedly cannot squat deep and narrow. After soft-tissue and stability work, she was sitting to the floor with her heels together.

How this sits with "structure changes what good looks like". Both are true. Anthropometric variation is real, and it does change what a good squat looks like for her. Most people are in the middle of the bell curve, and the middle has more position available than it is using. The question to ask before you accept a structural limit: have you tried trying?

Say it like this

Your hips are built the way they're built, and that's fine. Most of what we're going to get comes from position, not from bone. Let's see how much position you've actually got before we decide anything.
