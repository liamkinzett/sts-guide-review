Pregnancy discovered during competition prep

When a client in competition prep discovers she is pregnant, you keep training her under the pregnancy model. Whether she competes is hers to decide with her obstetric team.

Why you do not prep her. The pregnancy model is a coached, symptom-led pregnancy. Its anchor is 2 to 3 RIR, reps in reserve (RIR), and at that anchor the load is an output, not a target. A competition prep calls for staged dehydration, an extended late-prep deficit or peaking a competition-day appearance. STS runs none of those for a pregnant client.

So the question is not "can she still compete". It is "does anything license prepping her for it", and the answer is no. Not because competing is forbidden. The methods a physique or strength prep normally calls for sit outside anything STS runs for a pregnant client.

What you do. Keep training her under the existing pregnancy model. Whether to compete, withdraw or defer is hers to decide with her obstetric team. That is the same way any other complicated-pregnancy decision routes: you take the complication from the client rather than assessing it yourself. No return-to-performance date is given either way.

Verdict

Whether she competes is her decision with her obstetric team.

You keep training her under the pregnancy model and give no date either way.

Say it like this

I can keep training you through this the way I train every pregnant client. What I can't do is build you toward stage condition on top of it. None of what that takes is something I run in pregnancy. Whether you compete is a conversation for you and your obstetric team, not something I'm going to talk you into or out of.

Terms on this page

RIR: Reps in reserve. How many more reps she could have done when the set stopped. 2 RIR means she stopped two reps short of failure.
