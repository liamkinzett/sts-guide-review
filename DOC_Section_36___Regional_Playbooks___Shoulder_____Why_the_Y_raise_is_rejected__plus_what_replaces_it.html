Why we do not use the Y raise for the overhead position

We do not use the Y raise to build the overhead position. If she can already get into the Y raise position, the lower trap was never the limitation.

The critique. Trying to improve the overhead position by putting the arm over the head is the wrong way round. A light plate raised overhead can either resist a muscle or shift her centre of mass. In the Y raise it does neither.

What replaces it

# A dip variation

sub: Loads the lower trap's actual job

Why it happens: The lower trap stops the shoulder blades riding up toward the ears. At the start of a dip the arms are at the sides, a range nobody lacks, and the whole job is resisting that ride. That is the lower trap's function, loaded.

# Single arm overhead lat pulldown

sub: Lets the load pull her into the position

Why it happens: Lowering strength always exceeds lifting strength. A Y raise asks her to reach overhead under her own power, with many chances to go wrong. A pulldown loads her into the blade's overhead turn (upward rotation) on the lowering half (eccentric), and the load assists her into the position. A neutral grip adds a degree of outward turn at the shoulder (external rotation).

# Chest supported rear delt or mid back row

sub: Moves the blade around the rib cage

Why it happens: Trains the give-and-take between the rhomboids and the serratus, so the shoulder blade learns to travel around the rib cage rather than off it.

Terms on this page

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.

upward rotation: The shoulder blade turning so its outer corner rises and the socket tilts upward. It is what lets the arm travel overhead. A muscle that does it is an upward rotator.

external rotation: Turning a limb outward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face forward or up, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more to the side. A muscle that does it is an external rotator.
