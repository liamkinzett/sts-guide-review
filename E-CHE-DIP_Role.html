What it's for

The dip is the press where her hands stay fixed and her body moves (closed chain), into the arm travelling behind the body (shoulder hyperextension). It is the only chest movement STS programs that takes the upper arm behind the torso under load. That joint position is what it is for. It is also why STS treats it as a shoulder-function movement as much as a chest and triceps one.

The resistance is hardest at the bottom. It loads the stretched position hard. The depth at which that happens is not the same for every client.

Two mechanisms, both carried.

  • It trains the lat in its fully shortened range, with the upper arm turned inward (internally rotated). That lat function stabilises the ball-and-socket of the shoulder and protects the front of the shoulder from pinching.
  • It is a low-trap exercise. With the arms behind the body the shoulder blade wants to rise. The dip trains her to pull it down actively through shoulder extension.

    • Its slot. An upper-body standard and an assessment. The dip and the pull-up together test strength and stability at end range, core organisation, body awareness and relative strength. It is loadable, contrary to common practice. The named methods are a kettlebell hung from a belt and a cable-loaded dip.

    One exercise, two listings. The dip appears under chest and under triceps. It is one movement and one page.

    Terms on this page

    closed chain: The hands or feet stay fixed on a surface and the body moves, as in a push-up or a squat. In an open-chain movement the limb moves and the body stays put, as in a bench press.

    shoulder hyperextension: The upper arm travelling behind the line of the body. On a dip it happens at the bottom of the rep.

    internally rotated: The upper arm turned inward in the socket, so the palm faces back or down.
