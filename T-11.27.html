Keep a hard lower day clear of her sport night

A regular lower-body sport night, netball for example, is read like a connected training session. Keep a hard lower day away from it in both directions. It is not in the program, but it loads the same tissue a hard lower day does and it draws on the same recovery.

Why both sides. A hard lower day before a match sends her in already fatigued in the pattern the match loads hardest. A hard lower day the day after asks her legs to produce a quality session on top of what the match cost them. Both are crossover fatigue (crossover fatigue), arriving from a session outside the program rather than one inside it.

What to actually do. Protect the priority work, trained fresh, the way you would for any changed or crowded week. Sometimes a lower day and a match night cannot be spaced apart. Then the upper day or a rest day is what moves next to the match. Never the lower day. Steps and cardio owns how her outside activity is read and counted. This page only places the lower day around it.

Watch for

Treating a standing commitment like netball as unpredictable, the way a shift change is. It is not. It is a fixed, recurring weekly load. Build it into the split from the start rather than working around it session to session.

Terms on this page

crossover fatigue: Fatigue that shows up in a session other than the one that caused it. Heavy hinging on a pull day limiting her bracing on a later hinge session is an example.
