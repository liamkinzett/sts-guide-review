Setting the dose

# Baseline

sub: 2 to 3 sessions a week, 20 to 30 minutes each

Do this: Start here for every client who gets cardio.

# The cardio and the block share muscles

sub: Cycling in a lower-body block

Do this: Stay at the baseline.

# They do not share muscles

sub: Cycling through an upper-body emphasis

Do this: 4 to 5 sessions is available. This follows from the cost being local.

# The goal demands more

sub: A photoshoot eight weeks out, a sport

Do this: Go past it knowingly. Use every lever: modality, placement, and sequence and separation.

Where the baseline comes from. Muscle, strength and power all fall as endurance sessions get longer and as they get more frequent. Sessions beyond 20 to 30 minutes and frequencies beyond three days a week are where adaptation is compromised. We use the lower end of the range for the session length. The cost of being ten minutes conservative is small.

This is not a wall. Some goals need more cardio than is ideal for muscle. A client eight weeks out from a photoshoot may end up doing it daily. That is a real trade. Sometimes it is the right one.

What changes past the baseline is not permission. It is precision. Every lever in this topic gets more valuable the further past it you go.

  • Modality. Cycling over running matters more at six sessions than at two.
  • Placement. If the cost has to be paid, pay it in the muscles the block is not trying to build.
  • Sequence and separation. Different days. Failing that, hours apart.
    • Say it like this

    This is more cardio than is ideal for holding your muscle. It's the right call for what you want. Here's what I've changed so it costs you as little as possible.

    When you may deviate downward. Anything costing her sleep or her sessions is too much, regardless of where it lands or what the goal is.
