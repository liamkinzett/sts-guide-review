Cueing · T-34.11

The cue ladder: seven rungs, and past two cues you stop talking

When the first cue does not land, climb the ladder. Do not add more words. Past two cues, stop cueing and change the constraint or the exercise.

The cue ladder

One cue, largest downstream effect, then re-test. Works remotely.

A second cue, generally not verbal. Tactile or visual. Rarely available remotely. In person, ask before you touch her.

Re-phrase into her vocabulary. Same intent, her words. Works remotely.

Over-cue out of the bias. Name a position past the target. Works remotely.

Read the failure as a diagnosis. What did the failed cue rule out: coordination, capacity, setup or selection? Works remotely.

Constrain the movement instead of cueing it. Works remotely, because you choose the constraint when you write the program.

Change the exercise. Works remotely. Choosing exercises takes over from here.

The stop rule, three ways. Hold all three. A coach holding only one of them stops too late.

  • Past two cues, stop.
  • A long cue list means the wrong exercise.
  • If the failure is capacity rather than awareness, no cue will ever fix it.

    • Rung 2 is the one that barely survives remote coaching. Mirrors, demonstrations, hands on her shoulder blades, wedges under heels: all of them assume the coach is in the room. We coach remotely, and what stands in for rung 2 is the video she sends and the written word. A short note in her program, a demonstration video between sessions, and a filmed set to check it landed.

    A cue is not a change until it is subconscious. What she does when she is thinking about it is not yet hers. The job between sessions is repetition until she does it without the reminder.

    Watch for

    Climbing the ladder by adding words. Rungs 3 and 4 change the words. Rungs 6 and 7 remove the need for them. Nothing on the ladder is "say more".
