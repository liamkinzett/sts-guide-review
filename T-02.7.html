Strength and skill · T-02.7

Are getting stronger and getting bigger the same adaptation?

Muscle size and maximal strength are related, but not identical. A larger muscle can increase force-producing potential. But maximal strength also depends heavily on neural drive, coordination, technique and skill in the specific lift. Muscle size contributes to the capacity to produce force; strength is the expression of that capacity in a particular task. A strength stall does not automatically mean hypertrophy has stopped, and a size gain does not automatically prove a strength program is working.

Muscle cross-sectional area sets a structural ceiling on that capacity. More contractile material side by side (sarcomeres in parallel) means more possible force. But maximal strength itself is also strongly shaped by the nervous system, coordination and skill at the specific load and lift.

How they come apart. Someone can build muscle without a large change in a specific lift for a few reasons:

  • the training is not highly specific to that lift;
  • the movement skill is not practised often;
  • or the test itself is not sensitive to the change yet.

    • Someone can also improve a lift significantly through skill and nervous-system adaptation with very little visible change in size.

    Going closer to failure reliably builds more size. It has no reliable effect on strength. In one five-week block, lifters who trained close to failure and lifters who stopped well short gained the same strength. Only the close-to-failure group added any extra size, and only a little. Same strength outcome. Different size outcome. Same block.

    Why it matters. A coach who assumes bigger means stronger is reading one adaptation off the other. So is a coach who reads a strength plateau as growth having stopped. Ask separately whether performance is improving and whether the body is changing. Do not assume one answer gives you the other.

    Watch for

    Treating a strength stall and a size stall as the same problem. Or reading a size gain as proof a strength program is working. Progression diagnoses a stall without assuming either predicts the other.

    Say it like this

    Getting stronger and getting bigger are related. They're not the same dial. You can move one without much of the other.

    Terms on this page

    sarcomeres in parallel: More contracting units packed side by side across a muscle fibre, which is what makes it thicker and gives it more possible force.
