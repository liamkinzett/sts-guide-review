When to stop training in pregnancy

Some signs end the session and go straight to her obstetrician, GP or midwife. Learn the list before you coach a pregnant client.

Red flags

title: Stop the session and contact the obstetrician, GP or midwife

Vaginal bleeding or fluid leakage

Breathlessness before exertion

Dizziness or severe headache

Chest pain

Muscle weakness affecting balance

Calf pain or swelling

Signs of preterm labour

Decreased fetal movement

Postpartum: bleeding (lochia) turning heavier or redder, or restarting

Pain at a caesarean scar or stitches

Blood pressure reaches you two ways. Either she reports it from an appointment, or she shows symptoms and it is confirmed with a monitor.

High blood pressure

Test. Headaches, visual disturbance, swelling. Confirm with a blood pressure monitor.

Read. You are not diagnosing. You are deciding whether she keeps training today.

Do. Stop the session. If symptoms persist after the workout, refer.

Change. Nothing else comes out of the program while this is resolved.

Escalate. Straight to her midwife.

Verdict

Pelvic floor symptoms: stop the session, refer to a pelvic health physio, keep training everything else.

Leaking, heaviness, dragging or pain with intercourse.

Never say leaking is normal after a baby

It is common and it is a treatable dysfunction. Calling it normal stops her seeking care.

Some conditions rule training out altogether. These are absolute contraindications (absolute contraindication). With any of these, training stops entirely and her care is obstetric.

Red flags

title: Training stops entirely and care is obstetric

Ruptured membranes

Premature labour

Unexplained persistent bleeding

Placenta lying over the cervix (placenta previa), from 26 weeks. You will also see 28 weeks quoted. Use the earlier week.

Pre-eclampsia (pre-eclampsia) or pregnancy-induced hypertension

Incompetent cervix

Growth restriction

Triplets or more (high-order multiples)

Uncontrolled diabetes, hypertension or thyroid disease

Heart disease that significantly affects her circulation

Restrictive lung disease

A complicated pregnancy that has been cleared to train. You need to know what the complication is. Take it from the client, then adjust what she avoids. The model itself does not change.

Terms on this page

lochia: The normal bleeding and discharge after birth. It should get lighter and paler over the weeks, never heavier or redder.

absolute contraindication: A condition that rules training out altogether while it is present. Her care is medical, not a training decision.

placenta previa: The placenta sits low and covers some or all of the cervix. It can bleed heavily.

pre-eclampsia: A pregnancy condition of high blood pressure with protein in the urine. Headaches, visual disturbance and swelling are its warning signs.

high-order multiples: A pregnancy with three or more babies.
