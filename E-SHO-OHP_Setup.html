Setup

Set her up in this order

Rib cage stacked over the pelvis. This is the base position for every upper-body movement. Set it before the shoulder blade and before the arm path.

Hands relatively close. The narrow grip forces the elbows into a tucked position, roughly 30 to 40 degrees out from the body (abduction). They sit in front of her rather than flared to the sides.

Elbow directly under the hand before the first press. Where the wrist complains, a thumbless grip low in the palm is the answer (false grip).

Do not clean or kick heavy dumbbells into position. The kick-up is an athletic feat of its own and it burns a large amount of energy before the working set starts. The answers are a seated barbell press from the rack or a multi-grip bar (Swiss bar). See Swaps.

Barbell: press from the rack. Bench inside the rack, pins set to lightly touch the bar at her true bottom range. Hips slid a little forward on the seat so the shoulder path is not blocked.

Why the grip does the cueing for you. A narrow grip sets the elbow tuck mechanically. Cues explains why that beats relying on the cue alone.

Terms on this page

abduction: A limb travelling out and away from the midline of the body: the arm in a lateral raise, or the thigh moving out to the side.

false grip: A thumbless grip with the bar sitting low across the palm, so the wrist stays stacked under the bar.

Swiss bar: A multi-grip bar with parallel neutral handles set inside a rectangular frame.
