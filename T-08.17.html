Late reps matter most. The first set still pays best

Within a set, the last reps do the most for growth. Across a session, the first set gives the most growth for its fatigue.

Coaches often tell a client the last few reps are where growth happens. Then they extend it to sets: "set three is where the real work starts". The first half is right. The second half is not.

Within a set, the later reps are the best stimulus. Early reps sit at low tension, especially under a lighter load. The small, fatigue-resistant fibres (low-threshold fibres) do that work. Only fatigue drags the big, fast-growing units (high-threshold motor units) in. That is why a set stopped far from failure has done less than its rep count suggests.

Across sets, two things are true at once.

  • Fatigue carried from an earlier set brings the high-threshold units in earlier in the next one. A later set therefore packs more tension-bearing reps into the same rep count. Short-rest cluster methods (myo-reps) and rest-pause (rest-pause) are built on exactly this, and they grow muscle at least as well as straight sets.
  • Set one gives the most growth for the least fatigue (stimulus-to-fatigue ratio) of any set in the exercise. Each set after it adds real but shrinking stimulus while its fatigue cost keeps climbing. More sets still means more total stimulus across a session. It does not mean set four is a better stimulus than set one.

    • What this means for what you say to her. Do not tell a client set three or four is "where the real growth happens". Later sets add real stimulus at a fatigue cost that grows faster than the benefit. The six to eight per-session default already assumes this. Capping sets is the practical response, not a compromise.

    Do not put a percentage on it. Nobody can tell her how much more a late set gives on her squat. Use the idea. Do not quote her a figure.

    Say it like this

    The last couple of reps in each set are the ones doing the most, so don't cut them short. But your first set isn't a warm-up for the real ones. It's the best-value set you'll do today.

    Terms on this page

    low-threshold fibres: The smaller, fatigue-resistant muscle fibres that fire first at low tension. They do the early reps of a set and grow little.

    high-threshold motor units: The large motor units that drive the biggest, fastest fibres. They are recruited only when tension or fatigue is high, and they are where most growth happens.

    myo-reps: One hard set taken near failure, then several mini-sets of a few reps with very short rests, so she stays near failure with the big motor units already working.

    rest-pause: One hard set broken into pieces by very short rests, so more reps are done near failure with a given load.

    stimulus-to-fatigue ratio: How much growth signal a set or exercise gives for the fatigue it costs. It is a way of thinking about an exercise's price, not a number anyone measures.
