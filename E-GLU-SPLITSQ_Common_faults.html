Common faults

Four common faults

# Pelvis escaping to the back leg

sub: The pelvis pushes away toward the back leg

Why it happens: She avoids the side-to-side hip work (frontal plane) by leaning her pelvis onto the rear leg. The frontal plane is never accessed, so the exercise isn't doing its job.

Do this: Narrow the stance to femur-head width. Load the dumbbell in the opposite hand (contralateral load).

# Base too wide or too long

sub: The stance is so long it becomes a lunge

Why it happens: A wide or long base is the compensation for a hip that can't move sideways. Or the stance length was set by eye.

Do this: Shorten it. Re-find the stance length with the reverse-lunge procedure on the Setup page.

# Knee travelling inside the foot

sub: The front knee drifts in

Why it happens: The standard is a knee tracking over the middle to the inside of the foot, so some inward tracking is correct. Past that, the hip has stopped steering the knee.

Do this: Cue "big toe, knee, sternum in line" and watch whether it changes. Judge it against the standard, not against a knee pointing straight ahead.

# Body English

sub: The torso supplies what the hip should

Why it happens: The load or the pace has outrun her hip control, so the torso swings to make up the difference.

Do this: Slow it down.

Terms on this page

frontal plane: Side-to-side movement, like a lateral lunge or a side raise.

contralateral load: The weight is held in the hand opposite the working leg.
