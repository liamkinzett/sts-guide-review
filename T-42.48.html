A skipped deload followed by a heavy block is fatigue data

A skipped deload followed by a heavy block is fatigue data. Ask what drove it before you decide there is anything to correct.

What this page owns. The phases owns whether a deload (deload) was even due and what a real one has to remove. This page owns the conversation. A client who skips a prescribed deload and runs the next block at full weight is not, on that fact alone, defying you. It is a moment that tempts a verdict rather than a question.

Open with the question. Ask what drove it rather than stating what she did. She may have felt fine. She may have been avoiding a lighter week for reasons that have nothing to do with the plan. A verdict makes her defend the choice instead of explaining it.

Asking, not stating

You went straight through the deload week at full weight. What was behind that?

The vocabulary applies whatever she answers. She has not failed. Whatever elevated fatigue the skipped deload left behind is a trajectory to correct, not a lapse to discipline. Correct the programming under The phases. Leave the conversation where it belongs: a question, then a plan. Never a lecture.

Watch for

Treating the skipped week as the finding. The finding is the fatigue she is now carrying. Fatigue and recovery owns reading it.

Terms on this page

deload: A planned easier week that lets fatigue clear while the muscle and the skill stay.
