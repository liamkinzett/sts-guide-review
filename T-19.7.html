How to read an exercise page · T-19.7

Faults use the shared names, and only faults you can see

Every fault on an exercise page uses the name from Faults and what you can actually see. The page never invents a fault name. It takes the shared name and describes what that fault looks like on this movement.

Why it works. The whole point of shared names is that two coaches watching the same video write the same words. "Butt wink" means one thing on every page (butt wink). "Knees fall inwards" means one thing on every page (knee valgus). A fault named freshly on each page would give every coach a private vocabulary.

A fault only appears on a page if you can see it. The Common faults rows hold the gross loss of position you can detect on video. A fault you cannot see cannot be screened. Things she feels, like a pump in the lower back, are asked about instead. They appear under Watch for as questions, never as video faults.

How to use it. The fault rows on a page are enough to correct a rep in the moment. What you see, why it happens, what to do.

Writing the note in her file is different. That needs the shared name and the fault's tier, which tell you what you are obliged to do about it. Read Faults and what you can actually see once before you start using the exercise pages, so you meet the names knowing what they mean.

Terms on this page

butt wink: At the bottom of the squat her pelvis tucks under and her lower back rounds. It usually shows up when she runs out of hip range or loses her brace.

knee valgus: Her knees fall inwards, inside the line of her toes, as she squats or stands up.
