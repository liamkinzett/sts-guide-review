Returning to training · T-37.2

The ladder from an isometric to a plyometric

There is a ladder between a hold and a jump. Skipping it is where people get hurt.

The two ends are familiar. A hold (isometric) sits at one end and jumping and bounding (plyometrics) at the other. Most people treat them as two bookends of one adaptation, with the middle missing. The middle is where the return happens.

The ladder, in order

Overcoming isometrics. She pushes or pulls against something that will not move.

Yielding isometrics. She holds a load still, resisting it.

Very slow tempo, low volume.

Slow tempo, greater volume. The same tempo as rung 3, with more of it.

Regular no-tempo eccentric and stretch-shortening work. Normal reps, with the lowering (eccentric) and the rebound (stretch-shortening cycle) back in.

Progressively higher velocity of the above.

Light or underloaded bounding. Some of her bodyweight is taken off the landing.

Full bounding, bodyweight change of direction, low-ground-contact plyometrics.

The principle underneath it. To move fast, she has to be able to move slowly really well. Then a little faster, a little less well. Then a little faster again.

Volume is baked into the ladder. Rung 3 is low volume and rung 4 raises the volume at the same tempo. Tempo and volume never advance together. One thing changes per step, so you know what she tolerated.

Plyometrics are rehabilitation. Only as the end phase. Teaching that plyometrics are rehabilitation without saying when is the error. They arrive last, as proof that everything below them holds.

Watch for

Starting at rung 5 because she looks fine, when the first four rungs feel like a waste of a session. That is the instinct the previous page exists to interrupt. Pain-free is not ready.

Say it like this

We're going to start with holds and slow reps, and we'll speed up one step at a time. The jumping comes last, when everything under it is solid.

Terms on this page

isometric: A hold. The muscle works against the load but the joint does not move. It is the first rung of every return.

plyometrics: Jumping, hopping and bounding work where the muscle stretches and shortens fast, with time in the air. It is the last rung of a return, never the first.

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.

stretch-shortening cycle: The quick stretch and rebound in a normal rep or a jump, where the muscle lengthens and then shortens straight away.
