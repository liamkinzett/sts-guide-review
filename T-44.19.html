Motivation and adherence · T-44.19

Translate a request for a style that is not yours

When she asks to be coached in a style that is not yours, translate the request into what she actually wants from it. Deliver that in your own voice. Never perform someone else's persona.

The shoe has to fit. Ultimately the intervention has to fit the person. A motivational tactic is matched to what actually drives her, reward or consequence, not applied as a borrowed delivery style. And it is tested rather than assumed. A personality-type intake question does the testing. It tells you whether she needs a cheerleader, a challenge or reasons, and how she responds to a loss. Ask it before the first hard week arrives.

Belief without a persona. The worked line for delivering confidence is borrowed belief, not a performed personality.

Borrowed confidence

I will never give you an exercise or put a weight on a bar that I know you cannot do.

Someone believing in her, and telling her she will succeed, works for almost any type of client. It requires no act.

Applied to "be harder on me". Name what she actually wants from harder. Usually it is an external structure she can push against, a deadline or a consequence-framed check-in, rather than a tone. Supply that structure and the proof that you believe in her, in your own voice.

Ask her what harder means

What would "harder" actually look like, day to day? Tell me and I'll build it into how we check in.

Why it works. The intervention has to fit the person delivering it as much as the person receiving it. A performed drill-sergeant slips on the first bad day, and she sees it slip. A structure she asked for holds without you having to be anyone.

Watch for

Answering the request with a tone. She asked for a tone. She wants a structure. Build the structure.
