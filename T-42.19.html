The coaching relationship · T-42.19

Where a coach's remit ends and a therapist's begins

You are not her therapist. Listen, then send the therapy somewhere it can be treated. Being a good coach is sufficient. You do not have to be her friend and you must not be her counsellor.

Where the edge sits. In a lot of cases you are her friend as well. You do not have to be. You can just be a really good coach, and that is where the relationship ends. A coach who takes on the counselling because refusing feels cold has misread what she is paying for. She is paying for objectivity.

The distinction is between listening and treating. You will listen to her talk about her week, her stress, her life. That is inside the job. Treating the thing underneath it is not. Only the second is out of scope.

Verdict

Refer to a psychologist or a counsellor, one who specialises in the concern.

The fear underneath a delayed referral is losing the client. Refer anyway.

Body image and the referral line governs where the content is food or her body. This page is the general case.

Rest-day anxiety, specifically. Where the presentation is anxiety about not training rather than a diagnosable disorder, the line sits here. Naming her life stress as a factor in what you see on the floor stays inside scope. Prescribing changes to her life does not.

Inside scope, in practice: name the pattern to her plainly. Build recovery literacy into the programming itself by explaining what a planned deload (deload) buys her rather than springing it on her. Give a rest day a stated purpose, so the day reads as productive rather than empty.

Watch for

Managing it yourself because you are close to her. Closeness is the reason it feels possible. It is not a qualification.

Terms on this page

deload: A planned easier week that lets fatigue clear while the muscle and the skill stay.
