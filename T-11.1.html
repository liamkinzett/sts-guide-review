Split design · T-11.1

When her goal changes, re-evaluate the split

The split exists to serve her goal. Any time her goal or her pathway moves, the weekly structure gets looked at again. Not only between phases. The structure was built to serve a goal that may no longer be the one she has.

What "looked at again" means. Run the arithmetic again: her weekly band per muscle, the per-session ceiling, and the exposures (exposures) those give. Then check the bias still matches the goal. A client who moves from a glute-building goal to a powerlifting path needs equal exposure across her foundational patterns, not a lower-body-weighted week. Assessing a new client explains the three paths.

Why it works. A split is a delivery system for a dose. When the dose or the target changes, the delivery has to change with it. Left alone, the old structure keeps delivering work to last phase's priorities.

Watch for

A split that survives three phases because nobody questioned it. It was right once. That is not the same as being right now.

Terms on this page

exposures: The number of separate sessions in a week in which a muscle is trained. Two exposures means it is hit on two different days.
