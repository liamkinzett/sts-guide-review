Paused bench

Two different pauses do two different jobs: the competition pause rehearses the commands, and the training pause removes the bounce.

Compared

columns: Competition pause | Training pause

Tempo: 21X1 | 2110

Where it pauses: One second on the chest, and one second locked out | One second at the chest

Who: The strength path, from the first week of the strength phase | Every barbell press: the bench, the incline and the close grip

Why: It rehearses the start and press commands | The chest is a real loaded stretch and a bounce risk

In training, every barbell press she does pauses at the chest for one second. The reason is the bounce risk, not the platform.

The three-count pause for tension on the chest. For a lifter who cannot hold tension on her chest, a three-count pause bench. It is one of three parallel fixes. A tempo squat for tension through the hips. A three-count pause bench for tension on the chest. A pause deadlift for tension off the floor.

Order. A movement paused at its stretched point (stretch-paused) never goes first in a cold session.

Terms on this page

stretch-paused: A movement with a deliberate pause at the point where the working muscle is longest under load.
