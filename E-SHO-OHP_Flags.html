Shoulders · E-SHO-OHP#Flags

Watch for

  • Flaring the elbows straight out to the side jams the upper arm bone against the bony roof of the shoulder (subacromial space). That full sideways lift of the arm (abduction) is what the tuck prevents. The tuck is a joint-protection standard as well as a range argument.
  • Pressing off pins is the answer for a client training alone to failure. She dumps the bar onto the pins rather than being pinned under it. The seated press from the rack is how STS coaches the barbell strict press.
  • The kettlebell bottoms-up press is a shoulder-function test, not a shoulder-pain fix. A shoulder already bothering her does not get better this way. STS doesn't program the kettlebell bottoms-up press.
  • Prerequisite. She must be able to hold her lower back still while her arm travels overhead (shoulder flexion). Without it the low back supplies the range and the press trains the wrong thing.
  • A pain report triggers a video request before any programming change. The video almost always shows why. See Faults and what you can actually see.
  • Keep the nuance in proportion. The fine cable heights, fibre alignments and rotation angles in this topic are for advanced physique athletes. A general fitness client does not need them.
  • Scope. This is not medical advice. A clinical injury goes to a practitioner. See Pain rules and when to refer.
    • Test the prerequisite: the overhead reach, read in this order

    Can she keep her rib cage over her pelvis with her hands overhead? If no, that is the whole intervention. Stop here.

    Does the shoulder blade turn upward while staying pulled down? The bottom corner slides out and around, and the top inner edge slides back. The blade stays down throughout. The socket then finishes facing upward.

    Is there enough rotation at the shoulder joint itself to finish the reach?

    Demonstrate once, then watch. Do not over-cue during an assessment. The more direction you give her, the more you change how she does the task.

    Why the blade must stay down when she is standing. Upright, gravity is trying to shear the shoulder blade off the rib cage, so stability against load always has a downward component. Lying down changes which range you stabilise in, so this is not a universal cue.

    Terms on this page

    subacromial space: The gap between the top of the upper arm bone and the bony roof of the shoulder. The rotator cuff tendons pass through it.

    shoulder flexion: The arm travelling forward and up in front of the body, all the way to overhead.

    abduction: A limb travelling out and away from the midline of the body: the arm in a lateral raise, or the thigh moving out to the side.
