Trunk sag on a braced hold

Her hips sag or pike on a hold.

Brace, shorten the lever, and end the set on position loss.

Where it shows, and the fix

# Hip adduction (adduction)

sub: Copenhagen: side flexion of the trunk. Hips sag toward the floor

Do this: No lateral sag at any point. This is the single named fault for the Copenhagen

# Push-up

sub: Trunk sagging or piking

Do this: Brace. Regress the lever

# Isometric (isometric) hold

sub: Hips sagging. Lazy planking. Holding the breath. Weight shifted forward or back.

Do this: Shorten the lever. Mat under the elbow for the shoulder. End the set on position loss rather than on the clock

There is no set cue written for this fault. Say the fix in plain words, one thing this set.

Other cues on those exercises. Not about this fault. Use them when the same set needs them.

Cues

When: Hip adduction

Say: Drive from the adductors.

When: Hip adduction

Say: Keep your back pressed against the pad.

Why: It enforces the setup detail that decides the set.

When: Hip adduction

Say: Don't let the pads crash.

Why: It enforces continuous tension and stops the stack being rested. Correct the concept before you correct the rep.

When: Push-up

Say: Take your time on each repetition.

Why: The first cue. It fixes rate and trunk sag in one instruction. The full source line adds that her legs and core supply the stability for the upper body.

When: Push-up

Say: Yes, it's an upper body move, but try squeezing your quads and glutes for extra stability.

Why: The follow-up for a client whose hips sag or hunt.

When: Push-up

Say: Protract the scapulae to push the chest off the floor.

Why: Drawn from an activation drill STS performs in a push-up position.

Terms on this page

adduction: A limb travelling in toward the midline of the body: the leg pulled in by the inner-thigh muscles, or the arm brought in to the side.

isometric: A hold. The muscle works against the load but the joint does not move.

:

scapulae: The two shoulder blades. One is a scapula.
