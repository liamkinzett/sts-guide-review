Motivation and adherence · T-44.17

Pure external motivation is an entry point, not the plan

Pure external motivation is a non-starter on its own. Use it as the entry point and build a second target underneath it. Motivation anchored entirely outside her does not hold long-term.

Typing what she runs on already asks whether her drive comes from inside or outside. A client whose stated fuel is showing a specific person, an ex, a parent, a friend who doubted her, is the extreme case of outside. If all her motivation is external, that is a non-starter.

Read that plainly. It rules out leaving the fuel as the whole plan. It does not say to take it away from her. The fuel is real and it is producing behaviour today. The claim is about durability, not legitimacy.

Use it as the entry point. It is doing real work right now. Build a second, self-referential goal underneath it in parallel. Carry it the way any long phase is carried: novelty inside the plan, a goal tied to something that is not a date. It has to survive the day the original fuel stops mattering. That day comes through a reconciliation, a change of heart or simply time passing.

What carries her

The outside fuel | Real today. It gets her in the door and it will fade

A goal of her own underneath it | A lift, a capability, a standard she sets. It is still there when the outside fuel is gone

Why it works. External fuel depends on something you do not control staying exactly as it is. The day it changes, a client with nothing underneath stops. A client with her own target keeps going and barely notices the fuel changed.

Watch for

Arguing with the fuel. "You shouldn't be doing this for him" is a verdict, and she will defend it. Build the second target instead and let the first fade on its own.
