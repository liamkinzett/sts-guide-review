Banking calories before an event

Saving up a deficit before a planned indulgence is a deliberate, planned form of dietary restriction. You do not rule on whether it is a problem. You ask what the rest of her eating looks like around it.

Clients call it banking (banking). She eats well under her usual intake for a day or more to save up for an event.

The axis it sits on. The under-fuelling page names dietary restriction and tight mental control around eating (cognitive restraint) as signs to notice. It names the training-response row as the strongest signal you hold. Banking is one instance of restriction. Whether it is a healthy trade-off or a warning sign is never answered in the abstract. It is the same question that page already asks you to keep asking, whatever reason is given for the restriction.

How to frame the answer. Not a verdict on whether banking is disordered. A question back. What does the rest of her eating look like around it? Is this occasional, or how she runs every social event? Does she feel controlled by the plan or in control of it? The training-response row still applies underneath a banking pattern. If she is training hard and not adapting while she runs this pattern, that is the signal, not the banking itself.

Say it like this

I'm not going to tell you banking calories before a trip is automatically a problem. What I do want to know is what the rest of the week around it looks like. And whether it's something you're choosing or something that's choosing you.

Watch for

Inventing a position on banking itself. Nothing here adds a rule. It routes an unnamed practice through one that already exists.

Terms on this page

banking: Eating well under her usual intake for a day or more to "save up" for a planned meal, event or holiday.

cognitive restraint: Deliberately and continually limiting what she eats by rule, regardless of hunger.
