Split design · T-11.4

The gate: what stops the set, coordination or the muscle

She moves to the next split structure when the target muscle is what limits the set. Not her coordination and not her ability to hold position. That is the whole test. Take a set near its limit and ask what actually ended it.

Check

Did the set end because the muscle could not produce another rep?

Did her trunk hold, her balance hold and the pattern stay intact to the last rep?

yes: The muscle is the limiter. She is ready for the next stage.

no: Position or coordination failed first. She belongs where she is. Keep the frequent, low-fatigue exposures going.

Why it works. A push/pull/legs split concentrates volume so a muscle can be trained hard in one sitting. If coordination fails first, that concentration buys nothing. The muscle was never the thing being taxed. It is the same shape as leaving priming. It is the same logic as classifying her by how fast she adapts rather than by years trained. A capability test tells you something a date cannot. Both of those tests live in The phases and Assessing a new client.

Watch for

Judging it from a comfortable set. The gate only shows itself near the limit. Read it from a genuinely hard set (hard set), which is why this test sits alongside Effort and intensity rather than apart from it.

Terms on this page

hard set: A working set taken within three reps of failure.

exposures: The number of separate sessions in a week in which a muscle is trained.
