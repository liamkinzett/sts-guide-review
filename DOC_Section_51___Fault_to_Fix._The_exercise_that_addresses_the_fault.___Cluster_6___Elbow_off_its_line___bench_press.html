Elbow off its line on the bench: grip and bar

The setup owns this fault. Settle the elbows to roughly 45 degrees, and set the hands no closer than the plane of her own shoulder joints. The accessory is for the lifter who benches wide by choice.

Her elbows flare out wide, or jam into a hard tuck. Flared to 90 degrees pinches the shoulder. Over-tucked hands the lift to the triceps.

Elbow off its line

# A very wide grip, presenting as pain at the front of the shoulder

sub: Accessory: biceps and rear delts

What you see: She benches wide by choice and reports pain where the biceps tendon runs over the front of the shoulder.

Do this: The setup fix still owns the fault: settle to roughly 45 degrees. For the lifter who keeps the wide bench, program biceps work and rear delts. The biceps work is done with the intent of training the biceps' secondary job, lifting the arm forward. Those are what save her through that plane of motion. Pain itself goes to Pain rules and when to refer.

Exercises: DB bicep curl; Reverse pec deck

# Hands inside the plane of the shoulder joint

sub: Setup: widen the hands

What you see: A very close grip, a diamond push-up, a plate press. The wrist deviates and the elbow drifts.

Why it happens: With the hand inside the line of the joint on top of the shoulder (AC joint), the wrist and elbow have to deviate to press.

Do this: Widen the hands to at least the plane of her own AC joints. Off to the side, the deviation goes away and the wrist normalises. If the narrow, arm-across-the-body path is what you want, go single-arm, so the shoulder blade can move around the rib cage. Region by region carries this as a rule.

# A close palms-down (pronated) grip driving the elbows out

sub: Setup: change the implement

What you see: A close grip on a straight bar forces the palms down and the elbows out.

Do this: A Swiss or neutral-grip bar, or dumbbells, so the hands stack directly above the elbows. No twisting load at the elbow (torque), and a comfortable shoulder path. Treat it as an upgrade, not a compromise.

Exercises: Flat DB bench press

Terms on this page

AC joint: The acromioclavicular joint, where the collarbone meets the top of the shoulder blade. You can feel it as the bony bump on top of the shoulder. Its line is the narrowest a pressing grip should go.

pronated: Palms turned down, or facing away from her, as on a standard barbell grip.

torque: The turning force on a joint. The further the load sits from the joint, the more of it there is. A hand set out of line with the elbow puts a twisting load on the elbow.
