The highest-stress squat depth is one we program routinely

A knee bent to between 80 and 100 degrees carries the highest stress on the kneecap joint and the highest compressive stress. That is a "parallel" split squat, and the same knee angle as a half back squat.

At that depth she sits on a small support surface, without the thigh-on-calf contact that begins deeper and spreads the joint force.

Translate it before you use it. Depth words do not transfer between one leg and two. A unilateral "parallel" is the same knee angle as a bilateral half squat (One leg or two holds the terms). So the highest-stress zone is a depth we name and program routinely.

Why it matters. It inverts the reflex. Deep squats do not cause degenerative damage: measured forces sit well inside what the tissue tolerates. The range coaches retreat to is the loaded one.

Watch for

Shortening range as the first response to a complaining knee. The lever order still puts range before load, and this does not overturn it. What it says is that stopping at half depth is not a neutral place to stop.
