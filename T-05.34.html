Primary amenorrhoea in a teenage client

A teenage client who has never had a period is a different question from the under-fuelling sign in the last page. That sign is written for a period that stopped. You do not work out which cause explains it. You raise it, through whoever holds parental responsibility for her, and the destination is her GP.

Why it is different. The under-fuelling sign is written for a period that started and then stopped for three months or more (secondary amenorrhoea). A 17-year-old who has never menstruated (primary amenorrhoea) is a different presentation with a different set of causes. It might be a constitutional delay, genuine under-fuelling, or something else again. Nothing you hold tells the two apart, and you do not try.

What does not change. You never grade or diagnose which one it is. What you notice and refer on is unchanged from the recognising under-fuelling page: training response and the behavioural signs. The plain fact of a 17-year-old training heavily with no period yet is itself sufficient reason to raise it.

Who you raise it with is where this differs. A minor's medical conversations ordinarily involve a parent or guardian. Do not assume the adult referral pathway transfers unmodified to a client under 18. Raise it with whoever holds parental responsibility for her care, the same way any other health finding in a minor would be raised. The clinical destination is the same GP.

Verdict

Raise it with whoever holds parental responsibility. The destination is her GP.

A 17-year-old training heavily with no period yet is reason enough on its own.

Terms on this page

secondary amenorrhoea: Periods that started and then stopped for three months or more.

primary amenorrhoea: Periods that have never started by the age they would be expected to.
