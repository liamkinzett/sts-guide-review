How to do it

Common to both biases.

Every rep, whichever bias

The rib cage and upper back stay still. The shoulder blade moves on them. Separating the two is the whole point of the movement.

Set the arm path out from the torso rather than along it. That is where she has the most room to pull the blade in and take the arm back.

Hold the hinge. Losing it, rounding the low back, or leaning back to yank the cable are all named faults.

Expect the corrected rep to look smaller at the handle while range at the working joint goes up.

Upper-back-biased. Let the shoulder blades travel fully out around the ribs at the bottom (protraction). Draw the elbows back, then pull the blades in and squeeze hard at the end (retraction). Control the return and reset the stretch. The blades hang loosely at the bottom and pinch together at the top.

Lat-biased. Shoulder blades down and locked for the whole set (depression), elbow tucked, driving toward the hip crease. Do not pull back too far. Overshooting the lat's useful range and letting the shoulder roll forward are the same fault from opposite directions. The instruction is extension and depression together, not a rearward yank.

Bench-supported and half-kneeling single-arm. Progress opposite knee forward first (contralateral), cross-body and rotational. Same side forward second (ipsilateral). Keep the sternum turned toward the side being rowed. Then the rib cage and the shoulder blade pull in opposite directions, and the muscle's two ends genuinely separate.

Say before you correct her

The fixed rep is going to look smaller at the handle. Your spine has stopped moving, so your back is doing all of it now.

Terms on this page

protraction: The shoulder blade sliding forward and out around the rib cage, away from the spine.

retraction: The shoulder blade sliding back toward the spine, so the two blades pinch together.

depression: The shoulder blade pulled down, away from the ear. The opposite of a shrug.

contralateral: Opposite side. On a single-arm row it means the forward knee is on the opposite side to the rowing arm.

ipsilateral: Same side. On a single-arm row it means the rowing arm and the forward leg are on the same side.
