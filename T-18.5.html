One leg or two · T-18.5

Three unilateral movements in a session is the cap

Three single-leg or single-arm movements (unilateral) in a session is the cap. The reason is time, not training.

Every unilateral set is really two sets. Three unilateral movements at three sets each is eighteen working sets of wall clock, before rest. A session is built inside 45 to 70 minutes, and that window does not survive four or five unilateral movements.

This is a time constraint, not a training claim. Nothing says a fourth unilateral movement trains her worse. It says the session stops fitting. Building a session owns what happens then.

When you may deviate. A client with ninety minutes and a reason can go past it. A client with forty minutes will not reach three.

Terms on this page

unilateral: One limb doing the work, as in a split squat or a single-arm row. Where the load is held does not decide it.
