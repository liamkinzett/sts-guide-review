Watch for

  • Say which kind of pad is in play. The loads on a rear delt fly are light, which makes a chest pad a reference point rather than something she pushes into (counterforce). On a chest press the back pad is a counterforce and adding it raises the load. A setup that names a pad must say which one it is.
  • The upper traps are the thief. Shrugging and hard pulling back of the shoulder blades are how the work leaves the rear delt. The high-to-low line is chosen partly to prevent it.
  • Neck symptoms in any upper-body movement trace back to the shoulder blade not managing force well. The load goes through the upper traps and the muscle from the blade to the base of the skull (levator scapulae). Watch her from the side.
  • Keep the nuance in proportion. The fine cable heights and fibre alignment here are for advanced physique athletes, where small changes in proportion count. A general fitness client does not need them.
  • A rear delt fly is not shoulder rehab. More rotator-cuff turn-out drills (external rotations) and rear delt flies will not move a shoulder whose problem is upstream. That problem is the stability between the rib cage and the shoulder blade. Region by region explains the order of fixes.
  • Choose by position and constraint, not only by the muscle's name. What an exercise trains is decided by the position it puts her in and what it stops her doing. A rear delt fly is a rear delt fly because of the held shoulder blade and the arm path, not because of the label.
    • Terms on this page

    counterforce: A pad or support she can push into, so that the load she moves can go up without her losing position.

    levator scapulae: The muscle running from the top inner corner of the shoulder blade up to the neck. It shrugs and it carries neck tension.

    external rotations: Drills that turn the upper arm outward against a band or cable. The usual "rotator cuff work".
