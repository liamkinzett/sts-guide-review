Tendon rehab: three seconds up, three seconds down

For a diagnosed tendon problem under a rehab plan, the loading tempo is three seconds concentric and three seconds eccentric. This is the one correct slow concentric, and it applies to nothing else.

The protocol, called heavy slow resistance.

TempoThree seconds concentric, three seconds eccentric. Six seconds per repetition
FrequencyThree sessions per week for 12 weeks
Progression3×15RM, then 3×12RM, then 4×10RM, then 4×8RM, then 4×6RM
What it achievesMatched the eccentric-only protocol on symptoms and structure at 12 weeks and at 52 weeks
Why we prefer itFar more clients stick to it. Compliance 92% against 78%, satisfaction 100% against 80%

3×15RM means three sets with the heaviest load she can lift fifteen times. Eccentric-only tendon protocols have no special claim. Combining the concentric and eccentric phases works as well and is far easier for a client to actually do.

This applies to a diagnosed tendon problem under a rehab plan. Nothing else. It is not a training tempo and not a general tendon-strengthening method. It is not something to reach for because a client's knee feels off. Without that gate the rule reads as "slow concentrics build tendons", which becomes a reason to slow everything down. That is the exact behaviour the one-second concentric rule exists to stop.

Why this does not contradict the one-second concentric. There the goal is force production, and a slow concentric costs muscle recruitment. Here the goal is tendon remodelling, and the tendon is not recruiting anything. Different tissue, different objective, opposite answer.

When you may deviate. Three seconds each way is the validated number and the starting point. It moves in two situations. She cannot hold it through the prescribed repetitions, in which case the load comes down before the tempo does. Or a physiotherapist managing the case has prescribed something else, in which case theirs wins.

The limit is a real one

These numbers are validated for pain in the middle of the Achilles tendon only. Applying them to the patellar tendon, the rotator cuff or the elbow is an extrapolation. Make it knowingly.

Terms on this page

tendinopathy: A tendon that has become painful and irritable from more load than it has adapted to. It is diagnosed by a clinician, not by a coach.

concentric: The lifting half of a rep, where the muscle shortens under load.

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.

isometric: A hold. The muscle works against the load but the joint does not move.
