An even resistance curve is a resistance-profile difference

A well-built machine is engineered so the resistance tracks the joint's own strength curve through the range (resistance profile). That is a difference between two tools, not a ranking of them.

How it is done. A geared cam, a cable's pulley angle or a lever arm can be built to follow the strength curve. A free weight cannot, because gravity pulls straight down wherever she is in the rep. The three screens are where this is judged, using the lateral raise against its cable version as the worked example.

What it means in practice. On a well-profiled machine, her working reps start earlier in the range. A free weight needs stabilising skill first, which delays the point where the target muscle is genuinely under load. That is a mechanical difference. Machines and free weights are different biomechanically, not better or worse than each other.

Say it like this

A good machine is built so the resistance matches your strength through the range. A free weight can't do that on its own. It relies on you to stabilise it. That usually means the machine gets you into real working tension sooner. It's a real mechanical difference, not a better or a worse.

Watch for

Selling a machine to her as the better tool because its resistance is even. It is a different tool. Pick it for what it does in her program.

Terms on this page

resistance profile: Where in the range an exercise is hardest and where it is easiest. It is set by the line of the load against the joint, so a dumbbell and a cable can load the same muscle very differently.
