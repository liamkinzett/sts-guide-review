Swaps

The rule for this entry is a progression rule. The drill is progressed by complexity, never by hanging weight on it. Each rung moves her centre of mass further from her base and shrinks the base of support. When you progress the movement, it becomes more loadable.

The ladder, in order

Bird dog. Highly remedial. An entry point, not a destination.

Bear crawl. The base of support starts moving.

Single-leg RDL. Load in the opposite hand, on one foot (contralateral load).

Single arm dumbbell row. The first rung STS programs as an exercise in its own right.

Landmine row.

Of the five rungs, STS programs only the single arm dumbbell row as its own exercise. The bird dog, the bear crawl, the single-leg RDL and the landmine row are not programmed; they place her on the ladder. The order is printed because the order is what teaches.

Swaps

# Same quality, real load

sub: The stronger position is to replace the Pallof press, not substitute for it

Swap to: Single arm dumbbell row

Why: A heavy single-arm row is an extreme anti-rotation challenge. Done heavy, it removes the need for remedial anti-rotation work altogether.

# Load in the opposite hand

sub: Cue her into position, then hold the sensation for the set

Swap to: DB B-stance RDL; Split squat; Reverse lunge; DB walking lunges

Why: All of them load one side and ask the trunk to stay square (contralateral load). The single-leg RDL belongs on the same list and is not programmed.

# More external stability

sub: None

Why: No braced or machine-supported anti-rotation version exists to swap to.

# A sensory swap

sub: She cannot feel the oblique working

Why: A med ball throw in place of a rope oblique crunch. STS has no page for the med ball throw; use it to find the feeling and return to the ladder.

# Her joint says no

sub: Pain, not weakness

Why: No injury-specific anti-rotation alternative is named. Pain rules and when to refer decides whether she trains. Region by region covers the low back.

# Home

sub: No gym access

Why: STS coaches clients who train in equipped gyms, so there is no home version.

Terms on this page

contralateral load: The weight is held in the hand opposite the working leg.
