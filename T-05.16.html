Structuring the postpartum return to training

From week one of her return she trains full body, loaded, at low intensity, with more external stability. There is no unloaded phase, and the anchor is 2 to 3 reps in reserve.

Reps in reserve (RIR) is how many more reps she could have done when the set stopped. At 2 to 3 RIR every set stops two or three reps short of failure.

What actually sets the pace. Not a week count. Her healing status, medical guidance, her training history, her sleep and recovery context, pelvic-floor symptoms, abdominal-wall symptoms, and how she tolerates load. A postpartum client is not simply a normal client who happened to miss a block. Pregnancy, birth and the postpartum period create their own recovery context.

What it looks like. Full body sessions. Low intensity. More external stability early: machines, supports, a seat. That reduces the bracing and pressure demand the same way a lighter load does. Basic movement.

There is no unloaded phase. Full body is still loaded. It just needs to be low load and build incrementally over the weeks. The anchor is the same 2 to 3 RIR from week one. The load climbs underneath it.

The shape of the return

Weeks 1 to 6 | 6 | Full body, loaded, low intensity, more external stability. The six-week medical check ends this block

Weeks 6 to 12 | 6 | Load keeps climbing under 2 to 3 RIR. After a caesarean, deep core work is still out

Onward | | Load climbs as long as it stays symptom-free, for as long as that takes

Waiting is allowed. Cleared at six weeks and not feeling ready is not a problem to solve. Wait until she feels ready. No rush.

Return to impact is symptom-led. It needs no physiotherapist sign-off. That is the STS position. Pelvic floor symptoms remain the referral trigger, as on the stop-the-session page.

On the timelines you will meet. You will hear three different timelines for running. None before three months, with a graded return from three to six. Impact preparation at eight to ten weeks and running from thirteen. Or moderate-to-vigorous activity within the first twelve weeks. None of them draws a line between general activity and impact. That is why STS reads her symptoms rather than a date.

STS does not run the published readiness tests. A movement screen and a reps-to-fatigue battery exist for a coach to run before impact. STS does not use them. The tests don't tell you anything training doesn't. Her training is the assessment.

Watch for

An unloaded first block. Bodyweight-only "reconnection" weeks are not the model. Low load from day one, climbing.

Rushing a cleared client who does not feel ready. Her readiness is the gate, not the six-week date.

Terms on this page

RIR: Reps in reserve. How many more reps she could have done when the set stopped. 2 RIR means she stopped two reps short of failure.
