Bodyweight training grows muscle by the same rule

A hard set of push-ups is a hard set. Bodyweight training builds real muscle. It just runs out of a lever sooner.

Volume counts a hard set as one taken within three reps of failure, regardless of implement. A bodyweight movement earns its stimulus the same way a loaded one does. Her friend who trains push-ups hard and consistently is genuinely training, not doing something lesser.

What that program does not have is the second lever. Double progression runs reps to the top of a band, then load. A bodyweight movement runs the same ladder in whichever direction it has assistance or difficulty left to give, and that ladder runs out. Eventually she reaches the top of what her bodyweight and the hardest unassisted version can ask of her. Past that point she needs a bridging lever or external load. The page on bridging a plate jump has the levers.

So the answer to her question is not that bodyweight training does not work. It works exactly as well as anything else until it runs out of a lever to progress on. That happens sooner on an unloaded movement than on one that can simply take more weight.

Say it like this

Your mate's push-ups build real muscle. The same rules apply to any hard set. What they'll eventually run into is a ceiling. Once bodyweight and the hardest version of the movement stop being enough of a challenge, there's no more weight to add without external load. That's the point a gym gives you more room to keep progressing.

Watch for

Answering this as though bodyweight training were fake stimulus. It is real stimulus with a nearer ceiling, not a lesser one.
