What to do when she presents with ankle or Achilles pain

Do not treat the ankle at the ankle. The two levers in this region are the hip and the foot. The ankle is the joint in the middle that reports on both.

Work through it in this order

Test. The sort, the heel-elevation test, watch the foot change shape, where her weight sits.

Read. The whole injury history, by region. Then pain or discomfort. Then the two numbers. Then the twelve months before the session.

Do. Get the foot to move, train the ankle with calf raises, then add dynamic work.

Change. Return to impact on strength, not on feel or the calendar.

Escalate. A suspected rupture or tear, the same day. Tendon pain at the heel. Anything acute.

Going up: why the ankle recurs. The ankle sits at the structural end of the scale. There is very little there for a coach to influence: nobody can contract a ligament. That is why an old ankle sprain recurs for decades while a dislocated shoulder resolves. You have to make up for a structural deficit with a functional surplus, and the function lives at the hip.

Going down: the thing she calls a stiff ankle usually is not one. If you elevate her heels and she can suddenly squat to the floor, it was never ankle range. It was a centre-of-mass problem. And what looks like ankle range is mostly the shin rotating and the foot rolling in.

This region is the worst offender for treating the site of pain

After an injury everyone goes "foot and ankle, foot and ankle". The site of pain is where the problem arrived, not where it came from. The region that hurts is usually not the region to work on.

Step 1. Test

Four things. None of them is a diagnosis.

The four tests

# The sort

sub: Every movement into helps, no effect, hurts

Do this: The sort is the plan, not a step toward one.

# The heel-elevation test

sub: Re-run her squat with the heels on a wedge or plates

Why it happens: If depth appears, it was never ankle range. It is centre-of-mass control.

Do this: This is a constraint test, not a mobility measurement. It tells you which of two problems you have and nothing about tissue.

# Watch the foot change shape

sub: Does the foot flatten as the knee travels forward?

Why it happens: Many people grip the ground with their feet. The foot needs to relax a little for the ankle to move. A knee tracking outside the little toe limits it.

Do this: Watch her lunge, hinge and squat. Do not measure an arch against a reference. Foot structure varies too much for one.

# Where her weight sits in the foot

sub: Midfoot, not heels

Do this: Weight parked in the heels on split squats and hinges is the named common fault.

Two tests you do not run

A rules-based X-ray screen for ankle injuries, and a knee-to-wall lunge test. Neither is STS practice, and the second measures a range in order to judge it normal, which is barred for a coach. Do not write them in from general knowledge.
