Adherence as a signal · T-39b.2

Read the pattern, not the count. Four patterns, four program changes

Which sessions she skips matters more than how many. Four patterns, and each one changes something different in the program.

The four patterns

# The same session every time

sub: Program mismatch. Something in that session is the problem

What it usually means: Something in that session does not fit her. An exercise she dreads, a length she cannot face, a day that never works.

Do this: Rebuild that session. Check the exercise she is avoiding first.

# The last session of the week, consistently

sub: Life. The week does not have room for it

What it usually means: The week is one session longer than her life allows.

Do this: Compress the week deliberately rather than let it be dropped at random. Training around life says which sessions survive: protect the compounds.

# Sessions with one specific movement

sub: Often fear or discomfort rather than dislike

What it usually means: She is avoiding the movement, not the session.

Do this: Triage first. If she has ever been hurt on it, read the fear-avoidance page in Pain rules and when to refer before anything else. That has its own method, an external task for one exposure, and regressing is the wrong lever there. Where there is no injury history, check the hypothesis with a constraint as test, then regress or substitute. Never simply remove it without naming why.

# Everything, sporadically

sub: Life. Or the program is too long

What it usually means: Nothing in particular is being avoided. The whole thing is not fitting.

Do this: Reduce the session count before reducing the sessions.

The movement-specific row needs care, because it has two opposite answers. Where she was hurt on the movement, the avoidance is (fear avoidance). Its whole method is the opposite of regression: give her an external task and let her outpace the signal, in one exposure. Regression is how fear gets entrenched. A client taught that the only safe version of her training is a smaller one has learned twice over.

Where there is no injury history at all, the check is (constraint as test): regress the movement and see whether she does it. If she does, you found what it was. Then regress properly or substitute, and name the reason in the notes.

Post-injury clients are on every roster, and movement-specific skipping is one of the commonest patterns there is. That is why the triage comes first. It changes which lever you are allowed to touch.

Watch for

Treating a single skipped movement as fear. It is a hypothesis. Constraint as test is how you check it, and only where she was never hurt on the movement.

Removing the movement quietly. Never simply remove it without naming why. A silent removal teaches her the movement was dangerous.

Terms on this page

fear avoidance: Avoiding or bracing against a movement because she expects it to hurt, after it once did. The nervous system is protecting her from an outcome, not from tissue damage.

constraint as test: Adding the thing that should fix a fault, such as a heel wedge or a lighter version, to see whether the fault survives. Both outcomes tell you something.
