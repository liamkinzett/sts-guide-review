What to do when cardio enters her week

Route the nutrition half first. Then decide every line of the cardio prescription and write it into the program. This page is the whole topic in the order you price it.

Guide

name: Nutrition Manual

reason: Whether to cut calories or add cardio, in what order, and her step number all live there. The sequence it sets is calories, then steps, then cardio last. This topic answers the other question: what the cardio costs the training and where the cost lands.

Test. Three questions before a minute is prescribed.

Ask these first

# Which job is she buying?

sub: Cardiovascular ability, or expenditure

It decides: Whether the cost is worth paying. It never changes the bill.

# Does the cardio share muscles with what the block is building?

sub: Interference is body-part specific

It decides: Where the cost lands.

# What phase is she in?

sub: Priming, a peak or a deload each change the answer

It decides: A beginner in priming gets no cardio added. A peak or a deload brings cardio down.

Read. Does it cost her muscle? Yes, where it shares muscles with the block. Largely not where it does not. The cost is local.

Leg-dominated cardio does not blunt upper-body adaptation. Within the legs the machine decides most of it. Running blunts growth of the slow fibres (type I fibres) and cycling does not, while running loses more fat. That is a real trade rather than a free win either way.

Steps are not part of the bill. They raise expenditure without competing with the lifting.

Do. Decide every line.

The prescription

# Modality

sub: Cycling, or other low-impact work where the muscle only shortens (concentric) and never brakes a load

Default: Running needs a reason.

# Dose

sub: 2 to 3 sessions of 20 to 30 minutes

Default: 4 to 5 sessions is available when the cardio and the block do not share muscles.

# Shape

sub: A weekly total with a stated number of sessions

Default: No session under fifteen minutes.

# Intensity

sub: Zone 2

Default: Zone 3 and above, including intervals, only when the job is cardiovascular ability. In a deficit that hardens into a rule.

# Order

sub: Lifting first only when maximal lower-body strength is the goal

Default: Otherwise the order is hers. Better than sequencing is separating. Different days, otherwise hours apart.

# Steps

sub: A step goal for every client

Default: The number comes from the Nutrition Manual.

Change. The moment cardio enters the week it is part of the program. Prescribed, written down, reviewed. What else moves:

  • Past the baseline is precision rather than permission. Modality, placement and separation each matter more the further past it she goes. Some goals are worth it. Say what it costs and what you changed.
  • HIIT under another name still counts. Circuits, conditioning finishers, a quick metcon. Ask what it is buying and price it accordingly.
  • Steps quietly becoming cardio count as cardio. A step target achieved at pace on a treadmill is a cardio session. Count it against the dose.

    • What explicitly does not change. The step goal stays in the program in every phase. The session order for a hypertrophy client stays hers, because the order did not measurably change the outcome there. And the downward deviation is always available. Anything costing her sleep or her sessions is too much, wherever it lands.

    Do not promise her a number for how much muscle the cardio will cost. This topic holds directions and thresholds: a fibre-type effect, and the 20 to 30 minute baseline. It can say whether the cardio costs her, where the cost lands and how to shrink it. It cannot convert a week of cardio into a predicted amount of muscle lost.

    Terms on this page

    type I fibres: Slow-twitch muscle fibres. They tire slowly and suit long, easy work.

    concentric: The lifting half of a rep, where the muscle shortens under load. Pedalling is almost all of it, with no lowering half.
