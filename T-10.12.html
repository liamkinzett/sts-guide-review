Progression · T-10.12

Before you change anything, diagnose

A stall is a symptom. Four things cause it and only one of them is answered by progression. Diagnose before you change anything.

When a lift stalls

? Has the lift been flat for two exposures in a row, inside the same block?

no: Nothing to diagnose yet. Keep watching.

? Is she training it hard enough? Check the last two reps on video.

no: Raise her effort first. Effort and intensity has the check.

? Has her technique drifted? Has the range shortened?

yes: Fix the rep before you change anything else.

? Is she recovering: sleep, stress and food?

no: Fix recovery. Never add sets to an under-fuelled client.

= Only now pull a lever. Reps, then load, then a set.

Volume has the order: effort, then technique, then recovery, then volume. Three of the four are free to fix. The fourth costs her recovery, which is why it comes last. Work the list before you reach into this topic.

One scope note. That order assumes a client whose effort ratings mean something. For a beginner it inverts and execution comes first. Her effort ratings are the one signal you cannot yet read. Her first program has the detail.

And if the answer is a deload rather than a change, The phases owns that. A stall across several movements at once, with recovery markers to match, is not a progression problem wearing a disguise.

Watch for

Reaching for a lever because this is the progression topic and levers are what it contains. The most common correct answer to a single stalled exercise is that she is not training it hard enough. That answer lives in Effort and intensity.
