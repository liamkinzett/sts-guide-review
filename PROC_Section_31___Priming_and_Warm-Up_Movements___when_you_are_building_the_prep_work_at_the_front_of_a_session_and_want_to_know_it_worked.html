Building the prep work

You are building the prep work at the front of a session and want to know it worked. This page is the Priming lifts topic's pages in the order you use them. Nothing here is new doctrine.

One routing note first. If you reached here searching for the priming phase, you are in the wrong place. The phase is in The phases. These movements are what Warming up calls prep work (prep work), and Warming up owns whether any of them go into the warm-up at all.

1 · Ask what each drill feeds

A warm-up is built from components that lead into the main exercise of the day. Before a drill goes in, ask what it feeds. A position she struggles with goes into the warm-up or an early exercise, so she can call back on it later in the session. Activation drill has the drills.

On a half-kneeling drill (half-kneeling), use the alignment check as the first question, and check the pelvis before accepting it. The back knee is the canary. A lifter can pass the line by rotating the pelvis to buy the range.

The worked test comes afterwards. She reaches a range in the warm-up she could not reach walking in. Then the feeling planted in the drill shows up again in the first working exercise.

2 · Read what the drill is for

The purpose governs everything in this topic: sensation, not warmth and not tissue change.

A stretch is step one of three. Passive work restores access, an active drill follows, then full-range loaded patterning owns the range. The named failure is getting stuck at step one.

Rolling is a short-term sensory tool, not a tissue intervention. That must not be softened when you explain it to her.

Tightness is often a perception rather than a length problem. That is why more stretching is not automatically the answer.

3 · Do, in order

Do, in order

# Plant a feeling, then demand it back

Do: Plant a feeling in the drill, then demand she recreates it in the first working exercise. The two are placed close together on purpose.

Exercises: Kneeling landmine press; Captain's chair scap raise; Hanging scap raise; Incline prone Trap 3 raise

# Stretch: ease in

Do: A mild stretch sensation is enough. Stack the torso in every kneeling stretch. Do not stretch a completely cold muscle.

Exercises: PNF lat stretch; Couch stretch; Pigeon stretch

# Soft tissue: pin and stretch, then leave

Do: Pin and stretch at two or three tender points, then leave. The dose is the number of points, not a reading on a clock.

Exercises: Glute trigger point / foam roll; Quad trigger point / foam roll

# Every drill with intent

Do: A drill performed passively trains nothing. It is the most common fault in this topic.

4 · What moves around it

  • The set ends on sensation rather than on a rep count. Volume owns the count everywhere else.
  • Stretch durations are Warming up's, and this topic does not restate them.
  • A prep drill is not a lighter version of the work. Performed as though it were a set, it is a different thing done badly.
  • The layer exists to make itself redundant. Step three is the point. A stretch or release that has quietly become the whole intervention gets progressed or dropped.

    • 5 · When to send it up

    The worked test in step 1 is the pass mark. She reached the range, and the feeling came back under load. Do not look for a hold duration or a rep target to certify it.

    What does escalate: a torn cartilage pad or other knee pathology (meniscus) with the foot set wide on the pigeon. It is this topic's one named contraindication, and Pain rules and when to refer owns the triage.

    Whether this work runs at all, and whether the slot is worth its time, is Warming up's decision rather than yours mid-session.

    Terms on this page

    prep work: The movements inside the warm-up slot that get the first loaded movement ready. Chosen for the session in front of you.

    half-kneeling: One knee down, the other foot forward, with the hips square and the torso tall.

    meniscus: The cartilage pad inside the knee. Twisting the knee under load is what injures it.
