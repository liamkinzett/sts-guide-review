What the strength system leaves out

What the strength system leaves out is worth as much as what it uses. Each rejection is a diagnosis: the tool does not touch the real cause. These are the system's rules for a competitive powerlifter.

Rejected, and why

# A partial used to fix a fault

sub: Rejected as a class, because it solves a problem it is not causing

Why: A lift cut short by a box, a board or blocks (partial) does not touch a fault that starts elsewhere. Using one that way is like sending an ambulance to the bottom of a cliff people keep falling off, rather than putting up a fence.

Read with: Peaking for a meet programs box squats, board presses and a block pull for whole blocks of the conjugate plate. There they are heavy rotating variations, not fixes. Read both before you program a partial for a competitive powerlifter.

# High box squats

sub: For a lifter without supportive gear (raw lifter) who struggles above parallel

Why: They do not touch the pelvic and bracing problem (bracing) at the bottom, which is the actual cause.

# Block pulls, except for a sumo puller

sub: The sumo block pull is the exception, not the rule

Why: The objection is general. It is near impossible to reproduce the loading and hip position of a floor pull from a block (block pull). So a block pull will not fix a lockout whose cause is her position off the floor. The sumo block pull earns its place because it trains something specific: pelvic tension to break the floor, and the knee snap at lockout.

For a conventional puller: STS neither prescribes nor rejects the block pull as a training accessory. Deadlift variations holds the detail. Blocks stay the right lever for a deadlift that hurts off the floor, which is a pain decision rather than a training one.

# A board press for a lockout problem

sub: When the real problem is upper-back tightness

Why: The board trains the top of the lift. The fault is somewhere else (board press).

# Heeled shoes used as a crutch

sub: Masking a bracing deficiency

Why: They allow a more upright torso and more knee travel, and they make the squat harder by lengthening the distance to depth. Where they mask a bracing problem, pain turns up somewhere else later.

Terms on this page

raw lifter: A powerlifter who competes without a squat suit or a bench shirt.

bracing: Filling the trunk with air and tensing the whole midsection so the spine stays rigid under load.

partial: A lift done through part of its range rather than all of it, cut short on purpose by a box, a board, blocks or pins.

block pull: A deadlift pulled from blocks or pins, so the bar starts above the floor.

board press: A bench press where boards on her chest stop the bar a few inches short of touching, so the top of the lift is trained.
