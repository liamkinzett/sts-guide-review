When her relationship with food or her body concerns you

When something about how she talks about food or her body worries you, test for the three referral behaviours first. Any one of them means you refer. This page is the topic's rules in the order you use them.

Red flags

title: Refer if you see any one of these

Social, environmental or situational avoidance: she withdraws from things to avoid being put in front of food

Panic or obvious behaviour change around specific foods

Compensation: withholding food to make up for something eaten earlier, or training to punish a missed session or a meal

emergency: A period that has stopped is a medical referral. Pain rules and when to refer owns it.

Run it in this order

Test. Is there social, environmental or situational avoidance? Is there panic or obvious behaviour change around specific foods? Is she withholding food to compensate for something eaten earlier? Any one of the three is enough.

Read. You are testing for fear and rigidity, not for careful eating. Can the routine flex? Can she eat out without getting anxious?

Do. If any signal is present, you refer, to a psychologist or a specialised dietitian. Say it plainly and say it early. If no signal is present you are on the safe side of the line. Watch your own food language. Move what she is measuring, off the scale or off the mirror.

Change. Nothing in the program changes on the strength of a body-image conversation alone. Where fuelling is the issue, the diet does not start. Screen readiness before any deficit.

Escalate. The referral is the escalation. There is no version of this where you manage it yourself for longer to see how it goes.

Watch for

Noticing is not diagnosing. You never name a condition. You describe what you saw and you refer.

Waiting to be sure. One signal is enough. You do not need three.

She declines the referral. Note that she declined, with the date, in her file. Pain rules and when to refer holds the same rule for a medical referral. You still do not treat it.
