Glutes · E-GLU-HIPEXT#When to program it

When to use it

The problem you notice. You want the glute trained at its shortest (shortened position) and her hip thrust is not executing well. The finish keeps arriving from the lower back. Or her back is cranky and you need a hip movement she can load without aggravating it. Or the session has already put a heavy bar on her spine and you want hip extension without adding to it.

Reach for it when

# You want the shortened position trained and the hip thrust is not executing well

sub: The same position, easier to hit

Confirm it: Film her thrust. The pelvis dumps forward at the top and the lower back finishes the rep, set after set.

What it gets her: The same shortened glute position with less to go wrong. The 45 degree hip extension is easier to execute than a hip thrust. The thrust's own load pulls her into a forward pelvic tilt; this movement does not.

# Her back is cranky and you need to find out what hip loading she tolerates

sub: A loadable hip test she can pass

Confirm it: She reports a grumpy lower back and none of the red flags in Pain rules and when to refer. She has not yet found a hinge she tolerates.

What it gets her: The named entry point back to hinging. The bench stabilises her and gravity pulls her into a stacked position. Have her hug a medicine ball to her chest as a front-side brake (anterior constraint), then progress to the RDL. This is a training decision, not a clinical one. Pain itself is Pain rules and when to refer.

# The session has already put heavy compressive load through her spine

sub: Hip extension without adding to the compression

Confirm it: Heavy deadlifts or squats have just finished and the glute still needs work.

What it gets her: Hip extension trained without more load down the length of the spine (axial load). Placed immediately after heavy deadlifting for exactly that reason, it decompresses rather than reloads.

# She trains glutes three times a week and the third touch must not cost recovery

sub: Stimulus without fatigue

Confirm it: Two loaded glute days are already in her week and a third session is planned.

What it gets her: A light day that still stimulates. One way that has worked is two loaded days, then a third day of shortened-range work only. A hip extension and a kickback, and that is the glutes for the day. Easy to recover from, enough to stimulate something. That is one worked example for one client, not a frequency rule. Her frequency is Split design.

The one-line diagnostic

If she is feeling her low back, she is moving too much through it. The lower back is supplying range the hip should be supplying. It has stopped being glute work.

Setup. Pad below the front points of the pelvis, ankles locked behind the rollers, toes slightly out, ribs stacked, and enough load to be work. Setup has the order.

Terms on this page

shortened position: The muscle is working while it is bunched short. For the glute that is full hip extension, torso in a straight line with the legs.

anterior constraint: Something held against the front of the body, like a hugged medicine ball, that stops the ribs flaring and the lower back arching.

axial load: Load running down the length of the spine, as with a barbell on the back or overhead.
