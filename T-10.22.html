Bridge a plate jump that overshoots the band

When the next available jump is too big for the band, bridge it with a lever. Do not accept the jump as the smallest available increase.

A 5kg jump on a 30kg press is a 16.7% increase, well past any sensible weekly step. A sensible weekly step is 2 to 5%, which on 30kg is 0.6 to 1.5kg.

Six levers bridge the gap without micro-plates. They are a menu, not a ranked answer. Pick the one that fits the client and the movement.

Six ways to bridge the jump

# Equipment

sub: Magnetic micro-weights or fractional plates

What it is: The smallest jump the rack does not offer, bought instead.

# The plus-two scheme

sub: Earn 2 reps past the top of the band before the weight moves

What it is: On an 8 to 12 target, she reaches 14 reps before 30kg becomes 35kg. The extra reps make the bigger jump land inside the band.

# Tempo and execution

sub: Add a pause, or add a second to the lowering half, at the same weight

What it is: More time under load without more load. Beyond 4 to 5 seconds a lowering phase (eccentric) becomes counterproductive. It drains energy early in the rep and cuts the hard reps at the end.

# Ramping closeness to failure

sub: Same weight across the block, closer to failure week on week

What it is: The effort climbs while the load holds. The compound floor is not negotiable here. Compounds stop at 1 RIR and only stabilised isolation work runs to failure.

# Added sets

sub: More sets at the same weight

What it is: More total work at a load she already owns. It spends room in her weekly band, so it is a response to a diagnosed stall, never a plan.

# More external bracing

sub: Standing to seated, or an added back pad

What it is: Removes stabilisation as the limiting factor, so the target muscle reaches the heavier load sooner.

One pairing to watch. Some coaches pair a slow lowering with an explosive lift for extra tension and recruitment. Others avoid the pairing because a lifter can lose position doing it. If you use it, watch her position on video.

Why it works. The plate on the rack is not the progression. The stimulus is. A coach who treats the next plate as the only lever has two bad options. She stalls a client for weeks waiting on a jump that never arrives cleanly. Or she takes a jump three to five times the size any sensible band would allow.

A worked example. She is written 10 to 12 on a seated dumbbell press with the 10 kg dumbbells and reaches twelve on every set. The next dumbbells are 12.5 kg, a 25% jump that would drop her to six. Instead she keeps the 10s, adds a two-second pause at the bottom, and works back to twelve. Then the 12.5s land inside the band.

Watch for

Reaching for the same lever every session out of habit rather than picking the one that fits the client and the movement. A client who cannot tolerate more sets needs a different lever than one who can.

Say it like this

The next plate is too big a jump to be the right one. We'll get there a different way this week. The load catches up once it's actually earned.

Terms on this page

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.

RIR: Reps in reserve. How many more reps she could have done when the set stopped. Zero RIR is failure.
