What to do when she sends a video and something in it is wrong

This page adds nothing. It is this topic's rules in the order you use them, when a video arrives and something in it is wrong. The principles still stand: a coach who works them in a different order for a good reason is not breaking anything.

From footage to a fix

? Is there pain anywhere in her report?

yes: Pain rules and when to refer comes first. The video waits.

? Do you have the whole set, first rep to last, from the angle the fault needs?

no: Ask for it. One rep, or the wrong angle, cannot show you where form changed.

? Have you asked where she feels it before you watched?

no: Ask now, before you press play. Afterwards her answer is only confirmation.

? Can you name the fault, and the rep where it broke?

no: Faults and what you can actually see gives the name. Find the rep where it changed, not the worst one.

? Which of the three broke it: absolute load, nearness to failure, or skill?

= Test it with a constraint, then pick the regression that reaches that site. Cueing gives you the words.

Step 1. Test. A discomfort report gets a video before it gets a programming change. It does not have to work first time. Ask for the whole set, first rep to last in one continuous take, because one rep cannot show you the rep where form changes. Then ask before you look: where she feels it, where it stops, what it is like at the bottom. Once you have a hypothesis from the footage her answer stops being evidence.

Step 2. Read. Reconcile her account with the footage. The interesting case is when they disagree. Find the rep where form breaks. The diagnostic fact is what broke it. Sort the trigger into its bucket: absolute load, relative intensity, general movement skill, sometimes all three. Judge what you see against her own structure, not a uniform standard. And hold the structure story to its rank: structure indicates, position dictates, and the structure check comes last.

Step 3. Do. Constraint as test. Add the thing that should fix it and see whether the fault survives. Both outcomes are informative, and it is how you tell a fault from a structure remotely. Record the result. Where the movement itself is too supported to show anything, run the gatekeeper loop. Diagnose on a movement with nothing holding her up. Prescribe isolated work for the missing quality. Then retest on the same movement.

Step 4. Change. A regression has to address the site of breakdown. Say it to yourself first: I am moving her to X because the fault is at Y. If the sentence does not hold, you picked the regression for ease. The lever follows the bucket: cap load, end the set earlier, or skill work. She gets the change, not the reasoning. Keep the test and the fix apart. The same constraint is a test here and a fix in Cueing.

Step 5. Escalate. The moment pain enters the report it belongs to Pain rules and when to refer before it belongs here. What the faults are called, and whether one is visible at all, is Faults and what you can actually see. That topic also holds the camera angles. What you actually say to her is Cueing.

Watch for

A clean video read as a clean lift. If the camera could not have shown the fault, it was not screened. That includes the wrong angle and too few reps. Faults and what you can actually see covers what the film has to show.

Something that is not costing her anything. Often the right response to a video is nothing at all.
