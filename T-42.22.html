The coaching relationship · T-42.22

Ending it has mechanics

Ending it has mechanics. Honesty about why, resources onward, and money back where sessions sit unused. The decision to end it is the previous page. This is what the ending contains, and there are two kinds. This is the usual practice.

The good ending is one you initiate, and it is a graduation. Sometimes the conversation is: I've got to kick you out of the nest. You've been here too long. You're fully grown.

The success condition, said to her face

Is this why we're doing this? Yes, it is. You don't need me anymore.

That is the relationship worth having. She leaves able to do it without you.

The bad ending has three parts.

What the ending contains

Honesty, with no invented excuse. Be real with her. Do not make something up. Just be honest with the individual.

Resources onward. If you have resources available, another coach, a referral, that is the best way to end the conversation.

Money back where sessions sit unused. If there are still sessions purchased, here's your money back. You are not keeping her money.

The goal of the final conversation is de-escalation, not a win. End it on a good foot the best way you can, rather than letting it turn into an argument.

The worked case is one direct question, an immediate refund and a one-line ending. Why do you keep coming? Because I've paid you. Then here's your money back, we're not working, problem solved.

The refund half is a business decision. STS's refund position is not coaching doctrine and is not recorded here. Where an STS policy exists it wins. Until you know it, promising her money back commits STS to something you do not own. Take it to the head coach before you promise it.

What is not scripted. No farewell message text exists here. Nothing on asking for a testimonial at the end. The words are yours; the three parts are fixed.

Watch for

An ending that turns into a case for the defence. You are not there to win. You are there to end it well.
