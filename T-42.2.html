The coaching relationship · T-42.2

Set a response-time boundary before you need one

Set a response-time boundary before you need one. The window is 24 hours, and it is paired with a deadline on her side. This is the usual choice, with one reason to vary it below.

The expectation, as set with a client. A reply within 24 hours, not within the next hour.

Built into the check-in process. The check-in must be in before 10 a.m. her time on her check-in day. She is then guaranteed a 24 hour response. If it comes in after 10 a.m., it moves over to the next check-in day.

The two halves of the window

# Her deadline

sub: Submitted by a time on her day

What it does: Puts the onus on her to invest in the check-in. A late submission rolls to the next cycle rather than jumping the queue.

# Your window

sub: A reply inside 24 hours

What it does: Tells her what to expect, so a reply later that day is not read as being ignored.

Motivation and adherence owns the deadline half in detail: which day polices her weak window. This page owns your side.

Why it is set before you need it. The coach who admitted his own boundary around response time was not great described coming off a call to 48 unread messages. He named the cost as anxiety, his own and other people's. A window set on day one prevents that. A window set after the 48 messages is a retreat.

Where you vary it. The number is the usual choice, not a law. What is fixed is that a window exists and that she knows it. Nothing here separates weekends from weekdays or public holidays from working days. Absent a stated exception, the same window applies on any date.

Watch for

A window you do not keep. A client who messages at eleven at night and gets an answer at eleven at night has been taught the window is not real. The page on walking back a boundary covers the repair.
