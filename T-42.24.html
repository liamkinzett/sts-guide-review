When a client wants to add extra work

She wants to add extra work. The answer is usually no. Budget the extra rather than granting it raw. This is the usual choice, with a reason to vary it below.

The diagnosis is psychology, not programming. Some clients like to train. They want to feel they are going above and beyond. For clients like that, the answer is usually not to do the extra thing. Usually the thing holding them back is that they are doing too much. What they need is to feel they did the extra thing.

The technique. Plan for the extra before she asks for it.

Budgeting the extra

Know she will ask. This client wants to do extra after the session. Plan for it.

Program a deliberately shorter session. The main work fits inside it.

Have something ready for afterwards. Chosen by you, sized by you. It is the extra, and it is already in the plan.

Control the framing when she asks. She did everything she needed to do today. Now she wants to do more. All right, let's do more.

Why it works. She gets the box ticked. You keep control of the dose. Nothing about the total changed, because the extra was already counted.

A tension to know about. This tactic lets her feel the extra was extra while you control the dose. It sits beside the rule that you tell her what you know and what you do not. Deliver it as tactful framing, never as a secret she would object to if she saw it. Where you are unsure which side of that line you are on, ask the head coach.

Where you vary it. The word is usually. Where more genuinely is recoverable and is not what is holding her back, the answer can be yes as ordinary programming. Adherence as a signal owns what her adherence signals mean for the program.

When the extra is priced against a lapse. Work added to cancel out a missed session or a meal is a different thing entirely. Body image and the referral line owns exercise as punishment.

Watch for

Granting the extra raw. You have lost the dose, and next week she wants more.
