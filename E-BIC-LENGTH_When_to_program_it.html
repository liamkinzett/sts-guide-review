When to use it

The problem you notice. Every curl she does is standing and free-weight, so the bottom of every rep is unloaded. Or her shoulder is cranky and you want an entry-level way back into loading it. Or her incline curls drift across her body. The lengthened-position curl is the slot that loads the biceps where a dumbbell hanging from a standing arm gives her nothing.

Reach for it when

# Every curl she does is standing and free-weight

sub: The bottom of every rep is unloaded

Confirm it: List her curls. If every one is a standing dumbbell or barbell curl, the stretched range has never been loaded.

What it gets her: The long head loaded where a dumbbell gives her nothing (long head). A dumbbell loses all tension at the bottom, because gravity passes straight through the elbow. A cable keeps a lever on the joint (moment arm) and holds tension in the stretched position. Only a cable, an incline or a behind-the-back setup reaches full length. That needs the elbow straight and the arm behind the body together.

# Her shoulder is cranky and you want an entry-level way back into loading it

sub: A little movement and a little stability through the shoulder

Confirm it: The shoulder is irritable, but she can hang an arm behind the body line without provoking it.

What it gets her: Muscles trained across the shoulder joint with a small demand on it. Dumbbell curls on a slight incline are the named entry point. They give a little movement through the shoulder, a little stability, and muscles across the shoulder joint trained.

# Her incline dumbbell curls drift across the body

sub: She runs out of outward turn at the shoulder (external rotation)

Confirm it: Film from the front. On the incline the dumbbell travels in toward the midline as she curls.

What it gets her: An alignment you can set to her. Move to a cable, where the line of pull is set to the lifter rather than to the bench.

Never program a preacher curl first in a session

Second or third only. The stretch-loaded bottom is a tear risk on cold tissue. This is a hard sequencing rule, not a preference. The same reasoning forbids locking out at the bottom.

Two more contraindications, not coaching points

Do not raise the pulley high on a lengthened cable curl. It over-challenges the elbow tendon at full stretch and drops tension to near zero at the top.

Extreme shoulder extension loads the shoulder in shear if the position is not stabilised (shoulder extension). That applies to the face-away cable curls on this page. Set the shoulder before the load.

The preacher curls stay in this slot as long at the elbow, short at the shoulder. What it's for explains what that means and why it matters.

Setup. Preacher pad steep, upper arm near vertical, single-arm by default. Cable pulley a little below a right angle to the hand, upper arm slightly behind the torso. Setup has the full list.

Terms on this page

long head: The head of a muscle that crosses the shoulder joint as well as the elbow: on the triceps it only stretches with the arm raised, on the biceps only with the arm behind the body.

moment arm: The perpendicular distance between the joint axis and the line of force.

shoulder extension: The upper arm travelling back behind the line of the body.

external rotation: Turning a limb outward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face forward or up, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more to the side. Short of it, an incline curl drifts in toward the midline.
