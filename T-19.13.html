How to read an exercise page · T-19.13

A bare lift name means the barbell version

When a client or a coach says "deadlift", "bench press" or "back squat" with no other word, they mean the barbell version. Read a bare name as the barbell lift, always. If she wants a variation, she will name it.

What a bare name means

# Deadlift

Means: Barbell conventional deadlift. Not the Romanian deadlift, not a trap bar.

# Bench press

Means: Barbell flat bench press. Not incline, not a dumbbell press.

# Back squat

Means: Barbell back squat. Not a hack, pendulum or Smith machine squat.

Why it is a rule and not a style note. It caused a real error. A deadlift question was once answered from the Romanian deadlift page. "Deadlift" is in that page's name, and the conventional deadlift had no page of its own at the time. The answer was about the wrong lift.

Hold the rule even when a variation seems the better match. A page about a variation may look more relevant to her question. Answer about the barbell lift anyway. She knows the word for the variation she wants, and she will use it.

The companion rule runs the other way. When a session is named after a competition lift, "squat day" or "a bench session", that names the strength path too. Building a session covers it.
