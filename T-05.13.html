Setting effort and load in pregnancy and postpartum

In pregnancy and the postpartum return she trains at 2 to 3 reps in reserve. The weight is whatever satisfies that, start to finish.

Reps in reserve (RIR) means how many more reps she could have done when the set stopped. At 2 to 3 RIR she stops two or three reps short of failure. The anchor does not move across the pregnancy. It does not move across the postpartum return. As her capacity changes, the load follows it down and then back up. Nobody rewrites the program.

Reps in reserve in pregnancy and the postpartum return

3-2 | The anchor. Every set stops here, or lighter

Why bracing is not managed directly. We do not take her lifting belt away. We reduce the weight so the set sits at 2 to 3 RIR or lighter. A lighter set needs less of a brace, so the bracing demand falls on its own. This is the same load model as everyone else, applied to a population. Load is an output, not an input. Effort and intensity teaches it.

A worked example. Before pregnancy she squatted a weight for eight at 2 RIR. This month the same effort arrives at a lighter weight. You write nothing new. The set still stops at 2 to 3 RIR, and the bar shows what that weighs today.

Untrained before she fell pregnant? Same model, just slower progression. Only the rate changes.

Run it in this order, every session

Test. Nothing formal. Her training is the assessment. The tests don't tell you anything training doesn't.

Read. Comfort or discomfort. If there isn't any discomfort, nothing changes.

Do. Hold 2 to 3 RIR. Let the load fall where it falls.

Change. Only when she reports discomfort, when first-trimester fatigue arrives, or when a watch item from the calendar page appears.

Escalate. A stop sign ends the session. The stop-the-session page holds the list and the destination.

Never in pregnancy

The 140 bpm heart-rate cap is dead. Do not use it. Where you want an intensity gauge, use RPE 12 to 14 on the Borg 6 to 20 scale (Borg scale).

No one-rep-max testing in pregnancy.

Borg scale, where you want an intensity gauge

12-14 | Somewhat hard. The pregnancy intensity gauge

Terms on this page

RIR: Reps in reserve. How many more reps she could have done when the set stopped. 2 RIR means she stopped two reps short of failure.

Borg scale: A rating of how hard the whole effort feels, from 6 (nothing) to 20 (all out). 12 to 14 feels somewhat hard.
