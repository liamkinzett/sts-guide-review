Why an exercise works the muscle, and when it stops

She asks why the exercise works the muscle, or where in the rep it stops working. This page walks Levers and moment arms in the order you use it. The per-movement answers live on the exercise pages.

Test. Two questions, and both are geometry.

Check

Have you found the point in the range where the load produces the most turning force at the joint (torque)? Not where the muscle is stretched, and not where it burns.

At the point you want her to hold or slow, is the muscle actually loaded?

yes: Answer her from the resistance curve. Say where the load's torque meets what she can produce, and place the hold there.

no: Find where the load does produce torque at that joint. Move the hold or the pause there. Tempo and pauses owns the prescription.

Read. Keep the three properties separate before you answer anything: her strength curve, the resistance curve, and muscle length.

Muscle length will not tell you where the load stops. On the wrist, the one joint this has been checked against, what looked like a muscle-length effect was substantially geometry. It was a 50% drop in moment arm (moment arm). Levers and moment arms carries the full reasoning, and how much confidence to put on it. Apply that reasoning here, rather than re-deriving it.

Do.

In this order

Answer from the resistance curve against her strength curve. Where the load's torque meets what she can produce is where the exercise is hard. That is the geometry you can talk through with her.

For where tension peaks on a named movement, go to the exercise page. A page gives only three reasons. The moment arm, the muscle length, or the position of the machine's cam. Every exercise at a glance lists where tension peaks for every taught movement in one table.

Place holds and slow work where the muscle is loaded. Tempo and pauses owns the prescription.

Change. What moves on these mechanics is owned elsewhere. Which exercise to pick is Choosing exercises. Tempo and pause placement is Tempo and pauses. The per-movement tension answer is the exercise pages.

On every exercise page, the range-of-motion line is the load position: where the external load peaks. Where the muscle is longest is a second, separate line.

Escalate.

Where it goes

# A per-machine resistance profile, asked for exactly

sub: Read it by feel and by geometry

Where it goes: Say where tension peaks from coaching experience of the machine, as the exercise page does, and say that is what it is. Do not quote a measured profile.

# Pain in the movement

Where it goes: Pain rules and when to refer.

# Not a training question at all

Where it goes: The coaching relationship.

Reason from the movement, not a number

Never quote a length-tension figure for a hip, knee, shoulder or elbow. Reason from the resistance curve instead.

Never quote a measured resistance profile for a machine. Read it by feel and by geometry, and tell her that is how you read it.

Terms on this page

torque: The turning force on a joint. The further the load sits from the joint, the more of it there is. It is the force times its moment arm, so the same force can produce very different torque at different joint angles.

moment arm: The perpendicular distance between the joint axis and the line of force.
