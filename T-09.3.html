Effort and intensity · T-09.3

When form breaks, the set is over

If bar speed dies, position collapses or range of motion shortens, the set has ended. It does not matter that the program said two more reps. Movement quality outranks the number.

This is not a concession to the number. It replaces it. On every loaded compound, form breaks before she runs out of strength. The point where form breaks (mechanical failure) arrives first. The point where she cannot move the bar at all (volitional failure) comes after it. The reps between those two points are the ones that produce injuries and teach bad patterns.

Why it works. Range of motion is the first thing to go and the last thing anyone notices. A rep that got easier because it got shorter reads as progress in a logbook. It is the opposite. So the standard is the rep she can still perform to the same depth at the same speed. It is not the rep she can complete.

What it looks like. Her squat is written for ten. Rep eight is a little slower but the same depth. Rep nine stops two inches higher and her chest drops. The set ended at eight. Rep nine was a different exercise.

Watch for

A client who has learned that finishing the prescribed reps is the goal. She will find a way to finish them. The way she finds is the fault you will be correcting for the next three months.

Say it like this

If the last rep looks different to the first one, that's the end of the set. I'd rather you stop at eight good ones than grind out twelve where the last four are a different exercise.

Terms on this page

mechanical failure: The point in a set where she can no longer perform the rep with the same depth, speed and position. It arrives before she is truly unable to move the weight.

volitional failure: The point where she cannot complete another rep no matter how hard she tries. Also called true failure.
