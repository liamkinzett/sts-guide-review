Shoulder: the order of operations

Read a shoulder in this order: core and pelvis, then rib cage on pelvis, then shoulder blade on rib cage, then the joint itself. The joint itself is the ball-and-socket (glenohumeral joint). It is a hard sequence. Skip a link and you cannot get a read on the shoulder at all.

The chain, outermost first

Core and lumbopelvic control | Nothing above it is stable until this is

Rib cage on pelvis | Ribs stacked over the hips, not flared

Shoulder blade on rib cage | Full contact, held while the arm moves

Glenohumeral joint | The last link, and the one she points at

Why these links. People fail movements in two places: at the core and at the shoulder blade. Both are purely voluntary. Nobody falls into core stability or shoulder-blade stability (scapular stability) by accident. They have to be produced, every rep.

What it means on the floor. If a link earlier in the chain is wrong, everything downstream is skewed. Fix the earliest failing link first. A shoulder problem often stops being a shoulder problem.

Terms on this page

glenohumeral joint: The ball-and-socket joint where the upper arm bone meets the shoulder blade. It is what most people mean by "the shoulder".

scapular stability: The shoulder blade held still on the rib cage while the arm works.
