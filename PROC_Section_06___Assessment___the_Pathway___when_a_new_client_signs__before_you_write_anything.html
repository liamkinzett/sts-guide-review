When a new client signs, before you write anything

This page is the topic's rules in the order you use them across the intake window. The window opens when her onboard material lands in your inbox and closes when you send the onboarding video. You do not speak to her inside it, except for the one break named below.

Stop

Route one look-alike first. The client you inherit mid-phase is not this presentation. Inheriting a client governs her, and she is not re-primed. Her first month is reading rather than rewriting.

1 · Test. Open her Drive folder. Two documents should be waiting: the completed onboard form and the sales call summary. Read both. Where no summary exists, build from the form alone and build conservatively. Fill the gaps at her first check-in rather than guessing now.

Then read all seven things out of those documents before you decide anything: goal, height and bodyweight, training age, available days, injury history, lifestyle, equipment. The order is advisory. Having all seven in front of you first is not.

The one break in the no-contact default: the two documents conflict on something that would change what you build. Then you contact her and ask before you build.

2 · Read.

What you are reading for

# Goal

sub: The brief. Know it off by heart.

The read: The form gets the tidy answer and the call gets the real one.

# Height and weight

sub: An extreme, if there is one

The read: It is not there to classify her. Her maintenance passes to the Nutrition Manual.

# Training age

sub: One overall assumption

The read: Classify by how fast she adapts rather than years trained, read against the classification table. When the signals disagree, classify down.

# Her effort ratings

sub: Not built on yet

The read: Anchor prescriptions to load and reps until she has shown you an accurate rating.

# Days

sub: Build for the number she hits in a bad week

The read: A range means build the lower number. She earns the rest.

# Injuries and equipment

sub: Exercise selection and substitutions before she ever sees the program

The read: Assume a commercial gym unless her answers say otherwise.

3 · Do.

Do, in this order

Settle the pathway off her stated goal and nothing else. The whole pathway settles at intake.

Pick the emphasis path. Powerlifting strength, powerbuilding or hypertrophy. Fat loss is a nutritional context rather than a fourth path.

If she told you she wants to build, build. A recommendation is appropriate in three situations only. She asks, she has questions about the road ahead, or her goal genuinely needs more than one phase.

Everyone primes first. Everyone means everyone at intake. The phases sets the length.

Record three things before you open the program. Her assumed training age, the pathway and one line of reasoning. Three lines and about ninety seconds.

Write program one. Put the progression in the core days where her week is unpredictable. Write substitutions only for genuinely obscure equipment.

4 · Change. What waits for the end of priming and must not be forced at intake:

  • The per-pattern classification. At intake you carry one assumed level. A pattern you cannot classify is marked unknown and programmed as a beginner's would be. Revise sooner if week one contradicts you.
  • The body composition read, off her first check-in photos, in bands rather than figures. It never triggers a recommendation. The number never leaves your side of the conversation, and no number ever reopens the cut conversation later.
  • The movement screen, run from footage you choose across priming. It decides what she can be loaded on now, not whether she may load.

    • What does not change: her goal. You do not override it. The only exceptions are a goal that does not make sense, is unrealistic or is unsafe. Your read on what she should want is not a reason to override what she does want.

    5 · Escalate.

    Verdict

    Missed periods on the form alongside an underweight read

    A referral conversation rather than a programming one. Pain rules and when to refer has the line.
