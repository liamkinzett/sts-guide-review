Fatigue and recovery · T-03.3

Local versus systemic fatigue

Fatigue is not one thing. In the broadest sense, it is a temporary reduction in the performance she could produce in a fresher state.

That reduction can show up as:

  • lower force output;
  • fewer repetitions;
  • slower bar speed;
  • worse coordination;
  • reduced motivation or focus;
  • poorer tolerance for volume;
  • or a general sense that her system is not running at its usual level.

    • The important word is temporary. Fatigue is not the same thing as a permanent loss of fitness. Good training often creates fatigue as part of the process that eventually produces adaptation. So the coaching question is never how to stop her getting fatigued. It is what kind of fatigue this is, and whether it is an acceptable cost of the training she is doing.

    A useful first split is local versus systemic. Some fatigue sits in the muscle that did the work (local fatigue). Some sits in the whole body, more broadly (systemic fatigue). You use the split every time you program. You judge it by eye, because nothing in the gym measures either.

    Two observations show the two sides.

    Compared

    columns: Systemic | Local

    What you see: Sleep loss lowers her output on compound lifts. Single-joint lifts hold | Work at long muscle lengths leaves more nerve fatigue and muscle damage. It lasts up to 48 hours

    What it tells you: The cost is whole-body and depends on the task | The cost is attached to one muscle and to an exercise choice

    What moves it: Sleep, stress, total training load and heavy loads | Which exercises she does, and where in the rep they load the muscle

    How it is used. Hold her systemic fatigue roughly steady and move local fatigue to where you want it. That is how a specialisation block works. Heavier weights bring more systemic fatigue. Isolation work brings mostly local fatigue. A deload sheds systemic fatigue while keeping load in the program. The phases and Effort and intensity both lean on this split.

    Why this is your call. The split is useful, and it is judged rather than measured. No number tells you how much of each she carries. You read it from her logbook, her lifts and her report. Use it, and know that you are using a model.

    Terms on this page

    local fatigue: Tiredness in the muscle that did the work. It lowers that muscle's output and leaves the rest of her fresh.

    systemic fatigue: Tiredness that affects the whole body, not one muscle. It shows as lower output across every lift and a flatter client.
