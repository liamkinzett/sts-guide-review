Building a glute-focused lower body session

A glute session is built by the rules already in this topic, run in order. Before you build anything, check this is a bias job at all. A client who wants glutes and is training them last on a day she shares with quads does not need more glute volume. She needs them moved. Nothing below is a new rule.

Test: three questions, in this order

Are glutes trained fresh anywhere in her week? If not, stop here and move them. The first step of bringing up a lagging muscle costs nothing in recovery and is very often enough on its own.

Do her glute sets sit at the top of her band for her classification? Check the volume table in Volume. If she has headroom, add.

Is she already at the top? Hold a dominant group at maintenance and spend what you free up here. Split design has the maintenance ratio.

Four rules decide the session before you pick an exercise

Glutes go where she is freshest. This outranks everything else about order.

Cover different portions of the glute with the compounds, then one or two isolations.

Exercise count is calculated, never looked up.

The per-session ceiling on hard sets for one muscle holds. Volume owns the figure.

Build it on the length axis. Every exercise at a glance lists where each glute movement is hardest, and the library splits cleanly.

Where each glute movement is hardest

# In the stretch (lengthened position)

Exercises: Romanian deadlift

# Mid to stretch (mid-to-lengthened)

Exercises: Split squat; Lunge

# Mid-range

Exercises: Step-up

# Mid to short

Exercises: Cable kickback

# Short (shortened position)

Exercises: Hip thrust; 45 degree hip extension

Take one from each end, not two from the same end. That is the coverage rule, applied through where each exercise is hardest. Pairing has already judged every pair in this group. Read the verdict rather than deciding it here.

The week

# 1 · Glute day

The heavier compound, such as the Romanian deadlift | Main lift | Glutes go first, while she is freshest. The heavier of the two compounds opens

The second compound, from the other end, such as the hip thrust | Second compound | Covers the portion the first movement missed

One isolation, such as the cable kickback | Isolation | Fills what the compounds could not reach and completes the volume target

Effort and intensity owns the rep bands and the reps in reserve. Volume owns the set counts.

Change. Re-test at the end of the block with the same three questions. A bias block that produced nothing usually skipped step one. Adding volume is visible work and moving an exercise up the session is not.

How many muscles at once. Bias as few muscles as the goal allows. Every set you add to one target is taken from another, and a block that prioritises everything has prioritised nothing.

Escalate

# Pain during or after these movements

sub: Not a programming problem

What to do: Pain rules and when to refer owns it.

# A glute that does not respond across two full blocks of correct application

sub: A conversation about her data first

What to do: Reading her training data comes before another selection change.

Terms on this page

lengthened position: The point in the rep where the working muscle is at its longest while it carries the load. It describes the muscle's length, not whether she feels a stretch.

mid-to-lengthened: The part of the range from mid-way down to the stretched bottom.

shortened position: The muscle is working while it is bunched short. For the glute that is full hip extension, locked out at the top of a thrust.
