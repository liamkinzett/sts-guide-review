Counterbalance squat

The counterbalance squat is the first rung of the ladder, where a complete beginner starts. A light plate held at shoulder height at arm's length strips the load almost entirely.

What it changes. The stance is narrower than her low bar stance. Ribs tucked, tailbone underneath, knees travelling forward over the toes, torso fully upright. Of every squat, it asks the most of the knees and the least of the hips and back.

When to use it

# A complete beginner

sub: The first rung

Do this: Start here. She builds at this level before she advances to the goblet squat.

# Pass 4 of the butt-wink test

sub: Only after the other three passes all wink

Do this: Use it only if the normal, the heels-elevated and the heels-elevated-goblet passes all show the wink (butt wink).

# An accessory for the wink

sub: A hold, then an inch of tension

Do this: A counterbalance squat hold at the bottom, two to three minutes. Then she lifts an inch off the bottom and holds tension.

# The start of a depth regression

sub: Bring the weight closer, keep the range the same

Do this: Counterbalance box squat, then goblet box squat. That brings her centre of mass closer while keeping range the same. Then lower the box before you add load.

# A graded ladder with a timeframe

sub: Light counterbalance, goblet, front, Bulgarian, high bar

Do this: Light counterbalance squat, then goblet squat, then front squat, then the rear-foot-elevated split squat (Bulgarian split squat), then high bar. Then planned steps of more (graded exposure) over six to eight weeks. The Bulgarian split squat has its own page in Glutes.

When to leave the pattern. If even a heels-elevated counterbalance squat holding suspension straps (TRX) is beyond her, the squat conversation does not begin. Leg press, leg extension and hamstring curl first, and build resilience.

A beginner who is weaker than gravity does not get a barbell on her back. Under a load running down her spine (axial load) she goes hypervigilant, and recruits her lower back and calves simply to stay upright. If she cannot stand on one leg, she does not squat with two.

Terms on this page

Bulgarian split squat: A split squat with the back foot raised on a bench behind her. It has its own page in Glutes.

TRX: Suspension straps hung from above. She holds them for balance while she squats.

butt wink: At the bottom of the squat her pelvis tucks under and her lower back rounds. It usually shows up when she runs out of hip range or loses her brace.

graded exposure: Building her tolerance to a movement in planned steps, each a little more than the last, over a stated number of weeks.

axial load: Load running down the length of the spine, as with a barbell on the back or overhead.
