Choosing exercises · T-13.8

Three reasons to rotate: loadability, joint strain, variety

An exercise changes for one of three reasons. None of them is novelty.

The three reasons

# Loadability

sub: Change how much fatigue the pattern costs

Why: Rotating changes how much weight a movement can carry (loadability), which changes how much fatigue it generates. Moving from a barbell hinge to a machine version lowers the whole-body cost of the same pattern, which is what a cut needs. Moving the other way raises it, which is what a build can afford.

# Joint strain

sub: Spread the exposure

Why: Different variations of a pattern load different structures. Rotating spreads that exposure rather than concentrating it. That matters most when nobody is watching the sessions in between. Coaching in person, a developing irritation shows up in front of you and gets changed the week it appears. Coaching online, it is reported after it hurts. We coach online. Rotation is partly insurance.

# Variety

sub: A program she wants to run

Why: Clients get bored. A program she does not want to run is a program she runs badly. The effect on her results is larger than any difference between two comparable exercises.

Why this list matters. None of these is the reason usually given. The common story is that a new exercise produces a fresh stimulus because muscles get used to old ones. A new exercise does not earn a fresh stimulus just by being new. A coach who believes it does will rotate the wrong things at the wrong time.

Watch for

Rotating a movement that is still producing progress. All three reasons are about cost, exposure or adherence. None of them is about the exercise having stopped working.

Terms on this page

loadability: How much weight a movement can carry. The more it can carry, the more whole-body fatigue it generates.
