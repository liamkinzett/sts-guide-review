Coaching through perimenopausal symptoms

A client in perimenopause gets the same tools you use for any client. Do not let "she's perimenopausal" replace running the ordinary stall list.

Perimenopause is the years of change before her periods stop. Menopause is the point they stop for good. Perimenopause has no tools of its own in this topic. What you have are tools that do not depend on naming the cause.

Presentation by presentation

# New client, hot flushes, poor sleep

sub: Sleep is a training variable

Do this: Do not add volume to an under-slept client. Fatigue and recovery covers it.

# Recovery fell off a cliff

sub: Run the standard four-step order before assuming a cause

Do this: Is she training hard enough, has her technique drifted, is she recovering, then volume. Keep heavy compound work in rather than lightening everything.

# Night sweats, flat sessions, one terrible night

sub: The principle holds whichever night caused it

Do this: Do not add volume to compensate. No rule picks the worst night over the average for any client, menopausal or not.

# Diffuse joint aches, nothing acutely injured

sub: Sort pain from discomfort first

Do this: Where nothing is acutely wrong, sort her movements into three groups: ones that ease it, ones that do nothing and ones that provoke it. Train the first two. Pain rules and when to refer holds the sort.

# Hot flush mid-set, she goes red and sits down

sub: Not a training-safety event

Do this: Pause, let her recover, resume at her call. There is no more specific instruction to give.

# Weight up with no diet change, blaming training

sub: The scale is hiding the change, not measuring it

Do this: Fat mass can rise and lean mass fall while total weight barely moves. Change what she measures. The next pages cover it.

Why this is a judgement call. A coach reading "perimenopause" expects a perimenopause-specific mechanism. You work instead from the same tools you use for any client. You do not assume the hormonal cause explains the presentation better than a training or recovery cause would. Do not let "she's perimenopausal" substitute for actually running the stall list. The hormonal explanation is not free just because it is plausible.

Verdict

Cycle, perimenopause and menopause questions beyond training go to her GP or a women's health specialist.

Once the tools above are exhausted and something still does not resolve.

Watch for

Blaming "the hormones" first. Run the ordinary diagnostic first. It applies to any client at any age, before a stall, a weight change or a flat session is put down to hormones.
