The phases · T-07.3

Mobility clears in three regions, judged from video

Mobility clears in three regions: hip, shoulder and spine. You judge each one from her training footage, not from a test session.

The three regions

# Hip

sub: Depth without her pelvis tucking under

Clears when: She reaches depth without her pelvis visibly tucking under at the bottom (butt wink).

# Shoulder

sub: Full overhead reach without the lower back helping

Clears when: She reaches fully overhead without arching her lower back to get there (lumbar compensation).

# Spine

sub: Rounds and arches through its length without pain

Clears when: She can round and arch her spine one section at a time, with no pain.

These are the functional versions of the question. Can she get into the positions the program is about to ask her to load? You are watching the movements she is already doing, which is why her priming program has to cover every pattern you need to see.

Judge only what is visible. Under a bar of around 50% of her bodyweight the spine already flexes a little, even when she braces well. You cannot see that change, and no lifter avoids it.

Do not screen for an absence of spinal movement. Screen for the gross loss of position you can actually see.

The same applies at the knee. In a heavy squat the knee normally drifts inwards or outwards by up to about three centimetres. A small inward drift is not a fault to correct. One leg or two owns the knee figure.

A failure does not stop her training. It puts targeted work into her warm-ups, and it extends priming until the position is available.

On the degree measurements you may have seen elsewhere. Passive hip flexion figures, angles for turning the thigh outward (external rotation), targets for twisting the upper back (thoracic rotation). They need a person in the room with a goniometer. We coach remotely and cannot take those measurements with any accuracy. A criterion nobody can apply is worse than no criterion at all. Judge what you can see.

Terms on this page

butt wink: At the bottom of the squat her pelvis tucks under and her lower back rounds. It usually shows up when she runs out of hip range or loses her brace.

lumbar compensation: The lower back moves or arches to supply range that another joint has run out of. Reaching overhead with a stiff shoulder is a common example.

external rotation: Turning a limb outward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face forward or up, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more to the side. A muscle that does it is an external rotator.

thoracic rotation: Twisting through the upper and middle back, the part of the spine the ribs attach to.
