Variations

Walking, reverse and deficit reverse are the straight-line versions (sagittal plane), moving forward and back. Curtsy and lateral lunges are the side-to-side versions (frontal plane), and STS programs both. Seven straight-line versions are programmed. Here is what each one changes.

The programmed straight-line versions

# Dumbbell walking lunge

sub: The unconstrained version, and a diagnostic

What changes: Nothing constrains her. She has to decelerate, land and transfer weight in every plane on every step.

Use it as: An instrument as much as a stimulus. Pick a maximally unconstrained movement, reason from the visible fault back to the missing quality, then retest against the same movement.

# Dumbbell reverse lunge

sub: Loads back-leg hip extension

What changes: Stepping back removes the deceleration demand and keeps a negative shin angle. The back leg opens at the hip (hip extension) cleanly.

Also: The FFE split squat, the reverse lunge and the stationary lunge are the three ways STS loads back-leg hip extension. They are interchangeable for that job, and Choosing exercises owns the choice.

# Barbell reverse lunge

sub: Same pattern, bar on the back

What changes: Coach it to the same standard as the dumbbell version. The bracing point made for the barbell split squat is about that exercise. Don't carry it across.

# Smith machine reverse lunge

sub: Same pattern, fixed bar

What changes: Coach it to the same standard. The Smith machine cost named on the split squat page is about the front-foot-elevated split squat specifically. Don't carry it across.

# Dumbbell reverse deficit lunge

sub: Standing foot raised on a platform

What changes: A two to four inch platform under the standing foot. It extends the bottom of the range.

# Barbell reverse deficit lunge

sub: The deficit above, with a bar

What changes: The deficit description above, and nothing else.

# Smith machine reverse deficit lunge

sub: The deficit above, on the Smith machine

What changes: The deficit description above, and nothing else.

Deficit or flat. The deficit buys range at the bottom, and a clean flat lunge is the gate to it. See *Deficit or flat*.

Curtsy and lateral lunges. Both are programmed as the frontal-plane expression of the pattern. The setup, execution standard, cues and load position on these pages are written for the straight-line versions and don't transfer to them.

The stationary lunge is programmed, and it has two more jobs. It is the bodyweight test on the Split squat page (When to use it), and it is how you find her split-squat stride length.

Why the reverse lunge is the glute-bias answer. It removes the deceleration demand and preserves a negative shin angle (negative shin angle). That is what makes it the cleaner hip-extension driver. It is the same job the table above gives it alongside the FFE split squat and the stationary lunge. The walking lunge's extra demand is instability, used deliberately as a diagnostic. It is not an increase in glute stimulus.

Terms on this page

sagittal plane: Forward-and-back movement, like a squat, a hinge or a curl.

frontal plane: Side-to-side movement, like a lateral lunge or a side raise.

hip extension: Opening the hip so the thigh travels behind the body line. It is the glute's main job, and the reverse lunge loads it on the back leg.

negative shin angle: Her front shin leans back, so the knee sits behind the ankle rather than out over the toes. It shifts the work away from the front quad and towards the hips and hamstrings.
