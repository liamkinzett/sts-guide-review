Direct arm work only when she asks for it

A female client gets four to six direct arm sets a week unless she has made arms a priority.

This is one of the clearest places our programming differs by sex, and it is deliberate.

Compared

columns: Male client | Female client

Triceps movements: Overhead and non-overhead | One or the other

Curl variations: Two, a heavier mid-range one plus a lighter one | One

Direct arm work: Close to always programmed | Only when she has made it a priority

We coach women. The right-hand column is the default and the left is the exception. A program that looks light on arms is usually correct.

Where the figure comes from. Four to six direct sets a week is the working number when arms are not a stated priority. It is a low figure because the sets her arms receive from pressing and pulling (indirect volume) are doing real work. Read it alongside the splits and the pulling page, never on its own.

Why it works. Close-grip pressing is triceps work, not a substitute for it. It does not need direct work stacked on top. The same logic runs through the pulling movements and her biceps.

The figure stops being defensible the moment the indirect work stops. A lower-body block, a client who cannot press, a week with no pulling. In each, four direct sets is genuinely four sets, and that is under-dosed.

Watch for

Two failures in opposite directions. Adding male-pattern arm volume to her program by habit. Leaving arms at four sets for a client who has told you plainly she wants them.

The second is worse. She said what she wanted and the program ignored her. The client's goal is where coaching starts.

Say it like this

Your arms are getting hit hard on every push and pull day already. From a growth perspective they're already covered. If you want them to be a focus, say the word and we'll build it in properly rather than just tacking on curls.

Terms on this page

indirect volume: Hard sets a muscle receives from exercises that are not aimed at it. Her triceps get it from pressing and her biceps from pulling.
