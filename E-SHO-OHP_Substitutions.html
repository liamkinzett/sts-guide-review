Shoulders · E-SHO-OHP#Substitutions

Swaps

The ladder is the method. You do not change the pattern. You move down the rungs until one loads without provoking the shoulder, then you keep the rest of the session intact. The endpoint is stated plainly: "we can go all the way down that spectrum to a dumbbell front raise".

The vertical press ladder, top to bottom

Smith machine overhead press. The most externally stabilised rung, and the first one to reach for. STS doesn't program the Smith machine overhead press as its own exercise. Use it to place her, then write the machine shoulder press or the next rung.

Seated dumbbell overhead press. The free-weight rung. Often the first one she tolerates.

Kneeling landmine press. The press angle comes off vertical, which is usually what the shoulder objects to. A cable press or a half-kneeling dumbbell press does the same job; STS doesn't program either as its own exercise.

Alternating dumbbell front raise. The terminal rung. Chosen for the joint range rather than for the delt. See Front raise.

Swaps by situation

# Equipment missing

sub: No rack, or no barbell

Swap to: Seated DB overhead press; Machine shoulder press

Why: A Swiss bar or a seated press from the rack also avoids the dumbbell kick-up (Swiss bar).

# Injury

sub: The press provokes her shoulder

Do this: The ladder above, plus two levers that are not swaps at all. Slow the tempo (Tempo and pauses). Or run the lateral raises before the press (pre-exhaust) so the same perceived effort arrives at a lower absolute load (Effort and intensity).

# Home

sub: No gym access

Why: STS doesn't coach home training, so there is no home swap to give.

Terms on this page

Swiss bar: A multi-grip bar with parallel neutral handles set inside a rectangular frame.

pre-exhaust: Tiring one muscle with an isolation exercise first, so that it is the limiter in the compound lift that follows.
