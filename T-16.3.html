Warming up · T-16.3

Load decides the ramp, not habit

The number of feeder sets is set by the load of the working set that follows. It is not a fixed count applied to every exercise on the page.

A specific warm-up earns its place above 80% of her one rep max. A specific warm-up means light sets of the movement itself. Her one rep max (1RM) is the most she can lift once.

The reason it works up there is neural. Rehearsing the movement at rising loads improves how the nervous system recruits and fires the muscle before a near-maximal effort.

Below that, the picture changes. Ten reps at 50% of the working load before hard sets at 80% bought nothing over going in completely cold. In high repetition work the opening reps of the working set are their own specific warm-up.

This changes an old habit. The instruction used to be to feeder every exercise, always. Most STS muscle-building work sits below 80% of one rep max, so on most of the program that habit was buying least. Two feeders across eight exercises is sixteen preparation sets in a session. That is a lot of time and fatigue spent on sets that prepare nothing.

Why feeders still exist at all. Preparing the muscle to produce force is not their only job. A feeder finds the right working load. It rehearses a pattern. It gives a joint the chance to object. It gives you a look at her first reps on video. Those jobs are why the ramp survives on a light day, in a smaller form.

Watch for

Reading this as permission to skip the ramp on a heavy day. Above 80% is exactly where the ramp earns its place. The cut is on the light work, never on the heavy work.

Terms on this page

feeder set: A light set done before the working sets to rehearse the movement and find the day's load. It is never counted as volume.

ramp: The run of feeder sets that climbs toward the first working set.

1RM: The heaviest weight she can lift once with good form.
