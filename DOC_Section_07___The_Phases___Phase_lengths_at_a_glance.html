The six phases at a glance

Every length in this table is a starting point that its own rule tells you when to leave. The one thing that does not flex is the realisation phase.

How long each phase runs

Priming | 4 | The minimum. No upper limit: she leaves when she is ready

Hypertrophy | 3-6 | Default six, extended while overload continues

Cut | 3-6 | Default six. It ends at the projection or with her

Holding | 3-12 | Planned at three to six, extended to twelve when she needs it

Deload | 1-2 | One week. Two only where fatigue is significant

Strength | 8-16 | Default twelve. The last four weeks are never shortened

The realisation four are the four you never take from. Realisation (realisation) is where fatigue comes off. At the eight week minimum, accumulation and transmutation share four weeks between them and realisation keeps its four.

How much work, and how hard. Each phase also sets her weekly dose and her effort. A hypertrophy block runs 15 to 20 hard sets per target muscle for an intermediate. A cut and a holding phase both drop to 10 to 15. Effort climbs from 3 reps in reserve (RIR) to 0 across a hypertrophy block. It floors at 1 in a cut. It sits at 2 to 3 through a holding phase. The pages on each phase explain why.

Hard sets per muscle per week, by phase

Hypertrophy block | 15-20 | The intermediate figure, on the muscles she is targeting

Cut | 10-15 | Holding tissue takes less work than building it

Holding phase | 10-15 | Maintenance only

Effort by phase, in reps in reserve

3-0 | Hypertrophy block: 3 in week one, falling to one week at 0 on isolation work

3-1 | Cut: 3 down to 1, and 1 is the floor

3-2 | Holding phase: 2 to 3 throughout, with no progression to zero

Terms on this page

realisation: The last four weeks of a strength phase. Fatigue is shed so that the strength she has built can show up on test day.

RIR: Reps in reserve. How many more reps she could have done when the set stopped. 2 RIR means she stopped two reps short of failure.
