The coaching relationship · T-42.5

When a check-in does not arrive

Do not chase a client for a check-in. The check-in is hers to invest in. This is the usual choice, and it is read alongside the next page, never instead of it.

Why you do not chase. You should not be wasting your time as a coach trying to chase somebody down to get them to check in. The effort is asymmetric. Are you putting in a lot more effort than she is willing to give?

Whose responsibility it is. Not chasing puts the onus on her. She has to invest in this. It is her responsibility, not just yours to chase her down for information.

Not chasing is not the same as not asking. A missed check-in is a signal to have a conversation. Chasing is repeated unanswered prompting. Asking is one direct question about what is happening. This page rules out the first. The next page requires the second.

Why it works. Whether she meets the check-in standard is a very clear and distinct marker of whether this is the right coach-to-client relationship. It is information about the relationship, not just about the week.

Watch for

Three gentle nudges. That is chasing. Ask once, properly, and read the answer or the silence.
