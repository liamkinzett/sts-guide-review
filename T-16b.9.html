Mobility and stretching · T-16b.9

No loaded forward flexion in known osteoporosis or kyphosis

In known vertebral osteoporosis or spinal kyphosis, loaded forward flexion of the spine and rapid explosive spinal twisting are contraindicated. That means they must never be done. This is the exception to everything on the previous page, and it is not a judgement call.

Verdict

Known osteoporosis or kyphosis: no loaded forward flexion, no explosive twisting.

Suspect either one and she goes to her doctor. You never diagnose it.

What it rules out. Rows performed with a rounded spine. Sit-ups. Several common yoga and Pilates positions. The risk is the front of a vertebra crushing down, a wedge compression fracture, in bone that is already weakened (vertebral osteoporosis). A fixed forward rounding of the upper back (kyphosis) carries the same risk.

Why it sits in this topic. STS coaches women. Clients around and after menopause carry elevated osteoporosis risk. A client asking for a stretching or yoga routine is asking about exactly the movements this excludes. This is the topic she has prompted you to open. The female body owns the wider osteoporosis picture beyond the contraindication itself.

Why there is no deviation. The mechanism is a fracture in a bone that is already compromised. There is no version of the movement that is worth it.

Watch for

"Known" is doing work in that sentence. This applies to a diagnosed condition, not a suspicion. A coach who suspects one refers rather than diagnoses.

Terms on this page

vertebral osteoporosis: The bones of the spine have lost density and can fracture under loads a healthy spine would tolerate.

kyphosis: An exaggerated forward rounding of the upper back. It is usually diagnosed by a clinician.
