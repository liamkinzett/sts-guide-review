Choosing between them

Start from her problem, not from the list of presses. The flow settles the main choice. The list below it holds every problem this topic answers.

Which bench, from where she is

? Does she compete, or intend to?

yes: Competition-style bench, 21X1, from the first week of the strength phase. Once a week, two to three sets.

? Is she a new presser?

yes: Close grip, on tissue tolerance at end range.

? Does her position degrade on the way down?

yes: Spoto press. Feather touch or a paused hover an inch off, long pauses, moderate weight.

? Does she relax on the chest, or stick a few inches off it?

yes: Not the Spoto and not a board. The feather-touch descent, and upper-back tightness on the chest.

= Every barbell press she trains takes 2110. Pick the variation for the job from the list below.

Her problem, and the press that answers it

# She competes, or intends to

Do this: Competition-style bench, 21X1, from the first week of the strength phase. Once a week, two to three sets, for a competitive powerlifter.

# Physique goal

Do this: No competition-style bench. For her the flat barbell bench is optional; the Barbell bench press page in Chest holds when it earns a place.

# New presser

Do this: Close grip, on tissue tolerance at end range.

# Position degrades on the way down

Do this: Spoto press: feather touch or a paused hover an inch off, long pauses, moderate weight.

# Relaxes on the chest

Do this: Not the Spoto, not a board. The feather-touch descent.

# Cannot hold tension on the chest

Do this: Three-count pause bench.

# The bar slows (sticking point) a few inches off the chest

Do this: Not a board. It is lost upper-back tightness on the chest.

# Long-limbed, or benching in a shirt, triceps run out

Do this: Board or block press. Watch she does not become a board presser.

# Heavy work done, next press slot is to refine technique

Do this: Close grip, chains, boards or the floor press: same pattern, lower technical demand.

# Second bench exposure of the week, competitive powerlifter

Do this: Close grip or incline. Bigger lifter: overhead press, then chest press machine.

# Loading ceiling is the point on an incline

Do this: Barbell incline. Arm path and range the point: dumbbell.

# Sternum stuck down, cannot find a stacked rib cage

Do this: Externally supported low-incline press as a constraint. Landmine press for freedom of motion.

# Large deliberate arch shortening the range

Do this: Neutral-grip dumbbell press across flat, incline and upright.

# Weeks 1 to 4 of a block peak

Do this: Tempo bench, higher reps.

# Conjugate max-effort rotation

Do this: 3-board weeks 1 to 2, floor press week 3, close grip weeks 5 to 6, 1-board week 7, tested bench from week 9.

# Relearning the press after a layoff

Do this: 3-4/1/1/1.

# Close grip forcing her palms to turn down (pronation), elbows out

Do this: Swiss or neutral-grip bar, or dumbbells.

# Provocative shoulder

Do this: Cut the provocative part, keep the rest. Walk the ladder down: dumbbells, machine, cable, single arm. Rebuild from the floor press upward if the pattern has to be rebuilt.

# Pain the day after squatting

Do this: Move the sessions apart before changing the press.

Terms on this page

sticking point: The part of a lift where the bar slows or stops, the hardest point of the range for her.

pronation: Turning the forearm so the palm faces down or away. Of the foot, rolling inward so the arch flattens and the ankle drops in.
