Inheriting a client · T-41b.1

Read the program before you judge it

An inherited program is the visible half of a set of decisions. Most of what looks wrong is a decision you cannot see. So the first month is reading, not rewriting.

The invisible half is everything the previous coach learned about her: what she avoided, what hurt, what she could not do, what she asked for. None of it is on the page. All of it shaped the page.

Why it works. Every rule in this Library that looks odd on paper is a rule with a reason. The same is true of a colleague's program. Choosing exercises requires every exercise to name what it is buying, and an inherited exercise usually has an answer you have not been told.

In her first week with you

I'm going to run what you've got for a few weeks before I change anything. That way I can see how you respond to it rather than guessing.

The same logic applies one level up, to what a handover says about her. A label like "difficult", arriving with no context attached, is one coach's observation, not an established fact. Read it the way you read her program: for exposure and pattern, not for a verdict on the previous coach or on her. Test it against your own read over the first month before it shapes anything you do with her.

A standing restriction that arrives with her is the same. "She doesn't squat" or "no overhead work" is interrogated, never inherited. Pain rules and when to refer has the question to ask: is it structural, or did someone once tell her she could not?

And the same logic runs in reverse, when you are the one handing over. The invisible half, what she avoided, what hurt, what she could not do, what she asked for, is what you write down before you leave. Do not leave the cover coach to reconstruct it by inference. Run the completeness gate against your own program before you go, so the next coach gets a reading list instead of a mid-block discovery.

One thing she has not been through. She has passed none of priming's gates. There are three. The movement screen: footage of her moving, which decides what she can be loaded on now. The exit criteria: the standards she meets before priming ends. The per-pattern classification: a level written down for each movement pattern, rather than one assumed level.

She is not re-primed to earn them. What stands in for those gates is not a call you make alone. Escalate it before you assume she has passed them, and do not invent a screen of your own.

Watch for

The exercise you would never have chosen. It is the one most likely to have a reason. It is also where changing it costs the most trust.
