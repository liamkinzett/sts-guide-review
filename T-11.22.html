Split design · T-11.22

Two sessions close together: was the second one fresh?

Two sessions for the same muscle inside a day and a half count as two only if the second one was fresh. Exposures (exposures) are counted by fresh sessions, not by the calendar. Read the second session. Do not trust the calendar. Two glute sessions thirty-six hours apart may be two genuine exposures, or one exposure and one session run under leftover fatigue from the first.

Do not set a minimum number of hours between exposures. There is no such figure. What decides the case is the same read you use when a split underperforms: look at the second session for crossover fatigue (crossover fatigue). The video read from Effort and intensity is the tool.

Check

Do the second session's early sets move like her normal early sets?

Is her bar speed and position on set one as good as it was on set one of the first session?

yes: It was a fresh exposure. Count it.

no: Its early sets already look like the first session's late ones. It was not a fresh exposure, whatever the schedule says.

What follows. Where the read shows genuine fatigue carryover, treat the pair as one exposure with a light session attached rather than two. Then run the frequency arithmetic again to decide whether a third, properly spaced exposure is needed to hit her band.

Terms on this page

exposures: The number of separate sessions in a week in which a muscle is trained.

crossover fatigue: Fatigue that shows up in a session other than the one that caused it. Heavy hinging on a pull day limiting her bracing on a later hinge session is an example.
