Two types of pelvic floor dysfunction

Pelvic floor dysfunction presents two opposite ways, and you cannot tell which one you are looking at. Default to referral, not to teaching pelvic floor squeezes.

Those squeezes are Kegels (Kegels), and they are often taught as the fix for leaking.

The two presentations. A weak pelvic floor is the one everyone expects. But a large share of pelvic floor problems are an over-tight floor (hypertonic pelvic floor), not a weak one. The tension is held all day, often because the pressure system is compensating for something elsewhere. Both can leak and both can ache.

Why Kegels can make it worse. On an over-tight floor, Kegels are the last thing she needs. More contraction on an already-gripping pelvic floor makes it worse. What she needs instead is to let it down and lengthen it under control.

Why this is a referral, not a technique you hand over. Telling the two apart needs palpation, internal examination and grading. A coach never performs any of those. Teaching a generic Kegel without knowing which presentation is present risks making it actively worse.

Verdict

Pelvic floor symptoms go to a pelvic health physio before anyone teaches her a Kegel.

The referral is the whole answer. Build around what the physio finds.

Say it like this

I'm not going to teach you Kegels blind. For a lot of people that actually makes it worse. Let's get you in front of a pelvic health physio who can tell you which kind of problem this is. Then I'll build around what they find.

Terms on this page

Kegels: Deliberate squeezes of the pelvic floor muscles, often taught as the fix for leaking.

hypertonic pelvic floor: A pelvic floor that holds too much tension and cannot fully relax. It can leak and ache just as a weak one does, but squeezing makes it worse.
