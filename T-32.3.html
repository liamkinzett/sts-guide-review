Faults and what you can actually see · T-32.3

If you did not see it, you did not see it

Five faults are reported most confidently and cannot be screened on video at all. They are never written as observations. Say what you can see, which is usually the bar path or the head.

The Tier 1 list

# The onset of spinal flexion under load

sub: Flexion is present in every loaded lift, including the ones that look perfect

Why it cannot be screened: The spine flexes slightly under load from around half her body weight, whatever she does. The change is too small for a coach to detect by eye. This row is that small baseline change and nothing more.

Where it does not apply: Trunk flexion movements. On a crunch and its family, flexion is the rep standard and a flat back is the fault. Read the row against the movement.

# Small side-to-side knee travel in a squat

sub: Normal travel in a heavy squat runs to roughly three centimetres

Why it cannot be screened: A small inward drift is not a fault and cannot be measured by eye. This row is a planted foot moving side to side (frontal plane) and nothing else.

Not this row: The knee rotating about its own long axis with the foot free, on a leg extension or leg curl, is a separate fault. That one is screenable.

# Passive range measurements

sub: Hip flexion angles, rotation angles, measured with hands on her

Why it cannot be screened: These need a person in the room. We coach from video.

Not this row: An active screen judged by alignment, such as the one the cable curl page requires. That is a different thing and is not barred.

# Chronic elbow tendinopathy

sub: A slow injury, not an acute one

Why it cannot be screened: It presents over weeks, not in a rep. The answer is a movement swap, not a coaching correction, and Pain rules and when to refer comes first.

# Compliance with an unfakeable-sounding cue

sub: "Pull the bar apart" can be done with no upper back at all

Why it cannot be screened: You cannot see whether she did it.

Why it works. A fault you cannot see is a fault you will confirm whenever you expect it. That is not a failure of attention and more attention does not fix it. The list is the fix.

What this protects. A coach who stops reporting invisible faults has more room for the three things that are reliably visible. Those are over-extension, the head used as a release valve, and the involuntary slowdown across a hard set. Nearness to failure cannot be faked on video, which makes that last one worth more than everything on the list above.

Gross rounding is not on this list. You cannot see flexion begin. You can see a back round. If gross extension is visible then gross rounding is too, and a coach who says she saw the back round probably did.

The words a coach uses for it. "Rounded back", "her back rounds", "rounding", "she rounds at the bottom", "cat back", "the back rounds under load". None of them names a fault of its own, because gross rounding is a visible loss of position rather than a fault cluster. Route them to the movement.

Underneath is usually the bar off the midline, the slack never taken out of the bar, or the hips shooting up. On a deadlift the mechanism is a lost wedge: the pre-tension was taken out of the bar before the pull.

It is not the lift loading her back by design. Levers and moment arms explains it.

Do not read a report of rounding as a report of flexion onset. They are different claims, and only the second is unscreenable. And the scoping runs one way: it was written for the hinge and the hip extension, where flexion is the fault. On a trunk-flexion movement, flexion is the rep standard and "back stays flat" is the fault.

Watch for

"I think I can see a bit of rounding." You cannot. Say what you can see, which is usually the bar path or the head.

Cueing a neutral spine as though it were achievable and verifiable. It is neither.

Say when she worries about her back moving

Your back is not fragile. What we are avoiding is the very end of your range with a heavy bar on you, not the fact that it moves at all.

To yourself: would I bet on that if the video were two seconds shorter?

Terms on this page
