Motivation and adherence · T-44.2

Type what she runs on before you pick the tool

Type what she runs on before you pick a tool. Two questions, asked once, change every conversation after them. Is she moving toward something or away from something? Does the drive come from inside her or from outside?

Identifying which of these a client falls into is part of the job. Is it fear of losing something, or is she working toward something? Is she driven from within (intrinsic motivation) or by outside rewards and consequences (extrinsic motivation)? Which one works better for her?

Compared

columns: Toward | Away

What she says: "I want to deadlift double my bodyweight." | "I don't want to feel like this any more."

What it feels like: Striving. The carrot. | Survival. The stick.

How it burns: Steady while the goal is visible | Hot, and it fades when the threat fades

Fear of loss is real fuel and it produces behaviour. It is also survival rather than striving, and survival runs out once she feels safe again. Type her either way. Whether you build on the fear or build a second target underneath it is your judgement. The page on external motivation gives the second-target move.

Why it works. Every tool in this topic fits one kind of client and misfires on another. A deadline motivates a client who runs on outside structure and insults one who runs on her own standards. Typing her first is what stops you guessing.

Terms on this page

intrinsic motivation: Drive that comes from inside her: she trains because she values the work or the goal itself.

extrinsic motivation: Drive that comes from outside her: a reward, a deadline, a consequence or another person's approval.
