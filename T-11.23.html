Split design · T-11.23

No fixed days? Build a priority order, not a template

A split written against specific weekdays fails the first week her days off move. The fix is not a cleverer template. It is not writing one. Build her week as an ordered list of sessions by priority, and whatever days she gets, the list runs from the top.

What the list looks like. For a client whose goal is her glutes, session one is her priority lower day, trained fresh. Session two is her upper day. Session three is her second lower day. Session four carries accessory volume for muscles that were never the point. Whatever days she gets, she starts at the top.

How it works. The list is ordered the same way you protect priorities when a week compresses. The work that serves her goal first, accessory volume last.

A four-day week runs the top four sessions. A two-day week runs the top two, following the same selection Training around life gives a compressed week. Very short daily sessions are the same case taken further, and Training around life covers those too.

Nothing about the list itself changes from week to week. Only how far down it she gets to changes.

Watch for

Using this as an excuse never to re-evaluate the split. If her roster settles into a stable pattern, that is a new fixed week. Re-evaluate the split against it properly, rather than running a priority list built for chaos that has stopped being chaotic.

Say it like this

Your program is a list, not a timetable. Whatever days you get this week, start at the top and work down. Session one is the one that matters most, so it never gets skipped.
