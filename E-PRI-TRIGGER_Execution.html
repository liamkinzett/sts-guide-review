How to do it

The technique is pin and stretch (pin and stretch). It is the opposite of rolling like a pizza.

At each tender point

Find a tender point. Stop. Do not travel through it.

Under the pressure, she actively bends and straightens the joint next to it through its range. On the outer thigh that is active knee flexion and extension. Heel to the backside, then lock out with emphasis on the inner-knee quad (VMO).

Repeat at each of the two or three points, then leave.

The same principle applies to the spine. On the thoracic extension over a foam roller she bends and straightens the spine actively (thoracic spine). She works across several points of the mid to upper back. Roll only to move the pivot. Slow and relaxed. Gravity does much of the work.

The dose is the number of points rather than a reading on a clock. A joint's active range under the pressure is the unit of work.

Terms on this page

pin and stretch: Hold pressure still on one tender point while she moves the joint next to it through its range.

VMO: The teardrop quad muscle on the inside of the knee. It works hardest in the last few degrees of straightening the knee.

thoracic spine: The upper and middle back, the part of the spine the ribs attach to. It is the part of the spine that has to extend for the arms to get overhead cleanly.
