Practising a lift · T-48.7

Whole, then part, then whole

Teach a movement whole, break it into parts, then put it back together. The last "whole" is the step people drop.

This is the way to teach any skill built from simpler pieces (whole-part-whole). Show her the full movement first, so she knows what the parts are for. Then isolate the piece she cannot yet do and practise it on its own. Then reassemble the movement and run it whole again.

A movement broken into parts and never reassembled has taught her the parts. She can do the pieces beautifully and still not have the lift. The reassembly is where the learning lands.

What this rule does and does not permit. It governs the order in which you teach a drill that already exists on the exercise pages. It does not license inventing a drill. Choosing exercises covers which movements she does, and How to read an exercise page covers what an exercise page may add.

Teach it in this order

Whole. She does the full movement, light, so she knows what it is.

Part. Isolate the piece that is failing and practise it alone.

Whole again. Put the piece back into the movement in the same session. Never leave it as parts.

Terms on this page
