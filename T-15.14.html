Tempo and pauses · T-15.14

A stretch-paused movement never opens a cold session

A movement with a pause in the loaded stretch does not go first in a session on a client who has not warmed up. It belongs later, once blood flow is up.

The case to picture. A seated hamstring curl programmed as the opening exercise, on a client who has walked in cold. Cold connective tissue is rigid and vulnerable in a loaded stretch. The pause holds her there.

Why it sits in this topic. The pause is what creates the problem, which is why Tempo and pauses carries the rule. Warming up owns what an adequate warm-up actually is. Building a session owns session order generally, and this is the one order rule that comes from the tempo.

Watch for

It collides with a reasonable instinct. A coach who wants the lengthened work done while the client is fresh will put it first. That is exactly the wrong move for the one category of movement where the tissue needs preparing.

Terms on this page

lengthened position: The point in the rep where the working muscle is at its longest while it carries the load. It describes the muscle's length, not whether she feels a stretch.
