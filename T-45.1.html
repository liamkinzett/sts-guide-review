Breathing under load · T-45.1

The held breath belongs to the single heavy rep

Taking one deep breath, bracing on it and holding it for the whole lift is for a powerlifter doing one rep under very heavy load. It is not how she breathes.

That held-breath strategy has a name (Valsalva). For one maximal rep it is exactly what you want: a deep breath in, used to brace the spine, held the whole time.

The reason it is tolerated does not travel. Under a true one-rep lift, the pass-out risk is knowingly accepted because it is one rep. She is done before the lack of air matters. A client applying the same strategy to a set of ten has inherited the technique without the condition that made it acceptable.

So the question is always the rep count. One heavy rep: hold it. Anything longer than one breath cycle: breathe. The next page gives the switch point.

Watch for

The client who learned to lift from powerlifting content. She has copied the one-rep breath into her sets of eight. Nobody told her it was a one-rep tool.

Pregnancy and postpartum. The working rule there is effort at 2 to 3 RIR, and the load is whatever that effort allows. That lowers the bracing demand without banning anything. Coach "brace, do not bear down". A physio's technique restriction is hers to set and lift. The female body covers it.

Terms on this page

Valsalva: Holding her breath and bracing against a closed throat to stiffen the trunk under load. It is a tool for the single maximal rep.

RIR: Reps in reserve. How many more reps she could have done when the set stopped.
