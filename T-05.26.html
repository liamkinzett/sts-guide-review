Responding to a bone-health claim from outside

When a client brings a bone-health claim from outside, you neither confirm it nor debunk it. You cannot grade either claim, and you say so plainly.

Two claims you will hear

# She has read that lifting reverses osteoporosis and expects a normal next scan

sub: Do not confirm it

Why: Reversing a diagnosis is a clinical outcome. You hold no basis to promise it.

Do this: Say plainly that you cannot promise a scan result. Keep training her under the locked rule in Mobility and stretching, whatever the next scan shows.

# A podcast has told her to add jumping for her bones now she is postmenopausal

sub: Neither wave it through nor refuse it

Why: Waving it through because it sounds like the heavy-loads rule is a guess. Refusing it because impact sounds risky is a guess the other way.

Do this: Apply what does hold, whatever her reason for asking. The return-to-impact gate in Region by region: calf strength (plantar flexor strength) at or above bodyweight before explosive work. Then the ordinary volume and effort rules for introducing anything new.

Why neither answer is available. Confirming the reversal claim sets her up for a disappointment you cannot defend. Debunking it risks talking a postmenopausal client out of the one thing her physiology needs most. The answer to both is that you cannot grade either claim. A coach does not manufacture certainty she was not given.

What does not change. Nothing here loosens the locked bone rule. A client with a diagnosed condition is still referred rather than assessed.

Say it like this

I can't promise you what your next scan will show. Nobody can. What I can do is keep the heavy work in, because that's the part that matters for your bone. And I'll keep you inside the rules your diagnosis sets.

Watch for

Letting the client's confidence in a claim substitute for your own. She may be right. You cannot tell either way. Saying so plainly is the actual answer.

Terms on this page

plantar flexor strength: How much force her calves can produce pointing the foot down. It is the strength that absorbs landing.
