Hip: the intervention cluster, in order

Static stretch first, then stability. Briefly quieting the tight tissue re-centres the joint. There is no point stabilising a joint that is being pulled in every direction at once.

This is remedial work, not session prep. Warming up gates static stretching before a session, and this is the other thing: work done to change a position, in its own slot.

The cluster

Frog pose. Thighs out to the side (abduction) and turned inward (internal rotation) together.

Pigeon.

Couch stretch, performed in rotation. Turning the thigh in and then out (external rotation) reaches completely different tissue. A second version anchors the foot and moves the pelvis around the thigh bone rather than the thigh bone in the socket.

Then stability: a walking or stationary lunge, plus a single-leg RDL.

Monster walk for direct glute work.

What STS programs from this list. Pigeon and the couch stretch are Priming lifts. The lateral lunge is in the Library. Frog pose and the monster walk are described here so you recognise them; use the Library's hip work when you write the program.

Terms on this page

abduction: A limb travelling out and away from the midline of the body: the arm in a lateral raise, or the thigh moving out to the side.

internal rotation: Turning a limb inward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face back or down, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more toward the middle. The muscles that do it are the internal rotators.

external rotation: Turning a limb outward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face forward or up, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more to the side. A muscle that does it is an external rotator.
