Mobility and stretching · T-16b.6

Mobility work earns programmed time only when a position is unreachable

Mobility work goes in the program only when a position the program actually requires cannot be reached today. Not because a joint measures short, and not because a screen flagged it.

Why the trigger is the position rather than the measurement. Mobility tells you which version of a movement to program. It does not decide whether she is safe to train. Assessing a new client establishes that. A restriction that does not block anything in the program is not costing her anything. Time spent on it is time not spent training. A restriction that blocks a movement she needs is a real problem with a real cost, and that cost is what earns the slot.

When it earns the slot, keep it attached to the movement. It goes in the prep for that lift rather than as a standalone block. Warming up owns what belongs in that prep: dynamic movement, done with intent.

What it looks like. Her ankles will not let her reach depth on any squat variation you have, heels elevated included. That is a position the program needs and cannot get, so ankle work goes into the prep before her squat. Her hamstrings measure tight on a screen but she reaches a full Romanian deadlift with a flat back. That earns nothing.

When you may deviate. A client with a stated non-training goal for a position has a reason that is hers rather than the program's. She wants to sit cross-legged comfortably, or return to a sport that demands a range. That is a legitimate slot. Name it as her goal rather than dressing it up as programming.

Watch for

The permanent mobility block that has been in the program for six months because it was needed once. When the position is reachable, the slot closes.
