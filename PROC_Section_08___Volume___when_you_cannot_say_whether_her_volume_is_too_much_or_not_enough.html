Deciding whether her volume is too much or too little

This page is the whole topic in the order you use it. Nothing here is new.

The first half of the question is a lookup, not a judgement. How much work she should be doing comes from the volume table. Her training-age band comes from the classification in Assessing a new client. Her phase figure comes from The phases. The arm figure and the per-session default come from this topic. Look the figures up every time. The rest of this page is the second half, which is the half that takes work.

The five moves, in order

Read. Compare each muscle's total against her band, then open the logbook.

Test. Total her volume per muscle per week, on paper. Not in your head. The arithmetic is where the surprises are. Neither of the two errors this topic exists to prevent is visible by reading the program. A hard set (hard set) is the only unit.

Counting rules

Hard sets only. Within three reps of failure. Warm-ups, feeder sets and back-off sets at four or five reps in reserve (RIR) do not count.

Per muscle, never per exercise. Split each set using the exercise library's shares. Three sets of RDL is 1.5 glutes and 1.5 hamstrings.

Pulling to biceps has no library split. Vertical pulling counts close to fully. Rowing counts about half, as a working figure.

An indirect credit is only real if the program actually contains the movement that delivers it.

Then open the logbook. Whether she is progressing session to session is the signal every later step turns on.

Read. Compare each muscle's total against the band that fits her context.

What the totals say

# Inside her band and progressing

sub: The dose is right

Read: The question is never whether she could handle more. It is whether any of it is not earning its place.

# Inside her band and stalled

sub: Not a volume finding yet

Read: The stall list runs before any set moves. Effort, technique, recovery including food, then volume.

# Below her band

sub: Under-dosed, once the rest is cleared

Read: Under-dosed only once effort, technique and recovery are cleared. Minimum effective has a real floor. A client below her band is being short-changed.

# Light on arms

sub: Usually correct for a female client

Read: Wrong for one who has told you plainly she wants them. That is the worse failure of the two.

# A weekly total that cannot fit inside six to eight per session

sub: A frequency instruction, not a volume cut

Read: Split it across more sessions. A total she cannot fit is a total she is not really getting.

# One muscle far over the session default from stacked indirect work

sub: Quad and glute sessions break first

Read: Four exercises can be 11 hard sets for glutes. Count the indirect share.

There is no symptom checklist for too much. No soreness scale, no sleep score, no mood marker. Too much is defined by cost against return and by what the logbook does. It is never defined by how she reports feeling.

Do. In order. The early moves are free and the last one is not.

What to do

If she is progressing, hold the dose. Volume does not ramp on a schedule. A ramp is never written into the block in advance.

If she has stalled, work the stall list in order. Effort first, then technique, then recovery including food. Volume is fourth. It is the only item on the list that can make things worse, and it is a weaker lever than it looks.

Add sets only when the list says so. She trains hard, moves well, sleeps and eats. She sits at the bottom of her band and has genuinely stopped progressing. That is the condition. Anything else is a schedule wearing a reason.

If one muscle lags, climb the ladder. Move it fresh first, which costs nothing. Add only if the sets are cheap for the whole body. Redistribute from a dominant muscle held at maintenance if they are not.

If the weekly total will not fit per session, split it, do not cut it. Spread it across more sessions. Split design and this topic are read together.

Change. What moves with the volume and what explicitly does not.

  • In a deficit the weekly figure drops to ten to fifteen regardless of training age. Load and effort stay. They protect the tissue. The cheap holding dose that works for a fed lifter does not work in a deficit.
  • In a deload she is cut to one or two working sets per movement. Her load and her RIR do not move.
  • The bands themselves do not move for an individual. They are starting points that her own response corrects. The logbook outranks the general figure on any individual client.

    • Escalate. What this arithmetic does not cover.

  • Any pain goes to Pain rules and when to refer. Nothing in this topic's arithmetic covers it.
  • Rate of fat loss, deficit size and protein belong to the Nutrition Manual. They are never restated here.
  • Stalled, lean, trains hard, moves well, sleeps: ask about fuelling. Hormonal contraception hides the visible sign of under-fuelling for over half our clients. Adding sets to an under-fuelled client is the worst-timed move on the list.
  • A beginner inverts the stall list. Execution comes first because her effort ratings are the one signal you cannot read yet. She starts week one a set below target and steps up to target in week two.
  • How many muscles at once has no set limit. Do not read three as a ceiling or as a licence. Ask whether her recovery can pay for each one.
    • Terms on this page

    hard set: A working set taken within three reps of failure. It is the only kind of set that counts toward her weekly volume.

    RIR: Reps in reserve. How many more reps she could have done when the set stopped. Zero RIR is failure.
