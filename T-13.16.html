Choosing exercises · T-13.16

Record it when the lengthened floor cannot be met

Every muscle she trains needs one movement that overloads its lengthened position each week. When her only lengthened movement for a muscle is ruled out, the floor is not met. Record the breach. Never quietly promote a substitute.

Where this bites. For the glutes, the one movement that peaks in the lengthened position is the Romanian deadlift, in its split-stance form. Every other glute movement sits at mid to lengthened or below (mid-to-lengthened). So a client whose hips or back rule out a hinge cannot meet the lengthened floor for her glutes. The requirement does not lapse quietly.

It is unmet, and unmet is a thing you write down.

Read the qualifier correctly. The floor asks for a lengthened movement wherever the muscle allows it. That clause is about the muscle, not about this client. A muscle with no lengthened position is outside the requirement. A muscle that has one, in a client who cannot be put there, is inside it and failing it. Collapsing those two is how a floor disappears without anyone deciding to remove it.

What to do

Confirm the contraindication is real and current. Anything pain-driven goes to Pain rules and when to refer. It is never a selection decision.

Work the substitution ladder in How to read an exercise page, as written. If it returns a movement that genuinely peaks lengthened, the floor is met and there is nothing to record.

Where it does not, program the best available. For the glutes that is the mid-to-lengthened pair. Then record that the lengthened requirement is unmet, with the reason.

Re-test it when the contraindication changes. Returning to training owns the return.

Watch for

Promoting a mid-to-lengthened movement into the lengthened column to make the program look complete. What a movement counts as is fixed by the exercise pages. Changing it to satisfy a rule is the failure those pages exist to stop.

Treating any length of time with the floor unmet as acceptable. No period is allowed by default, and do not tell yourself the loss is small. Re-test at every change.

Say it in the notes, not to her. She does not need to be told her program breaches an internal floor. Writing program instructions owns what goes in her program notes. Writing to a client owns what reaches her.

Terms on this page

mid-to-lengthened: The part of the range from mid-way down to the stretched bottom.
