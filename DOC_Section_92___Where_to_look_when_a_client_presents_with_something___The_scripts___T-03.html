Fatigue and recovery: what to say

Words for her on fatigue and recovery, by situation.

A script is only right inside its rule's conditions. Read the rule in Fatigue and recovery before you use the words.

One bad night measurably suppresses the adaptation. Treat sleep as a training variable, not a lifestyle one.

Say it like this

If sleep is the thing that's off this month, that's the thing we fix. I can write you more work but you won't get the benefit of it.

Soreness is not a measure of anything. Stop reading it as feedback.

Say it like this

Being sore doesn't tell us the session worked, and not being sore doesn't tell us it didn't. The logbook tells us.

She slept badly last night and has a heavy session today.

Say it like this

Train today. If it's a heavy squat or deadlift session this afternoon, expect a bit more grind than usual. That's the sleep, not you losing fitness overnight.

Active recovery is real for how she feels. It is not doing the tissue-repair job it is usually sold as doing.

Say it like this

It'll make you feel better today. It's not speeding up what your muscle is actually doing to repair itself. Do it if you enjoy it.

DOMS is an inflammatory response to mechanical damage. It is not lactic acid. Eccentric work produces more of it because eccentric work produces more of the damage. (eccentric)

Say it like this

That burn during the set and the soreness two days later are not the same thing and they're not caused by the same thing. Neither one is lactic acid.

Her heart rate stays up longer after a leg session than after an arm session. More muscle moved more blood; it is not more fatigue.

Say it like this

Your heart rate stays up longer after legs because legs are a bigger job for your heart to service. Not because something's wrong or you overdid it.

Tired and damaged are not the same thing. They do not run on the same clock.

Say it like this

Tired means your muscle ran out of gas today and it'll be back tomorrow. Sore means something got torn a little and it's rebuilding, on its own timeline, and it isn't a sign either way that the session worked.

Week two, not week one, is where soreness peaks. Week one was never the full dose.

Say it like this

Week one was deliberately lighter so we could dial in your form. Week two is where we go to the real number, so don't be surprised if week two is actually the sorer one.

Terms on this page

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.
