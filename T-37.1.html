Returning to training · T-37.1

Pain-free is not the same as ready

The absence of a symptom is not a qualification. "It doesn't hurt any more" is the beginning of the conversation, not the end of it.

Just because she can jump does not mean she should. Doing a movement without pain does not mean her tissue has earned the right, physiologically, to that movement as its training stimulus.

Why it works. Pain resolves before capacity returns. That is exactly why re-injury clusters at the point a client feels fine. She will tell you she is ready, and she will be telling the truth about what she can feel. She has no access to what her tissue can currently tolerate. Neither do you. That is why the ladder on the next page exists, rather than a judgement call.

What ready looks like. One demonstrated capacity beats any date. Before she returns to running, hopping or jumping work (plyometrics), her calf has to move her own bodyweight. That criterion lives in Region by region, with the ankle and calf. It is the shape every readiness test takes: something she can show you, not something she no longer feels.

Watch for

The client who reads clearance as permission to resume everything. Clearance ends the medical question. It does not answer the loading one.

Say it like this

Good, that's the first thing sorted. Now we build back up to it properly, because feeling fine and being ready to load it are two different things.

Terms on this page

plyometrics: Jumping, hopping and bounding work where the muscle stretches and shortens fast, with time in the air. It is the last rung of a return, never the first.
