Split design · T-11.3

Full body, then upper/lower, then push/pull/legs

Her split moves through three stages as what she can do changes. Full body across three days, then upper and lower alternating, then push, pull and legs. Capability moves her from one to the next. Time never does.

The three stages

Stage one. Full body, three days. Every session touches every pattern. The point is not to exhaust a muscle. It is to give her frequent, low-fatigue exposures (exposures) to the patterns she is still learning. Her weekly volume builds up without any single session having to be hard.

Stage two. Upper and lower, alternating. Once her work capacity (work capacity) has risen and the patterns are no longer what limits her, the work concentrates. She can now tolerate a session that genuinely taxes a region.

Stage three. Push, pull and legs. Once she can take a set close to real failure with the target muscle as the thing that stops her. This is where a session can carry enough localised volume to need its own day.

What stage one looks like. Three days, every pattern in every session, three or four sets on each. The sets are light and the sessions are short. She learns the patterns by meeting them often.

What stage three looks like. Push, pull and legs each get their own day, so one muscle can carry enough volume to need a whole session. The week is often run as a rotation rather than a fixed seven days; the page on a rolling split covers how to track it.

Why it works. Each stage asks for something the one before it built. Concentrating volume into a lower-body day is only useful if she can produce force in a lower-body pattern without her coordination being the ceiling. Give her stage three too early and she is running an advanced structure with a beginner's bottleneck.

Watch for

Moving her on because the calendar did. The stages are capability, not time. The gate on the next page is the test.

Terms on this page

exposures: The number of separate sessions in a week in which a muscle is trained. Two exposures means it is hit on two different days.

work capacity: How much training she can do in a session and a week and still recover from it.
