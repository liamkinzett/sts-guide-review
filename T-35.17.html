Pain rules and when to refer · T-35.17

A symptom the tables do not name is still triaged

Missing row is not missing risk. A coach who believes something needs urgent care acts on that belief without waiting for a named row to permit it. That sentence is the whole rule. Two shapes recur in the questions coaches ask.

Red flags

title: Same-day medical review, even though neither has its own row

Chest tightness or pressure that starts with effort and settles with rest. Not a joint complaint. Systemic, not local

Unexplained weight loss reported alongside a new pain. Systemic, not explained by training

A tight or swollen calf after a long flight or a long drive

Lightheaded, dizzy or grey under load, or lightheaded on a hinge. A health sign, not a technique fault. Stop the movement and refer

emergency: Where any of the triple zero chest pain triggers is present, call the ambulance. Where none is, she is seen today, not at the next check-in.

Exertional and systemic, not local. Chest tightness that comes on with effort and settles with rest does not fit anywhere in the stop-and-refer table. Unexplained weight loss alongside a new pain is the same shape. Both get the same-day answer, not silence because neither has its own row.

Swelling after prolonged immobility. A tight or swollen calf following a long flight or a long drive is the same category. The female body already carries calf pain or swelling, and chest pain, as stop-the-session signs in pregnancy. The reasoning does not stop being true the day she is no longer pregnant. Read it as the same threshold for any client.

Lightheaded on a hinge is not a technique fault. Where she goes lightheaded, dizzy or grey under load, it stops being a technique conversation. The plausible causes are the balance system of the inner ear or her blood pressure. Stop the movement and refer. Any actual loss of consciousness, even brief, is a triple zero call.

What you do. You do not name any of these. You do not train around them while waiting to find out. The "anything acute" row already sends her to a GP. Where the pattern is exertional and systemic, she goes today, not at the next scheduled check-in. You describe what you saw and when, to her and to whoever she sees next.

Two clocks

When to call triple zero sets a harder, separate threshold for an ambulance. Neither page substitutes for the other.

Where none of the triple zero chest pain triggers is present, the action here is same-day medical review, not triple zero. That judgement stays yours to make.
