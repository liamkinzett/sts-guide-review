Body English

Her trunk, hips or whole body supply what the target muscle should.

Almost every page gives the same fix: reduce the load.

Where it shows, and the fix

# Hip adduction (adduction)

sub: Trunk swaying into the rep, one side leading

Do this: Hands lightly on the handles, trunk still, load down

# Seated cable row

sub: Losing the hinge, rounding the low back, leaning back to move the weight

Do this: Reduce load

# Pull-up

sub: Swing. The feet travelling behind the body at the bottom, which unloads the core and gives up the spinal position the scapula (scapula) needs

Do this: Use the whole-body cues below: the gymnast pull-up, feet in front. The fault is a bottom-position failure, not a pulling one.

# Standing curl

sub: Swinging. *"A case of the wiggles"*

Do this: Reduce load

# Trunk flexion

sub: Rocking the hips to move the trunk

Do this: Reduce load. Control the eccentric (eccentric)

# Cable glute kickback

sub: Leaning onto the upright. Twisting the torso to move the weight

Do this: Reduce load

# Split squat

sub: Torso supplies what the hip should

Do this: Slow it down

# Step-up and step-down

sub: Relying on the support as a crutch

Do this: Reduce the support

# Front raise

sub: Pushing the hips forward to start the rep

Do this: Reduce load

# Lateral raise

sub: *"Michael Jackson lean."* Body swing and hip drive

Do this: Reduce load

# Rear delt

sub: Swinging to move the weight

Do this: Reduce load

# Overhead cable extension

sub: Swinging to move the load

Do this: Reduce load

# Triceps pushdown

sub: Torso sway

Do this: STS's fix is a chest-supported setup, which removes the sway rather than cueing against it.

# Triceps pushdown

sub: Straight bar specifically. Hunkered over a short bar, sternum down, fully internally rotated, using bodyweight over the bar as a lever. Coaches call it a "T-Rex jam".

Do this: Stand up. Sternum up, ribs stacked over pelvis. Expect the load to fall a long way. That is the trade.

Cues written for this fault.

Cues

When: Pull-up · external cue

Say: gymnast pull-up

Why: The whole-body standard in one image. Use it when the fault is swing rather than position.

When: Pull-up · external cue

Say: the feet never come behind the body

Why: The rule underneath the gymnast image. Reach for it when that image does not land.

When: Pull-up · external cue

Say: feet actively in front of your body

Why: As against feet merely dangling.

When: Pull-up · internal cue

Say: Contract your quads

Why: Contracting it creates hip flexion and holds the pelvic position the other three cues describe from outside.

When: Triceps pushdown · external cue

Say: Use chest-supported setups to eliminate torso sways.

Why: A positional fix rather than a verbal one. Reach for it where cue 1 does not hold.

Other cues on those exercises. Not about this fault. Use them when the same set needs them.

Cues

When: Hip adduction

Say: Drive from the adductors.

When: Hip adduction

Say: Keep your back pressed against the pad.

Why: It enforces the setup detail that decides the set.

When: Hip adduction

Say: Don't let the pads crash.

Why: It enforces continuous tension and stops the stack being rested. Correct the concept before you correct the rep.

When: Seated cable row

Say: use your legs as an anchor

Why: The setup cue that makes everything after it possible. It is also the reason this row can be driven closer to failure than a free-weight one.

When: Seated cable row

Say: top of the head to the ceiling

Why: A deliberate replacement for "chest up"

When: Seated cable row

Say: let your shoulders flow freely

Why: Upper-back bias only. Expanded as hang loose at the bottom and pinch at the top. Do not give this cue on a lat-biased row.

Terms on this page

adduction: A limb travelling in toward the midline of the body: the leg pulled in by the inner-thigh muscles, or the arm brought in to the side.

scapula: The shoulder blade. The flat bone on the back of the rib cage that the arm hangs from.

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.

:

internally rotated: Turning a limb inward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face back or down, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more toward the middle.
