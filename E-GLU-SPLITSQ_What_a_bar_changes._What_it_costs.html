Choosing the equipment

Dumbbells are the default. A barbell or the Smith machine is a choice you make for a reason, and each one changes something.

The barbell. Its place is late in the learning sequence. She explores the pattern unloaded first. Then a bar on her back is the bridge to the two-legged back squat. The load sits above her centre of mass instead of below it in her hands. What changes is the bracing she needs, not the pattern (see Variations).

The Smith machine. It is programmed, and it has a named cost. The bar travels in a fixed plane, so her hip never has to stabilise sideways (frontal plane). Having to stabilise and organise herself is a big part of why single-leg work is in the program at all. A Smith bar takes that job away however much she loads it. So use the free-weight version whenever she can do it well.

When you do use the Smith machine, ask "where is this exercise going to fail me?" and program the complement. Place a walking or stationary lunge with an opposite-hand load (contralateral load) on another day, specifically to restore frontal-plane hip function.

What a bar takes away. The load-side logic of same-hand (ipsilateral load) against opposite-hand has no bar equivalent. A bar across her back and a Smith bar are both symmetrical load, the neutral case. So the opposite-hand fix for a pelvis drifting to the back leg is not available. Fix that in the program (the lunge above) or in the setup (a narrower stance), not in the set.

Terms on this page

frontal plane: Side-to-side movement, like a lateral lunge or a side raise.

contralateral load: The weight is held in the hand opposite the working leg.

ipsilateral load: The dumbbell held in the hand on the same side as the working leg. STS doesn't program it for the split squat.
