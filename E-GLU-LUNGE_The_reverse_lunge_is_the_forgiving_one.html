The reverse lunge is the forgiving one

Two things separate the reverse lunge from the walking lunge. Both are mechanical. It removes the deceleration demand, because she steps back into position rather than landing on a moving body. And it preserves a negative shin angle (negative shin angle), so her front knee stays behind her toes. That is why STS reaches for it where the walking lunge is beyond her. It is also why it is the easier assessment of the two.

It is still a loaded exercise rather than a remedial one. Load it. What it trains is the side-to-side shift of the front hip (lateral hip shift), plus hip extension of the back leg (hip extension). We are not trying to make it a remedial exercise.

Don't program it alongside a Bulgarian split squat

The pairing is redundant by primary function. Both have the front hip as their main job.

A step-up or step-down beside it is not redundant. Those are genuinely on one leg, so they train what the reverse lunge can't.

Barbell and Smith machine. Dumbbells are the default (see Setup). A bar or the Smith machine is a loading choice, not a different exercise, so coach it to the same standard. One thing not to carry across. The walking lunge trades output for instability on purpose. Where output is wanted, the Smith machine or a single-leg press is the tool. That was said about the walking lunge, not the reverse.

Terms on this page

negative shin angle: Her front shin leans back, so the knee sits behind the ankle rather than out over the toes. It shifts the work away from the front quad and towards the hips and hamstrings.

lateral hip shift: The hips shifting sideways over the working leg as she lowers. It is the glute controlling the pelvis, and the reverse lunge trains it.

hip extension: Opening the hip so the thigh travels behind the body line. It is the glute's main job, and the reverse lunge loads it on the back leg.
