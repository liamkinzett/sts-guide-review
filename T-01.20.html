Muscle and growth · T-01.20

Does a 30-second isometric hold grow muscle like reps do?

A 30-second isometric hold does not grow muscle the way reps do.

Not because tension stopped being real, but for four reasons of load, range and time. A hold means the muscle works while the joint stays still (isometric). Isometric training can build muscle. The question here is narrower: should a long hold be treated as an equivalent substitute for dynamic reps when growth is the goal? STS does not treat it that way.

A heavy load can't be held for 30 seconds.

A hold to 30 seconds forces the load down to roughly 50 to 60% of the maximum, or the lifter fails at once. Dynamic reps can be loaded to 70 to 85% of the max. Lower load means less force on each fibre for most of the hold.

A hold does not move the joint. A rep sweeps through the range, and the leverage changes as it goes (moment arm). A hold sits at one angle and never gets the rest. Levers and moment arms explains why the turning force on the joint (torque) is a moving target through a rep.

There is no lowering phase. Reps couple lifting with lowering. The lowering half of a rep (eccentric) produces the highest forces at the lowest energy cost. A hold has none of it.

The direct comparison found no advantage for the hold. Resistance-trained adults held a light 30% knee-extension effort to failure for 5 weeks. They got 24% better at sustaining it. Their thigh muscle barely changed. Endurance at the hold went up. Size did not.

Four reasons

# A heavy load can't be held for 30 seconds

What this does and does not rule out. Holds can build muscle. The next page covers where a hold grows most.

This page answers a different comparison: a hold trades load and range for duration, against reps at the same closeness to failure. That trade did not pay off in the direct test. The test itself used a light 30% hold rather than a maximal-effort one. So it answers a closely related question, not the exact case of someone trying to hold as heavy a load as possible. Reps remain the growth tool. A hold is not a substitute for them.

Terms on this page

isometric: A hold. The muscle works against the load but the joint does not move.

moment arm: The perpendicular distance between the joint axis and the line of force. The longer it is, the harder that joint has to work.

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.

torque: The turning force on a joint. The further the load sits from the joint, the more of it there is.
