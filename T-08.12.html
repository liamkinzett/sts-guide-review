Volume · T-08.12

Volume rises when progress stalls, not on a schedule

A block does not start light and climb. It sits at the dose she needs and stays there until the logbook says otherwise.

Sets are added in response to her, never on a calendar.

Why it works. Adding sets on a schedule means adding fatigue for a reason that has nothing to do with her. Find the smallest dose that keeps her progressing session to session (minimum effective dose). Hold it for as long as it keeps working. A client progressing on fifteen sets does not need eighteen. The three extra sets are charged to her recovery whether or not they do anything.

When it does go up. First the stall list runs. It comes later in this topic. It checks her effort, her technique and her recovery, including food, before volume. Volume goes up only when all three are clear, she sits at the bottom of her band, and progression has genuinely stopped. That is the condition. Anything else is a schedule wearing a reason.

A worked example. She is written 15 glute sets in a hypertrophy block and progresses for five weeks. Week six is flat. You check the video: her sets stop at 2 RIR and the pattern holds. Her range has not shortened. She is sleeping and eating. She sits at the bottom of her 15 to 20 band. Now, and only now, she goes to 17.

Watch for

Building the ramp into the program before the block starts. Once written down, it is no longer a response to her. It becomes something you do to her.

One thing that is not a ramp. A brand new client runs week one a set below target and goes to target in week two. That is a single step onto a level road rather than a climb. It happens once, for a client the logbook has never seen.

Terms on this page

minimum effective dose: The least amount of training that keeps her progressing. Every set past it costs recovery without adding growth.
