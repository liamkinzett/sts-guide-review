Constipation, straining and the pelvic floor

Managing her bowel habits is not a coach's job. Knowing why a physio cares about it is what stops you second-guessing a caution that looks unrelated to training.

The mechanism. Bearing down under strain sends excessive pressure (intra-abdominal pressure) straight down onto a weakened pelvic floor. It is the same downward-pressure pathway as a heavy lift with poor bracing, covered on the pelvic floor page. A repeated daily habit, like straining at stool, plausibly matters for someone already managing a dropped pelvic organ (pelvic organ prolapse) for the same reason. Same mechanism, more repetitions.

Why it works as a scope answer. The physio is working the same pressure system you are, from a different angle. Her caution about something that seems unrelated to training is part of the same job. You do not manage the bowel habit. You do not override the physio who does.

Verdict

Her bowel habits are hers and her physio's to manage.

Do not manage it yourself, and do not override a physio's caution about it.

Watch for

Overriding a physio's restriction because it does not look like a training concern from the gym floor.

Terms on this page

intra-abdominal pressure: The pressure inside the abdominal cavity. A brace raises it on purpose to stiffen the trunk.

pelvic organ prolapse: One or more pelvic organs has dropped from its normal position and presses into the vaginal wall. It is felt as heaviness or dragging.
