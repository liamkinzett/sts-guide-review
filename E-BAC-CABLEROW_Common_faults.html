Common faults

Six common faults

# Undeclared scapular bias

sub: Shoulders held stiff on an upper-back row. Hard pinching at the finish on a lat row

Why it happens: Nobody declared the shoulder-blade (scapular) bias, so she rowed the standard for the other one. Each is the other's standard.

Do this: Declare the bias. Then coach the finish that belongs to it.

# Spine doing the scapula's job

sub: The upper back rounds and arches across the rep

Why it happens: The shoulder blade (scapula) cannot yet move on a still rib cage, so the spine supplies the travel.

Do this: Hold the trunk. The blade moves, the spine does not.

# Body English

sub: Losing the hinge, rounding the low back, leaning back to move the weight

Why it happens: The load has outrun what her hinge can hold against the stack.

Do this: Reduce the load.

# Position degrading across the set

sub: Sliding the hips back in the seat mid-set

Why it happens: Fatigue. The brace against the footrests is the first thing to go.

Do this: End the set.

# Head as a release valve

sub: The head moves, nodding forward and back

Why it happens: Range she does not have at the shoulder blade is being found at the neck.

Do this: Head still.

# Shruggie

sub: The shoulders creep up toward the ears

Why it happens: She has run out of range at the shoulder blade, so the traps take over. It is a range signal.

Do this: End the rep.

Terms on this page

scapular: To do with the shoulder blade, the scapula. A scapular bias is which way you coach the blade to move.

scapula: The shoulder blade. The flat bone on the back of the rib cage that the arm hangs from.
