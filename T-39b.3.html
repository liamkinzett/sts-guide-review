Adherence as a signal · T-39b.3

For a new lifter, confidence in the room is a programming variable

A new lifter avoids movements she does not feel competent doing in front of people. That shows up as adherence rather than as a conversation.

She will not tell you the barbell area intimidates her. She will skip the session that has the barbell in it, or do everything except that lift, and log the rest.

What changes. Prefer movements she can perform without an audience forming. Where possible, put the least familiar work early in the session rather than last, when the gym is busier and she is more tired.

This is the usual choice, and there is one reason to depart from it. Deviate for a client who is plainly comfortable, which you will know within two sessions.

Why it works. A movement she cannot yet perform competently wrecks adherence, and the fix is selection. Nothing about the exercise itself changed. What changed is whether she will do it with people watching.

Watch for

Reading it as dislike. She may like the movement and hate the audience. Ask where in the gym she does it, and when.
