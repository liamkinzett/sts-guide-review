The foot cheat code: which toe lifts

Watch her toes under load. Which one lifts tells you which hip rotation she is avoiding.

  • Little toe lifting off the ground: she is avoiding turning the thigh outward (external rotation) at the hip.
  • Big toe lifting off the ground: she is avoiding turning the thigh inward (internal rotation) at the hip.

    • The fix is physical. Put a finger on the toe, push it into the ground, and tell her to press there. That closes the system so the rotation cannot escape downstream into the knee.

    In a lunge. Rear-leg toes drifting outward means she is losing internal rotation at the hip.

    Be careful cueing

    Hip internal rotation is usually the real limiter. "Knees out" is external rotation plus the thigh moving out to the side (abduction). It is often the opposite of what she needs.

    Cues

    When: The toe lifts

    Say: Press the big toe into the floor and keep it there.

    Why: Closing the foot closes the system. The rotation has to happen at the hip.

    Terms on this page

    external rotation: Turning a limb outward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face forward or up, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more to the side. A muscle that does it is an external rotator.

    internal rotation: Turning a limb inward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face back or down, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more toward the middle. The muscles that do it are the internal rotators.

    abduction: A limb travelling out and away from the midline of the body: the arm in a lateral raise, or the thigh moving out to the side.
