Fault or compensation, braced or organised

STS keeps the words "fault" and "braced". Carry two ideas behind them anyway, because they change what you may say to her.

Two words, and the idea to carry with each

# Fault

sub: She may not be making an error. She may be compensating

What to carry: She is working at the limit of her understanding, or of what her tissue will allow. She physically cannot flex the hip further. Her brain is not what is limiting her.

# Braced

sub: The useful picture is "organised"

What to carry: The position that generates most force. She keeps access to the pelvic floor, the diaphragm, neck rotation and shoulder blade movement.

Why "braced" on its own misleads. Told only to brace, a client becomes rigid, maximally tight and afraid to breathe or move. Breathing under load teaches the brace and the breath as two separate dials for exactly this reason.

The reframe that is more useful than either word. The objection to a rounded-back deadlift is not about pain or an injury mechanism. It is that the position transfers poorly. She cannot move fast from there, she cannot reach overhead from there, and she will not jump highest from there.

Why that matters to you. It gives you grounds to change something without claiming it is hurting her. You never tell her a position is damaging her. You may tell her it is not the position she is strongest from. Pain rules and when to refer covers what you may and may not claim.

Say when you change a rounded pull

That position isn't where you're strongest from. Let's set you up so you can drive from your legs.
