Writing program instructions · T-40.1

Every slot carries its intent, not just its numbers

A slot that says "3 × 8" tells her what to count. It does not tell her what the set is for. She cannot execute an intent she has not been given.

A slot is one line of the program: one exercise with its sets and reps. The numbers are the container. The intent is what makes two identical prescriptions into different sets. That is the whole argument Effort and intensity makes about effort. Three sets of eight stopped well short of failure and three sets of eight taken close to it are different work.

What every slot must carry

Load, or the method for selecting it. Never a weight in kilos. She arrives at the load from the reps and the effort you wrote. Effort and intensity owns this.

Reps, as a band. A lowest and a highest rep count for the set, not one number. Which band depends on the implement, the barbell, dumbbell or machine she is using. Effort and intensity owns this too.

Effort, as reps in reserve. Every STS set is written in (RIR).

Tempo or pause intent, where the movement needs one. Tempo and pauses decides, using the point in the rep where that exercise is hardest. The exercise page names it.

What to focus on. One cue, not four. Cueing owns the cue itself.

What the difference looks like on the page.

Compared

columns: The numbers only | The numbers and the intent

The slot: Hip thrust, 3 × 8 | Hip thrust, 3 × 8

Load: Nothing written, or a weight in kilos | The weight that lands eight at the effort written. If it is easy, go up

Effort: Nothing | The reps in reserve for this block

Tempo: Nothing, or a tempo on every line | A pause at the top, because that is where the hip thrust is hardest

Focus: Nothing | The one cue from the exercise page

On day one there is no logbook to choose a load from. Her first program says what you do instead: start below what you think she can do, and let week one show you the weight.

Why it works. She can only execute what she has been told. A slot with the intent written is a set she can run the way you meant it. A slot without it is a set she runs the way she guesses.

Watch for

A program where every slot has a tempo because the field has one. Tempo and pauses decides where a pause belongs. Most slots do not need one.

Terms on this page

RIR: Reps in reserve. How many more reps she could have done when the set stopped. Every STS set is prescribed in it.
