When to use it

The problem you notice. She is new to lat work, or she tells you she cannot grow her back, or her shoulder will not tolerate loaded overhead work. The lat pulldown answers all three. It is the first lat exercise STS gives a client, in single-arm form. It doubles as the test you run when a back is not growing.

Reach for it when

# She is new to lat work and you are choosing her first one

sub: Nothing in her history says she can hang from a bar and organise herself

Confirm it: Ask her to show you a pull-up or a hang. If she cannot hold her rib cage stacked there, she starts here.

What it gets her: A lat exercise she can organise before she is asked to hang from anything. Start her single-arm, with the range scaled to what a stacked rib cage can hold.

# She tells you she cannot grow her back

sub: A diagnosis rather than more volume

Confirm it: Ask her for a video of her lat pulldowns. The rib cage, not the arm path, is usually what the video shows.

What it gets her: The real fault found before you add sets. A flared rib cage takes the lat's fixed point away, and no amount of volume fixes that.

# Her shoulder will not tolerate loaded overhead work

sub: A scaled entry point that keeps the pattern

Confirm it: Overhead loading provokes the shoulder. The pull-up is out for now.

What it gets her: The pattern kept at a range she can hold. Single-arm, range cut to comfort, off hand braced on a bench.

# You want her overhead later in the session and she cannot organise the position on her own

sub: A position she gets pulled into before she has to press out of it

Confirm it: Her overhead press position is poor when she sets it up herself.

What it gets her: It is easier to organise a position when she is pulled there first. The pulldown before the press does that.

Single arm first is a difficulty step, not an injury swap

Both arms at once doubles the load her trunk has to organise. Most machines also have no shoulder-width grip that matches her own arm path. A single arm lets her move herself to fit the path.

Name the goal before you lean on the machine. A pulldown gives her external stability, and that is exactly why she can organise herself on it. It is where a new client starts and where a painful shoulder re-enters. It does not build the hanging, self-stabilised shoulder function the pull-up builds. See Pull-up. Choose the pulldown for the lat and for the entry point. Choose the pull-up for shoulder function.

Grip is a separate choice. Neutral and palms-facing grips bias the lat. The palms-away grip is chosen for the upper back. What it's for explains.

Setup. Rib cage stacked over the pelvis, thigh pad at the edge of the knees, neutral grip at a moderate width. Setup has the full list.
