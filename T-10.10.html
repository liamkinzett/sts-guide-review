A decline needs reading before it needs fixing

A number going down is information. It is not automatically a fault. Treating it as one will have you reversing correct decisions.

A decline can come from any of the causes below. More than one at once is normal. The list is what you check against, not a ranking. Nothing about the number on the page tells you which one you are looking at.

Seven causes, one observation

# Life

sub: Sleep, stress, illness, a heavy week outside the gym

What it looks like: Nothing in the program changed and nothing about her training changed. Volume puts recovery in the diagnosis for this reason.

# Under-fuelling

sub: The one cause nobody else can see

What it looks like: She is lean. She trains hard. She sleeps. She has stopped progressing anyway. The female body owns this cause and calls the logbook the only place it shows. Hormonal contraception hides the visible sign for over half the roster.

# A deficit

sub: Retention is the job in a cut

What it looks like: Holding a number in a deficit is a result, not a stall. Assessing a new client frames a cut this way.

# Deload or phase change

sub: Volume is meant to be lower. Load is not.

What it looks like: The phases holds the weight on the bar through a deload. A load that dropped in a deload week is a fault to correct, not the program working. After the deload she resumes exactly where she left off: pre-deload loads, the block's ongoing reps in reserve, no ramp back in.

# Context reset

sub: The lift moved, or the work before it changed

What it looks like: Nothing about her changed at all. Compare the lift within its block only.

# Correct coaching

sub: Something hurt and the movement was pulled or regressed on purpose

What it looks like: A decline on the chart that was a decision, not an accident.

# Genuine regression

sub: The one this page exists to stop you assuming

What it looks like: None of the rows above explain it.

Two lists, two jobs. The seven rows above are the ways to read a number that is falling. The four items on the stall list, effort, technique, recovery, volume, are what you fix once you have read it. Read first, then fix.

Two causes can look identical and need opposite answers. Seven causes, one observation. Only one of the seven is a fault. It is also the one a coach assumes by default. Every other row is either the program working correctly or something that has nothing to do with the program. Acting on the number before you know which row it is means changing training that was not the cause.

The most expensive one to get wrong is correct coaching. A movement was pulled because it was hurting her. The data shows a decline. Then a coach reading the chart puts it back.

When she asks why the number holds rather than climbs, she is asking a fatigue question, not a what-do-I-do question. Fatigue and recovery covers it.

Watch for

Declines clustered in one region across one window. That pattern usually means something structural happened to the program rather than to her. Look up what changed in the block, not what is wrong with her.
