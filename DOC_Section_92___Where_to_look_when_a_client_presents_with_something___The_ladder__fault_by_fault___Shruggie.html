Shruggie

Her shoulders creep toward her ears.

Read it as a range signal as much as a form fault: she has run out of range where tension is still being added. End the rep there.

Where it shows, and the fix

# Seated cable row

sub: Getting shruggie. Range signal

Do this: End the rep

# Machine and chest-supported row

sub: Getting shruggie

Do this: Range signal. End the rep

# Lat pulldown

sub: Getting shruggie overhead. A range signal as much as a form fault

Do this: End the rep there

# Standing curl

sub: Traps rise at the top, blades creep forward

Do this: End the rep

# Pec fly

sub: Shrugging at the end range

Do this: End the set. Range signal

# Machine and cable chest press

sub: Blades riding up. Read this as a range signal as well as a form fault, and end the set there.

Do this: Stop the rep

# Activation drill

sub: Getting shruggy. A range signal: she has run out of range where tension is still being added.

Do this: End the rep

# Front raise

sub: Shoulders creeping toward the ears

Do this: Range signal. End the rep

# Lateral raise

sub: Traps taking over. Read it as a range signal: she has run out of range where tension is still being added. End the rep.

Do this: Stop there

There is no set cue written for this fault. Say the fix in plain words, one thing this set.

Other cues on those exercises. Not about this fault. Use them when the same set needs them.

Cues

When: Seated cable row

Say: use your legs as an anchor

Why: The setup cue that makes everything after it possible. It is also the reason this row can be driven closer to failure than a free-weight one.

When: Seated cable row

Say: top of the head to the ceiling

Why: A deliberate replacement for "chest up"

When: Seated cable row

Say: let your shoulders flow freely

Why: Upper-back bias only. Expanded as hang loose at the bottom and pinch at the top. Do not give this cue on a lat-biased row.

When: Seated cable row

Say: drive down

Why: Lat bias only. Biases shoulder extension plus depression rather than pulling back. It is the direct fix for pulling back too far or for letting the shoulder roll forward.

When: Machine and chest-supported row

Say: top of the head to the ceiling

Why: The primary cue and a deliberate replacement for "chest up"

When: Machine and chest-supported row

Say: spine long

Why: The same intent with an internal anchor. Use whichever she responds to.
