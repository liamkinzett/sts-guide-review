Triceps · E-TRI-OVERHEAD#When to program it

When to use it

The problem you notice. Her triceps have stalled and every triceps slot in her program is a pushdown. Or you audit a push day and nothing in it gets her arm overhead. Or the overhead position is what her shoulder objects to, not the load. The overhead cable extension answers all three.

Reach for it when

# Her triceps have stalled and every triceps slot is a pushdown

sub: The head the pushdown cannot reach

Confirm it: Read the program. Count the pushdowns. Look for anything that takes her arm overhead under load.

What it gets her: The long head loaded at length (long head). It crosses the shoulder, so an overhead position is required to load and grow it. "Not optional." That is why the two are separate slots rather than two ways of filling one. Her default is one triceps movement, overhead or not, so this replaces a pushdown rather than joining it, unless arms are her stated priority. Volume holds the count.

# Nothing in her push day gets her overhead

sub: The overhead box ticked without another press

Confirm it: Every press in the session is horizontal.

What it gets her: An overhead position without a corrective drill. "it doesn't mean it needs to be a functional exercise. It doesn't mean it needs to be remedial in nature". The list that follows ends "Could be as simple as an overhead tricep extension". On a short push day this is the slot that gets her overhead.

# The overhead position is what her shoulder objects to, not the load

sub: The elbow work kept and the shoulder spared

Confirm it: Light overhead work provokes her as much as heavy.

What it gets her: The cable version is preferred precisely because it spares her extreme overhead reach (shoulder flexion), "and by extension it spares the elbow too". Establish her comfortable limit of shoulder flexion before loading. That limit sets the bottom of the rep, not a textbook depth.

# She is not reaching the stretch

sub: A deeper loaded stretch without more shoulder range

Confirm it: The elbow stops near a right angle at the bottom.

What it gets her: The named fix is slightly more load plus a pause at the bottom. Never more shoulder range forced.

A lower back that arches as the arms go overhead is not a trigger to cue harder

The cause is restricted overhead shoulder mobility. Cueing the brace harder is the wrong response.

Regress the variation to a lying version that needs less shoulder range. STS doesn't program the lying French press as its own exercise, so use it to place her and send the question up. Until the range is there, the standing version is the wrong tool for that client.

Program it for the head it reaches, not for a growth bonus

This is a lengthened-position choice. The long head is loaded at length here, and that is certain. Do not sell it on the stretched position growing more muscle.

Setup. Pulley at chest or waist height with a dual rope, upper arms vertical and close to the head, glutes and core squeezed before rep one. Setup has the full list.

Terms on this page

long head: The head of a muscle that crosses the shoulder joint as well as the elbow: on the triceps it only stretches with the arm raised, on the biceps only with the arm behind the body.

shoulder flexion: The arm travelling forward and up in front of the body, all the way to overhead.
