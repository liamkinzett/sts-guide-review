What to do when she presents with hamstring pain

Nothing about a hamstring is answered by how bad it is. The number is the last input in this region, and the commonest mistake is opening with it.

Work through it in this order

Test. Six things you can watch or ask. None of them is a diagnosis.

Read. Pain or discomfort, then the red flags, then what changed, then the two numbers. Last.

Do. Sort every movement and build from what does not hurt.

Change. Rest the part, not the person.

Escalate. A suspected tear, pain at the attachment, anything below the knee. None of it waits.

Two things make this region harder than the others. It crosses two joints, so a knee complaint can be a hamstring problem and a hamstring complaint can be a hip one. And back-of-thigh pain is frequently not the hamstring at all: the buttock, the SI joint and a nerve all refer there.

Step 1. Test

Six things, in this order. None of them is a diagnosis. You never put hands on the leg to find out how bad it is.

The six tests

# Pain or discomfort, before anything else

sub: Do not ask for a number yet

Do this: "Is this pain, or is this discomfort? Don't rate it out of ten yet. Just tell me which one it feels like."

# Active versus passive

sub: She moves it herself, then it is moved for her

Why it happens: She bends her own knee, heel toward her backside, unloaded. Then the same joint is moved for her. A passive-then-active leg lift is a screen that is yours to run.

Do this: Painful when she does it, quiet when it is done for her: more likely muscle. Painful when moved for her: more likely the structure of the joint, and that is a referral.

# Where, to a hand's width

sub: Location does more work here than in any other region

Do this: Mid-belly, at the sit bone, inside of the back of the knee, outside of the back of the knee. Mid-belly is probably that muscle.

# Blood where blood should not be

sub: Bruising, swelling, heat, discolouration

Do this: This one ends the session. Suspected tear. Refer today.

# The lying curl under a deliberate tuck of the pelvis

sub: You are looking for strength that disappears

Do this: Lying curl. She uses her lower abs to drive her pelvis into the bench, then curls. Watch how much strength she does not have now.

# The twenty-three hours

sub: The position she holds all day, not the lift

Do this: Ask about a locked knee plus a slight hinge, held. Doing the dishes. Hiking uphill with a pack. The named hamstring exposure outside the gym.

Step 2. Read

The order is the point in this region, and it is not negotiable.

Read it in this order

Pain or discomfort. A severity number does not do this sorting. Discomfort can be brutal. Pain can be a four out of ten.

The red flags, which refer regardless of severity.

What changed, before you change anything. Not what is wrong.

The two numbers, last. Seven out of ten reported consistently. Still consistent between three days and one week.

Worked, because this is the question that gets got wrong

"Her hamstring has been sore for two weeks. When do I send her to a physio?" You do not open with "how bad is it". You ask which kind of sore it is. If it is the warning kind, two weeks of consistent symptoms is already past the three-days-to-one-week window. She was referable a week ago, and no score changes that. If it is the working kind, you still take the history, because two weeks of consistent anything means something changed.
