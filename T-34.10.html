Cueing · T-34.10

Two gates come before any cue, and neither can be cued

Before you say anything, check two things. Is the fault in the setup? Is it a strength gap rather than an awareness gap? Neither can be reached by cueing.

Is the fault in the setup? A position that was wrong before the rep started cannot be coached out downstream. No amount of talking reaches it. A lifter who has to throw her hips back to start a squat looks like a cueing problem. She is a setup problem: the bar is sitting too low on her back. Fix the setup.

Is this capacity rather than awareness? If she cannot hold the position because she is not strong enough to hold it, every cue is noise. Regress the exercise (regression) and train the missing quality. No cue will ever fix it.

The question that decides whether cueing is the right tool at all is where she feels it. Ask it as a question, never as a confirmation. "Where do you feel this?" tells you whether the movement is landing where it should. "You feel that in your glutes, right?" tells you nothing.

Check

Was the position right before the rep started?

Can she physically hold the position you want, at this load?

yes: Awareness or coordination is the gap. Cueing is the right tool. Build one cue.

no: Fix the setup, or regress the exercise and build the strength. No cue reaches either.

Watch for

Cueing something a strap, a wedge or a different bar height fixes in one move. Do that instead of talking.

Asking "where do you feel it" after you have already named the muscle. You have led her to the answer and lost the information.

Say before you decide whether to cue

Where do you feel that one?

Terms on this page

regression: An easier or more supported version of the same movement that keeps the pattern but asks less of her range, balance or load. It is chosen so she can hold the position the harder version breaks.
