Rest and intensity techniques · T-14.3

The usual one to two minutes for hypertrophy is too short

The common guideline is three to five minutes for strength, one to two minutes for hypertrophy (muscle growth) and thirty to sixty seconds for endurance. The middle figure is wrong. Rest longer.

What longer rest does. Three minutes between sets grows more muscle than one minute. Two and a half minutes beats one minute too. For strength in trained lifters, two minutes or more is needed to get the most from the work. Under a minute still builds strength, to a lesser degree. For strength in the untrained, one to two minutes is enough.

The direction is consistent, and it is the opposite of the guideline. Shorter rest does not grow more muscle. The reason a coach believes one to two minutes is optimal for hypertrophy is that it has been repeated, not that it was demonstrated.

Why it matters practically. A coach who has read the standard advice will look at the rest table and think the rests are too long. They are not. If anything, the top of each range is the better end.

Watch for

Cutting rest to fit a session into her time. Building a session owns that problem. The answer there is to change the program, not the recovery between sets.
