Variations

STS programs one push-up: the floor push-up. Two questions come up about it, and both have an answer.

Loading it past bodyweight. Do not load a push-up with a vest, a band or elevated feet. None of those is an STS method. When she has outgrown the floor push-up, the load goes into the loaded presses and the dip. That is what the push-up was preparing her for.

Two tempo tools you will hear about are written for pressing in general, not the push-up. One is a stretch-biased tempo of 3-2-1-1. The other is a 5-second lowering half as an intensifier. Tempo and pauses owns both.

Regressing it below bodyweight. There is no wall-to-incline-to-knee-to-floor ladder. Do not build one. A client who is weaker than the forces of gravity acting on her, put under unassisted bodyweight pressing, goes into a protective state. Her nervous system manages not falling over ahead of anything else, and it recruits compensating muscle rather than the target pattern. A lighter bodyweight variation does not fix that.

The fix is an externally braced machine press loaded below her bodyweight. Use a chest-press machine or an assisted machine. There the load itself can be turned down in a way a wall push-up cannot. Build back up from there.

What to do with a client who cannot do a floor push-up

Default to a machine press below her bodyweight. Not a wall or incline push-up.

A client who wants a push-up loaded harder than bodyweight is ready for the presses and the dip.

Deficit push-ups on handles. Handles let the upper arm travel deeper than the floor allows, which extends the stretch on the pec before she presses. They also keep the wrist neutral instead of forced back by the floor. Treat the extra depth as a stretch she may or may not need, not as an upgrade. The rest of her program should already be loading the mid-to-stretched range with a standard press.

Two conditions on any deficit

Any shoulder pinching or pain at the deeper angle means stop it immediately.

The depth must not exceed her own active range. That varies with her rib cage and torso, so a deficit that helps one client is redundant or harmful for another.
