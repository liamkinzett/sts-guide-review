When to use it

Use anti-rotation work for the client whose loads have outrun her movement awareness, and for the client who is too regressed to organise anything yet. Three of the reasons below are for the anti-rotation quality itself. Only the last names the Pallof press as the tool to reach for.

Reach for it when

# She is getting strong and the load has outrun her awareness

sub: Beginner to intermediate, enjoying training, loading in a way that does not suit how she is built

Confirm it: Her numbers are climbing and her low back is starting to complain. This is the population most affected by low-back trouble.

What it gets her: A trunk that transfers force between her upper and lower body, which is the root cause rather than a weak muscle. Resisting bending, arching and twisting through the lower back is ongoing program content for her. So are upper-back rotation and learning to turn from the hip rather than the lower back.

# She is genuinely regressed and cannot organise anything yet

sub: An entry point, and only that

Confirm it: She cannot hold a neutral trunk on hands and knees while a limb moves.

What it gets her: A place to start. The bird dog is highly remedial. You do not finish 15 bird dogs and then weight the bird dogs. You get comfortable on the bird dog and try the bear crawl. The failure is not doing it badly. It is staying in it.

# You need to take load off without taking training away

sub: Exercise selection is the first lever of load management

Confirm it: She needs a lighter week or a lighter slot, and you do not want to hand her nothing.

What it gets her: Load managed by what you picked rather than by what you loaded. The worked example of getting it wrong is a weighted bird dog: a pink dumbbell in one hand, an ankle weight on the other. Progress the drill by complexity and it becomes loadable on its own.

# Her back is what you are protecting, you have decided anti-rotation strength is part of it, and it is a hinge day

sub: The warm-up does two jobs

Confirm it: She is about to hinge heavy and her trunk is the weak link you have identified.

What it gets her: A range turned from mobile into strong before the main lift. Staggered-stance single-arm dumbbell rows, single-leg RDLs, bird dogs and Pallof presses, run in the warm-up. They bridge the gap from mobility work to strengthening those ranges inside the session.

Know what the Pallof press is not. It is a coordination and load-tolerance drill, and four things it is often reached for are not what it does.

What the Pallof press is not

# Not capacity work

sub: A coordination and load-tolerance drill

Why: There is no stretch, no shortening and no growth in it, so it does not build capacity.

# Not the first tool for teaching bracing

sub: A lying 90-90 sit-back teaches bracing better

Why: The press is the wrong tool for stage one, not a bad tool.

# Not stronger than a heavy row

sub: For most clients a heavy single-arm dumbbell row does the job better

Why: It challenges the trunk against rotation far more than a plank, a bird dog or a Pallof press. It replaces them rather than substituting for them.

# Not the answer for the obliques

sub: Train rotation itself

Why: Seated rotary machine, kneeling cable chop, half-kneeling chop, standing split-stance chop, in that order.

Do not stretch the list of reasons. The first three rows are what the quality is for. The third tells you what to do with the drill once it is in: progress it, never load it.
