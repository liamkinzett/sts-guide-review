Why a coach is needed when the science keeps changing

When she asks why she should trust a coach when the science keeps changing, answer with what a general number cannot do. A coach manages her, in real time, against the individual spread that an average erases.

General guidance does not change its mind arbitrarily. It updates when a large amount of new information has built up, often years apart. Good coaching practice routinely runs ahead of it. Almost every expert coach sets deload practice by experience rather than by a published figure.

The deeper reason. A population average does not predict an individual. Put a room of people on the same program for three months. Some barely get stronger. Some more than double their strength. Some add no visible muscle and some add a great deal. That spread is exactly what a coach manages week by week through reps in reserve, the logbook and the day's set deciding the load (autoregulation). A general figure cannot do that for a single client.

This is why her logbook outranks any general figure. A coach fielding this question is not defending herself. She is explaining what autoregulation is for.

Watch for

Answering with confidence instead of mechanism. "Trust me" is not an answer to "why should I trust you". A client sharp enough to ask will notice the dodge.

Say it like this

The science tells you what happened to a room full of people on average. On the exact same program, some people in that room barely moved and some got wildly stronger. Nobody can tell you in advance which one you'll be. That's my job. I read your numbers every week and adjust to you, not to the average.

Terms on this page

autoregulation: Letting how she performs on the day set the load, instead of a number fixed in advance.
