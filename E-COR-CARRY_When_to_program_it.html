Core · E-COR-CARRY#When to program it

When to use it

A carry teaches her trunk to accept load while she walks. It is not core-capacity work. Reach for it to teach load acceptance and coordination. Reach for trunk flexion to build capacity.

Reach for it when

# She already holds a plank for thirty seconds to a minute

sub: The only progression left in the plank is a longer clock

Confirm it: Her plank times keep climbing and her position never breaks.

What it gets her: The trunk under load instead of under a timer. Nobody should progress to holding a plank for 10 minutes. The carry is one of the two exits. Pick it when the demand you want is walking under load rather than moving through range.

# Her shoulders ride up toward her ears and she cannot get overhead

sub: The usual overhead correctives have been tried and have not held

Confirm it: More overhead drills for someone who cannot get overhead is already a lot of overhead work. She has done them and nothing moved.

What it gets her: Position bought from below instead of chased from above. A palms-forward carry is a loaded pull down the arm. It draws the shoulder blade down (scapular depression) and turns the shoulder outward, with gravity doing the reinforcing. It is the lowest-barrier entry of the low-reach options.

# She is a novice and the next thing you want is the Roman chair

sub: Accept a load before resisting one

Confirm it: She has never held a heavy weight in her hands while standing tall.

What it gets her: A range in which to accept load before she is asked to resist one. The carry applies a net downward force. The Roman chair then asks for a net upward one. The order is the point, and it is a real distinction.

# The traps are the target and the pressing slot is already spent

sub: Trap loading by net force

Confirm it: The session already has its press, and the traps still need work.

What it gets her: The shoulder blade being pulled off the rib cage loads the trap maximally and on the lengthening (eccentric). No hunting for a line of pull. Traps has the rest.

Not one of those is a core-capacity reason. A carry is not a good core exercise compared with what is possible when the trunk is challenged from insertion to origin. Capacity is built by loading the trunk through a range, on the trunk flexion page.

There is no dose. Every row says when. None says how much, how far, how long or how many, because no figure exists for it. Run the effort standard on Dose and record the numbers as your own.

One ordering rule. She should own a walking lunge before you ask her to carry anything.

Where the carry stands

A carry has no place of its own among STS's exercises yet, so it is not written into a client's program. This page teaches you to coach one for when it appears: in the strength system's lower session, or in a client's existing training.

The strength system's trunk slot reads "core, or a carry", and the suitcase carry is its stated favourite. Until STS programs a carry, fill that slot with core work.

Terms on this page

scapular depression: The shoulder blade drawn down the back, away from the ear.

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.
