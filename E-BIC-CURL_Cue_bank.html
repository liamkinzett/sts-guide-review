Cues

Cues go in the order printed. Try the first, and only move on when it fails.

Cues

When: Before the first rep

Say: Lock your upper arm and shoulder blades in stone.

Why: Internal cue. The first cue. It closes the two biggest leaks in one instruction: elbow drift and the shoulder blades shrugging.

When: The top of the rep

Say: Smash your forearms directly into your biceps

Why: External cue. It stops her cutting the last 10 to 15 degrees of elbow flexion.

When: She is lifting the handle rather than bending the elbow

Say: Forget about hands, focus on elbow flexion.

Why: Internal cue. It moves her attention off the weight and onto the joint that should be moving.

When: The wrist travels on a diagonal

Say: Rotate your torso outward so your palm can face straight up.

Why: External cue. A setup-level fix for the diagonal wrist path. Pair it with "Grip a post with your opposite hand to keep your torso angle locked."

For curls and most arm work, the internal cue is the one that grows the muscle. External cues win when the job is force on a single heavy rep. On a curl the job is growth, so the internal cues above are the focus, and the external ones fix a position fault. Cueing explains the split.
