Writing to a client · T-46.7

The channel makes a promise about your response time

Every channel promises a response time whether you set one or not. A text message wants an instant reply. Choose the channel knowing what it promises.

The coaching relationship tells you to set a response boundary before you need one. This rule says the channel sets one for you if you do not. A message on a chat app implies minutes. An email implies a day. A booked call implies that day, at that time. Pick the channel that promises what you can keep.

The hard conversation comes off text. There is no list of subjects that must never be handled in writing. There is a category, which is any conversation you are dreading. If you are dreading it, it is too big for text.

The two-step for opening it without forcing it. A coach saw a client who clearly just wanted to get through his workout. The music was loud and five other people were in the room. He did not force it there.

The text that opened the door, sent at the end of the day

Hey, all good. I'm around if you want to chat.

The client approached him the next day. The text opened the door. The conversation happened in person.

The blunter position is to refuse text entirely for anything difficult. Sit down and have it. Look her in the face. Over text you can do nothing. Either way, the conversation you are dreading is not a message.

Watch for

Sending the hard thing as a paragraph because it is easier for you. It is easier for you. It lands worse for her, and you cannot read her face while it does.
