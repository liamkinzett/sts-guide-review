Muscle and growth · T-01.24

Is there a genetic ceiling on muscle growth?

Do not quote her a genetic ceiling on muscle.

People clearly differ in how much muscle they can build. What we cannot do is look at one individual and accurately work out her personal ceiling. On one identical ten-week program, beginners gained anywhere from 3% to 14% in whole-muscle size, and their fibres changed anywhere from -19% to +22%. That spread is real. A number for anyone's personal ceiling is not.

Several factors are often presented as though they set a person's maximum muscular potential. None of them delivers what it promises.

The explanations she will name, and what to say

# Myostatin (myostatin)

sub: The protein that "limits growth"

What to say: In trained lifters it predicted nothing. Neither the starting level nor how it changed with training separated the high responders from the low. The one case for it as a limiter is a child with a rare mutation. Do not offer it as her ceiling.

# Satellite cells and added nuclei

sub: High responders add more nuclei to the fibre

What to say: Among beginners, high responders did show more of it (satellite cells). It did not separate trained lifters. Not a ceiling.

# The size of the fibres before training

sub: Bigger fast fibres at the start

What to say: In trained men, larger fast fibres at the start went with a smaller six-week response. One pointer, not a ceiling.

# Frame size and bone structure

sub: Small frame, small ceiling

What to say: Plausible, and taught in biomechanics. No number links frame size to a muscle limit. Do not give one.

# Fibre type itself

sub: Born slow-twitch or fast-twitch

What to say: In beginners training to failure, fibre type explained none of the difference. In trained lifters, fibre percentage and size were the strongest pointers. Not a ceiling either.

Rare genetic abnormalities affecting myostatin can produce dramatic muscularity in isolated cases. That does not mean normal variation in myostatin gives a coach a useful way to work out a trained person's ceiling.

What this means for coaching. There is no number and no single mechanism to hand her. What is real is that people vary enormously on the same program. Fibre size before training explains some of it, for a trained lifter. The two limiters everyone quotes, myostatin and satellite cell count, did not predict who grew in trained lifters. Train her, measure the response, and update expectations from her actual data.

Say it like this

Nobody can tell you your ceiling, and I won't pretend to. What we know is that people vary a lot on the same program. So we run yours and watch what you do.

Terms on this page

myostatin: A protein the body makes that holds muscle growth back. Popular as an explanation for a ceiling, it does not predict who grows.

satellite cells: Cells that sit on the surface of a muscle fibre and help it repair and grow.
