Returning to training · T-37.5

Start work on the compensation before it becomes the complaint

Rehabilitation has two jobs running at once. The second is nearly always skipped.

  • Manage the injured region: pain, swelling, range, guarding.
  • Mitigate the compensations the injury is about to create, before they are symptomatic.

    • The mechanism is the whole argument. The injury is protected by pain. The compensations are protected by nothing. She still gets out of bed, still walks, still loads one side more than the other. Best case, that runs four to six weeks. Realistically, twelve to twenty-four.

    Given enough exposure to a position, it always feels normal. Normal is a non-sensation. By the time the injury is resolved, the asymmetry has become her perceived neutral. Breaking it then takes a conscious phase where she does not trust her own movement. Starting compensation work early keeps the deviation small enough to be worth breaking.

    What the off-side work is. The diagonal chains of muscle (oblique slings), front and back, that transfer force through the opposite hip, the trunk and the lats. These are the tissues that will be overloaded on one side and underloaded on the other for months.

    Why it works. It is the difference between rehabilitating one region and rehabilitating a person. A client who returns with a clean knee and a six-month load asymmetry has not finished.

    A personal best that encodes the compensation

    Squatting through a healing ankle sprain. The squat went up. The load split moved from roughly three quarters and one quarter to two thirds and one third at best. It stayed there after the ankle healed.

    A number going up is not proof the problem went away. The load split can encode the compensation while the squat climbs.

    When it is disproportionate. Where the injury is minor and the interruption is days rather than weeks, this is too much. The rule is aimed at anything that changes how she moves for more than a fortnight.

    Say it like this

    The ankle's healing, but the rest of you learned to work around it. We're training the other side now, so the workaround doesn't become the new normal.

    Terms on this page

    oblique slings: Diagonal chains of muscle and fascia running across the body, front and back. They pass force from one hip through the trunk to the opposite shoulder and lat.
