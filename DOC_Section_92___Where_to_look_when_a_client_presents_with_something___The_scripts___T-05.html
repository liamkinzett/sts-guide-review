The female body: what to say

Words for her on the female body, by situation.

A script is only right inside its rule's conditions. Read the rule in The female body before you use the words.

No cycle phase reliably shifts her maximal strength by enough to program around. Do not adjust percentage loading for it.

Say it like this

We don't see a phase of the month that reliably shifts your strength enough to change what's on the bar. Some weeks a set of ten feels harder than others, and that's real. We work off what you actually report.

She asks you to program around her cycle calendar. STS does not.

Say it like this

I'm not going to program from a cycle calendar, because it doesn't hold up and because your cycle isn't the average one. I'd rather work off what you actually report week to week.

A withdrawal bleed is not a period. The pill hides low energy availability. Screen anyway.

Say nothing about her contraception. Ask about fuelling directly, the way you would with any client.

After menopause, heavy loads stop being optional.

Say it like this

We're going to keep some genuinely heavy work in here. It's the part that protects your bone and your power, and it gets more important with age, not less.

Invisible remodelling. The scale hides what is happening after menopause.

Say it like this

The scale is going to be the least useful number you have from here. Strength on the bar and how your clothes fit will tell you far more.

She asks whether holding her breath under a true 1RM is safe for her pelvic floor.

Build the line from the mechanism only: "brace, don't bear down". Tell her this exact situation has not been tested in women like her. Never borrow a pregnancy statistic.

She asks when her postpartum return is finished. There is no completion date.

Say it like this

There's no date where this is officially over. We keep the load climbing as long as it stays symptom-free. That's true years out, not just in the first year.

A clinician's "light," "moderate," or similar qualitative clearance is never a percentage for a coach to guess. Run it through the effort model instead.

Say it like this

Your physio's the one who gets to define light for your body right now. Tell me what that feels like to you and I'll build around it, rather than me guessing a percentage.

Pelvic floor dysfunction presents two opposite ways. A coach cannot tell which one she is looking at. Default to referral, not to teaching Kegels.

Say it like this

I'm not going to teach you Kegels blind. For a lot of people that actually makes it worse. Let's get you in front of a pelvic health physio who can tell you which kind of problem this is. Then I'll build around what they find.

First trimester: fatigue and nausea are the presenting problem. Volume is the first thing to come down, not load.

Say it like this

This isn't you falling behind. First trimester fatigue is real and it's expected, and the answer is fewer sets at the same weight, not pushing through it. Whatever you can do this week still counts.

She is breastfeeding and asks whether looser joints change her training. The mechanism is real; load off how the joint responds.

Say it like this

There's a hormone that stays elevated the whole time you're breastfeeding and it can loosen joints a bit. Whether that's actually what you're feeling, nobody can say for certain yet. Either way we load off how the joint responds, not off a theory.

The intake form asks about fuelling and menstrual regularity. It does not ask what phase of her cycle she is in.

Say it like this

I ask this of every client, not because I'm going to plan around your cycle. I don't, because it doesn't hold up. I ask because how regular your periods are tells me something about whether you're fuelling enough, and that's something I do want to know.

She asks about banking a deficit before a planned indulgence.

Say it like this

I'm not going to tell you banking calories before a trip is automatically a problem. What I do want to know is what the rest of the week around it looks like. And whether it's something you're choosing or something that's choosing you.

A scale jump right after starting or switching a hormonal method is read as a trend question, not a fuelling or training question.

Say it like this

One number the morning after a change like that doesn't tell us much on its own. Let's watch it over the next couple of weeks rather than react to today's reading. Nothing about your training changes either way.

She discovers she is pregnant mid-prep for a competition.

Say it like this

I can keep training you through this the way I train every pregnant client. What I can't do is build you toward stage condition on top of it. Nothing I'd be using to do that has ever been checked against pregnancy. Whether you compete is a conversation for you and your obstetric team, not something I'm going to talk you into or out of.

Early menses feels harder for a real reason. That reason is not evidence training changes how bad the cramps are.

Say it like this

The first day or two can feel harder because of where your hormones sit that week, not because anything is wrong. I can't promise training makes the cramps themselves better or worse, that hasn't been tested directly.

She has heard she faces higher ACL risk because she is a woman. Never tell her that.

Say it like this

Training builds the strength and control that protect a joint. I'm not going to tell you you're more at risk because you're a woman. Nothing solid says that.

Female tendon injury risk is site-specific and driven by energy availability. It is not a global sex difference to repeat as fact.

Say it like this

Some tendons genuinely show up more in women, but it's usually not about your tendons being weaker. It's usually about whether you're fuelling enough for what you're training.

She is trying to conceive. Training itself changes nothing; the energy-availability screen still runs.

Say it like this

If your cycle's regular and you're eating enough for what you're training, nothing needs to change yet. The thing worth watching is training right at the edge repeatedly, not training itself.
