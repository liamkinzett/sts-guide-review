Assessing a new client · T-06.12

Mobility decides what to load, not whether to load

Every client is screened during priming, regardless of goal. She does not progress load in a pattern she cannot yet get into. But a position she cannot reach never stops her training. She trains the version of the movement she can reach.

What the screen is not. It is not an injury filter. Movement screening scores are poor predictors of injury. The strongest predictor of lower-body injury risk is relative strength. Women who squat more than 1.6 times their bodyweight carry a much lower risk. Withholding load indefinitely on a screen can delay the very thing that reduces her risk. STS names no branded screening system for the same reason.

So read the screen as a selection instruction rather than a gate. It tells you which patterns she can be asked to load now. It tells you which need an easier version (regression). It tells you where pain needs looking at. The phases holds the criteria, and a failure there does not stop her training either.

How the screen actually happens. There is no separate battery of tests. She films her training and sends it to you across priming (movement screen). Choose what she films rather than leaving it to her. Priming's exercise selection should cover every movement pattern you need to see. Ask for footage of the lifts that give you that coverage. The phases covers how priming is built to make this possible.

It is coaching as well as assessment. Every clip that comes in goes back with feedback. So her technique is improving across the same block you are assessing it in. By the end of priming she is better than she was, and you know what she can actually do. That footage is what makes the per-pattern classification possible.

What a screen is for. It tells you which positions she can achieve today. That is a programming input, and it is the whole point. If she cannot reach depth without her pelvis tucking under (butt wink), you are not loading that depth yet. You shorten the range, load what she can hold properly, and use priming to open the rest.

Never tell her a screen shows she is at risk

Screens do not predict injury. Systems sold on that promise have been tested repeatedly, and they do not deliver it. Tell her a screen shows she is at risk of hurting herself and she will carry the fear a lot longer than the finding deserves.

What you are looking for

# Can she reach the positions the program asks for?

sub: The first question

Why it matters: It decides exercise selection before anything else does.

# What happens at the end of her range?

sub: A position either holds, or something else moves to allow it

Why it matters: The compensation is more informative than the range.

# Side to side differences

sub: Worth knowing before you load her on two legs

Why it matters: Load a difference bilaterally and the strong side quietly does the work.

# A baseline

sub: Re-check it at the end of priming

Why it matters: If nothing changed, the priming work was not doing what you thought it was.

What you do with it. Three options, and you will use all of them.

  • Shorten the range and load what she owns.
  • Swap to a pattern she can achieve while the original one improves.
  • Keep the pattern, keep the load light and let priming do its job.

    • What you never do is program the position and hope she grows into it under load.

    Watch for

    Screening her and then writing the program you were always going to write. A screen you do not act on is worse than no screen. It cost her time and told you something you then ignored.

    Terms on this page

    regression: An easier or more supported version of the same movement that keeps the pattern but asks less of her range, balance or load.

    movement screen: Watching her footage across priming to see which positions she can reach and hold. It decides what to load now, not whether she may train.

    butt wink: At the bottom of the squat her pelvis tucks under and her lower back rounds. It usually shows up when she runs out of hip range or loses her brace.
