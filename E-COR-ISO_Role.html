What it's for

A hold trains her to produce force without moving a joint, and to keep doing it as fatigue builds (isometric). She holds one posture against gravity. The fatigue resistance is the training effect, not a side effect of one.

What each hold is for

# Front plank

sub: Stabilises against spinal extension under fatigue

What it does: The trunk is held at a single length. No part of the range is loaded through movement. The demand rises with time under tension.

# Side plank

sub: Three separate jobs

What it does: Side-to-side mobility with the midsection stable against forces that try to slide the spine (shear). Compressing the rib cage from the sides as preparation for overhead work. And the entry point to resisting the rib cage tipping sideways, which comes before any dynamic side-bending is asked for.

# Where it sits in a session

sub: Three placements

What it does: A warm-up systems check before a squat session, asking whether she can hold a pelvic position while force runs through it. An upper-body warm-up before overhead work. Or a muscular-endurance slot, where progress is tracked as the time she can hold the position.

# Why it suits a less-trained client

sub: Put her in the position and let her feel it

What it does: She sits in the position until she becomes aware of it (forced awareness). That awareness is what she carries back into her lifts.

Terms on this page

isometric: A hold. The muscle works against the load but the joint does not move.

shear: A force that slides one part of a joint sideways across the other. In the lower back it is one vertebra sliding forward on the one below.

forced awareness: Putting her into a position and letting her sit in it until she can feel it. The feeling she keeps is the product, not the time she held it.
