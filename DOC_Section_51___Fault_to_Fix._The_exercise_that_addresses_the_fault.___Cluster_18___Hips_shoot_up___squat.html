Hips shoot up on the squat: raise the bar

If she has to throw her hips back to start the rep, the bar is too low on her back. Raise it until she can start with hips and knees together. This is a setup fault, not a cueing one, and this is the setup.

Hips shoot up on the squat

# She throws the hips back to initiate

sub: Setup: raise the bar

What you see: The hips open before the knees bend. The shin goes vertical.

Why it happens: With the bar low, the only way to move is to throw the hips back under it.

Do this: Raise the bar on her back until she can initiate with hips and knees together.

# Her chest drops out of the hole

sub: Accessory or regression: load in front, or a machine

What you see: She cannot hold tension through the hips, the chest drops out of the hole, and she good-mornings the weight up.

Do this: Front squat, high bar, or leg press. Take the load to where she can keep the trunk up.

Exercises: Barbell front squat; High bar barbell back squat; Leg press

# The over-corrected heel

sub: Setup: reduce the heel

What you see: With a big heel elevation she points forward, falls forward, and good-mornings the weight up.

Why it happens: Too much heel produces this fault. It tips her forward.

Do this: Reduce the heel elevation. A fractional plate, not a bumper.
