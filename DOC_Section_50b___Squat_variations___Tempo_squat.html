Tempo squat

A tempo squat slows the rep down, and it is the fix for a lifter who cannot hold tension through her hips. It is one of three parallel fixes. A tempo squat for tension through the hips. A three-count pause bench for tension on the chest. A pause deadlift for tension off the floor.

It is also the squat for achy knees. No bounce out of the bottom (the hole). Rep ranges run 10 to 12, 12 to 15 and 15 to 20 with a slow tempo. The rep range is what caps the bar weight.

How long the descent takes

Standard | 2-3

Learning the movement, or a joint needs caution | 3-4

The ceiling | 4 | Nothing goes past four seconds

A very tall client | 5 | The one exception to the ceiling

The counts. The lowering (eccentric) is two to three seconds as standard, three to four while she is learning the movement or where a joint needs caution. Nothing past four seconds, five for a very tall client.

Relearning a movement after a layoff or a corrected fault is `3-4/1/1/1`. The long descent lets her feel the position. The pause at the stretch proves she owns the bottom. The pause at the top stops her chaining reps and hiding the fault in the rhythm.

The strength-system rule for the secondary-day variation. For a competitive powerlifter, the tempo goes on the lowering and on the lifting (concentric), with the focus on position. The common pattern of a tempo eccentric and then exploding up is rejected. Lifters lose position on the way up, or fall back on their strongest technique, which is the technique being fixed.

Terms on this page

the hole: The bottom of the squat, where she turns around and drives up.

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.

concentric: The lifting half of a rep, where the muscle shortens under load.
