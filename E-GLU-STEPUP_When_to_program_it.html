When to use it

The problem you notice. She looks clean on every two-footed single-leg movement and you cannot tell whether she owns one leg. Or her knees are achy. Or a hip, or a cranky low back, cannot take a hinge every session. The step-down answers all of those, so most of this page is about the step-down.

For the step-up itself the reason to program it is simple. It has a low barrier to entry, and it exposes a left-right imbalance quickly.

Reach for the step-down when

# She looks clean on every other single-leg movement

sub: Split squats, B-stance RDLs and stationary lunges all leave her on two feet

Why it happens: On two feet she can always shift into the back leg or get the base of support too wide. Even the Bulgarian is front-leg dominant rather than genuinely one-legged.

Do this: A step-down. It is the only truly single-leg option in the family. She cannot compensate to a back leg, so the compensation has nowhere left to go.

# Her knees are achy, or she is an older client

sub: She needs loaded single-leg work she can tolerate

Do this: A supported low-box step-down. Step-downs are great for knees, and the supported low box is the named regression for exactly this.

# She has hip pain and can't take a B-stance RDL every session

sub: The hip still needs training

Do this: A step-down. It is something slightly less hinge-heavy, so the hip is still trained with the hinge demand taken off.

# She is coming back from a cranky low back

sub: A single-leg hinge is still too much

Do this: A step-down, as a bridge rather than a jump. It combines hip and knee, rather than being a pure single-leg hinge.

The coaching error that manufactures a weak-glute diagnosis

Blocking her pelvis from shifting sideways during a step-up removes the hip rotation that force production depends on (closed-chain internal rotation). Staging the box against a wall does the same thing.

Then she gets blamed for the result and diagnosed with weak glutes.

Let the hip come out; keep ten toes down. The sideways shift is the internal rotation. Don't block it.

If she objects that it will grow her quads

A low-load, position-focused movement doesn't build a body part. A bodyweight side bend doesn't build your obliques either.

Know what it is. The step-down is a quad exercise as much as a glute one, and there are better glute isolations. Its job is standardising the single-leg position before you bias anything. Standardise the basics first, then bias.

Terms on this page

closed-chain internal rotation: With her foot fixed on the box, the pelvis shifting sideways over it turns the thigh inward at the hip. That rotation is where the glute produces force from, so blocking the shift weakens her.
