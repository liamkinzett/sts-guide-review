The phases · T-07.37

Prescribe the deload, do not negotiate it

A client who skips or PRs through her deloads is not withholding buy-in. She is being asked to agree with a self-assessment she cannot make. Prescribe the week rather than negotiating it.

Why the compliance read is backwards. Less experienced lifters consistently misjudge how close they are to failure and how much fatigue they are carrying. One client type does this reliably: the one who does everything you write and hides her fatigue (Upholder). Asking her to agree that she needs a lighter week asks her to self-diagnose the one thing she cannot reliably self-diagnose. Assessing a new client and Reading her training data both cover this.

The deload already removed most of what she would object to. Load, effort, exercises and days all hold. Only the set count drops. So the line to her is not "do you think you need this."

Say it as a decision

Same weight, same effort, fewer sets this week.

What changes is the delivery, not the design. State it as a decision rather than a request. The three deload triggers are read from her data. They are not agreed with her in advance. A client who PRs through a deload week has broken the one variable that must hold, the load. That is a programming instruction to enforce, not a buy-in failure to solve with more conversation.

Watch for

Treating a repeated pattern as a motivation problem. Where a client structurally cannot self-report fatigue, the fix is prescriptive, not persuasive.

Terms on this page

Upholder: A client who does everything you write and pushes through fatigue rather than reporting it. She cannot be relied on to tell you she is tired.
