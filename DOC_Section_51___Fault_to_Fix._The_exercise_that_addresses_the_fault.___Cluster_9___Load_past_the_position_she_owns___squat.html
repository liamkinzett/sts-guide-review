Load past the position she owns: constrain, do not cue

If you are telling her 13 things during a squat and every one matters, you picked the wrong movement. Constrain the movement rather than cue it. The cue count is the tell.

The position breaks down once the bar is loaded. This is a capacity problem, not a technique problem, and no cue reaches it. Every fix here is a regression.

The graded ladder, in order

Strip the bar. A PVC pipe or a 15-pound training bar until she does it right.

Counterbalance squat, then goblet squat, then front squat, then Bulgarian split squat, then high bar. Light, and graded. Six to eight weeks is the timeframe on that exposure.

A variation so hard that going heavy is impossible. A five-second eccentric, two-count pause front squat to a box. The constraint caps the load, not her intent.

If even a heels-elevated counterbalance squat is beyond her, leave the pattern. Leg press, leg extension, hamstring curl. Build resilience in isolation first. You do not even begin the squat conversation until then.

Why each rung works

# Stripping the bar

sub: Regression

Why: The position is all she is asked for. Load returns when the position holds.

# The graded ladder

sub: Regression with a timeframe

Why: Each rung brings the centre of mass closer or gives her more support. She earns the next rung.

Exercises: Goblet squat; Barbell front squat; DB Bulgarian split squat; High bar barbell back squat

# The unloadable variation

sub: Regression by constraint

Why: A five-second lowering (eccentric) and a two-count pause to a box make heavy impossible. Her intent is not what caps the load; the variation is.

Exercises: Barbell front squat

# Leaving the pattern

sub: Regression out of the pattern

Why: A lifter who cannot own a heels-elevated counterbalance squat needs strength before she needs a squat.

Exercises: Leg press; Leg extension; Lying hamstring curl

Watch for

A long cue list. Stop cueing and change the exercise. A leg press needs none of those 13 cues, which is the point.

Terms on this page

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.
