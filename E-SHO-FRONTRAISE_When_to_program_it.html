When to use it

Start with the case against. For anyone already pressing heavy, added front raises are "redundant junk volume" (junk volume). Pressing has already overloaded the front delts from mid-range to the stretched position.

Do not reach for this movement to build front delts. Its clearest use is a kind of client, not a goal.

The problem you notice. Overhead pressing is provoking her shoulder, you have worked down the press ladder, and she is on the bottom rung. That is when the front raise earns its place.

Reach for it when

# Overhead pressing provokes her shoulder and you have worked down the press ladder

sub: Shoulder function kept in the program while the overhead position is out

Confirm it: Every rung of the vertical press ladder above this one provokes her. See Overhead press, Swaps.

What it gets her: Three things, through 0 to 90 degrees. The arm travelling forward and up (shoulder flexion). The upper arm turning out (external rotation). The delt loaded on the way down (eccentric). In the prescriber's own words: "I'm not programming this from a delt perspective, although it is a delt exercise. I'm programming it more as a shoulder function thing at the end of her session".

# She is already on the bottom rung of the vertical press ladder

sub: The terminal rung, chosen for the joint range rather than for the muscle

Confirm it: There is nothing below it. "We can go all the way down that spectrum to a dumbbell front raise."

What it gets her: The joint range kept. There is no further swap named below it, and its own escalation ladder ends in stopping the exercise. See Swaps.

Two goals, two answers, and the goal decides. For size, front raises on a client who presses heavy are junk volume: do not add them. For shoulder function under a load restriction, the front raise is a deliberate prescription. Name which of the two you are programming before you write it.

The slow eccentric is the point when it is used this way

Loading the delt on the way down is part of the stated target (eccentric). It is not a flourish and it is not a strength movement. Fight the urge to let the weights drop.

Setup. Stand tall, shoulders down, dumbbells in front of the thighs and not resting on them. Setup has the full list.

Terms on this page

junk volume: Sets that add fatigue but no extra growth, because the muscle has already had all the productive work it can use.

shoulder flexion: The arm travelling forward and up in front of the body, all the way to overhead.

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.

external rotation: Turning a limb outward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face forward or up, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more to the side. An arm held that way is externally rotated.
