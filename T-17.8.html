Steps and cardio · T-17.8

HIIT buys cardiovascular ability. For calories, low intensity is almost always better

If the job is cardiovascular ability, interval work is a legitimate and efficient way to buy it. If the job is calories, low intensity does it better in almost every case.

High-intensity interval training (HIIT) means short, very hard efforts separated by easier recoveries.

"Almost" is doing real work in that sentence. We usually stick to low intensity for expenditure. Intervals are not banned. A client with twenty minutes and a strong preference is better served by intervals she will do than by a walk she will skip.

Why it works. Go back to which job she is buying. HIIT buys a smaller time cost by paying a much larger metabolic one. When you are buying a cardiovascular adaptation, that trade can be worth it. The metabolic disturbance is the stimulus. When you are buying calories, you have paid a large fatigue price. A longer, easier session would have delivered the same calories for almost nothing.

The one place it hardens into a rule is a deficit. She is already under-recovered. The interference cost is highest exactly when recovery is lowest. And the calories were available cheaply.

When it is used, separate it from lifting. The metabolic disturbance can acutely inhibit the muscle-building pathway (mTOR). Different day where possible, several hours otherwise.

Watch for

HIIT arriving under another name when the job is really expenditure. Circuits, conditioning finishers, "a quick metcon". The label is not the variable. Ask what it is buying and price it accordingly.

Say it like this

Intervals are great if we're training your engine. If we're just after the calories, a longer easy session costs you far less and burns the same.

Terms on this page

HIIT: High-intensity interval training. Short, very hard efforts separated by easier recoveries.

mTOR: The signalling pathway inside the muscle that switches on protein building after training.
