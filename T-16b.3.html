Mobility and stretching · T-16b.3

Weeks of stretching buy stretch tolerance, not longer tissue

The extra range she gains from a few weeks of stretching is not a physical change in the muscle or tendon. Her nervous system has agreed to let her go further before it complains (stretch tolerance).

She is usually right about the range and wrong about the reason. She can reach further. Her tissue has not lengthened. The nervous system permits a greater joint angle before registering the stretch as pain, and that is the whole of the change.

Why the distinction is not pedantry. Stretch tolerance is a real, useful adaptation, and it fades when the stimulus stops. Structural change is durable enough to survive a break. Telling her she has lengthened her hamstrings sets an expectation the adaptation cannot meet. When the range disappears over a fortnight off, she concludes she has lost something she never had.

What it looks like. She stretched every evening for three weeks and can now put her palms on the floor. She goes on holiday for two weeks and comes back unable to. Nothing was lost. The permission was withdrawn.

Say it like this

You have not made the muscle longer. You have taught your body to allow more range before it protests. That is worth having. It goes away when you stop.
