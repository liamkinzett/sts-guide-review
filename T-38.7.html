Very short sessions or two days a week change the arithmetic, not the logic

A session capped well under the design window, or a week cut to two days, is the compressed-week rule taken further. The selection and progression logic do not change. Only the arithmetic does.

Selection. Protect the compounds, and let isolation be what a fuller week affords, exactly as before. The degree of compression changes nothing about that.

Frequency. Split design calculates frequency as her weekly set band for a muscle divided by the per-session ceiling. That arithmetic still runs. It simply returns more sessions carrying less each, or fewer sessions carrying more. Daily short sessions are the first. Two days a week is the second.

Progression. Reps first, then load, then a set (double progression) applies at two sessions a week exactly as it does at five. What changes is how many chances she gets to earn the next step, not the order in which she earns it. Progression has the rules.

Session length. Sessions are built inside a 45 to 70 minute window. Treat that window as the reference. When a session must run shorter than it describes, cut content, never structure. The order stays. The number of exercises and sets comes down.

Watch for

Inventing a new system for a short session. A short session is the same session with less in it, priority work first. If the structure changed, something other than time changed it.

Say it like this

Two days is enough. We put the big lifts on both days and skip the small stuff until your week opens up again.

Terms on this page

double progression: Add reps inside a fixed rep range until she reaches the top of it on every working set, then add load and drop back to the bottom of the range. Only then is a set added, and the order never changes.
