Before you call it genetics, climb the ladder

"That's just her" is a claim about her genetics. Before you make it, the ladder has to have actually been climbed. There is no number of blocks that proves a ceiling. What there is instead is a test of whether a real attempt was ever made.

People vary enormously on identical programs. Over 10 weeks on the same program, one lifter may grow a muscle by 3% and another by 14%. That is real. It is a different thing from a ceiling, and it does not tell you when to stop trying on a given client.

So the test is not a count. It is whether the ladder was actually run.

For one lagging muscle. Check her execution and the structure of the movement first. Both sit before the dosing ladder and both are frequently skipped. Then ask what the ladder has actually done for that muscle. A muscle that has only ever had sets added to it has not been tested against her genetics. It has been tested against a dose that never took anything away from a competing muscle.

Redistribution is the one part of the ladder that counts as a real test. A dominant muscle genuinely held at maintenance for a full block, while the lagging one gets the freed volume. The threshold is at least one full hypertrophy block run at the redistribution step, with execution and structure cleared first.

Two such blocks back to back with nothing else changing is a stronger claim than one. Both are more than most coaches actually run before saying "that's just her".

A common shape. Her glutes grow off anything while her hamstrings on the identical program will not move. Before that becomes a genetics claim, check whether the hamstrings have ever had a dominant muscle's volume redistributed to them. Only ever having sets added on top does not count.

For the whole physique. The same order applies at a larger scale. The stall list has to be genuinely clear before "gaining nothing" means anything: effort, technique, recovery including food, then volume. A client who has not been screened for under-recovery or under-fuelling has not tested a ceiling. She has tested whether her coach checked the first three items.

What this licenses and what it does not. Climb the ladder first. Then you may tell her plainly that one muscle responds slower than the rest of her. It does not license treating one flat block as proof of a ceiling. Nor one missed item on the stall list.

Watch for

Reaching for a biological explanation. Three get cited: frame size, the cells that repair muscle (satellite cells) and the protein that limits growth (myostatin). None of them predicts who responds. Do not reach for one of these as the reason.

Terms on this page

satellite cells: Cells that sit on the surface of a muscle fibre and help it repair and grow. Their number does not predict who responds to training.

myostatin: A protein the body makes that holds muscle growth back. Levels of it do not predict who responds to training either.
