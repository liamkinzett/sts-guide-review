Common faults

Four common faults

# Elbow off its line

sub: Elbow drifting forward. Wrists shifting diagonally on a two-arm curl. A palm-up position forced on a wrist and shoulder that cannot support it

Why it happens: Her external rotation runs out before the palm reaches straight up (external rotation). The dumbbell then travels on a diagonal while the load falls straight down. That is the mechanism behind medial elbow pain on curls.

Do this: Change the implement before changing the cue. Single-arm, torso turned outward, free hand braced. If the palm-up position still cannot be held, hammer or neutral grip.

# Body English

sub: Swinging. "A case of the wiggles"

Why it happens: The load is more than the elbow flexors can move on their own, so the hips and shoulders start the rep.

Do this: Reduce the load.

# Range cut short

sub: Cutting the top of the range

Why it happens: The last 10 to 15 degrees of elbow flexion are the hardest to finish, and she stops where the handle stops rising.

Do this: Complete the rep. Cue "Smash your forearms directly into your biceps".

# Shruggie

sub: Traps rise at the top, blades creep forward

Why it happens: The shoulder blades were not set before the rep. Or she has run out of range at the elbow and is finding it at the shoulder.

Do this: End the rep.

Terms on this page

external rotation: Turning a limb outward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face forward or up, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more to the side. On the screen it is what she does with the arms held straight out in front.
