Getting sick repeatedly is training-relevant

A client getting sick for the third time in one training block is showing a training-response signal in a different costume. Screen recovery and energy availability before calling it bad luck.

Why. Too little food for her training and the rest of her body (low energy availability) shuts down immune function, among other things. The usual sign to watch for is an inability to gain or maintain muscle. Recurring illness is the same mechanism reporting through a different system.

The route in is the one every stall takes. Run the stall checklist in Volume, in its order: training, technique, recovery, volume. Recovery and energy availability are the third and fourth steps, and that is where this lands. It is the same place a plateau in a lean, hard-training client already routes to.

What this is not. A licence to attribute every cold to the program. Illness has causes that have nothing to do with training: a household with young children, a change of season, an unrelated exposure. The trigger is the pattern, not the single instance: repeated illness inside one block, alongside training that is not producing the response it should.

A single cold or a fever is a different question. While she is unwell, training stops, and a head cold gets the same advice. Her doctor decides when she is well enough to come back. Returning to training's illness pages hold the protocol and the way back.

Why this is your judgement. How many is "repeated enough to investigate" is your call. What is fixed is the order: run the checklist before defaulting to coincidence. A stalled lift gets audited before you conclude she has hit a ceiling. Treat this the same way.

Say it like this

Getting sick this often while training hard isn't nothing. Before I chalk it up to bad luck, I want to go through the usual checklist: training, technique, recovery, volume. The same way I would for a lift that's stopped moving.

Terms on this page

low energy availability: Not enough food coming in to cover both her training and the rest of what her body needs to run. Immune function is one of the things it shuts down.
