Muscle and growth · T-01.5

Does biasing load toward the stretched position help growth?

Bias her load toward the stretched position where the movement allows it. It is a modest edge, not a decisive one. Coach it that way.

Training a muscle while it is producing meaningful force at longer muscle lengths appears to offer a hypertrophy advantage. The useful word is modest. This is not a reason to rebuild an entire program around chasing stretch at any cost.

The stretched position means the muscle is at its longest while it carries the load (lengthened position). A muscle is in that position when it is relatively long while still carrying meaningful load. This is not the same thing as simply feeling a stretch. What matters is the combination of muscle length and loading.

How big the edge is. Training a muscle at long lengths grows it a little more than training it at short ones. Changing the average length a muscle works at across ordinary dynamic training changes very little.

Those are two different things. One looks at where in the range the load bites. The other looks at the muscle's average length across a whole rep. So the bias is real where the load bites at length, and modest.

What to do. Bias load toward the stretched position where the movement allows it. Use it where it is free. Do not chase it where it costs position or load. Do not sell it to her as the secret.

The phases has her bias her load toward the stretched position. Keep doing it.

Why it matters. A coach who treats the stretched bias as decisive will over-sell it and reorganise programs around it. A coach who treats it as a modest edge uses it where it is free. It is not chased where it costs position or load.

Say it like this

We load the stretched part of the movement where we can, because it helps a bit. It's a small edge, not a secret.

Terms on this page

lengthened position: The point in the rep where the working muscle is at its longest while it carries the load. It describes the muscle's length, not whether she feels a stretch.
