Choosing exercises · T-13.17

Upper-back burn on a front squat means her position ended the set

Her quads feel fine and her upper back is smoked. That means her position ended the set, not her legs.

A front squat holds its quad-dominant, upright-torso demand only while her upper back can hold the bar in place (rack position) without moving (isometric). When that hold fails first, the set ends there. Her quads never reach the load the program assumed they would.

What to check before concluding anything about her quads. Watch the footage. Is her torso losing its upright position as the set goes on? Is she failing on rack position before she fails on depth or drive? If either is true, the exercise is not currently quad work in the volume the plan credited it with, whatever it says on paper.

What to do about it

# Build upper-back capacity

sub: So the hold outlasts the set

Do this: Add work that builds her ability to hold the rack position for longer.

# Reduce the load

sub: Until the rack hold outlasts the set

Do this: Drop the weight until her upper back is no longer what ends the set.

# Select a different quad-dominant movement for now

sub: One that does not load the upper back isometrically

Do this: Give her quads a movement that does not ask the upper back to hold a bar, while that capacity catches up. A selection choice, not a step down.

Why it works. A coach who reads "quads feel fine, upper back is smoked" as proof the exercise "isn't working the quads" will chase the wrong fix. The muscle assigned the work and the muscle that ends the set are not always the same muscle. This is a training-tolerance and technique question, not a medical one.

Terms on this page

rack position: The bar held across the front of her shoulders with the elbows high, as in a front squat.

isometric: A hold. The muscle works against the load but the joint does not move.
