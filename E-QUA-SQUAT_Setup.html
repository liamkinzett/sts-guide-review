Setup

Set her up in this order

Stance is individual and it is never copied. Bone length, hip structure and the muscle around the hip decide it. The standard is a stance that is comfortable and lets her keep a neutral pelvis at the bottom. Toes turn very slightly out, roughly 20 degrees from centre at shoulder width. That minimises unnecessary twisting load at the knee and the hip.

The bar sits on soft tissue, never on bone. High bar crosses the upper traps just below the bony bump at the base of her neck (the C7 spinous process). Low bar sits roughly 5 to 6 cm lower, on the shelf built by the rear delts. A front squat sits behind the shelf of the front delts, not on the muscle belly. If it feels to her like it is resting on bone, it is in the wrong place.

Full fist, hands as close together as her shoulder mobility allows. A closer grip bunches the upper back into a thicker, more protective shelf. A loose, open-fingered grip is a major fault.

Set the upper back before the unrack. Pull the shoulder blades back and down, squeeze the elbows back to switch the mid-back on, then drop the shoulder girdle to lock the lats. This is what stops energy leaking out through the upper body.

Brace in order: abs first, then breath. Contract the abdominals and bring the ribs down towards the pelvis to set it neutral. Then take the air.

Unrack with the legs. Feet directly under the bar at midfoot, then stand straight up rather than hinging the weight out of the rack. Three steps back into the stance, looking straight ahead.
