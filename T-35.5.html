Pain rules and when to refer · T-35.5

The bucket method: sort every movement into three

Sort every movement, position and daily activity she does into three buckets: it makes it better, it changes nothing, it makes it worse. This is where you will spend almost all of your time. It needs no diagnosis, which is exactly why it is yours.

Ask her. Then watch her.

BucketWhat it isWhat it becomes
PalliativeMakes it feel betterThe program
No effectChanges nothingAlso the program
ProvocativeMakes it feel worseThe queue, ranked

A movement that helps is (palliative). A movement that hurts is (provocative).

Then four steps

Train the palliative and no-effect buckets. That is the whole session. It is not a holding pattern until she is better. It is the thing that makes her better.

Rank the provocative bucket from most tolerable to least. "Uncomfortable but not bad" sits at the top. "Absolutely not" sits at the bottom.

That ranking is the order movements come back. You do not have to decide when. The ranking decides.

Re-test the top of the queue after a block of palliative work. If it is better, that is your progress marker. If it is not, she stays where she is.

Reason backwards from the buckets to the mechanism. If stairs hurt going down, the shared feature is a loaded lowering (eccentric) at depth. Other loaded eccentrics at depth move to the queue with it. If a hip flexor stretch relieves it, things that unload the same tissue go into the program. You are not naming the problem. You are describing what the problem responds to, which stays inside your scope.

When a movement has to stay in: the lever order

Change the position. Tendon pain is position-specific rather than load-dependent (Triceps explains why). If the elbow hurts on skull crushers and not pushdowns, the variable is the shoulder position. Scaling the position is not the same as scaling the load.

Reduce range to what is painless. Keeps the load and the pattern.

Reduce load. Keeps the range.

Change the exercise. A long list of modifications is the wrong exercise.

Stop the movement, and review.

The wrong regression makes it worse

The lever follows the mechanism, not the movement's name.

Her low back hurts on a high-bar squat. Switching to low-bar is wrong where the mechanism is shear, since low-bar increases it.

Her deadlift hurts off the floor. Pulling from a raised platform (a deficit pull) is wrong for the same reason. Pull from blocks. Then progress down.

Why it works. It converts an injury from a reason to stop into a program, without anyone naming a condition. Avoiding provocation is not treatment. A client who only ever removes the painful thing ends up in a smaller and smaller box, until walking hurts too.

Watch for

Explaining this to her is not admin. It is part of the treatment. Showing a client that you understand what provokes and what relieves is your biggest lever on the part of her pain that is fear. Coaches internalise the plan and skip the why. Say the why out loud.

Say it like this

We're going to find everything that doesn't hurt, and that's your program for now. Not a lighter version of your program. Your actual program. Then we bring things back in the order you can tolerate them.

It does not outrank your scope, the emergency flags or the referral triggers. It does not need to. It runs inside them.

Terms on this page

palliative: A movement or position that makes her pain feel better. Palliative movements go straight into the program.

provocative: A movement or position that makes her pain feel worse. Provocative movements are ranked and queued, and come back in that order.

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.
