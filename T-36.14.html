Before she returns to impact, the calf must move her own bodyweight

Do not return her to running, hopping or plyometrics on how she feels or on the calendar. The gate is a calf that can lift at least her own bodyweight on one leg.

Key figure

value: 1 × bodyweight

unit: seated single-leg heel raise, the floor to clear before impact

note: Progression tracks strength, not weeks

Where the figure comes from. A group of male recreational runners with long-standing mid-tendon Achilles pain was taken back to hopping and running by strength, not by date. Pain fell as heel raise strength rose from just under bodyweight to about one and a third times it.

One man tore his calf on the injured side. He was the only person at the final level whose seated heel raise was below his own bodyweight.

He tore it playing basketball, against advice, not doing the protocol. The failure was returning to sport too early.

Why it works. It replaces the two things coaches use instead: the calendar, and whether it hurts. Returning to training already says a pain-free rep does not mean the stimulus has been earned. This says what earning it looks like.

Read the figure for what it is

It was measured on fifteen men, not on anyone like her, and it describes the Achilles. Treat it as a floor to clear, never as a validated threshold or a promise.

It sits alongside, never instead of, the impact ceiling: pain of 3 out of 10 or less on a five-rep single-leg hop. Pain rules and when to refer holds that number.
