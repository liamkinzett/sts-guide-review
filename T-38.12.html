A major life stressor is a circumstance, not a choice

A major life stressor is a circumstance, not a choice. Whether it stays temporary or turns permanent decides whether you hold the plan or replan it.

A client training "for normality" while a diagnosis in her family sits underneath the week is not asking you to manage the diagnosis. She is asking you to keep one part of her life recognisable. Hold the line where the compressed-week rule and her goal pathway already hold it. Keep the compounds. Keep the goal. Let the plan absorb whatever the week gives you room for.

Name the stressor once, plainly. Stop there. You may name the factor. You are not her counsellor, and you do not go further into managing it.

Then the test. If the stress becomes consistent, a part of her normal lifestyle, it has to be accounted for properly. The plan is adjusted to be achievable, and the expectations around her goal are adjusted with it.

Temporary circumstance is exempt from any adherence judgement. Permanent circumstance is a replan.

Compared

columns: Temporary | Permanent

What it looks like: A new diagnosis whose shape over the next months is still unknown | Ongoing caregiving, or a chronic new normal

What you do: Hold the plan and the goal. Absorb what the weeks give you | Renegotiate the plan and the goal timeline out loud

What you never do: Judge the missed work | Ride it out silently

Watch for

A new diagnosis is temporary and exempt while its shape is still unknown. Where it hardens into ongoing caregiving or a chronic new normal, the plan and the goal timeline get renegotiated out loud. Not silently ridden out.

What this page does not do. It does not manage the grief itself. Where the pattern needs more than naming, you escalate rather than improvise: The coaching relationship has the rule. Anything the situation touches that is clinical belongs to Pain rules and when to refer.

Say it like this

I'm not going to pretend this isn't happening. We keep the two big sessions, we keep the goal, and the rest of the week bends around what you've got.
