Steps and cardio · T-17.3

Choose the machine first. Cycling over running

Cycling is the default. Running is the exception and it needs a reason.

Cycling and other low-impact work where the muscle mostly shortens under load (concentric) cost the legs the least. Running costs them the most.

What running costs. Lifting done alongside running loses real lower-body muscle and strength. Growth of the slow fibres (type I fibres) is blunted by running and not by cycling.

Why it works. Running is heavily eccentric (eccentric) and carries high ground-reaction forces. It produces micro-damage and a long recovery tail. Cycling is almost entirely concentric and mechanically close to a leg press. It lines up with the strength work rather than fighting it.

Running loses more fat. The rule has to say so. Lifting alongside running loses noticeably more fat mass than lifting alongside cycling. The difference is large. It is the strongest argument anyone will make for the treadmill.

So the choice is a real trade, not a free win. Cycling protects the training. Running loses more fat, at a cost to the legs. Which is right depends on the job the cardio is buying.

Compared

columns: Cycling | Running

Choose it when: Her cardio is for cardiovascular ability. Or it sits alongside a block that trains legs | Her cardio is for expenditure and fat loss is the goal. The block is not leg-focused

What you are buying: Cardio without the interference cost. That cost is the one thing you are not buying | More fat lost, at a cost to the legs. The page on where the cost lands tells you where

The default stands because most of our clients train legs hard. Know the trade all the same. A coach who puts everyone on a bike without knowing running loses more fat is following a rule. She is not making a decision.

This is the largest single lever in the topic. Compare sixty minutes on a treadmill with sixty on a bike. They are not the same amount of damage to her training.

Watch for

Prescribing minutes and a zone without naming the machine. That is not a complete prescription.

Say it like this

Same time, same effort, bike instead of the treadmill. It costs your legs a lot less.

Terms on this page

concentric: The lifting half of a rep, where the muscle shortens under load.

type I fibres: Slow-twitch muscle fibres. They tire slowly and suit long, easy work.

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.
