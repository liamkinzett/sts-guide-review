Supersets are not programmed by default. Never to save time

Logistically, supersets can be a nightmare. Unless absolutely necessary, no supersets. Reach for one only where being continuous is the mechanism, which is the narrow case the three superset structures exist for.

Never use a superset to fit a session into a time limit. Cut sets instead. A five-day program once used paired isolation work to fit sessions into 50 to 70 minutes. The pairing was the wrong tool. A short session is built by the same arithmetic as a full one. Building a session has that rule.

Which gives when time is fixed: volume. Buying session time by cutting sets puts 8 to 10 sets for non-focus muscles against a 50 to 70 minute session. The session length is the hard constraint. When time is genuinely fixed, non-focus volume drops to fit. Know this going in. A time-boxed client's non-focus work falls toward maintenance, whether or not she asked for that. That is the cost, and it is accepted deliberately.

This is the second lever, not the first. Move the work before you change the dose. The first step of the lagging-muscle ladder in Volume costs nothing in recovery and is very often enough on its own. Cut sets only once the time cap is real rather than assumed. And Building a session still says to check her execution before redesigning the session at all.
