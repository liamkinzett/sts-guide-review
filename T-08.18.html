A static hold does not replace dynamic reps

Holding a weight still does not replace moving it. A hold trades load and range for time, and that trade does not pay off in growth.

A hold is an isometric (isometric). This page covers the general comparison. A hold taken at a long muscle length is a different question, and Muscle and growth covers training at long lengths.

Four reasons, and none of them is "tension isn't real".

Why the hold loses

# A heavy load cannot be held for long

sub: The hold forces the load down

Why: To hold for any length of time she has to use a load well below what a dynamic rep can carry. At a genuinely heavy load, true failure arrives almost immediately. A lower load means lower peak force on the muscle for most of the hold.

# A hold sits at one joint angle

sub: One position, one leverage

Why: A movement gets harder and easier through its range because the leverage changes. A hold sits at one angle and cannot use any of that.

# There is no lowering phase

sub: The eccentric is missing

Why: A dynamic rep pairs the shortening half (concentric) with the lengthening half (eccentric). The eccentric reaches the highest mechanical forces at the lowest metabolic cost of anything in the rep. A hold has none of it.

# A hold makes her better at holding

sub: Better at holding, not bigger

Why: Lifters who held a light knee-extension to failure for five weeks got much better at holding it. Their muscle barely grew, however close to failure they held. The hold got harder to sustain. It did not build muscle.

How to use it. A plank, a wall sit or a paused hold can have a place for skill, bracing or position. Do not count one as a hard set for growth, and do not swap it in for the reps it is meant to replace.

Say it like this

Holds build the skill of holding. They don't build much muscle. Keep the reps, and use the hold for what it's good at.

Terms on this page

isometric: A hold. The muscle works against the load but the joint does not move.

concentric: The lifting half of a rep, where the muscle shortens under load.

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.
