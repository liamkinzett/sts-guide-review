Returning to training · T-37.4

Coming back from a layoff: compounds, few of them

A detrained client comes back to compound work with minimal isolation. The same prescription covers a client who only has two days a week.

A compound movement (compound) trains several joints and muscles at once: a squat, a hinge, a press, a row. An isolation movement (isolation) trains one. On the way back, the compounds are the program.

Why it works. A detrained client improves on almost anything. Strength and skill explains why: she sits at the adaptation end of the spectrum, where coordination returns fast and every session buys a lot. The scarce resource is her sessions, not the stimulus. Compounds buy the most per session. Isolation is what a fuller week can afford.

Deviate where a specific tissue is the thing being returned. That is the ladder on the injury pages, not a general program.

Watch for

Rebuilding the old program. The program she stopped was calibrated to the training age she had, not the one she has now (training age).

When she asks how much a six-week layoff cost her, give her the protocol rather than a number. Never give her a week count of what she lost, at any training age. The three-week reassurance, that the mirror is showing lost fluid and glycogen rather than lost tissue, weakens by six weeks, though the direction still holds. The warning that the same load feels heavier for a session or two applies whatever the exact week count.

What you can tell her. She will very likely come back faster than she went from zero. She is rebuilding the nervous system's groove for the movement and the signalling that drives growth, not tissue she never lost.

How fast depends on her training age and her age. A beginner who stopped regains with little friction. An advanced lifter was already near her ceiling and regains more slowly.

Scale her expectation by those two things, never by one universal rate. Muscle and growth has the detail.

Say it like this

You'll come back faster than you built it the first time. We start with the big lifts, few of them, and the rest comes back as the weeks fill out.

Terms on this page

compound: An exercise that moves several joints and loads several muscles at once, like a squat, a deadlift, a press or a row.

isolation: An exercise that works one muscle across one joint, like a leg extension or a cable curl.

training age: How far along she is as a lifter. It is how long she has trained properly, and it is lower after a layoff than before it.
