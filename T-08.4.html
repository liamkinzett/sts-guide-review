How much biceps work pulling really gives her

Vertical pulling counts as close to a full set for her biceps. A row counts as about half. The exercise library splits lower-body and shared pressing sets for you. It does not give pulling an arm share, so use these two working figures.

How much a pulling set counts for biceps

# Vertical pulling

sub: Counts close to a full set

Why: Pulldowns and pull-ups bend the elbow against real load through a long range. Pulldowns grow the elbow-bending muscles (elbow flexors) about as much as curls do.

Do this: Count a set of pulldowns or pull-ups as close to a full set for biceps.

Exercises: Lat pulldown; Pull-up; Assisted pull-up

# Rows

sub: Counts about half a set

Why: A row bends the elbow less, and the back takes more of the load. A curl grows the elbow flexors about twice as much as a row does.

Do this: Count a set of rows as about half a set for biceps.

Exercises: Dumbbell row; Seated cable row; Chest-supported row

A worked example. Her week holds three sets of pulldowns and three sets of rows. Her biceps have received about three sets from the pulldowns and about 1.5 from the rows. Call it 4.5 indirect sets before a single curl.

Treat both as working figures, not constants. Her arms are the final check. If the count says her biceps are covered and they are not growing, trust her arms.

The practical version. A program with real vertical pulling gives her biceps close to what a curl would. A program with only rows gives her something, but less than the set count suggests. Neither is a licence to stop counting.

Watch for

Reading this as permission to drop direct arm work. It is the opposite. It tells you which programs can afford minimal direct arm work and which cannot. You only find out which one you are looking at by counting.

Terms on this page

elbow flexors: The muscles that bend the elbow, mainly the biceps and the brachialis underneath it. Curls train them directly and pulling trains them indirectly.
