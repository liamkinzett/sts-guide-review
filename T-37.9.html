Returning to training · T-37.9

A post-illness performance spike is not a new baseline

A performance spike in her first sessions back after illness is fatigue clearing, not new strength. Do not build the next block on it.

The spike is real and it has a mechanism. Heavy repeated training accumulates fatigue, across the whole body and in the working muscle, and that fatigue masks her underlying strength. Fatigue also directly impairs how well she recruits muscle and how cleanly she executes. Stopping, in this case because she was ill, clears the mask. She finally expresses what was already built.

Two to three weeks is the window before genuine detraining starts. It takes roughly 2 to 3 weeks of low-volume training or rest before muscle mass and work capacity begin to drain. A deliberate taper of 2 to 4 weeks works on the same fact: drop the fatigue while keeping the structure intact.

Three weeks off a main movement does not cost an experienced lifter her technical coordination. Baseline execution returns within a single session once fatigue clears.

A two-week illness layoff sits inside that window, so what you see is fatigue dissipation, not new adaptation.

The name does not matter. Whether it is called a spike above her old baseline (supercompensation) or simply fatigue clearing before retraining does not change the instruction. The first session back is not a number to plan from. 3 weeks off followed by 6 weeks of training ends in the same place as training straight through.

Why this is not only a labelling question. Her force output returns faster than her connective tissue's tolerance. Tendons and ligaments lag behind muscle and the nervous system. Injury is force applied beyond what the tissue can tolerate. Treating the post-illness spike as a new baseline means applying force the tissue has not caught up to yet. Never test a max in the first sessions back.

What to do. Do not rebuild the program around the number she hit. Keep loading conservative through the two to three week detraining window, even though the lift felt easy. Let the load climb back on the normal progression rules rather than anchoring it to the spike. Progression has those rules.

Say it like this

That felt easy because you came in fresh, not because you got stronger while you were sick. We keep it steady for a couple of weeks and let the numbers climb properly.

Terms on this page

supercompensation: A rise in performance above her old level once the fatigue from a hard stretch of training clears.
