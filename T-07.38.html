The phases · T-07.38

A strength phase has no separate readiness gate

A strength phase has no separate readiness gate. It inherits the five priming exit criteria, read on the specific patterns the block will load.

The five exit criteria already gate every phase after priming, strength included. The second of the five, technique to standard, is the direct proxy for whether she is ready to load heavily.

Read it pattern by pattern. What a strength phase needs from that criterion is specific. Read her per-pattern classification from Assessing a new client, not the single headline level from intake. A client whose squat is solid and whose deadlift breaks down under load is not uniformly ready. Read her against the patterns the block's main lifts actually use.

Where the patterns disagree, classify down. She enters the strength block on her weakest qualifying pattern, not her strongest.

A strength-path client has usually done some of this already. On a powerlifting or powerbuilding path she works the loadable barbell progression inside priming itself rather than staying supported throughout it (externally stabilised). So her priming footage already carries evidence on the exact lifts a strength block will load.

Watch for

Waiting for a separate signal that never arrives. There is not one. The exit criteria she already cleared to leave priming, read pattern by pattern rather than as one number, are the readiness test.

Terms on this page

externally stabilised: The machine, bench or support holds her position so she does not have to. Leg press, hack squat, chest-supported row, cable work.
