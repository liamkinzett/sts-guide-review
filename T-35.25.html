Pain rules and when to refer · T-35.25

Never shade what you tell a clinician

What you tell a clinician about her training is the whole value of your role in a referral. A client never talks you into softening it. That half has no deviation.

The trade you make instead of diagnosing is accuracy. You describe what you saw and what she reported, to whoever you refer her to. It is valuable precisely because you are the only person watching her train. A description softened at her request is no longer that thing. It is a document produced to get a specific answer out of a clinician rather than to inform one.

A client asking you to under-report a load, a symptom or a frequency is asking you to turn your one real asset into a liability. The clearance is only as good as what it was based on. A clearance obtained on a fudged report is not a clearance. It is a document that says something true about a client who does not exist.

The refusal is simple. What comes after it is the harder part. That belongs to The coaching relationship. This page governs only the accuracy of what you report.

Say it like this

I can't shade what I tell your physio, because the whole reason my read is worth anything is that it's accurate. If I soften it to get you cleared faster, I've just made it worthless. Worse, I've made it wrong at the exact moment it matters most.

Watch for

Reading this as a relationship problem you have to manage alone. What happens to the coaching relationship after a refusal like this is real, and it is not this page's question.
