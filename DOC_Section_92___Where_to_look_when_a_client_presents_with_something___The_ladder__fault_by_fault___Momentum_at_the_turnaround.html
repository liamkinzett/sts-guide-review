Momentum at the turnaround

She bounces, dive-bombs or drops into the change of direction.

Control the entry and the eccentric (eccentric); on a fatigued machine squat, end the set.

Where it shows, and the fix

# Hip adduction (adduction)

sub: Audible clash as the pads meet

Do this: Control the entry

# Cable glute kickback

sub: Rushing into the shortened position

Do this: Control the entry

# Step-up and step-down

sub: Bouncing off the floor with the non-working leg

Do this: Remove the bounce

# Hamstring curl

sub: Bouncing the roller off the ankles at the transition. Banging the stack.

Do this: Control the entry. Slow the turnaround

# Machine squat

sub: Dive-bombing when fatigued

Do this: End the set

# Barbell squat

sub: Dive-bombing

Do this: Control the eccentric

# Front raise

sub: Dropping the eccentric

Do this: Control it

# Triceps pushdown

sub: Rushing the return on the cross-body variant

Do this: Control it

# Skull crusher and JM press

sub: Bouncing the transition

Do this: Control it. This is the safety-relevant one

There is no set cue written for this fault. Say the fix in plain words, one thing this set.

Other cues on those exercises. Not about this fault. Use them when the same set needs them.

Cues

When: Hip adduction

Say: Drive from the adductors.

When: Hip adduction

Say: Keep your back pressed against the pad.

Why: It enforces the setup detail that decides the set.

When: Hip adduction

Say: Don't let the pads crash.

Why: It enforces continuous tension and stops the stack being rested. Correct the concept before you correct the rep.

When: Cable glute kickback

Say: Anchor yourself in place.

Why: Everything else on this list fails where the base is not fixed first.

When: Cable glute kickback

Say: Contract only your glutes

Why: Or: "movement from your butt should move that pulley, nothing else."

When: Cable glute kickback

Say: Brace your body in stone

Why: Only the leg moves.

Terms on this page

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.

adduction: A limb travelling in toward the midline of the body: the leg pulled in by the inner-thigh muscles, or the arm brought in to the side.
