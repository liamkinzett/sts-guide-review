When to use it

The problem you notice. You are about to introduce dips and she has never pressed her own bodyweight. Or her pressing is strong and it is her trunk that gives out. Or a coach has carried the bench's locked shoulder blade onto everything she presses. The push-up is the answer to all three. It is not a chest-size pick.

Reach for it when

# You are about to introduce dips

sub: She has not done bodyweight pressing

Confirm it: Ask her to do a set of floor push-ups. If she cannot hold the trunk and press cleanly, she is not ready for the dip.

What it gets her: The competence the dip sits on top of. Master push-ups before introducing dips. The sequence runs one way only.

# Her pressing is strong and her trunk is what gives out

sub: Hips sagging or piking before the arms fail

Confirm it: Film from the side. The line from shoulder to ankle breaks before the elbows stop moving.

What it gets her: The pushing muscles integrated with the trunk. As pressing strength grows, the push-up becomes a torso endurance builder as much as an upper-body one.

# A coach has carried the bench's locked shoulder blade onto everything she presses

sub: She pins her blades on every press

Confirm it: Watch the top of her push-up. The blades stay pinned back and the chest never leaves the floor fully.

What it gets her: Normal shoulder-blade pressing mechanics, seen and felt. The push-up is the reference case for what the blade does when nothing is pinning it. Spreading and rising at the top (protraction and elevation) are coached, not merely tolerated.

The contraindication a coach is most likely to miss

It is not about her shoulder. It is about gravity. A client weaker than the forces of gravity acting on her, put under unassisted bodyweight pressing, recruits compensating muscle rather than the target pattern. Her nervous system manages not falling over first. The fix is an externally braced machine press loaded below her bodyweight. Not a wall push-up. Not an incline push-up. Variations explains why.

Setup. Whole hand on the floor, whole-body tension, ribs stacked, blades free. Setup has the list.

Terms on this page

protraction and elevation: The shoulder blades spreading apart around the rib cage and rising. On a push-up this is the finish of the rep. On a bench it is a fault.
