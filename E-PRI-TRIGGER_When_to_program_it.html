When to use it

The problem you notice. She is a lifter with big quads reporting pain on the outside of the knee. Or glute tension is what is stopping her moving well, and you want the range before the session. Or you have found two or three genuinely tender points on the outside of her thigh. Soft tissue work buys a short-term change in what she feels, and nothing else.

Every row below buys a short-term sensory change. This is not a tissue intervention, and that governs the indications as much as the technique.

Reach for it when

# She is a lifter with big quads reporting pain on the OUTSIDE of the knee

sub: The lifter's version of an irritated IT band (IT band)

Confirm it: Big quads, heavy knee flexion in her training, and the pain sits on the outer knee.

What it gets her: The right target, which is not the band. Look at the outer quad underneath the band (vastus lateralis). When the hip gets tight, the band is pulled tight onto the muscle beneath it. The band itself is not muscle, so deep pressure cannot act on it. Pin the muscle underneath.

# Glute tension is what is stopping her moving well

sub: You want the range before the session rather than after it

Confirm it: Her hinge or squat is short of range, and the glutes are what she reports as tight.

What it gets her: Access restored so the next two steps can happen. Pigeon stretch and foam rolling are things done to alleviate tension in the glutes so she can move better. That is step one.

# You have found two or three genuinely tender points on the hip-to-knee line

sub: Not the whole thigh

Confirm it: Press along the outer thigh from hip to knee. Two or three points make her flinch. The rest does not.

What it gets her: A joint moved through range under pressure at each one, then done. The prescription is pin and stretch (pin and stretch), never rolling like a pizza. Heel to the backside, then lock the knee out with emphasis on the inner-knee quad (VMO). The dose is the number of points, not a reading on a clock.

Same band, different complaint, different client

A distance runner with an irritated IT band presents as one-sided LOW BACK pain, not knee pain. She never goes through the shearing at the knee that a lifter does. The first row is scoped to the lifter.

Never reach for it as the intervention

If she only stretches and foam rolls, she will never access permanent improvements. That takes strengthening, and training better patterns in those positions. The failure has a client's voice: "My coach told me I need to work my core, so I'm going to start stretching and foam rolling". Overselling the mechanism is what produces that client.

It is step one of three, and it is the step people already over-invest in. Watch the warm-up that grows session on session, collecting a lacrosse ball and a dowel and a band. It is an indicator that something is missing in the program, not a reason to add another tool.

Roll a site because it is stopping her moving today, never because of the muscle's name. The detailed map here is the outer thigh. The same standard applies at every other site.

Setup. Side-lying for the outer thigh, two or three tender points from hip to knee. Setup has the full list.

Terms on this page

IT band: The long strip of connective tissue down the outside of the thigh, from the hip to below the knee. It is not a muscle, so it cannot be stretched or released.

vastus lateralis: The outer quad, the big muscle on the outside of the thigh. It sits directly under the IT band.

pin and stretch: Hold pressure still on one tender point while she moves the joint next to it through its range.

VMO: The teardrop quad muscle on the inside of the knee. It works hardest in the last few degrees of straightening the knee.
