What we hold about loading a spine

Three things govern how a coach thinks about a spine under load.

A little rounding under load is more tolerable than gym folklore says. The rules on spinal flexion under load carry the full picture. Do not teach her that any rounding is damage.

You can only screen what you can see. You cannot see spinal flexion begin (spinal flexion). You can see a back round. So the fault you record is a back that visibly rounds, not a spine that flexed a degree.

The hard limit. No deviation

In known vertebral osteoporosis, loaded forward flexion and rapid explosive spinal twisting are contraindicated. No judgement call, no exception. A coach who suspects thinning bone in the spine (osteoporosis) refers rather than diagnoses, and does not load those two patterns while she waits.

Terms on this page

spinal flexion: The spine bending forwards, the back rounding. A little of it under load is normal and hard to see. A visible round is the fault you record.

osteoporosis: Bone has lost density and can fracture under loads a healthy bone would tolerate. In the spine it makes loaded forward bending and fast twisting off limits.
