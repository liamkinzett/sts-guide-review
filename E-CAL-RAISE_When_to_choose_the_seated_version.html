When to choose the seated version

The seated raise is chosen for a job, and growing the calf is never that job. Two jobs are named for it as training, below. Its third use is preparation: early in the session, so the ankle bends before the squat asks it to. When to use it covers that one.

The two jobs

# Load it heavy, for ankle stiffness

sub: A stiff ankle is what lets her put force into the ground

Why: With the knee bent, the force at the ankle matches the early stages of acceleration. There the soleus is the muscle that lets the ankle stay stiff. A weak soleus shows as looseness through the ankle and an inability to express force into the ground.

Do this: Maximum loadability on this machine, shoes on.

# Tibial rotation

sub: A joint-preparation job, not calf training

Why: The seated calf raise is the best movement STS has for isolating the shin turning on the foot (tibial rotation). Done unloaded and one leg at a time, it is reported as the single biggest win against a year of chronic knee pain.

Do this: Descending into the stretch (dorsiflexion), keep the pressure at the base of the big toe and turn the shin inward. Rising onto the toes (plantarflexion), let the pressure roll toward the little toe as the shin turns outward.

Two rules that look like they clash, and do not. Defaulting to seated raises for the soleus is a coach-side fault. Putting the seated raise at the front of a lower-body day for tibial rotation is correct. They answer different questions. The first is about growing a calf muscle. The second is about preparing a joint.

Bending the knee takes the gastrocnemius out. It does not add soleus. So a seated raise is not a soleus specialisation. It is a de-loaded gastrocnemius. Choose it for one of the two jobs above, never for the muscle story.

Which machine for the gastrocnemius. The machine standing calf raise is the STS gastrocnemius raise. You will hear a leg-press calf raise preferred over it. The case is the support it gives, plus the length hip flexion adds through the back of the body. You will also hear a hip-loaded calf raise preferred, with any leg press as its substitute. STS programs neither.

Terms on this page

tibial rotation: The shin bone turning inward or outward. It happens on the foot with every bend and straightening of the ankle.

dorsiflexion: The ankle bending so the shin and the top of the foot move closer together, as when the toes pull up toward the shin or the shin travels forward over the foot. A squat needs it at the bottom.

plantarflexion: Pointing the foot down, as when she rises onto her toes.
