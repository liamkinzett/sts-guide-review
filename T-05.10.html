Osteoporosis, kyphosis and a coach's scope

Treat a diagnosed bone condition as diagnosed and refer. A coach who suspects one refers rather than diagnoses.

Perimenopausal and postmenopausal clients carry a higher osteoporosis risk. That is why bone sits here, straight after menopause.

The locked rule that touches bone. It applies in known thinning of the bones of the spine (vertebral osteoporosis). It also applies in a fixed forward rounding of the upper spine (kyphosis). Bending the spine forward under load (loaded forward flexion) is out. So is rapid explosive spinal twisting. There is no deviation. The risk is a fracture in a compromised bone, and the rule stands on that alone. Mobility and stretching holds it.

What that excludes in practice. Rows performed with a rounded spine. Sit-ups. Several common yoga and Pilates positions. A client asking for a stretching or yoga routine is asking about exactly these movements.

What you have. That rule and the referral line. There is no bone-specific programming beyond them, and nothing loosens the rule. Where a client brings a bone-health claim or a scan result, the two pages that follow cover what you do.

Verdict

A suspected bone condition is referred, never diagnosed by you.

Train her under the locked rule while her clinician answers.

Terms on this page

vertebral osteoporosis: The bones of the spine have lost density and can fracture under loads a healthy spine would tolerate.

kyphosis: An exaggerated forward rounding of the upper back. It is the hunched upper back seen in some older clients.

loaded forward flexion: Bending the spine forward while it carries weight, as in a rounded-back lift or a loaded sit-up.
