Faults and what you can actually see · T-32.11

A cramp mid-set ends the set, and the setup is checked before hydration

She does not finish the set. Then check her setup on the machine before you check her hydration.

A cramping muscle is not producing the tension the set was written for. Pushing through it buys nothing.

Then find out how much of the session survives. Move to another movement and see whether it is fine. Cramp on one exercise and nothing else is different information from cramp on everything. Usually the rest of the session runs.

The order of causes

Her setup on the machine. The one cramp with a named cause is a setup fault, not a fuelling one.

Hydration and electrolytes. The default assumption. It goes second.

The one cramp with a named cause. The "bro bench" leg cramp: heels set outside 90 degrees at the knee, then leg drive attempted from there. It presents as a cramp in the front of the hip or the quad mid-set that locks the leg straight (TFL). It is visible and predictable on video before it happens. The fix is the foot position: at least 90 degrees at the knee, heels directly under it.

Why that reorders the checks. A cramp with a mechanical cause has a mechanical fix. A coach who reaches for electrolytes first will keep watching it happen. A setup you can see outranks a cause you cannot.

Other movements. The named mechanism is a bench-press setup. Carry the order of the checks to any other cramp; do not assume the bench mechanism transfers. A hamstring cramp on a leg curl is something she tells you, not something you see. Ask.

Watch for

Talk of a "crampy pump" as a reason to choose an exercise. That is about exercise selection, and STS is against it. It is not guidance on a cramping client.

Say when she cramps

Stop there. We're not finishing that set. Let's see if the next exercise is fine, and I'll look at your setup on that one.

Terms on this page

TFL: The tensor fasciae latae, a small muscle at the front and side of the hip that runs into the band down the outer thigh. It can cramp and lock the leg straight when leg drive is attempted from a poor foot position.
