Warming up · T-16.13

What is not prep work

Four things a coach might program in good faith as preparation do not prepare her for a session. Every one of them is common.

Not prep work

# Localised joint isolation

sub: Clamshells, banded terminal knee extensions

Why: Isolating a small muscle against trivial resistance does not prepare the pattern the session is about to load.

Where it does belong: Rehabilitation. Pain rules and when to refer may prescribe them for a specific presentation.

# Foam rolling as tissue work

sub: It does not do what she has been told it does

Why: It does not break up scar tissue or adhesions. It stimulates the pressure receptors in the tissue and turns down her sense of pain and tightness.

Where it does belong: Her own time, if she likes it. It does no harm and it is not preparing her to lift.

# Eccentrics beyond five seconds. Five second squeezes at the top of the rep

sub: Fatigue without preparation

Why: Both cost her effort she needs for the working sets and prepare nothing. Tempo and pauses already caps the lowering half of the rep (eccentric) at four seconds.

# Ten minutes on a treadmill

sub: The general warm-up, already out

Why: It raises her temperature and heart rate and does not prepare the movement. Test day is the only exception.

This page says these are not prep work. It does not say they are never used. The distinction is deliberate. Clamshells and banded terminal knee extensions are rehabilitation staples. What they are not is preparation for a session.

Foam rolling is worth being accurate about. It genuinely reduces the sensation of tightness, which is a real effect and often why she likes it. It is not doing what she has been told it does, and it is not preparing her to lift. A client who finds it useful is not doing herself harm and does not need to be talked out of it.

Terms on this page

prep work: The movements inside the warm-up slot that get the first loaded movement ready. Chosen for the session in front of you.

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.
