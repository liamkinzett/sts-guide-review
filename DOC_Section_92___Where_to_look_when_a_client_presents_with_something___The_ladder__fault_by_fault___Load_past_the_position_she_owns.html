Load past the position she owns

The weight is too heavy for the position, so the range shortens or momentum appears.

Reduce the load.

Where it shows, and the fix

# Hip adduction (adduction)

sub: Reduced range, visible momentum, pads snapping rather than squeezing

Do this: Reduce the load. The test is whether she can bring the pads together and control the return. If either half fails it is too heavy.

# Shortened-position curl

sub: Loaded as though it were a mid-range curl. Elbow position breaks. The body swings to start the rep

Do this: The position sets the ceiling rather than her effort. Reduce the load

# Pec fly

sub: Too heavy for the position

Do this: Reduce load

# Trunk flexion

sub: Too heavy. The range shortens

Do this: Reduce load

# Cable glute kickback

sub: Too much weight for the position

Do this: Reduce load

# Hip thrust

sub: Range shortens, the finish is never reached

Do this: Reduce load

# Leg press and hip press

sub: Heavy loading with the heels off the plate

Do this: Reduce load

# Lateral raise

sub: Too heavy at the expense of tension

Do this: Reduce load

# Overhead cable extension

sub: Too heavy to stabilise overhead

Do this: Reduce load

There is no set cue written for this fault. Say the fix in plain words, one thing this set.

Other cues on those exercises. Not about this fault. Use them when the same set needs them.

Cues

When: Hip adduction

Say: Drive from the adductors.

When: Hip adduction

Say: Keep your back pressed against the pad.

Why: It enforces the setup detail that decides the set.

When: Hip adduction

Say: Don't let the pads crash.

Why: It enforces continuous tension and stops the stack being rested. Correct the concept before you correct the rep.

When: Pec fly

Say: Think wide first.

Why: The first cue. Initiating by pushing outward is what maximises lengthened-range tension and stops the movement collapsing into a press.

When: Pec fly

Say: Give a big hug.

Why: For the concentric. Use it on the client who drives with the hands instead of sweeping with the chest.

When: Pec fly

Say: Stop before feeling your biceps or delts take the stretch.

Why: The active-range limit, made self-verifiable. Use it on anyone chasing depth at the back end.

Terms on this page

adduction: A limb travelling in toward the midline of the body: the leg pulled in by the inner-thigh muscles, or the arm brought in to the side.

:

concentric: The lifting half of a rep, where the muscle shortens under load.
