Reading her training data · T-39.5

No data, no new program. But the first block still gets written

Every rule in this topic reads a logbook. None of them work on a logbook you cannot trust. Sessions with no loads against them, missing reps, numbers rounded up to what she meant to do.

Reading a stall per exercise, judging progression by the rep band, looking after two flat exposures. All of it needs numbers that mean something.

This is not the same problem as a client who is not training. Adherence as a signal owns that. She may be training well and logging badly. The check-in form and her own messages will usually tell you which.

First time, you write the block anyway.

The first patchy check-in

Raise it. Say plainly that logging has a role in tracking her training and in deciding what the next phase looks like.

Take her word. Assume training is going well on what you do have: her check-in form, her messages. Write the block you would have written.

Same loads, same progression. The block she would have got with clean data. Not a hedged one.

Preface it. Tell her you have had to make assumptions and may need to adjust.

The preface on the block

We may need to make further adjustments as I've had to make assumptions.

Why the same block and not a conservative one. Hedging the loads treats an administrative problem as a physiological one. Nothing about her recovery, her capacity or her training age changed because a spreadsheet is incomplete. The lack of data is a service problem. The answer to it is the conversation and the preface, not a smaller program.

Second time is different. She has been told. The block went out carrying the assumption line. The next check-in is patchy again. You stop progressing the program. No new block until there is data to write it from.

Why it is firm at that point. Continuing means a second block of assumptions stacked on the first. By then you are not coaching her. You are guessing at a client who has been asked once already. We end up making assumptions, which does not provide the service to the standard we want.

The stopping point is one raise, then the stop. There is no count of patchy check-ins to reach before you stop. Once is the warning. Twice is the stop.

Watch for

Quietly absorbing it. A coach who keeps writing blocks on no data has made the problem invisible. The client never learns it matters. Neither does whoever inherits her and finds a training history that cannot be read.
