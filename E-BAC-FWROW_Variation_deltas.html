Variations

STS programs five versions: the single arm dumbbell row, the dumbbell bent over row, the palms-down and palms-up barbell bent over rows, and the trap bar bent over row. What it's for declares each one. This page covers the one variation coaches ask about that is not on that list: the Pendlay-style row.

A Pendlay-style row is a bent-over barbell row with the torso parallel to the floor and a dead-stop reset on the floor every rep. It sits at one end of a torso-angle dial, and the dial is what matters.

Compared

columns: Torso parallel to the floor | Torso pitched up to 35 to 45 degrees

What it recruits: The horizontal fibres of the upper back and the lat fibres from the middle of the back (thoracic). The pull lines up with them. | The lowest lat fibres, from the lower back (lumbar), which work to stop the trunk twisting.

The cost: The hip extensors and spinal muscles hold the hinge still for the whole set and become the limiter before the lats reach real force. | Less hinge demand, so more load reaches the back.

Whether the unbraced row belongs in a size block is decided by the goal, not by a verdict. Two positions exist and each is right for its own job. The unbraced parallel row is the row for building spinal resiliency. For hypertrophy its resistance profile is poor, because the hinge ends the set before the lats do. The ceiling shows it: the same lifter who rows 60 to 80 lb unbraced can move six plates on a chest-supported row.

Do this

# The job is hypertrophy and load is the limiter

sub: Her hinge ends the set before her back does

Do this: The chest-supported alternative. It removes the spinal-muscle bottleneck.

Exercises: Neutral grip chest supported row; Pronated grip chest supported row

# The job is spinal position and hinge capacity

sub: Building position for a squat, not isolating tissue for size

Do this: The unbraced barbell row, palms down (pronated grip) or palms up (supinated grip). Its case for spinal resiliency stands.

Exercises: Pronated grip barbell bent over row; Supinated grip barbell bent over row

Let her goal decide it. Building position for a squat is a different job from isolating tissue for size. The same row cannot be selected for both without saying which.

Terms on this page

thoracic: The upper and middle back, the part of the spine the ribs attach to.

lumbar: The lower back: the part of the spine between the ribs and the pelvis.

pronated grip: Palms facing down or away from her, knuckles up.

supinated grip: Palms facing up or toward her.
