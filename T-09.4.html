Effort and intensity · T-09.4

Close to failure beats failure

The growth from a set peaks just short of failure and falls a little when the set is taken all the way.

This contradicts what most coaches arrive believing, and it is the single most useful thing in this topic.

Reps in reserve and what the set earns

4-5 | Too far from failure. Not a hard set, so it does not count toward her volume

2-3 | A real hard set. Where a block starts, and where a beginner is held

1 | The peak. The floor for every compound

0 | True failure. A little less growth, a lot more fatigue. Stabilised isolation work only

The shape of the curve. Stimulus rises as the set gets closer to failure. A set stopped far from failure earns less than half of what a set stopped just short of it earns. Then, at the very end, the line turns down. A set taken to absolute failure produces a little less growth than one stopped just short.

Why. Grinding to failure creates a large amount of nervous-system and metabolic fatigue. That fatigue costs her the volume and the recovery that would have produced the growth. Stopping one rep short, once total work is matched, grows the same muscle. Lifters who stop short of failure also keep more of their speed and power than those who grind every set.

The cost compounds within the session. In a five rep max bench press the first rep takes about 1.2 seconds. The fifth takes about 3.3 seconds. Take an early set to failure and every set after it is performed by a weaker lifter.

How we use it. Aim for the last rep she could complete without the pattern changing. That is where the curve peaks. Going past it is not extra credit. It is paying more for less. That makes the 1 RIR floor on compounds the best place to be, not the safe option.

Watch for

Reading this as permission to train easy. The far end of the curve is far worse than the peak. Training well short of failure is the more common error on a real roster. This rule narrows the target from one side. It does not move it down.

Terms on this page

RIR: Reps in reserve. How many more reps she could have done when the set stopped. Zero RIR is failure.
