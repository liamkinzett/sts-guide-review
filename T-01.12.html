Muscle and growth · T-01.12

When does muscle actually get built?

Training is the signal. The building happens in the hours and days after the session, not during it.

Say that to a client who thinks the work is where the muscle is made. The session is the stimulus. The building is what recovery does with it.

A workout gives the body a reason to adapt. The physical work of building and remodelling the muscle carries on after the workout is over. When a muscle is trained, the rate at which it builds new muscle protein rises. That rise is called muscle protein synthesis (muscle protein synthesis).

How a session becomes muscle

Session | The muscle is loaded. That is the signal

Building rises | For a day or more, the fibre makes new protein faster (muscle protein synthesis)

Back to normal | About 28 to 29 hours after an ordinary session. Up to 72 hours after an exhaustive one

Next session | The next signal. Growth is the sum of many of these

How long after depends on the session. After an ordinary session, protein building settles back to normal at about 28 to 29 hours. After a very hard, exhaustive session it can take up to 72 hours. What moves that window is how much total work was done, how close to failure it went, and how well she is eating. A harder session raises the window. Quote the range, never a single number.

Breakdown rises too, and that is not damage to prevent.

After a session the body also clears out damaged or old protein, a process called proteolysis (proteolysis). Clearing old contractile protein is part of building the new. What matters is the net balance between building and clearing across the whole window, not whether clearing happens at all. A session does not make a visibly bigger muscle by itself. Growth is the accumulated result of many of these cycles.

One figure for the food conversation. The muscle stays extra sensitive to protein in food for up to 24 hours after a session. That conversation is not yours to run. What follows from the figure belongs to the Nutrition Manual. The training lesson here is simply that the recovery period is biologically active. The session ends before the adaptation does.

What this means for coaching. Do not let her think that the moment she leaves the gym, the job is finished. Training creates the signal, and the body then spends hours and days responding to it. Soreness is not evidence that something has been broken in a way that must be prevented. Repair, breakdown and rebuilding are normal parts of adaptation.

Say it when she thinks soreness means damage

The session is the signal. Your body does the building over the next day or two, and part of that is clearing out old protein to put new in. Soreness isn't a sign you broke something.

Terms on this page

muscle protein synthesis: The process that builds new muscle protein after training. It rises for a day or more, then returns to normal.

proteolysis: The breakdown and clearing of damaged muscle protein after a session. It is part of rebuilding, not damage to be prevented.
