Common faults

Four common faults

# Elbow off its line

sub: Flared elbows. Elbow drift out of the vertical stack

Why it happens: A wide grip lets the elbows wander, and the cue alone does not hold under fatigue.

Do this: Narrow the grip so it forces the tuck mechanically. Then cue "Tuck the elbows, do not flare them."

# Rib flare

sub: Leaning back so the shoulder shoots up. Or leaning forward

Why it happens: The rib cage was never stacked before the rep, or she is buying range from her lower back.

Do this: Ribs to hips.

# Tension dumped at an end range

sub: Locking out at the top

Why it happens: Lockout feels like the finish, and it is a rest.

Do this: Stop short.

# Head as a release valve

sub: Head tipping forward. Head lifting off the bench

Why it happens: Range is being stolen from a joint that is not the working one.

Do this: Head neutral.

# A shrug on one side only, every rep, with no pain

sub: Compensation for a shoulder blade that is not rotating, not a technique fault to fix with a cue alone

Check first: Test pain first. If she reports pain, Pain rules and when to refer takes over and this row does not apply.

Why it happens: Healthy overhead reach to 180 degrees runs on a 2 to 1 rhythm: 120 degrees from the upper arm and 60 from the shoulder blade. When the muscles that turn the blade upward (upward rotators), the serratus and lower traps, are weak or not firing, the blade cannot rotate smoothly. The upper trap on that side is over-recruited to force the arm up.

A second cause: Pressing with the arm flared compresses the space under the bony roof of the shoulder, and the body shrugs to buy clearance. Tucking the elbows into the plane of the shoulder blade recovers 30 to 40 degrees of comfortable range and removes the urge to shrug. Both can be present in the same client.

Do this: Check the pressing plane first. Then fix everything upstream, in order: core and pelvis, rib cage on pelvis, shoulder blade on rib cage. A shrug that survives all of that is the one you treat.

Then: Reactivate the upward rotator with a serratus press. Face down on the forearms, she pushes the chest off the floor by sliding the blades forward (Priming lifts). Press with dumbbells or cables in the plane of the shoulder blade instead of a fixed bar. A fixed bar locks both hands and can mask one-sided instability. Add a staggered-stance single-arm row for the trunk stability the shoulder is borrowing from.

A lower back that arches to finish the rep is not on this list on purpose. Coaches call it a lumbar finish (lumbar finish). It is usually a shoulder fault, not a bracing fault. A lumbar finish here is usually a shoulder fault explains.

Terms on this page

upward rotators: The muscles that turn the shoulder blade upward so the arm can go overhead, mainly the serratus and the lower traps.

lumbar finish: Finishing a hip movement by arching the lower back instead of extending the hip. On an overhead press the same arch supplies shoulder range instead.
