Cues

Cues go in the order printed. Try the first, and only move on when it fails.

Cues

When: She lives in her heels

Say: Forward shin angle.

Why: The master cue. It is an external cue and a deliberate over-correction, not a biomechanical target. Aiming for a forward shin lands her on the midfoot.

When: The hips have nowhere left to go

Say: Fall forward before you push back.

Why: External cue. If her weight is already back before the rep starts, there is no travel left. Loading the front of the foot first gives the hips room.

When: B-stance, and the back leg is joining in

Say: Keep the main thing the main thing. It's a front leg exercise.

Why: External cue. It stops the back leg quietly sharing the load.

When: The lowering phase (eccentric) is rushed

Say: Control the eccentric and move origin to insertion.

Why: An internal cue for a lifter who already owns the pattern. It puts her attention on the muscle lengthening under control.

Do not cue 'push through the heels'

Say "full foot pressure" instead. Heels sends the weight back, which is the most common RDL fault. It is one of the cues STS has retired, and Cueing explains why.

Terms on this page

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.
