Diagnosing from video · T-33.1

A discomfort report gets a video before it gets a programming change

She says something does not feel good. Before you change the exercise, drop the load or reach for an explanation, you ask for footage.

Why it works. Ninety-nine times out of a hundred, when the video arrives, there is something in it that explains why she is not feeling good. The alternative is diagnosing from a sentence. Her description of what hurts is a report about sensation. The cause is usually mechanical and visible. Changing the program without looking treats a symptom you have not identified.

It does not have to work first time. Often it does not. The video gives you something to be wrong about specifically, which is worth more than being vaguely right.

Discomfort, not pain. This rule is for the report that something feels off, wrong or heavy in the wrong place. The moment she describes pain, Pain rules and when to refer comes first, and the video is for afterwards.

Watch for

The report you think you already understand. Those are the ones you answer without looking.

Say when she reports discomfort

Send me a video of that next time you do it. Doesn't need to be anything fancy.
