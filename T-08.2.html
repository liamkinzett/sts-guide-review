Look every volume figure up. Never quote from memory

Every volume figure is looked up, every time. None is quoted from memory. This topic teaches you to count and to read the total. The bands you compare the total against are decided elsewhere and gathered in the table on the previous page.

Her band by training age comes from Assessing a new client, because it follows from how you classify her. Her band by phase comes from The phases. The arm figure and the per-session ceiling come from this topic.

Why we do it this way. One number, one home. The moment two pages state the same figure, they start to drift apart. A coach quotes the stale one to a client and nobody notices for months. Keeping each figure in one place means a change lands everywhere at once.

Why never from memory. Figures move. The deficit figure, the arm figure and the deload instruction have all been refined over time. What you remember is what was true when you learned it. What the table holds is what is true now.

The deload is the one that catches people. A deload has no volume figure to look up. She is cut to one or two working sets per movement. Her load and her reps in reserve (RIR) do not move. It is a cut in sets and nothing else.

Watch for

Remembering a deload as "go easy for a week". Coaches who do that drop the load and the effort too. That is not what we do. The sets come down. Everything else holds.

Writing a figure into a program note from memory. Open the table and check it, even when you are sure. Especially when you are sure.

Terms on this page

RIR: Reps in reserve. How many more reps she could have done when the set stopped. Zero RIR is failure.
