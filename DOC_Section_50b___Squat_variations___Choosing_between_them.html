Choosing between them

Start from her problem, not from the list of squats. The flow settles the main choice. The list below it holds every problem this topic answers.

Which squat, from where she is

? Is she a complete beginner, or unable to hold position under load?

yes: Start at the first rung: counterbalance, then goblet. Build at one level before advancing.

? Is she a competitive powerlifter?

yes: The competition low bar squat once a week, two to three sets, 20X1, plus a machine squat on the second lower day.

? Does her lower back limit the squat or tire before her legs, or does she fold forward?

yes: Front squat.

? Does she already own the goblet and front squat positions?

no: Goblet, then high bar, then low bar, inside priming.

= Back squat, high bar or low bar. The most loadable squat she owns, for every strength-block squat session.

Her problem, and the squat that answers it

# Complete beginner, or cannot yet hold position under load

Do this: Start at the ladder's first rung: counterbalance, then goblet. Build at one level before advancing.

# Beginner who holds position

Do this: Goblet, then high bar, then low bar, inside priming.

# Intermediate or advanced

Do this: Straight to high bar or low bar.

# Goal is the most load and strength, every strength-block squat session

Do this: Back squat.

# Competitive powerlifter

Do this: The competition low bar squat once a week, two to three sets, 20X1, plus a machine squat on the second lower day.

# Lower back limits the squat, or tires before the legs

Do this: Front squat. If the back still hurts and the day needs a squat, counterbalance or goblet, heels elevated if needed.

# Quads are the priority or lag

Do this: Front squat.

# Folds forward or sits back in a back squat

Do this: Front squat. The load will not let her.

# Chest drops out of the bottom of the squat (the hole), cannot hold tension through the hips

Do this: Front squat, high bar or leg press. Tempo squat for the hip tension.

# Hips shoot up to start the rep

Do this: Raise the bar. It is too low. Check she is not over-elevated at the heel.

# Butt wink

Do this: Test it four ways first. Heel elevation plus a load in front plus a slow heavy tempo, before any cue. Let a high bar squat be hingier if she needs the hinge.

# Cannot reach depth

Do this: A box at the depth she has, lowered before load is added. Depth built on externally stabilised patterns, filmed.

# Bounces out of the bottom, or her joints move past normal range (hypermobile) and she dumps into them

Do this: Box squat hover, then pause squat.

# Cranky knee

Do this: Goblet box squat. Chronic knee complaint on the hack: pendulum with pauses and a controlled tempo. Acute flare: neither.

# Low back hurts on high bar, and the cause is shear

Do this: Not low bar. The lever follows the mechanism.

# Lower back, the muscles beside the spine (erectors), gives out before the quads

Do this: Hack, pendulum or Smith. No quad stimulus lost.

# Not skilled at the back squat, block needs a loaded squat

Do this: Pendulum or hack, and teach the barbell anyway.

# Upper back is the weak point

Do this: Safety bar squat. Otherwise leave it on the rack.

# Shoulder will not tolerate low bar

Do this: Hands closer first, then high bar. Not the safety bar as a default.

# Elbow pain in the low bar or front rack

Do this: Unloaded position test. Cannot hold it empty: do not load it. Pull the accessories before the squat.

# Elbow pain on bench the day after low bar

Do this: Move the two sessions apart, then external-rotation work.

# Weeks 1 to 4 of a block peak

Do this: Pause squat, higher reps.

# Conjugate max-effort rotation

Do this: Box squat weeks 1 to 2, chain squat weeks 5 to 6, tested squat from week 9.

# The week she feels good in a rehabilitation

Do this: Five-second lowering (eccentric), two-count pause front squat to a box.

# Late in a prep

Do this: Hack squat. Same stimulus, less fatigue.

Terms on this page

the hole: The bottom of the squat, where she turns around and drives up.

hypermobile: Her joints move past the normal range. She drops into deep positions easily and has to work harder to control them.

erectors: The long muscles either side of the spine that hold the back straight and extend it. They hold her back from rounding under a load.

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.

priming: The opening phase of a client's program, where she learns the lifts and earns her positions before the load is pushed.

butt wink: At the bottom of the squat her pelvis tucks under and her lower back rounds. It usually shows up when she runs out of hip range or loses her brace.

shear: A force that slides one part of a joint sideways across the other. In the lower back it is one vertebra sliding forward on the one below.
