What she means by lengthened partials, and the answer

"Lengthened partials" covers three different things (lengthened partials). Each gets a different answer.

Three things she might mean

# Train the muscle at long lengths

sub: Yes, every week

The answer: Yes. It is programmed every week by exercise selection, and a building phase biases toward it. Choosing exercises and The phases cover it.

# Partial reps interleaved through the set, 1.5 reps and quarter reps

sub: No

The answer: No. More reps with heavier weight does the same job better. The half rep becomes a bounce.

# A block of bottom-range reps after full-range failure

sub: Yes, gated

The answer: Yes, on an exercise that actually reaches the lengthened position. It is an intensifier, so it goes in only when you can say what it buys, and only on the final working set. It waits until she can take a straight set to the prescribed effort.

The permitted form's conditions are not optional. It goes on a lengthened-biased exercise rather than any exercise. An intensifier must match the logistics of the movement. A partial on a movement whose resistance already peaks where she is stopping buys sensation rather than stimulus. Partials on a dumbbell lateral raise are the classic example. Faults and what you can actually see owns reading a bounce off video.

A partial-rep prescription still needs a stated reason that is not "more stretch". The stretch is already bought by exercise selection and the phase bias, at no cost in control.

Give the post-failure block no dose of its own. No set count, rep count or load. Do not invent one. The final-set limit is its only dose.

Terms on this page

lengthened partials: Reps performed only in the stretched part of the range. The one form we allow is a block of them after full-range failure, on an exercise that reaches the lengthened position.
