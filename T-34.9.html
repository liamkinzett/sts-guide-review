Thirteen common cues that can produce the fault they aim to prevent

Thirteen cues in everyday use can create the very fault they are meant to fix. Using one is not an error. Using one without knowing how it fails is.

Eleven of these are in normal use by good coaches, including ours. This is not a ban list. A coach who knows how a cue can fail uses it as a probe and watches. A coach who believes it is doctrine uses it and stops looking.

The thirteen, and how each one fails

# "Knees out" on a squat

sub: The most argued-over cue in lifting

How it fails: Pushed hard enough, she rolls onto the outside edge of her foot. It asks her to turn the thigh out (external rotation), and turning the thigh in at the hip (internal rotation) is usually the actual limiter. And it can fight her build. If her knees cannot go any further out and you keep yelling "knees out", she asks why she cannot do it.

Read her knees in context: Knees caving above 90 degrees, before depth, is a genuine fault. Knees travelling in below parallel is loading the hip stabilisers. Differences here run person to person, not cleanly by sex. Never cue "knees out" on a hinge.

# "Pull the bar apart" on a bench

sub: The fakeable cue

How it fails: It can be performed with no upper-back involvement at all.

# "Retract" on pressing

sub: End-range retraction becomes elevation

How it fails: Pinning the blades back rides them up. Cue more depression than retraction.

# "Chest up" on rowing and pressing

sub: Half a roster has the opposite fault

How it fails: Check whether her ribs are flared or her sternum is stuck down before you say either "chest up" or "chest down".

# "Push through the heels"

sub: Heels-back is a weak position

How it fails: Her weight goes into her heels and her toes lift.

# "Retract and depress down, protract and elevate at the top" on a dip

sub: The second half is itself a named fault

How it fails: Protracting and elevating at the top of a dip is the shoulder dumping forward.

# "Avoid elevating your shoulders toward your ears" on a lateral raise

sub: Collides with the shrug

How it fails: Some elevation is part of the raise. Cued too hard, it stops the movement short.

# Protraction on the rear delt

sub: Cue it lightly, or only allow it

How it fails: Over-executed, the shoulder dumps forward.

# Deliberately lifting the shoulder blades (scapular elevation) on the shrug

sub: The sharpest one here

How it fails: The shrug's only common cue argues against the movement's own action. On the shrug, elevation is the work.

# "Turn your pelvis towards the front leg" on a split squat

sub: Right mechanics, unusable words

How it fails: Most clients cannot act on it mid-set. Find her words for it.

# Tipping the pelvis forward on purpose (anterior pelvic tilt) on a hinge

sub: Not more hamstrings, not more glutes

How it fails: It is really not more hamstrings. It is not more glutes. It puts her in a weaker position.

# "Stay low" and "run under a fence"

sub: Directionally right

How it fails: Over-execution. It is an over-cue pointed the wrong way.

# Collecting cues as a skill

sub: "I have the best cues"

How it fails: Having the best cues is one of the lowest-coaching-IQ things you can do. The skill is choosing and testing, not collecting.

Watch for

Treating this list as a ban list. It is not. Use these cues as probes, and watch what she does with them.

Terms on this page

external rotation: Turning a limb outward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face forward or up, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more to the side.

internal rotation: Turning a limb inward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face back or down, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more toward the middle. A deep squat needs some of it.

scapular elevation: The shoulder blades lifting towards the ears. On most upper-body lifts it is a fault; on the shrug it is the movement.

anterior pelvic tilt: The pelvis tipped forward, so the lower back arches more. In the gym it looks like a big arch with the ribs up.
