When to use it

The problem you notice. Her program presses well and pulls badly, or it pulls only from a seat. Nothing in it makes her shoulder work overhead with her whole body hanging. The pull-up is the movement that builds shoulder function rather than expressing it. A bench press is how she expresses shoulder function, not how she builds it.

Reach for it when

# You want shoulder function built rather than expressed

sub: Her week has plenty of pressing and no loaded overhead hang

Confirm it: Look at her program. If every pull is seated or chest-supported, her shoulder never works overhead with only her trunk holding her.

What it gets her: The shoulder blade turning fully upward (upward rotation) under load, with her body free in space (open chain). The upper arm also turns in and out in the socket (internal rotation) (external rotation).

# You need a standard, and a dip is already one half of it

sub: The pull-up and the dip together are the upper-body standard

Confirm it: You want one movement that reads her trunk organisation, body awareness and relative strength at once.

What it gets her: A read on all three in one movement. The pair is named together as the upper-body standard.

# Her pull-up has stalled and the rest of her program is light

sub: Check the week before you rebuild the movement

Confirm it: Look at her rowing and hinging loads. If they are light, the pull-up is not the problem.

What it gets her: A pull-up that works because the week around it does. One client fixed hers with no pull-up work at all. Once her rowing and hinging loads were high enough, the pull-up simply worked.

# Her wrist or elbow complains under a palms-away grip

sub: A palms-away grip (pronated grip) that hurts

Confirm it: The complaint is at the wrist or elbow, and it arrives with the grip rather than the load.

What it gets her: The movement kept, with the strain taken off. A palms-facing grip (neutral grip) is the named swap for exactly this.

Take it fresh

Grip-burning work goes at the end of a session so the hands are not pre-exhausted. The pull-up is the exception. Take it early, while her grip and her trunk are fresh.

Name the goal before you build the pull-up out of machines. Chest-supported machines and pulldowns give her external stability. The pull-up's job is stability she supplies herself, hanging in an open chain. Built out of machines alone it is a lat exercise, not a shoulder-function one. If shoulder function is the goal, the ladder on the Swaps page leads to the bar itself. Which goal the slot serves is the decision, and you make it before you pick the rungs.

Setup. Full closed grip, knuckles above the bar, trunk set before the hang loads. Setup has the full list.

Terms on this page

open chain: The hand or foot on the working limb is free in space rather than fixed to the floor or a pad, like a leg extension or a curl. Hanging from a bar, her body is free and only her trunk ties the shoulder to the pelvis.

neutral grip: Palms facing each other, or facing in toward her body. On a pull-up it takes the strain off a wrist or elbow that complains under a palms-away grip.

upward rotation: The shoulder blade turning so its outer corner rises and the socket tilts upward. It is what lets the arm travel overhead.

internal rotation: Turning a limb inward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face back or down, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more toward the middle. An arm held that way is internally rotated.

external rotation: Turning a limb outward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face forward or up, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more to the side. An arm held that way is externally rotated.

pronated grip: Palms facing down or away from her, knuckles up.
