Choosing exercises · T-13.1

Every muscle gets mid and lengthened range every week

Every muscle she is training gets at least one movement that overloads the middle of its range (mid range). Wherever the muscle allows it, she also gets one that overloads it at its longest (lengthened position). Those two are the requirement. Shortened-range work is optional.

A complete quad session can be two exercises. A squat variation that biases the lengthened position, and a leg press covering mid to lengthened. Nothing is missing from that session.

Why it works. Muscle grows in response to mechanical tension, the pull on its fibres while it works against a load. Mid range is where the muscle produces the most force, so it is where the most tension is available. Lengthened work adds the tension a mid-range movement cannot reach. Shortened-range work loads the position where the muscle is weakest (shortened position). That is why it is the least productive of the three per unit of fatigue.

What the phase does to this. A hypertrophy block, a muscle-building phase, biases toward lengthened work. A cut, a fat-loss phase, biases toward shortened. Those move the ratio between the three. They never remove the mid and lengthened floor this rule sets. The phases has the detail.

Watch for

A session filled with shortened-range work because it feels like it is working. Cable kickbacks and leg extensions produce a strong sensation. The sensation is not the stimulus.

Terms on this page

mid range: The middle of the movement, where the muscle is neither fully stretched nor fully shortened. It is where the muscle produces the most force.

lengthened position: The point in the rep where the working muscle is at its longest while it carries the load. It describes the muscle's length, not whether she feels a stretch.

shortened position: The muscle is working while it is bunched short.
