When she has stopped doing what she said she would

When she has stopped doing what she said she would, work out which kind of stop it is before you say anything. They have different answers, and picking the wrong one wastes the conversation. This page is the topic's rules in the order you use them.

Start by knowing whose job this is. Your work is building her self-awareness rather than supplying her motivation. Everything below is diagnosis before it is encouragement.

Which stop is this?

? Has the novelty worn off, around week six?

yes: Engineer novelty inside the plan. A harder variation now, and the main lift feels like a win when she returns to it.

? Is it a slump you could have seen coming on the calendar?

yes: Build through it. The foundations go in four to six weeks before it lands, with her goal tied to something that is not a date.

? Is it a scale that stopped moving?

yes: Move the affirmation onto every set. Reps and load are output she can see improve weekly.

? Is it "I know what to do, I just don't do it"?

yes: That is a hidden constraint, not defiance. Find it and design around it. Do not instruct her a fourth time.

? Has she asked you outright to motivate her?

yes: Ask the questions. Open-ended questions, affirmations, reflecting back, summarising. Never supply the answer.

= Then read her against the environment she was actually in before you judge the week at all.

Before you pick any tool. Type what she runs on. Toward something or away from it. From inside or from outside. And in every case your own language is governed first: she never failed. Fix the word before you fix the feeling.

Watch for

Changing the program on one bad week. Nothing does. Adherence as a signal is the gate for any actual change, and it wants a pattern of weeks, not one.

Her language about food or her body has changed. That is Body image and the referral line, not this topic.
