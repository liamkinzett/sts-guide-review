Warming up · T-16.4

The ramp table

How many feeder sets she does depends on how heavy the working set is. Look the working set up in this table and take the ramp from its row.

Working setRampWhy
Above 80% of one rep maxFull ramp. Three feeders at roughly 40, 60 and 80%This is where a specific warm-up actually earns its place
Mid range, roughly 60 to 80%One feeder at roughly 60% of the working loadEnough to find the load and see the first reps
Late isolation, muscle already workedNone neededThe opening reps do the job
Anything loaded in a deep stretchOne feeder, wherever it sits in the sessionCold tissue in a loaded stretch is the risk. A warm muscle is not the same as a muscle warmed at length

The 80% line is where the ramp starts paying. It is measured against her one rep max (1RM), the most she can lift once. The numbers inside the ramp are the STS convention. Every coach on the team ramps the same way, so a client moving between coaches meets the same warm-up.

Worked example. Her leg day opens with a hack squat at a load she can lift about six times. That is above 80%, so she gets the full ramp. Later in the session she does a leg extension on quads that are already worked. That gets no feeder. The seated hamstring curl after it loads the hamstrings in a deep stretch, so it gets one feeder even though the muscle is warm.

When you may deviate. A client new to a movement ramps more regardless of load. There the ramp is teaching rather than preparing. A client returning from a layoff, the same.

Terms on this page

feeder set: A light set done before the working sets to rehearse the movement and find the day's load. It is never counted as volume.

ramp: The run of feeder sets that climbs toward the first working set.

1RM: The heaviest weight she can lift once with good form.
