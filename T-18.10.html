One leg or two · T-18.10

The extra core demand is a bonus, not the reason

A single-leg or single-arm multi-joint movement (unilateral) works her core harder than a stable two-limbed (bilateral) equivalent. That is real. It is still not why you programmed it.

You programmed it for the position it reaches, or for the loadability it offers when a bilateral pattern has run out of room. The core demand comes along with those.

Why the distinction is not pedantry. A bonus does not have to justify itself. The moment the extra demand becomes the reason, it has to pass two tests that it fails. First, the squat already works the mid-section more than isolated or unstable core work does. Second, unilateral training carries over to her bilateral lifts very little once both limbs are being trained, which is every real program (cross-education).

So take it, do not sell it. A client asking why she is doing split squats should hear about her glutes, not about her core.

When you may deviate. Nothing here stops you valuing the demand. It stops you counting it as the justification when the position and the loadability are what actually earned the movement its slot.

Terms on this page

unilateral: One limb doing the work, as in a split squat or a single-arm row. Where the load is held does not decide it.

bilateral: Both limbs working together, as in a two-footed squat or a standard Romanian deadlift.

cross-education: The strength one limb gains when only the other limb is trained. It is minimal once both limbs are being trained.
