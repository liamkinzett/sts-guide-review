When she tells you how many days she can train

She names her days. The days are the container and the volume is the constraint, so the answer never starts with picking a split. Sometimes the number she named later drops. Run the same order again on the days she actually has.

Test: three things decide the whole shape

Her goal and pathway. The split serves the goal. A strength pathway flips two defaults below: it wants equal exposure across the foundational patterns, not a lower-body or shoulder bias. Assessing a new client has the three paths.

Her weekly band per muscle and the per-session ceiling. Volume holds both. Frequency is calculated from these, never chosen.

What ends a hard set: coordination or the muscle? This is the stage gate. Her trunk giving way, her balance going or the pattern falling apart means she stays where she is. The muscle running out of reps means she moves on. Judge it near the limit, never from a comfortable set.

Read

# Weekly band divided by the per-session ceiling = exposures needed

sub: Two is the floor for most muscles

What it gives you: Fifteen to twenty sets on a targeted muscle is two exposures, sometimes three.

# The gate result = her stage

sub: Full body across three days, then upper/lower, then push/pull/legs

What it gives you: Capability moves her on. The calendar never does.

# No split is superior

sub: Stop reading the shape as the decision

What it gives you: The decision is fit. Her capability, her week, her recovery.

Do: build the week in this order

Volume first, split second. The split is the solution to the volume, never the other way around.

Count frequency per pattern, not per exercise. Hack squat Monday plus leg press Thursday is a squat pattern twice.

Cap frequency by her absolute load. Twice weekly per pattern is a ceiling for an advanced lifter, not a target. A beginner practising light patterns tolerates far more.

Bias the week toward the goal. Lower body weighted, with glutes prioritised inside it. Delts ahead of chest, and a program with no direct chest work is a normal program. Both hold off the strength path only, where equal exposure across the foundational patterns is correct.

Place the priority where she is fresh. The front of a session, or a day where nothing has already fatigued it. Arms and delts at the start of lower body days rather than after the pressing that would bury them.

A specialisation block moves volume rather than conjuring it. Volume decides whether to specialise at all. Deliver the freed sets as more exposures at moderate per-session volume, so the per-session ceiling still holds.

Change. When the days drop or the week breaks some other way. Splits break for four reasons and only one of them is a programming error.

ChangesDoes not change
The container. Rerun the arithmetic of the Read step on the days she actually hasThe priorities. Keep the work that serves her goal, trained fresh

Never pick a split from a day count. There is no lookup from days to a named split, on purpose. The arithmetic and the stages are the answer. A week shorter than stage one's three days is not a split at all. Build it as a priority order and run the sessions from the top, the way the page on a roster with no fixed days describes. Training around life covers a two-day week and very short sessions in full.

Terms on this page

exposures: The number of separate sessions in a week in which a muscle is trained. Two exposures means it is hit on two different days.

hard set: A working set taken within three reps of failure.

crossover fatigue: Fatigue that shows up in a session other than the one that caused it. Heavy hinging on a pull day limiting her bracing on a later hinge session is an example.
