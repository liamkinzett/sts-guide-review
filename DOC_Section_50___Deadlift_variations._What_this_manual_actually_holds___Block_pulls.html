Block pulls: sumo only, and a pain lever

A sumo block pull is a highly valuable accessory for a sumo puller. For a conventional puller it is neither prescribed nor rejected. And for any deadlift that hurts off the floor, blocks are the right lever. Same implement, two different jobs.

The strength system makes the same general objection. A block cannot reproduce the position of a floor pull, and the sumo block pull is its one exception.

Compared

columns: Sumo block pull | Conventional block pull

Verdict: A highly valuable accessory | None. Neither prescribed nor rejected

What it trains: Getting into a good position, holding intense pelvic tension to break the floor, and the knee "snap" at lockout | Nothing specific. If you program one, it is your decision, recorded as yours

The limit: A block pull will not fix a lockout whose cause is her position off the floor | The same

For a conventional puller, treat the block pull as neither prescribed nor rejected. It is not banned, and it is not a fix.

Compared

columns: Blocks as a pain lever | Blocks as a training accessory

The decision: Pain. Her deadlift hurts off the floor | Training. She is a sumo puller and wants a stronger break and lockout

What blocks do: Shorten the range at the position that hurts. Start there and progress down toward the floor | Let her drill position, pelvic tension and the lockout snap

Who: Any puller, decided by Pain rules and when to refer | Sumo pullers. For a conventional puller it is your call

Neither a block pull nor a rack pull fixes a lockout whose cause is position off the floor. It is almost impossible to create the same position from blocks that she would be in from the floor. So the carry-over to her floor pull (specificity) is lost.

The issue is a loss of position from the floor, not from block height, and the root is left untouched. The usual cause of a lagging lockout is loss of trunk rigidity off the floor.

After bracing (bracing) has been taught, fix it with rigid-torso core work: the RKC plank, the GHD sit up, the ab wheel. Core has those pages.

Both are true at once. Blocks are the right lever for pain off the floor, because that is a pain decision. For a conventional puller they are a training accessory STS neither prescribes nor rejects, because that is a training decision. Keep the two jobs apart when you write the program.

Terms on this page

specificity: The closer the training is to the quality or lift she wants to improve, the more it carries over. It is how closely a training movement matches the lift you want to improve.

bracing: Filling the trunk with air and tensing the whole midsection so the spine stays rigid under load.
