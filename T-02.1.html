Strength and skill · T-02.1

In what order do tissues and systems adapt to training?

These systems adapt on different timescales, and they overlap rather than occurring as separate stages. Nervous-system and skill changes usually become obvious first. Visible muscle growth usually takes longer. Connective tissue and bone generally adapt more slowly again, bone by a wide margin.

Beginners often make rapid early progress because they are not only adapting their muscle. They are also learning to use the strength they already have more effectively. To produce force in a lift, the nervous system has to do several jobs at once:

  • switch on the right muscle fibres;
  • coordinate several muscles together;
  • stabilise the joints involved;
  • and organise the movement pattern itself.

    • A beginner is usually inefficient at all of them, and the early phase of training improves them quickly.

    This is why someone can add weight to a squat or a row every week while looking almost the same in the mirror. The body is improving the use of existing capacity before enough structural change has happened to create a large visible difference. The lift really has improved. The mistake is assuming the cause must already be muscle growth.

    Roughly, fastest to slowest

    Nervous system | Fastest. Stability, then skill, then strength

    Muscle | Next. Size follows the skill

    Tendon and ligament | Slower again. Slow to toughen

    Bone | Slowest, by a wide margin

    Inside the nervous system there is its own order. A brand-new lifter usually becomes steadier first, then more technically consistent, then better at expressing force. That is why a movement that looked chaotic in week one often looks stable by week three. That can happen even before a lifter is dramatically stronger in a general sense.

    The risk in the same picture. Force-production capacity can improve faster than some slower-adapting tissues are prepared to tolerate. A beginner can become able to produce much more force before the tissues that transmit and tolerate it have changed to match.

    What it means for coaching. The early weeks are a skill problem wearing a strength problem's clothes. That is why Her first program starts a beginner on pattern, not load. It is also why a technique breakdown under rising load is read as a diagnosis rather than cued away.

    A worked case. A client three weeks in has added weight to her goblet squat every session and says nothing has changed. Her nervous system has learned the lift. Her muscle, tendon and bone are only starting. Tell her that is the order they tend to show up in, and that the load comes later.

    Watch for

    Reading fast early progress as talent, or as a well-designed program. It is the nervous system. It is temporary. It will stop.

    Say it like this

    The first stretch moves quickly because your nervous system learns fast. Your tendons and bones take much longer, so we build the habit now and the load later.
