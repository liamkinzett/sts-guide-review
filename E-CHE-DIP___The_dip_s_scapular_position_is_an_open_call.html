Chest · E-CHE-DIP#⚠ The dip's scapular position is an open call

What the shoulder blade does on a dip

Pull the blades down through the descent, hold them back, and delay any rise as long as possible. Forward tip is never permitted. That is the STS standard for the shoulder blade on a dip.

What the dip is not. It is not a bench press. On a bench the blade is locked and immobile against the board. A dip has nothing behind the blades, so they are not pinned. They are held.

The standard, part by part

# On the way down

sub: Blades pulled down and held back

Do this: Actively pull the shoulder blades down (depression) as the arm travels behind the body. Hold them back. This is the whole reason the dip is a low-trap exercise.

# At the bottom

sub: No forward tip

Do this: The blade stays flat on the rib cage. The top edge tipping forward (forward tip) is never permitted, at any load.

# At the top

sub: No shrug

Do this: Do not let the blades rise. Early rise is the named fault on this movement, under Shruggie in Faults and what you can actually see. Do not coach spreading and rising at the top the way the push-up does.

# Across the set

sub: Whatever position she starts with survives to the last rep

Do this: The position degrading late in the set is a fault, not fatigue to be tolerated.

Why not the push-up standard. You will hear a case for letting the blades spread and rise at the top of a dip, because nothing is behind them. The rise is a named fault on this movement, and holding the blade flat on the rib cage is what keeps the shoulder stable. So the dip is coached the way this page states it. The push-up is the movement where the blade moves at the top, and Why the blade moves here covers it.

Film it from the side

Forward tip and an early rise are best seen side-on, not from the front. Diagnosing from video covers the camera angles.

Terms on this page

depression: The shoulder blade pulled down, away from the ear. The opposite of a shrug. On a dip she does this actively through the descent.

forward tip: The top edge of the shoulder blade tipping forward, so the bottom edge lifts off the rib cage. It shows as the shoulder rolling forward at the bottom of the rep.
