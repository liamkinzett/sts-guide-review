Muscle and growth · T-01.11

How much muscle can someone realistically gain, and on what timescale?

Muscle is built on a timescale of a year. Clients want it in cycles of 12 weeks. Give her that mismatch. Never give her an annual gain figure as a target.

Muscle growth is slow. That sounds obvious, but it is one of the most important expectation-setting points a coach can teach. Clients often think in blocks of eight, ten or twelve weeks. Real growth is better understood on the scale of months and years.

Key figure

value: A year

unit: the timescale muscle is built on

note: She wants it in 12 weeks. Twelve weeks buys a fraction of a year's growth.

Why exact gain targets are a problem.

You will hear simple rules for how many pounds of muscle someone "should" gain per month or per year. These numbers can work as rough context, but they turn dangerous once they are handed over as promises. People differ in training age, genetics, body size, sex, recovery, nutrition, adherence and starting condition. Because of that, an annual gain figure should not become a target she is expected to hit.

The number you will hear. The source gives it two ways in the same breath. They are separate estimates, not one worked through into the other. First, an upper limit of about two pounds a month for a natural lifter. Then, discounted because not all of that gain is muscle, a pound of bodyweight a month, which is 12 pounds over the year. Rounded down, call it 10 pounds of muscle. Treat all of it as a rough remark, not a measurement.

The usable part is the timescale. If 10 pounds of muscle a year is near the ceiling, 12 weeks buys a fraction of it. Most clients want growth in 12-week cycles. It does not happen on that clock. That mismatch is solid. The figure behind it is not.

The default, and its one strict edge. The default is to give her the timescale and no pounds. The strict edge is that the figure is never presented as a target, however hard she presses. If she needs a number, the number is twelve weeks against a year.

Why it matters. A figure that earns authority through repetition ends up in a client's head as a promise. When the weeks arrive and the pounds do not, she reads it as failure. The next page has the words for the conversation.

Say it like this

Muscle is measured in years, not in twelve-week blocks. Twelve weeks is a great start. It's not the finish line.
