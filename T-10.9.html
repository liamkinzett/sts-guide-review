Two flat exposures on one lift means go and look

Two sessions in a row with no movement on one lift, inside the same block, is when you open the question.

The unit of judgement is one movement over time. Each session where a lift is trained is an exposure (exposure). The question is not how many of her exercises are stalling. It is how long this one has been.

Exposures, not weeks. A lift trained twice a week gives you the signal in half the calendar time. A lift trained fortnightly should not be judged on the calendar at all.

It is a trigger to diagnose, not to change something. Two flat sessions tell you to go and look. They do not tell you what is wrong. A stall has as many possible causes as a decline does. Volume has the order you work through. Reaching for a lever at this point is guessing with extra steps.

Why two. One flat session is a bad night, a poor warm-up or a week of bad sleep. Two is a pattern. Waiting for three means a fortnight or more on a movement that has stopped moving, which is time she does not get back.

Why not a count across the program. The number of stalling exercises tells you almost nothing on its own. A program with six stalled movements might have five in a deliberate maintenance block and one genuine problem. The tally cannot tell you which. The stall you act on is always a specific lift with a specific history.

Watch for

Acting on the first flat session because you were looking. Checking a logbook weekly is right. Changing something every time you check it is not.

Terms on this page

exposure: One session in the week where a lift, a pattern or a muscle is trained. A lift trained twice a week gets two exposures a week.
