Swaps

The swaps here are about kit. The ladder for a painful back is about direction. Sort out which one you need before you change anything.

When the crunch machine, cable or GHD is not there

# No crunch machine

sub: Adjustable resistance in a simple floor-supported setup

Swap to: Kneeling cable crunch

Why: STS has no page for the kneeling cable crunch, so use it as the stopgap and return to the machine ab crunch. Setup has how to set it up.

# No cable

sub: A mat and body weight

Swap to: Curl-up

Why: Lying on her back, knees bent, feet drawn in, hands face down. Exhale through the rep. Control the way down while breathing in. STS has no page for the curl-up either; it keeps the session moving.

# No GHD

sub: No stand-in

Why: There is none. The 45 degree bench is the platform for side bends, not a GHD stand-in.

# Her back says no

sub: The ordered ladder below

Why: Establish her direction bias first, then run the rungs.

# Home

sub: No gym access

Why: STS coaches clients who train in equipped gyms, so there is no home version.

The ladder for a painful back. Find her direction bias before rung one (direction bias). Map three ranges first: the ones that are fine, the ones that increase her symptoms, and the ones the injury restricts. Where she feels better in flexion, the corrective work is done in flexion. Pain rules and when to refer decides whether she trains at all. Region by region covers the low back.

The ladder

Curl-up. The entry position.

Legs at 90-90. Hips and knees at a right angle each. The same movement with less lever.

Feet on a bench. The end of the ladder.

The intent across all three is to create space at the back of the lower back (lumbar spine), where the nerves exit.

Terms on this page

direction bias: The direction of spinal movement that eases her symptoms, flexion or extension. The corrective work is done in the direction that feels better.

lumbar spine: The lower back, the part of the spine between the ribs and the pelvis. It is made of five vertebrae.
