Tracking her symptoms instead of the calendar

A calendar cannot tell you how she will feel this week. What replaces it is her own reported fatigue and symptoms, week by week. That report is the instrument.

In practice this is one plain question in a check-in: does anything feel different this week? She tells you, and you adjust to what she reports rather than to a predicted phase.

What she might report. Cramps, headaches or poorer sleep. Gut symptoms, a heavier scale reading, or a change in appetite. Or simply a session that feels harder than usual. All of these are real and any of them can move week to week. None of them tells you to touch her percentages.

A scale move is not automatically fat gain. Her body holds different amounts of fluid at different points in the cycle. A client who sees the number rise and reads it as fat gain or a failing plan needs the fluctuation named, not denied. That is different from a scale jump tied to a pill change. A scale jump after starting or switching contraception already covers that version.

A worked example. A client says her sets of ten felt much harder this week and she slept badly. You do not look up her phase. You treat it as you would any client reporting a hard week, and Fatigue and recovery tells you how. The reason does not change the response.

When to deviate. Where she has a diagnosed condition whose management is phase-specific. That is a clinical matter, not a programming one.

Verdict

A diagnosed condition managed by phase is her clinician's call.

Pain rules and when to refer holds the routing. You program around it.

Watch for

Turning this into a survey. She needs to be able to tell you something is different, not fill in a form.
