What to do when she reports neck pain in a session

Ask where the load went before you look at the neck. Gym neck pain has one causal model, and it is mechanical: shoulder blades that are not managing force.

Work through it in this order

Test. Is she rowing or pulling? Is her head leaving the bench? Is her rib cage stacked?

Read. Describe what you saw. Name a guess as a guess, and hold it loosely. This region is thinner than any other.

Do. Stack the rib cage. Fix the shoulder blades. Hold the head neutral. In that order. None of it is neck work.

Change. On an arched bench, the head stays down.

Escalate. Any arm symptom goes now. Whiplash, seeing stars or vomiting is not yours to touch.

Step 1. Test

Four checks

# Is she rowing or pulling?

sub: The shoulder-blade-to-skull chain is the likeliest reading

Do this: Watch the shoulder blades. If they barely move, the neck is doing their job.

# Is her head leaving a bench, or moving through the rep?

sub: Head as a release valve

Do this: Load is escaping through the neck. Find where it should have been held.

# Is her rib cage stacked?

sub: The chain starts there

Do this: Ribs over pelvis before anything else. A flared rib cage un-anchors the shoulder blades.

# The sort

sub: Every movement into helps, no effect, hurts

Do this: The sort is the plan.

Step 2. Read

Name a working read the way you would anywhere else, and hold it loosely. Describe what you saw and what she reported, then give the guess as a guess. Never grade or stage it, and never let it delay a referral.

Step 3. Do

Stack the rib cage. Fix the shoulder blade control. Hold the head neutral. In that order. None of it is neck work.

Your working read. Name it, label it a guess

The one causal model. Muscles run from the shoulder blade up to the base of the skull. The upper traps are one. The other is the muscle from the top inner corner of the blade to the neck (levator scapulae). If the shoulder blades are not moving well, those muscles are not managing force well, and she gets an achy neck. Two different exercises in two different clients produced the same neck pain by the same route.

What you see, what you might guess, what changes on Monday

# Neck aches during or after pressing, dips or heavy pulling. Shoulder blade barely moves

sub: Working read: shoulder blades not managing force

Why it happens: The muscles from shoulder blade to skull absorb what the blade did not.

Do this: Coach the shoulder blade before you remove the exercise. Awareness alone has resolved this: one case resolved on cues and intent, with no drills and no time off.

# Neck and upper-trap tension that arrived with a "shoulders back" cue

sub: An imposed deviation, not a problem she had

Why it happens: Pulling the shoulders back creates too much upper-trap tension and literally gives her a headache.

Do this: Drop the cue. Stack the ribs over the pelvis instead.

# Soreness at the base of the neck under a squat bar

sub: The bar is on the bony bump at the base of the neck (C7), not the muscle below it

Do this: Reset the bar onto the shelf of the upper traps. The squat set-up page in Quads owns the position.

# Upper traps doing the retracting on rows and pulldowns

sub: Working read: upper-trap dominance

Do this: Do not retract with the upper traps. Use the lower traps to pull the blades back and down. A high-to-low path, and "don't think shrug".

# Neck ache alongside a shoulder that has been grumbling

sub: One presentation, not two

Why it happens: A neck pain patient has a shoulder issue, and a shoulder pain patient has a neck problem. The two interlock.

Do this: Run the shoulder pages and this one together. Do not fix one and re-test the other.

# A knot under the shoulder blade, aching into the neck

sub: Working read: levator scapulae, rhomboid or serratus

Do this: Shoulder blade position and force management, per the model above. Not a trigger point to dig into.

# Upper traps doing the work the levator should share

sub: The levator is a helper, not a mover

Why it happens: The levator scapulae is to the traps what the rotator cuff is to the delts: there to create joint stability, not to move the load.

Do this: Stop trying to strengthen it as a mover. Fix what it is compensating for.

A working read never changes how it is said

It is a best guess, named with the proper clinical term, with a physio to confirm it. Never graded. Never a reason to delay a referral. This region is thin, so hold the guess more loosely than anywhere else.

How a working read is said

My best guess, and it is a guess, not a diagnosis, is that your shoulder blades aren't doing the pulling. Your neck is picking it up. A physio confirms it. Meanwhile we change how you row, not your neck.

Step 4. Change

On an arched bench, the head stays down. That is the one contraindication this region carries.

Step 5. Escalate

Red flags

title: Refer now

Any arm symptom: pain, tingling, numbness or weakness travelling down the limb. The any-limb trigger owns it, and there is nothing here to guess with

Everything in the general stop-and-refer list, plus seven out of ten reported consistently, plus pain still consistent between three days and one week

emergency: Whiplash, seeing stars, or throwing up. Not yours to touch. She goes for imaging, to an outpatient clinic, or to the hospital.

What we do not hold for the neck. Nothing on nerve root, joint or disc involvement at the neck beyond referring it. Neck pain that is not rowing-related and not head-position related has no page here. Refer it on the general table.

Terms on this page

C7: The seventh neck vertebra, the bony bump at the base of the neck. A bar sits below it, never on it.

levator scapulae: The muscle running from the top inner corner of the shoulder blade up to the neck. It shrugs and it carries neck tension.
