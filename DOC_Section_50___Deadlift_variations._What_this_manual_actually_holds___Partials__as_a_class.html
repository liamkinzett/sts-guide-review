A partial never fixes a problem it is not causing

Using a partial to solve a problem it is not causing is like sending an ambulance to the bottom of a cliff. People keep falling off it, and nobody has put up a fence. Find the cause, then pick the tool that touches it. This is the strength system's rule, written for a competitive powerlifter.

The named example is the high box squat for a lifter without supportive gear (raw lifter) who struggles above parallel. The box squat trains the top of the lift. The cause is the pelvic and bracing problem (bracing) at the bottom. The box squat does not touch it, so it fixes nothing.

Apply the same test to any deadlift partial. A block pull will not fix a lockout whose real cause is her position off the floor. Before you shorten a lift to attack a sticking point (sticking point), ask one question. Is the sticking point where the problem starts, or only where it shows?

A variation programmed to build tissue is doing a different job. A peaking cycle runs variations, the deficit pull among them, through its whole first block. That is not a partial sent to fix a fault. It builds tissue and work capacity at higher reps and a lower joint cost, while the competition lift is not yet the point. Peaking for a meet covers it. The rule here is about the partial as a fix, and it stands.

Terms on this page

raw lifter: A powerlifter who competes without a squat suit or a bench shirt.

bracing: Filling the trunk with air and tensing the whole midsection so the spine stays rigid under load.

sticking point: The part of a lift where the bar slows or stops, the hardest point of the range for her.
