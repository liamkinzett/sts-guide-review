Using the pregnancy calendar as a watch-list

Trimester tables are averages and she is not an average. The calendar tells you what to watch for; she tells you what comes out.

Most pregnancy guidance is written by trimester: what comes out and when. STS demotes that to a prompt. What comes out is decided by her, not by the week.

What the calendar prompts you to watch

First trimester | | Fatigue and nausea are the presenting problem. Fewer sets first, at the same effort

From the second trimester | | Lying on her back becomes symptom-conditional. Watch bump size limiting depth, pelvic floor symptoms and blood pressure

Any week | | A stop sign ends the session. The stop-the-session page holds the list

Lying on her back. From the second trimester, modify lying flat (supine) only if she feels lightheaded, nauseous or unwell on her back. It is symptom-conditional. The growing uterus can compress the large vein returning blood to her heart (vena cava). Incline, seated or half-kneeling variants are the fix.

Two popular rules STS does not run

"Remove all front-loading from the second trimester." It is not a rule here. Front-loaded movements stay as long as she is symptom-free, like every other movement.

"Avoid breath-holding in pregnancy." Holding her breath and bracing under load (Valsalva) does not reduce blood flow to the placenta. It is not a rule here. Pelvic floor symptoms are handled on their own, later in this topic. The effort anchor already lowers the bracing demand without banning anything. Breathing under load covers the brace itself.

Terms on this page

supine: Lying flat on her back.

vena cava: The large vein that carries blood from the lower body back to the heart. A heavy uterus can press on it when she lies flat.

Valsalva: Holding her breath and bracing against a closed throat to stiffen the trunk under load.
