Faults and what you can actually see · T-32.13

The conventional deadlift is coached through this topic's names and its tempo

The conventional deadlift is coached through the fault names in this topic and its own tempo. Where the pull breaks down at the floor, the fix is timing, not a new technique.

The conventional pull carries a tempo of 11X1: a settled pause on the floor and at lockout, rehearsed as a command. Tempo and pauses explains the notation, and Deadlift variations covers the pull's family.

The faults already have names. Hips shoot up, including knee extension before hip extension and getting jerked forward at the floor. Slack never taken out, including no wedge and the click. Bar off the midline, including the shoulders too far forward over the bar.

Why the floor is where it breaks. A deadlift starts with the lifting half of the rep (concentric). Most lifts start with a lowering phase (eccentric), which gives her time to find and correct position before load peaks. A deadlift does not. She has to create tension on the bar off the ground and be where she needs to be the moment she starts producing force.

The fix is timing. Pull the slack out of the bar with the shoulders and the lats, and push into the ground at the same time. That creates pre-tension, so she does not shoot forward. Sync hip and knee extension together off the floor rather than letting the hips shoot up first. Keep the shoulders over the midfoot rather than drifting in front of the bar.

Cues

When: Before the bar leaves the floor

Say: Pull the slack out of the bar.

Why: The wedge. A bar that clicks free has no tension in it, and she shoots forward.

When: Off the floor

Say: Push the floor away.

Why: Leg drive. Hips and knees extend together instead of the hips shooting up first.

Watch for

A back rounding off the floor read as the deadlift loading her back. The mechanism is usually a lost wedge, not the lift by design. Levers and moment arms explains it.

Terms on this page

concentric: The lifting half of a rep, where the muscle shortens under load.

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.
