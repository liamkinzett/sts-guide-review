Warming up · T-16.8

Prep work answers the first movement first

Prep work is now the only thing standing between a client walking in cold and her first loaded rep. So the menu is chosen for the first movement of the session before anything else.

Removing the general warm-up removed something. Be clear about what took its place. That imposes a requirement the old warm-up never had. The menu answers the first movement first. A session opening with squats prepares ankles and hips. A session opening with pressing prepares the shoulder. Whatever else the menu covers, it covers that first.

Why it works. Tempo and pauses forbids putting a stretch-paused movement first in a cold session and asks what "adequately warmed" means. This is the answer. Cut the general warm-up and most of the feeders without saying what replaced them. The removal then reads as an omission rather than a decision.

What it looks like. Her session opens with a barbell row. The prep is arching the upper back over a roller (thoracic extension), plus trap 3 raises. Those get her upper back and shoulder blades ready to pull. The hip airplanes she likes can come later or not at all. The first movement decides.

Watch for

A prep routine that never changes while the session does. That is a routine she performs, not preparation for what is coming.

Terms on this page

prep work: The movements inside the warm-up slot that get the first loaded movement ready. Chosen for the session in front of you.

thoracic extension: Straightening the upper back so the chest comes up and the rounding between the shoulder blades disappears. Over a foam roller it is a standard prep movement for a hinge or a pull.
