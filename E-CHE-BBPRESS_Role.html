What it's for

The barbell bench press is STS's heaviest loadable horizontal press and the competition lift. It sits at the top of a pressing ladder that runs from most loadable to least. That is why it is both the strength standard and the first movement to leave when a shoulder is provocative.

The resistance rises as she lowers (ascending resistance). It is hardest at the bottom, where the levers on the shoulder and elbow (moment arm) are longest.

It loads the mid and stretched positions and gives nothing at the squeezed end. The pec fly covers what it misses.

Its slot. The primary press for a powerlifting-goal client, where it is a judged lift and non-negotiable. For a physique-goal client STS treats it as optional. That is a positioning call about her goal, not a technique call.

The six STS variations, and what each one is for

# Barbell bench press

sub: The flat press and the loading ceiling

Use it for: Strength, and the heaviest load the pattern allows. The Setup, How to do it and Common faults pages are written for it.

# Comp style bench press

sub: A separate row because the pauses make it a separate movement

Use it for: A powerlifting-goal client, from the first week of the strength phase. A physique-goal client has no reason to take it. Tempo and pauses owns the commands and writes them into the tempo field. The pauses go in early, not in the final weeks.

# Close grip barbell bench press

sub: A plane-of-motion decision, not a triceps preference

Use it for: Two slots. The novice default, where a new presser starts close so the end range asks less of her tissue. And the de-specified variation in a strength block, the same motor pattern at a lower technical demand, which The phases runs in the specific phase.

Why the grip matters: A narrow grip trades sideways lift of the arm (abduction) for rotation and for bending and straightening. The shoulder sits in a more stable position and the triceps becomes the limiter. A wide grip does the reverse, with stability coming from the smaller muscles rather than the rotator cuff. That is why a very wide bencher's typical complaint is pain in the long head of the biceps. Region by region defines the width and sets the floor under it.

Watch for: Do not pair it with a big arch. The close grip's asset is bar speed. Cutting the range with an arch cuts the runway the bar has to accelerate into the sticking point. And closer is not better: it's not dose dependent. Like if close is good, closer isn't better. The case for a neutral-grip bar is aimed at hands set inside shoulder width, where a palms-forward grip forces the elbows out. At shoulder width it does not bite.

# Block press

sub: The board removes the bottom, and that is the whole point

Use it for: A max-effort variation in the variety phase, where The phases places it. Thicker board early, thinner as the funnel narrows toward the tested lift. That is a placement, not an indication.

Why: It deliberately takes out the stretched position and leaves mid-to-squeezed. That is why it is the one row in this family that loads differently from the rest.

Watch for: The board height, any dead stop, any pause length and who it suits all come from The phases. This page adds nothing to them.

# Incline barbell bench press

sub: What changes is the bar, not the bench

Use it for: The incline slot where the loading ceiling is the point. Take the dumbbell where arm path and range are the point.

Why: The angle is chosen and recorded exactly as it is on the dumbbell version. Dumbbell, barbell and machine are accepted interchangeably at a low incline. The fixed path, the higher ceiling, the shoulder-blade lock (scapular lock) and the elbow tuck all carry over from the flat version.

Watch for: On a higher incline, tuck the elbows slightly so the line of force meets the upper chest (clavicular fibres) rather than the front delt.

# Spoto press

sub: The bar stops an inch short of the chest

Use it for: A lifter who loses tightness on the descent. Variations has the fault it does not fix.

The tucked-grip novice default, explained. A new presser is started close on tissue tolerance at end range, not on muscle bias. A tucked grip is less compromised by the upper-back and shoulder-blade positions most desk-bound clients cannot yet hold.

Terms on this page

ascending resistance: The load feels heavier the further into the movement she goes. On a bench press that is the bottom, bar at the chest.

moment arm: The perpendicular distance between the joint axis and the line of force.

abduction: A limb travelling out and away from the midline of the body: the arm in a lateral raise, or the thigh moving out to the side. On a bench press, a wider grip means more of it.

clavicular fibres: The upper part of the chest muscle, running from the collarbone to the upper arm. An incline biases them.

scapular lock: The shoulder blades pulled back and down before the unrack and held there for the whole set. Scapular means to do with the shoulder blade.
