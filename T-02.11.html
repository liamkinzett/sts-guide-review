Is mental rehearsal the same as the mind-muscle connection?

When she asks about rehearsing a lift in her head, do not answer with the mind-muscle-connection material. They are different questions. Mental rehearsal with no movement is motor imagery (motor imagery). It never replaces practising the lift itself.

Motor imagery means no movement happens at all: the lift is rehearsed in the head while the muscle rests. Where attention is placed while actually lifting (attentional focus) is a related question and a different one. The two share the words attention, muscle and contraction, and nothing else. Practising a lift covers how a movement is learned.

What we hold is about attention during a real lift. Focusing outside the body (external focus) gives a small real edge in strength. The bar or the floor, say. It shows on the day and over time, most clearly in lower-body compound lifts.

Focusing on the muscle itself (internal focus) raises that muscle's activity at lighter loads. The rise is 7 to 10 percentage points, up to 60% of max. That boost vanishes at 80% and above, where the nervous system has to recruit everything regardless.

In one group of untrained men, internal focus roughly doubled bicep growth, 12.4% against 6.9%, and did nothing for quads. Do not build a program on that.

Why it matters. A coach asked about motor imagery who reaches for the mind-muscle material gives a confident, wrong answer. Substituting one for the other is a silent swap. A confident wrong answer costs more than a plain "I don't know yet".

Watch for

"Visualisation" used loosely to mean either one. Ask what she actually means before answering. Is the muscle moving, or is she only imagining it?

Say it like this

That's a good question and I don't have a solid answer on it yet. What I can tell you about is where you put your attention while you're actually lifting, which is related but not the same thing.

Terms on this page

motor imagery: Mentally rehearsing a lift or a contraction while the muscle stays at rest. No movement happens.

attentional focus: Where attention is directed during a real rep. On the muscle working, or on something outside the body, such as the bar.

external focus: Attention on something outside the body, such as pushing the floor away or driving the bar up.

internal focus: Attention on the working muscle itself, the "mind-muscle connection".
