Building a session · T-12.19

A session that benches must restore upward rotation

Any session with a barbell bench press in it needs an overhead press or a pulldown later in the same session. A barbell bench pins the shoulder blade (scapula), pulled back and down, held there for every rep. Overhead pressing or a pulldown afterwards restores its upward rotation (upward rotation). This is a requirement, not a balance preference.

So an upper session built around pressing is not finished when the pressing is. At least one overhead press or one pulldown belongs in it. A vertical pull is the cheapest way to satisfy it and it is not optional.

Why coaches miss it. STS deprioritises direct chest work, so many coaches never think about what the bench does to the shoulder. The sessions that break this rule are usually the ones written quickly. Build the pull in from the start.

Position and volume are two separate requirements, and both are live. Across the week her pressing to pulling stays no worse than 65/35. That is counted in hard sets (hard set). This page adds the position requirement on top of that. Choosing the pulldown rather than the overhead press satisfies both in one slot. It restores the position and it buys a pull toward the ratio.

Terms on this page

scapula: The shoulder blade. The flat bone on the back of the rib cage that the arm hangs from.

upward rotation: The shoulder blade turning so its outer corner rises and the socket tilts upward. It is what lets the arm travel overhead. A bench press holds it in the opposite position.

hard set: A working set taken within three reps of failure.
