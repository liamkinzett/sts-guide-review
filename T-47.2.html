What she will ask you · T-47.2

"Has sitting ruined my back?"

Posture is not the problem. Not moving is the problem. It does not matter what position she is not moving in. It is the fact that she is not moving.

The test that makes it concrete. Stand for eight hours and you will be equally wrecked, in different places, as if you sat for eight hours. The chair is not the villain. The stillness is.

This reframes her question rather than answering it. She asks whether sitting ruined her back. The answer is that no position ruins a back. Staying in one does. That also tells her what to do about it: move, often, in more directions.

The syndrome labels are rejected by name. A postural assessment that hands her "upper cross syndrome" is a low-resolution way of looking at human movement. Most people simply lack movement variability somewhere. That is trainable. A syndrome label is not.

Say it like this

Sitting didn't wreck your back. Sitting still for eight hours did, and standing still for eight hours would have done the same thing somewhere else. The fix is moving more, not sitting differently.

Why it works. A posture answer gives her a position to hold, which is one more way of not moving. A movement answer gives her something to do.

Watch for

Prescribing a resting posture. You have replaced one stillness with another.
