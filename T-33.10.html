The gatekeeper loop: diagnose on an unconstrained movement, then retest on it

Pick a movement with as little support as possible and use it to see the fault. Fix the missing quality with isolated work, then retest on the same movement. The loop without its last step is how a coach accumulates correctives.

Use the movement as an instrument rather than a stimulus. A standing single-arm overhead press. A walking lunge. Nothing holding her up, so nothing hiding what she cannot do. Reason from the visible fault back to the missing quality, prescribe isolated work for that quality, then retest against the same movement.

The worked example. Her lower back arches under an overhead press. That is a shoulder-flexion fault, not an extension fault. She is arching because the arm will not go where it needs to. Train the shoulder, then press again and look.

Removing constraints removes the diagnostic signal. Seated, two-armed, on a Smith machine, belted, strapped: each one makes the movement more stable and less informative. This runs the opposite way to most of what a coach reaches for. Stability is right when you want output. It is wrong when you want information.

So some exercises earn a place for what they tell you rather than what they train.

The gatekeeper loop

Diagnose | Watch the fault on a movement with nothing holding her up

Reason back | Which quality is missing to produce that fault

Prescribe | Isolated work for that quality

Retest | The same unconstrained movement. Did the fault go?

Watch for

The loop without its last step. Prescribing the corrective and never retesting is how a coach accumulates correctives.
