Triceps · E-TRI-PUSHDOWN#Role

What it's for

The triceps pushdown gives smooth, continuous tension that peaks from mid-range to the shortened end of elbow extension. The shoulder stays neutral, so this is the slot for the medial and lateral heads. The long head crosses the shoulder and cannot be loaded at length here (long head). That is why STS treats overhead work as a separate movement rather than a variant of this one. See Overhead cable extension.

What kind of movement it is. A single-joint movement against resistance. Any movement that extends the arm against resistance works the triceps this way. The usual slot is isolation work late in a push or arm session. Building a session decides the order.

Effort. The pushdown is one of the movements that may be taken to true failure. She is braced, the path is fixed, and a failed rep means the weight stops rather than her folding. A dip, by contrast, is a compound and holds at one rep in reserve (RIR) whatever assistance is added. Effort and intensity holds the rule.

Its two secondary roles

# The entry point for a shoulder that will not tolerate overhead work

sub: Nothing sits below it

Why: The ladder comes down through single-arm cable work to a rope pushdown at the bottom rung. Arm training is the basic introduction to working the stabiliser shoulder, because these muscles cross the shoulder joint. See Swaps.

# The cross-body version is a joint-position drill for the dip

sub: Exposure first, triceps second

Why: The intent is exposure to the joint positions the dip demands, which carries over to the dip itself. See Chest. As a triceps exercise it is a shortened-position movement, effective, and it would not rank at the top.

The machine pushdown is the braced setup this page already prescribes, pre-built. Setup step 5 sends a lifter whose torso sways to a chest-supported or braced setup rather than coaching the sway. The machine is that setup.

Choose it over the four cable versions where the fault is torso sway. Choose it where she is late in a session and grip or forearm fatigue is building. Choose it too where the single-arm versions expose a balance problem, which is not a load problem. Balance trouble on one arm is not a load problem explains.

What the machine costs. Machine work is the choice "if we wanted to only train for hypertrophy". The price is training for function and end ranges. Removing the sway also removes the signal that she cannot hold the position. Machine and chest-supported row states the same trade for rows.

What the grip changes on a triceps movement: nothing at the tricep. Turning the palm up (supination) or down (pronation) is a biceps action. "so it's not really going to do anything from a supination/pronation standpoint. However, the grip position sets up our shoulder position." That is the whole mechanism. The attachment is a shoulder-position dial wearing a hand-position costume, which is why this page has no rope-against-bar ranking to give you.

So select the grip at session level, not at exercise level. The worked negative example is a session of four palms-down movements. A palms-down machine overhead press, then a palms-down dumbbell overhead press, then a straight-bar pushdown, then a palms-down machine dip. "We've got redundancy in terms of movement selection, but we've got mass redundancy in terms of grip selection".

The palms-down pushdown (pronated grip) is the right call where the session's other shoulder positions are not already palms-down. It is the wrong call where they are. That is a forward selection rule, and it does not depend on ruling out the other three grips.

One positive use of the straight bar is named. For a lifter who has never taken her arm overhead, the straight-bar cable pushdown is the first exercise to reach for. It builds tone and force output in a structurally stable shoulder position before any integrated pattern. The palms-down grip is not incidental there. The stable shoulder position is the point.

Terms on this page

long head: The head of a muscle that crosses the shoulder joint as well as the elbow: on the triceps it only stretches with the arm raised, on the biceps only with the arm behind the body.

RIR: Reps in reserve. How many more reps she could have done when the set stopped.

pronated grip: Palms facing down or away from her, knuckles up.

supination: Turning the forearm so the palm faces up. A supinated grip is palms up.

pronation: Turning the forearm so the palm faces down or away. Of the foot, rolling inward so the arch flattens and the ankle drops in. A pronated grip is palms down.
