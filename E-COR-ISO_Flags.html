Watch for

  • Not a gate. STS does not run pass-or-fail screens. Nobody has to hold a plank before she is allowed to lift.
  • The order of teaching. The side plank comes before any dynamic side-bending work. She resists the movement before you ask her to produce it.
  • The scaling ceiling. A plank is static limbs plus a static trunk. It is low level, hard to scale and easy to over-use. Once she holds thirty seconds to a minute, move her on to a loadable flexion or extension exercise or a carry.
  • The front plank is not one of the McGill big three. The same stiffness principle still applies to it.
  • Stimulus density. Picture an athlete given weeks of remedial holds, several times a day, for a back. Properly loaded work would have fixed it in a fraction of the time. Low-stimulus work has to be stacked with something that carries a real stimulus. Volume counts her sets; Building a session decides where the hold sits.
  • Not in her rest periods on a strength or hypertrophy block. A tired trunk leaks force on the next heavy set. Pair core into rest only in a fat-loss or conditioning block. Rest and intensity techniques has the rule.
  • After an injury. An injured client braces everything for everything because she remembers the pain. Breaking that habit is part of the work. Pain rules and when to refer decides whether she trains. Returning to training owns the return.
