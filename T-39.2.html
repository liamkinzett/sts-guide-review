Reading her training data · T-39.2

A stall is read per exercise, not across the program

The unit of judgement is one movement over time. The question is how long this one has been flat, not how many exercises are stalling.

Two flat exposures means go and look. It does not yet mean change anything. Progression owns this read. Two sessions in a row with no movement, inside the same block, is when you open the question.

Count (exposures), never weeks. A lift trained twice a week gives you the signal in half the calendar time. A lift trained once a fortnight should not be judged on the calendar at all.

A worked read. Her hip thrust is trained twice a week. It was flat on Tuesday and flat again on Friday. That is two exposures in one week, and you go and look. Her deadlift is trained once a fortnight. Two exposures there is a month, and a month of flat deadlift is still only two exposures.

Why it works. The number of stalling exercises tells you almost nothing on its own. A program with several stalled movements might have most of them in a deliberate maintenance block and one genuine problem. The tally cannot tell you which. The stall you act on is always a specific lift with a specific history.

Watch for

A count. "Three of her lifts have stalled" is not a diagnosis. It is three separate questions wearing one coat, and a fourth about why you counted.

Terms on this page

exposures: The number of separate sessions in a week in which a muscle is trained. A stall is counted in them, never in calendar weeks.
