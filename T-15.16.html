Tempo and pauses · T-15.16

Relearning a movement: slow down, pause, up, pause

`3-4/1/1/1` while a client is rebuilding a pattern. That means after a layoff, after a technical fault has been corrected, or on the way back from an interruption. The movement itself is what is being restored. It comes off when the pattern is back.

Why it works. Every digit is doing a teaching job. The 3 to 4 second descent gives her time to feel the position. The pause at the stretch proves she owns the bottom rather than falling into it. The one second up keeps intent in the lift. The pause at the top stops her chaining repetitions together and hiding the fault in the rhythm.

Where the number comes from. It sits inside the four second ceiling and matches the learning range for the lowering half (eccentric), which is the argument for it. It is a different problem from tendon rehab, which is about tissue. The reasoning for one does not transfer to the other.

This is a deliberate exception to the pause rule. That rule makes the top of a repetition a direction change rather than a held squeeze. There is nothing to hold at the top of most movements. The pause here is not buying tension. It is buying a beat in which she cannot rush, which is a teaching device. Same digit, different job.

Watch for

Leaving it on. It is a phase, not a setting. It comes off when the pattern is back. When it does, the logged numbers will move, because the tempo was part of the context.

Terms on this page

tempo: The four-digit code that sets how many seconds each phase of a rep takes: lowering, pause, lifting, pause.

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.
