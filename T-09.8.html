Effort and intensity · T-09.8

Start a block well short of failure

Week one of a block sits several reps short of failure, on purpose. Effort climbs across the block and finishes where the block is hardest. Accuracy is earned across the block, not assumed on day one.

Two reasons, and the second is the one coaches forget. The obvious one is soreness. A first week taken close to failure on unfamiliar movements produces damage that costs her the following week. The one that matters more is that week one is when the logbook baselines are set. A baseline recorded from a set she misjudged is a baseline you program against for the rest of the block.

How it moves. Effort climbs across the block and starts below where it finishes. The phases owns the week-by-week numbers. This page only states the principle.

Why it works. It gives her something to calibrate against. A client who spends week one at three reps in reserve and week three at one has felt the difference between them. That is the only way the vocabulary ever becomes real to her. Starting at the top of the range teaches her nothing. She has no contrast.

Watch for

Treating the conservative first week as wasted. It is where her numbers come from and where her sense of the scale is built.
