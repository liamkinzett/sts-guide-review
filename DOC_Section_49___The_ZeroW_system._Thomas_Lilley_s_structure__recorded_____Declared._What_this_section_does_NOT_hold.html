The upper session, the accessories and the three rules

The strength system gives you the weekly pressing structure, the accessory dose, and three biomechanical rules. It does not give you a slot-by-slot upper session, so build the rest of that session with Building a session.

The upper body: what the system sets

# Two bench exposures a week

sub: A main bench, then a variation

What it sets: A competition bench press, and a second exposure that is normally a variation. Close-grip bench or incline bench, or a pressing variation like a chest press machine (exposure).

# The second day for bigger lifters

sub: Overhead press first, chest press machine second

What it sets: For most bigger lifters day two is a main bench, then an overhead press, then a chest press machine. That is the only ordering the system gives for the upper body.

# The competition lift once a week

sub: Two to three sets of squat, bench and deadlift per week, and that is it

What it sets: The competition lift itself is programmed once a week as a default (competition variation). Two sets a week sounds low to most coaches. It is meant to.

# Secondary lifts use tempo on the way up

sub: A slow lowering and a slow lift, focus on position

What it sets: The system rejects the common pattern of a slow lowering (eccentric) followed by an explosive lift (concentric). People lose position and butcher their technique when they explode up, or fall back on their strongest technique, which is the one being fixed. So the second-day variation is lifted slowly on purpose (tempo concentric), with the focus on position.

Do not build a full upper session from this

The system sets only the weekly pressing pattern and the second day's order. It does not say what follows the competition bench inside a session. It does not place back, rear delt, side delt or arm work. It gives no rest or warm-up for upper slots.

Fill those from Building a session. Do not invent an order for them.

The accessory dose

# Two working sets per accessory

sub: Dropping to one in the deload

What it sets: Most lifters get two sets of most accessories. In the deload week that becomes one. The 2-3 sets of the competition lifts, sometimes four or five, come down to one or two.

# One to two RIR is the standing figure

sub: Two to three RIR is deload only

What it sets: Accessories run at 1-2 RIR through the block (RIR). 2-3 RIR belongs to the deload week alone. Reading 2-3 RIR as the standing figure is an easy mistake.

# No accessory rep range

sub: The block waves belong to the competition lifts only

What it sets: Block periodisation in this system is only on the main lifts. The 8/6/6, 6/5/4 and 5/5/4/3 waves are competition-lift schemes and must not be read across to accessories. For accessory reps, use the STS strength-phase figure in Accessories in a strength phase.

The three biomechanical rules

# Rule one: where does the power come from

sub: Differs by lift

What it means: Each lift has its own power source, and the rule names it.

# Rule two: breathing and bracing

sub: The same across all three lifts

What it means: Rule two is about transferring that power into the bar (bracing). The spine sits between the power source and the bar, and it can move, bend and twist in every direction. Bracing makes it a rigid conduit instead of a leak. That is why this rule does not change between lifts the way rules one and three do.

# Rule three: connecting the dots at the end

sub: Differs by lift

What it means: How the lift finishes, and where the power connects at the top.

Terms on this page

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.

concentric: The lifting half of a rep, where the muscle shortens under load.

exposure: One session in the week where a lift, a pattern or a muscle is trained. Two exposures means it is trained on two different days.

competition variation: The exact lift as it is performed on the platform, with her competition stance, grip and bar position.

tempo concentric: Lifting the weight slowly and on purpose, rather than exploding it up, so she holds her position through the whole rep.

RIR: Reps in reserve. How many more reps she could have done when the set stopped. 2 RIR means she stopped two reps short of failure.

bracing: Filling the trunk with air and tensing the whole midsection so the spine stays rigid under load.
