Choosing between the rows and pulls

You have a back slot to fill and six exercises to fill it with. This page is the Back topic's exercise pages in the order a selection is made. Nothing here is new doctrine.

1 · Declare the bias, then name the job

Declare the bias before anything else: lats or upper back. A row run without one declared is not a neutral row, it is an accidental one. Choosing without declaring is the reason backs fail to grow.

Then name the job the slot is doing. Four different jobs live in this topic.

The four jobs a back slot can do

# Hypertrophy

sub: Building the lats or the upper back

Why: Most back slots are this. The bias you declared decides the setup on every page.

# Shoulder function

sub: The pull-up slot is really this

Why: The pull-up builds the shoulder's overhead position under load in an open chain. It is not primarily a size exercise. See Pull-up.

# Diagnosis

sub: She tells you she cannot grow her back

Why: The first response is to ask for video of her lat pulldowns. The rib cage, not the arm path, is usually what the video shows. See Lat pulldown.

# Systemic loading

sub: A heavy row for load on her skeleton

Why: This is the trap bar row and nothing else. It is never a hypertrophy row. See Free-weight row.

Last, name the limiter. Where the low back or the core ends a free-weight row before the back muscles reach failure, that is the chest-supported family's job. See Machine and chest-supported row.

2 · Read the bias off her body, not the machine

The bias is read off the elbow path and the shoulder blade, never off the machine's name. Tucked elbows with the shoulder blades held down and locked is a lat row. Wide elbows with the blades travelling their full range and a hard pinch at the finish is an upper-back row.

Two consequences follow. Every machine row STS coaches is upper-back biased. If the lat is the target she goes to a cable or a free-weight setup instead. And the barbell row is taught as a hinge-control movement with no bias attached. A barbell row prescribed for back hypertrophy needs a bias declared by whoever prescribes it.

Read the resistance with the equipment. Free-weight rows load the mid-range and the stretched bottom. Cables hold continuous tension with no dead spot. The machine and chest-supported rows peak at full retraction.

Two more reads. A client who cannot feel the lat is telling you her rib cage has flared, not that the lat is weak. Neck pain from rowing is a rib-cage problem before it is a neck one.

3 · Pick by job

The job, and what you take

# Lat, vertical

Take: The lat pulldown. Neutral grip, moderate width.

Also: The palms-away grip (pronated grip) is the upper-back option, not a lesser lat grip. It is a different exercise for a different target.

Exercises: Neutral grip lat pulldown; Supinated grip lat pulldown

# Lat, horizontal

Take: The seated cable row in its lat setup. Or the single arm dumbbell row.

Exercises: Neutral grip seated cable row; Single arm dumbbell row

# Upper back

Take: The machine and chest-supported family, which is coached upper-back biased throughout.

Exercises: Machine low row; Pronated grip chest supported row; Neutral grip chest supported row

# Shoulder function and a standard

Take: The pull-up, taken fresh.

Exercises: Pronated grip pull-up; Neutral grip pull-up

# The hinge itself

Take: The free-weight row. Its defining feature is the hinge she holds, not the pull.

Exercises: Pronated grip barbell bent over row; Supinated grip barbell bent over row

# A lat she cannot find, a deadlift gate, or a pre-exhaust

Take: The straight-arm pulldown. Its three jobs pull it in different directions, so name which one you are programming.

Exercises: Straight arm cable lat pulldown

# Heavy systemic loading once the family has run out of load

Take: The trap bar row. Never as a hypertrophy row.

Exercises: Trap bar bent over row

Then the handle rules. The close-grip V-bar is banned for back rows. Prefer two separate handles to one rigid bar. Single arm comes before both arms as a difficulty step, not as an injury swap. The bench-supported high-to-low row is for advanced lifters with poor development, never novices. Do not count it as a loadable row while it is being learned.

4 · What moves around the choice

  • The pull-up is the exception to grip-burning work going last. Take it fresh. Building a session owns the order.
  • Straps are allowed on the free-weight row family and refused on pull-ups.
  • Where the barbell row is the heavy row of the session, pick the grip her shoulder tolerates loaded and leave it there. Take grip variety on the cable and dumbbell rows instead.
  • Warn her the corrected rep will look smaller before it happens. Otherwise the fix reads as a step backwards.
  • Run the junk-volume audit. The working feel moving to her rear delts and biceps mid-session is the signal that the productive sets are over. Chest-supported rows are expensive, and Volume owns the count.

    • What does not change: her load (Effort and intensity), her tempo (Tempo and pauses), and the rest of the session's order (Building a session).

    5 · When to send it up

    A shoulder that will not tolerate loaded overhead work leaves the pull-up and takes the scaled ladder. First a single-arm cable pulldown at a scaled range, then the single arm cable high row, then arm isolation. Pain itself is Pain rules and when to refer. Anything about her being a woman is The female body.

    Four questions go to the head coach rather than being settled on the gym floor. Where the pull-up finishes, which bar, where the feet go, and why she is turning her palms up on a row (supinating). Coach the printed default and send the question up. On the palms-up row, say which reason you are following when you prescribe it. See The trap bar row and the supinated grip.

    The trap bar row and the dumbbell bent over row have a role but no setup, cue or fault list of their own. Coach them to the free-weight row's standards and do not write standards of your own.

    Terms on this page

    pronated grip: Palms facing down or away from her, knuckles up.

    supinating: Turning the forearm so the palm faces up. A supinated grip is palms up.
