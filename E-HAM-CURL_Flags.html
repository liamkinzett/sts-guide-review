Watch for

The one hard caution

Never let her lock the knees out fully under load on a seated or preacher-style setup. At full extension with the hip bent, the hamstring is stretched at both ends. Loading it there raises the risk of a tendon tear.

  • The thigh pad is a counterforce, not a marker. It gives her something to drive into, which locks the hips and isolates the hamstring. The lying curl has effectively no bracing, which is exactly why lifters lift the hips to buy leverage. A pad is sometimes a counterforce and sometimes only a reference point. On this movement it is the counterforce.
  • Recovery cost differs between the two machines. Both preserve muscle equally well. The lying curl leaves far less recovery debt, because it avoids the taxing stretch at the long end of the range. That is an input to selection, not a rule. Choosing exercises owns the decision.
  • The Nordic curl is a different animal. STS doesn't program it. It needs enough control of the lowering phase (eccentric) to resist rather than free-fall, plus a reliable anchor and clear landing space. Without both, the machine curl comes first.
  • Cramping in the hamstring during curls. You cannot see it on video. Ask after the set. See Faults and what you can actually see.
