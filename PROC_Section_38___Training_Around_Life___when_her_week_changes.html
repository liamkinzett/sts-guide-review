What to do when her week changes

Nothing here is new doctrine. It is this topic's rules plus Split design and Building a session, in the order you use them. The first question is which of four things happened. They have different answers and they look alike in a message from a client.

Step 1. Test. Sort it before you touch the program.

Carrying on, or coming back?

? Has she missed two consecutive weeks of sessions?

yes: Returning to training. This topic owns carrying on, not coming back.

? Was she ill?

yes: Her doctor decides she is well. Returning to training's illness page.

= Carry on. Sort what happened below.

What happenedWhich page owns it
The week compressed. Fewer days available, same intentProtect the compounds
A session was missed. The week was normal otherwiseA missed session is not a session to make up
The equipment changed. A different gym, a busy gym, travelA substitution problem, or a rationed station
A holiday weekA deload she did not plan

Step 2. Read. A compressed week is not a scaled-down version of the full week. It is a different selection. Protect the compounds; isolation is what a fuller week affords. At two days the prescription is compound work with almost no isolation, the same instruction Returning to training gives the very detrained client.

Volume is why this works. We program toward the least work that keeps her progressing (minimum effective dose), not the most she can recover from. A compressed week is that principle arriving involuntarily. A coach who has been programming that way has less to cut.

Step 3. Do.

Do, by what happened

# The week compressed

sub: Fewer days, same intent

Do this: Keep the compounds. Cut isolation first. Count patterns, not exercises, to check the week still covers her.

# A session was missed

sub: The week was normal otherwise

Do this: Nothing. It is not a session to make up. Zoom out to the month rather than the week.

# The equipment changed

sub: A different gym, a busy gym, travel

Do this: Open the exercise page and read down its substitution ladder. Pairing tells you which swaps are redundant. A rationed station: sequence it first, work in, then swap.

# A holiday week

sub: A week or more away

Do this: Decide deliberately, then say which you chose. If it lands where a deload was due, take it. If it does not, it is a missed week.

Step 4. Change. Do not rewrite the block because one week moved. Progression reads a stall per exercise across two exposures. Reading her training data puts a single missed session on the changes-nothing side of its table. One disrupted week is not data.

Split design owns what breaks a split and what to protect when it does. Reach for it before you redesign anything. Inside a session, the cut order is written into the program, so an early stop still banks the priority work.

Step 5. Escalate.

PresentationWhere it goes
Repeated compression. Her available days have genuinely changed rather than blippedSplit design re-evaluates the split against the goal. Assessing a new client re-checks that the pathway still fits the life she actually has
Adherence that keeps failingNot a scheduling problem. Adherence as a signal
A roster that changes weeklyA broken split every time it changes. This topic's shift-work page
A stressor underneath the weekTemporary or permanent. This topic's life-stressor pages
A week disrupted by how she feels rather than by her diaryTrack her symptoms, not the calendar. The female body
A bad session at the same hour every weekA fuelling question. Route it to the Nutrition Manual
A stretch long enough to be a layoffTwo consecutive weeks of missed sessions. Returning to training
