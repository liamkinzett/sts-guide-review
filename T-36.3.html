Reduce the visible left-right difference without knowing the cause

You do not need to know why one side is worse. You need to see that it is, and shrink the gap.

Lay three inputs over each other to find the difference (asymmetry):

  • What you see. One knee caves, one hip drops, one shoulder hikes.
  • What you measure. How far she gets, or how many clean reps she gets, on each side.
  • What she reports. Which side feels tight, weak or wrong.

    • Where all three point at the same side, you have a target.

    Start the set on her worse side. Set load, volume and frequency by what improves there, not on the good side. Left to herself she starts on the easier side every time, and then the easier side sets the dose.

    Why it works. It gives you something to act on that needs no diagnosis. You are not naming a condition or guessing a cause. An asymmetry you can see is a target. A cause you have guessed is not.

    This is the usual choice, not an absolute. Where the asymmetry is the sport, chasing symmetry is wrong. Nobody is symmetric. A difference becomes a problem only when symptoms arise from it.

    Terms on this page

    asymmetry: A left-right difference in how she moves, how far she gets or how it feels. Everyone has some. It matters when it is visible under load or when symptoms come from it.
