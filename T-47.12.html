What she will ask you · T-47.12

"Shouldn't my knees stay behind my toes?"

Knees past toes is not a hazard. Restricting it just moves the force somewhere else. Nothing is prevented. Load is relocated, from the knee to the spine.

The mechanism, from the myth's own origin. With less knee travel over the toes, there is less shear force at the knee. Shear is the force that tries to slide one bone across another at a joint. She hinges more, and the force moves to the hips. The same finding shows there is then more shear force through the spine. So the force has moved from the knee to the spine.

That is the client-ready rebuttal. And when you squat, you probably want your knees to go more over your toes, not less.

The capacity the myth forbids is a checkpoint a full squat has to pass. Can she move vertically, control her pelvis, get to a neutral pelvic position, and get adequate knees over toes? Its limiter is ankle mobility, not danger.

Knee position is a dial coaches turn on purpose, in both directions. More knee travel lengthens the moment arm (moment arm) at the knee and biases the quads. Less biases the hinge and the glutes. Levers and moment arms owns the mechanism.

Across the range

Where the discourse comes from. The knees-over-toes brand itself programs knees-behind-toes work. Walking backwards and sled drags push the knee behind the toe. Neither is knees over toes.

The loading caveat. A heavy loaded knee extension exercise pushes her centre of mass forward. Knee travel under load is a dosage question, not a hazard.

Say it like this

Keeping your knees behind your toes doesn't remove the force. It moves it off your knee and onto your lower back. Your knees are built to travel forward. We just load that sensibly.

Watch for

Coaching knees back to be safe. You have loaded her spine to protect a knee that did not need protecting.
