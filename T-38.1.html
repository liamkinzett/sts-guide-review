When the week compresses, protect the compounds

When the week compresses, protect the compounds. Isolation is what a fuller week affords.

A client with two days a week gets compound work with minimal isolation. The same prescription covers the very detrained client in Returning to training. A compound movement (compound) trains several joints and muscles at once. An isolation movement (isolation) trains one.

So a compressed week is not a scaled-down version of the full week. It is a different selection. Do not take every session and shrink it. Keep the compounds and drop the rest.

Why it works. We program toward the least work that keeps her progressing (minimum effective dose), never toward the most she can recover from. Volume explains that rule. A compressed week is the same principle arriving involuntarily. A coach who has been programming toward a minimum has less to cut.

How to check the week still covers her. Split design counts movement patterns (pattern), not exercises: a squat, a hinge, a press, a pull. If the compressed week still lands each pattern she needs, the week still covers her.

Watch for

Cutting the compounds because they are the hardest sessions to fit around a life. They are the ones worth the fitting.

Say it like this

This week we're keeping the two big sessions and dropping the rest. That's not a compromise, it's the right call for a week like this.

Terms on this page

compound: An exercise that moves several joints and loads several muscles at once, like a squat, a deadlift, a press or a row.

isolation: An exercise that works one muscle across one joint, like a leg extension or a cable curl.

minimum effective dose: The least amount of training that keeps her progressing. STS programs toward it, not toward the most she can recover from.

pattern: A family of movements that load the body the same way, like squats, hinges, presses and pulls. Frequency is counted per pattern, not per exercise.
