Body image and the referral line · T-43.7

Do not take control away

Do not take control away. Control is the mechanism an eating disorder runs on, and removing it drives concealment. Every tool in this topic can be delivered as confiscation. None of them may be.

Eating disorders thrive off a sense of control. Taking that control away, not letting her prepare her own meals, makes it worse and creates mistrust with everyone around her. That is one woman's account of her own recovery, and it governs how you deliver everything here.

What confiscation looks like from a coach.

The same tool, delivered two ways

# The scale

sub: Banning it by instruction is confiscation

Instead: Re-tether her to different data and leave the scale in her hands. The next page.

# Her food

sub: Taking it over is confiscation

Instead: You do not take over her food at all. Where fear and rigidity are present, you refer.

# A new rule

sub: A rule she did not agree to is confiscation

Instead: Options, the trade-off, your recommendation, her call. The coaching relationship holds that order.

Why it works. A client who feels controlled hides. What she hides, you cannot see, and what you cannot see, you cannot refer. Keeping her in control of her own decisions is what keeps the behaviour visible.

Watch for

Rules you set for her own good. Delivered as instruction, they read as control. Offer them as choices.
