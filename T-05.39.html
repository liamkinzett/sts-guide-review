Training a client pregnant with twins

An uncomplicated twin pregnancy is not an absolute contraindication. It becomes one the moment her obstetric team flags her as at risk of premature labour, at any week.

Two different triggers. Twins on their own make a relative contraindication (relative contraindication) only after the 28th week. Nothing is restricted before that for twins specifically. Being flagged at risk of premature labour makes an absolute contraindication, regardless of trimester. That trigger is clinical risk, not a week. STS runs both triggers.

What you do

Ask her obstetric team at every check-in whether she has been flagged as at risk of premature labour. Not only once she reaches 28 weeks.

Flagged? Treat it as the absolute contraindication on the stop-the-session page, whatever week she is at.

Not flagged and under 28 weeks? Standard prenatal coaching applies.

After 28 weeks Treat it as a relative contraindication. Have the risk-benefit conversation, with her obstetric team part of it. That is the same standing a complicated pregnancy already has.

What arrives earlier and harder with twins. The uterus is heavier. Lying on her back (supine) needs to come out sooner than the symptom-led trigger for a single pregnancy. Joint laxity driven by the pregnancy hormone (relaxin) means asymmetrical or wide-stance loading should reduce sooner. Single-leg deadlifts and wide-stance plié squats are the examples. Favour glute and hamstring work for pelvic stability instead. Every stop sign carries higher stakes, because her baseline preterm-labour risk is already elevated.

Verdict

Flagged as at risk of premature labour: training stops and care is obstetric, at any week.

Ask at every check-in. Do not wait for 28 weeks.

Watch for

Looking for twin-specific numbers. No twin-specific load percentages, rep ranges or weight caps exist. Everything here is the single-pregnancy model with earlier, harder triggers.

Terms on this page

relative contraindication: A condition where training is not ruled out, but the risks and benefits have to be weighed with her medical team before it continues.

supine: Lying flat on her back.

relaxin: A hormone of pregnancy that loosens ligaments and joints. It stays elevated after birth and for longer while she breastfeeds.
