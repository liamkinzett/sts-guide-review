Heel elevation, anterior load and stance: the setup levers

Heel elevation, a load held in front and stance width are not variations, but they change every squat. Use each one as a test before you use it as a fix.

Constraint as test. Give her the heel wedge. Give her the load in front. Narrow the stance. If the fault disappears, you have found what it was. If she still squats like a hinge with the wedge under her heels, that is the finding too.

Three levers, and what each one tests

# Heel elevation

sub: A wedge or plates under her heels

What it tests: Re-run her squat with the heels raised. If depth appears, it was never ankle range. It is control of the point her weight balances around (centre of mass). The test tells you which of two problems you have, and nothing about tissue.

The dose: A fractional plate. A 2.5 kg plate gives a very slight slope. Minimum effective dose, with removal planned.

Watch for: Too much heel elevation produces the opposite fault: pointing forward and falling forward, hips shooting up, the squat good-morninged up. Reduce it. Heeled shoes that allow a more upright torso and more knee travel while masking a weak brace (bracing) move the pain somewhere else later.

# A load held in front

sub: Goblet, front or Zercher

What it tests: Whether staying upright is her problem. Moving the load forward makes staying upright easier.

For the wink: Heel elevation, plus a load in front, plus a slow heavy tempo. All three, before any cue.

# Stance

sub: Narrower as a test, wider as a lever

What it tests: Narrow the stance as a constraint. If the fault disappears, that was the fault.

Wider: A wider stance lets the hips move forward so she can sit up. That shortens the leverage on her back (moment arm) and cuts low-back strain in a forward-leaning squat.

Her own stance: Stance is individual and never copied. Bone length, hip structure and the muscle around the hip decide it. The standard is a stance that is comfortable and lets her keep her pelvis level, neither tucked nor tipped (neutral pelvis), at the bottom. Toes turn very slightly out, roughly 20 degrees, at shoulder width.

If she asks whether the wedge is cheating

Elevating your heels isn't cheating. It makes your centre of mass easier to manage while you learn the position.

Do not tell her the wedge lengthened her calf. It did not. It moved her weight.

What is gated and what remains. If she cannot reach a deep heels-down squat position, that squat variation is gated. Heel elevation, a different stance width or a different bar position all remain available.

The constraint is warm-up, not furniture. The wedge or the front load that worked in the test becomes her warm-up, scheduled for removal rather than kept forever. And do not buy depth by dropping load.

Terms on this page

neutral pelvis: The pelvis level and still, neither tucked under nor tipped forward, so the lower back keeps its natural curve.

bracing: Filling the trunk with air and tensing the whole midsection so the spine stays rigid under load.

centre of mass: The single point her bodyweight and the load balance around. Where it sits decides how upright she can stay.

moment arm: The perpendicular distance between the joint axis and the line of force.

Zercher: A squat with the bar held in the crooks of her elbows, in front of her body.
