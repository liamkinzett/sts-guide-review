A stall on a round number gets one extra test

A stall at her own bodyweight on the bar is a stall like any other until proven otherwise. The trigger still applies: two flat exposures on that lift is when you go and look. Then run one extra test.

The extra test is the one this topic already trusts over her own report. The list of causes has no row for "believes the number is a wall". That is not a hole in the list. A belief about a number and a genuine ceiling look identical in her report and different on tape. Run the rest test and the video audit at that exact weight before reading anything else. Effort and intensity has both.

Check

Does the bar move at the same speed as a set she is not afraid of?

Does she comfortably repeat the set after sixty seconds of rest?

yes: The ceiling is not physical. Do not reach for a lever. Keep the corrected load moving cleanly and let repeated clean sets do the work.

no: Read it as an ordinary stall. Work the causes list and the stall order.

Where the finding is confidence rather than capacity, the program is not the fix. Adherence as a signal covers training confidence for a new lifter avoiding a movement in front of others. A trained client stalling on a specific number is a different shape of the same problem. What she needs is clean, repeated exposure to the weight, not a new lever.

Watch for

Concluding "it's in her head" from a hunch rather than from the video and the rest test. The hunch is exactly the kind of unverified read the effort check exists to replace.
