Chest · E-CHE-MACHPRESS#Role

What it's for

The machine and cable chest press is the braced horizontal press. The machine removes the stabilising tax. Sitting flush against a back pad means her nervous system is not spending output on holding the torso together (externally stabilised). Maximum force goes into the pecs instead.

Machines and free weights do different jobs. Neither ranks above the other. The barbell gives the loading ceiling. The braced machine gives more stimulus for the fatigue it costs. It lets an advanced lifter reach genuine failure with minimal risk of the joint breaking down. Highly braced converging machines are the preferred pattern for that job. The machine is also the first rung of the pressing ladder for a cranky shoulder.

The grip is the variation, and what it buys is an arm path. A palms-in grip (neutral grip) produces the tucked, arms-close path by itself. A palms-forward grip (pronated grip) does not, so on a palms-forward machine the tuck has to be coached. The rule is blunt: a flared, arms-wide path loads structure, and a tucked, arms-close path loads muscle. The elbow angle follows the chest standard either way, roughly 45 degrees and never 90.

The four STS variations

# Machine chest press, neutral grip

sub: The machine delivers the tucked path

Use it for: Where the tucked path is the goal and you would rather the machine deliver it than the cue. Also the rung to reach for where a palms-forward press provokes the shoulder. The palms-in position stops the upper arm sitting turned inward (internal rotation) and opens the chest. Region by region governs the wrist at the bottom of any press.

# Machine chest press, pronated grip

sub: The same job with the tuck cued rather than built in

Use it for: Where the machine's neutral setting does not fit her. Also where the session already holds a palms-in pressing movement.

# Machine incline chest press

sub: The braced press with the angle set by the manufacturer

Use it for: The output movement of a push day, behind a skill movement and ahead of isolation. Also the lever where a flat machine press stalls: change the angle inside the machine family rather than change the pattern. Choosing exercises covers changing for a reason. Everything in Setup and How to do it applies unchanged.

# Cable chest press

sub: Seated, against a back pad. Never standing

Use it for: Two jobs the machine press is worse at. It is the low-fatigue second pressing touch in a week. Use it to bring up a lagging chest, or as a swap late in a fat-loss phase. Loaded light, it is also a feel-the-position movement early in a session (proprioception), ahead of the heavy work. The machine press is the volume movement later. Building a session owns the order.

Setup rule: Sit against a back pad. STS rejects the standing unbraced version outright. Standing, balance becomes the limiting factor instead of the pec and the load drops. Add the pad and the load goes up. Do not arch on it either. Back flat to the pad adds points of contact and stacks the rib cage without her having to think about it.

Grip redundancy is a real selection fault. Grip position sets up shoulder position, even where the grip has nothing to do with the target muscle. A push day running a palms-forward machine press, a palms-forward overhead press and a palms-forward dip has one shoulder position three times. It matters most for the intermediate-to-advanced client, which is who we coach.

The resistance is continuous, and the machine holds it. The top is kept short of lockout so the pec is never unloaded. That makes this the press that holds tension through the mid range without dumping it at the squeezed end.

Terms on this page

externally stabilised: The machine, bench or support holds her position so she does not have to.

neutral grip: Palms facing each other, or facing in toward her body. On a chest press it produces the tucked arm path by itself.

pronated grip: Palms facing down or away from her, knuckles up. On a chest press the tuck has to be coached.

proprioception: Her sense of where her body is and what position it is in, without looking.

internal rotation: Turning a limb inward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face back or down, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more toward the middle. An arm held that way is internally rotated.
