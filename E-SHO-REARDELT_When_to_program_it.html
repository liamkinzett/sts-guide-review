When to use it

The problem you notice. She tells you she cannot feel her rear delts. Or her shoulder is provoked and you are cutting the session back and need something that stays in. Or the rear delt is one of her neglected groups and it keeps losing its slot. The rear delt fly answers all three, and it doubles as a warm-up that is a real exercise.

Reach for it when

# She says she cannot feel her rear delts

sub: The muscle is not being loaded, not the wrong cue

Confirm it: Watch the shoulder blade. If it pulls back with the arm (retraction), origin and insertion travel together and nothing loads.

What it gets her: The muscle actually loaded. Hold the shoulder blade slightly forward (protraction), drive away, and slow the lift (concentric). "Rear delts are incredibly hard for people to connect with. If you slow down, you're going to really feel that rear delt actually doing what it's supposed to do as you drive away".

# Her shoulder is provoked and you are cutting the session back

sub: Something that stays in

Confirm it: The press is what hurts. The fly does not.

What it gets her: In the worked case the overhead press came out of the day. The single-arm rear delt fly was kept unchanged alongside the rest of it. That is the 80/20 rule executed, and the question it answers is "what can I still do?"

# You need to confirm she can hold core and shoulder position before anything more dynamic

sub: A warm-up that is an exercise rather than a drill

Confirm it: She is about to press or row and you have not yet seen her hold a stacked rib cage under load.

What it gets her: The fly is the entry point for upper-body rib-cage work with the ribs held still. Keep it to the shoulder blade sliding forward and back, protraction and retraction, until she owns it.

# She rows and pulls down every week and you wonder whether that already counts

sub: It does not, by the volume convention STS programs from

Confirm it: The convention credits pulling movements to the lats and mid back, and the elbow-flexor share separately. It credits nothing to the rear delt.

What it gets her: A dedicated slot, if she needs one. But watch the footage before you conclude that. A row done with the shoulder blades doing the retracting and the rear delt under tension is one set. The same row done as an arm-pull, blades along for the ride, is a different set. Only the first is even a candidate for crediting the muscle, and the convention does not credit it even then. Choosing exercises and Volume hold the rule.

# The rear delt is one of her stubborn or neglected groups

sub: It keeps losing its slot

Confirm it: It is never in the program, or it is always last and always cut.

What it gets her: Trained without spending a headline slot on it. Rear delts sit with the "odds and ends", forearms, calves, traps and abs, the class of lagging groups you weave into a session. Not during maximal-strength work: that is the one place the method is not used.

Prefer a cable over the reverse pec deck when you do reach for it

Rear delt fibre direction changes with where the upper arm sits. The cable lets you match the line of pull to her. A machine fixes one angle for everyone.

Keep the nuance in proportion

The fine cable heights and fibre alignment are for advanced physique athletes. A general fitness client needs only to pull back and work the muscle.

STS doesn't program the lying cable face pull, so nothing on this page is written for it.

Setup. Pulleys close together, grab the cable ends directly, a high-to-low line of pull. Setup has the full list.

Terms on this page

retraction: The shoulder blade sliding back toward the spine, so the two blades pinch together.

protraction: The shoulder blade sliding forward and out around the rib cage, away from the spine.

concentric: The lifting half of a rep, where the muscle shortens under load.
