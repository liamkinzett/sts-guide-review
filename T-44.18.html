Motivation and adherence · T-44.18

Praise the behaviour and state the link. A compliment is easy to deflect

Praise that names her as a person is easy to deflect. Praise that names the behaviour and states the causal link is not. For a client who waves compliments away, turn the personal compliment down and keep the behaviour-linked version.

Praise that cannot be waved off

This is because we changed X, Y and Z. This is because you're eating more protein.

Link the two things together, because she does not inherently have that link. Reinforcement works because the win is tied to something she did, not to a compliment about who she is. That version is data rather than flattery, and data is harder to deflect.

Volume is a dial, not a script. The same praise lands differently by personality. A self-starter needs little of it. Another client is genuinely carried by it. Calibrate how much rather than forcing it.

Compared

columns: Names her | Names what she did

The words: "You're a machine." | "Your squat moved because you hit every session this block."

What she can do with it: Shrug it off | Nothing to argue with. It happened.

Turn it down for: A client who deflects | Nobody. Everyone gets this one.

Writing to a client makes the same point about good-week feedback: it names what she did. This page is what to do when the compliment itself is the thing she cannot accept.

Watch for

Pushing the compliment harder when she deflects. Drop it. Give her the behaviour and the result instead.
