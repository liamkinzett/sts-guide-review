Positions that go against the grain

Several of the system's positions run against common coaching practice. Know them, because a client or another coach will challenge them. Where a position is specific to a competitive powerlifter, the general-client default sits beside it.

The contrarian positions

# How often she peaks

sub: Each peak costs weeks of building

Competitive powerlifter: Peak only for the meets that matter. Peaking builds nothing long term, and three peaks a year spend about eighteen weeks not adapting.

General client: A strength phase ends in a peak and a test, capped at two realisation phases a year. The phases has the reasoning.

# Accessories stay in during a peak

sub: Muscle loss starts quickly

Competitive powerlifter: Accessories stay in the peak, heavy, at one to two sets, because muscle loss starts quickly when a muscle stops being trained (catabolism).

General client: Accessories stay in but come down a set in the last four weeks. See Accessories in a strength phase.

# Strength is an expression, not an adaptation

sub: The gym builds the engine; the platform only shows it

Position: A coach can control three adaptations: coordination and skill, muscle growth, and how much of the muscle the nervous system switches on (motor-unit recruitment).

# One gold standard of technique

sub: Same human animal, same joints

Position: There are no per-lifter technical styles. Hand placement, foot placement and grip width are the only real individual variations.

# Teach effort by going to failure once

sub: A supervised leg-press set to absolute failure

Position: One supervised leg-press set as many reps as possible (AMRAP) to absolute failure, so she learns what nought RIR is. What she was calling an eight was a four.

Check: Run it on a leg press, where Effort and intensity allows a set to true failure.

# A beginner and the barbell

sub: The three lifts from day one, or priming first

Competitive powerlifter: A beginner squats, benches and deadlifts from her first session, ten rounds of two reps with a light rubber plate (bumper plate).

General client: Priming is externally stabilised by default. A barbell on a novice's back puts her nervous system into a vigilant state. The barbell progression runs inside priming only when strength is her goal. See The phases.

Terms on this page

bumper plate: A rubber-coated weight plate. The light ones let a beginner practise the three lifts with almost nothing on the bar.

catabolism: Muscle being broken down faster than it is built. It starts quickly when a muscle stops being trained.

motor-unit recruitment: How many of a muscle's fibres the nervous system switches on at once. More recruitment means more force from the same muscle.

AMRAP: As many reps as possible. One set taken all the way to the point where the next rep will not move.
