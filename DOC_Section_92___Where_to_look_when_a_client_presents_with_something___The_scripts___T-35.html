Pain rules and when to refer: what to say

Words for her on pain rules and when to refer, by situation.

A script is only right inside its rule's conditions. Read the rule in Pain rules and when to refer before you use the words.

Red flags for cauda equina syndrome: bladder or bowel changes, saddle numbness. This does not wait for an appointment.

Say it like this

I need you to get that looked at today, at an emergency department rather than a GP appointment. It's probably nothing, and the one thing it might be is time-sensitive, so I'd rather you went and it was nothing.

Verdict

Emergency department today. This does not wait for an appointment.

Say it plainly and make sure she goes.

The middle state has a method. Sort every movement into three buckets.

Say it like this

We're going to find everything that doesn't hurt, and that's your program for now. Not a lighter version of your program. Your actual program. Then we bring things back in the order you can tolerate them.

The first question is never how bad. It is which of two things it is.

Say it like this

Is this the kind of sore that feels like the set is working, or the kind that feels like a warning? Don't rate it out of ten yet. Just tell me which one it feels like.

She is afraid of the movement. Pain is a learned response and the position is often fine now.

Say it like this

I want you to bounce this ball while you do it. Don't think about the knee. Just keep the ball going.

Medication is not yours. Not the dose, not the timing, not what training does around it.

Say it like this

That one's for whoever prescribed it, not me. What I can do is tell you exactly what I've seen in your sessions and when, so you've got something concrete to take to them. And we keep training in the meantime.

She asks you to soften what you tell her physio so she is cleared faster.

Say it like this

I can't shade what I tell your physio, because the whole reason my read is worth anything is that it's accurate. If I soften it to get you cleared faster, I've just made it worthless. And worse, I've made it wrong at the exact moment it matters most.

Getting sick repeatedly inside one block is training-relevant. Screen recovery and energy availability before calling it bad luck.

Say it like this

Getting sick this often while training hard isn't nothing. Before I chalk it up to bad luck, I want to go through the usual checklist. Training, technique, recovery, volume, the same way I would for a lift that's stopped moving.

The threshold for an ambulance has been reached.

Say it like this

I'm calling an ambulance now.

Verdict

Call the ambulance yourself. Say "I'm calling an ambulance now."

Never ask "do you think we should call someone."

Watch for

The question invites her to talk you out of it. A client in this position very often will, because embarrassment arrives faster than symptoms do.

Red flags

title: Call triple zero (000) if she has any of these

Loss of consciousness (blackout or fainting), even if brief

Chest pain

Sudden weakness in the face, arm or leg, or trouble speaking

Breathing problems

A sudden collapse or unexplained fall

A seizure

Uncontrollable bleeding

Any other major concern about her health or wellbeing

Chest pain that is severe, getting worse or has lasted longer than 10 minutes

Chest pain that feels heavy, crushing or tight

Chest pain with breathlessness, nausea, dizziness or a cold sweat

Chest pain with pain in the jaw or down the left arm

Palpitations (a racing heartbeat)

emergency: While you wait: stop all activities and rest, loosen any tight clothing, nothing to eat or drink. You are reporting what you see and hear, not ruling anything out.

She describes chronic fatigue or post-exertional malaise. Nothing here is programmed around it.

Say it like this

What you're describing doesn't match anything I can safely program around. I want you seen by someone who treats this specifically before we plan anything.

She has taken a knock to the head.

Say it like this

That's you done today. I need you seen before you lift again.

Verdict

The session is over. She is seen before she lifts again.

Nine critical signs send her to an emergency department. You never clear her yourself.

Watch for

Do not negotiate it. And do not let her drive herself anywhere if any critical sign is present.
