How long does the 'neural phase' last?

Never put a number on how long early nervous-system progress lasts. Coaches call that stretch the neural phase (neural phase). It has no fixed length.

You will hear 8 to 12 weeks, 13 weeks, 13 or 16 weeks, and 14 to 16 weeks. STS uses none of them, and neither do you.

How long it takes depends on several things:

  • training age;
  • the exact task being practised;
  • how unfamiliar the movement is;
  • a lifter's starting coordination;
  • the quality of the practice;
  • and the way the program is written.

    • The mechanism is real. The timetable is not fixed.

    Why. A number implies a date on which something changes. A coach who has that date will blame a stall on it instead of actually diagnosing why a lift stalled.

    Say it like this

    Your early progress comes from your nervous system learning the lifts. It's fast, and it won't last forever. I'm not going to give you a date, because there isn't one.

    Watch for

    "She's out of her newbie gains" as the explanation for a plateau. Progression owns reading a stall, and it does not run on a calendar.

    Terms on this page

    neural phase: The early stretch of training where most of the early strength gain comes from the nervous system learning the lift, before the muscle has grown much. It has no fixed length.
