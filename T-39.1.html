Reading her training data · T-39.1

Diagnose before you change anything. The order is already written

Before you change anything, work the diagnosis list, in order. Volume owns it, and volume is fourth on it.

The diagnosis order

Is she training hard enough? The most common answer and the one most often skipped. Less experienced lifters believe they are closer to failure than they are.

Has technique drifted? Range shortens under load before anything else does. A rep that got easier because it got smaller is not progress.

Is she recovering? Sleep, stress and food. A program is not under-dosed if she is under-slept. A stall in a lean client who trains hard, moves well and sleeps is a reason to ask about fuelling.

Then volume. Never before step three is clear. Adding sets to an under-fuelled client is the worst-timed move on the list.

The first question is whether she is training hard enough. Ask it first, every time. Effort and intensity has the ways to check it without asking her.

Why it works. Almost every adjustment a coach makes is a guess dressed as a response. Three of the four items on the list are free to fix. Volume is the only one that costs her recovery, which is why it comes last. Progression says the same thing from the other side: before you change anything, diagnose.

Watch for

Changing two things at once. You will not know which one worked. Half the time neither did.
