What she will ask you · T-47.21

"Don't high reps tone and low reps bulk?"

Neither. The premise is wrong, and the correction runs the opposite way to what she expects. This is one of the two or three questions a female client asks most.

Kill the premise first. The growth signal is proximity to failure, not a rep zone. As a set gets harder, more muscle fibres are called into the work, in groups that each answer to one nerve (motor units). The stimulus for their growth is always how close the set gets to failure. There is no toning zone and no bulking zone. Both rep ranges grow muscle, to the extent that the set approaches failure. Effort and intensity owns that mechanism.

Then the part she will not expect. Very low reps add tissue more slowly than a moderate range. Compare a one to three rep range against five to eight, or ten to twelve, both taken close to failure. The lifter doing singles and triples stops there. She never grinds out a fourth rep, because the style of lifting makes it impossible. Her exposure to the thing that drives growth is therefore slow. The moderate range accumulates that exposure inside every set.

Compared

columns: One to three reps | Five to eight, or ten to twelve reps

What she thinks it does: Bulks | Tones

What it actually does: Builds strength and skill. Adds tissue slowly. | Builds muscle efficiently, taken close to failure

Why: Exposure to near-failure work is brief | Exposure accumulates inside every set

What this means at the rack. Her heavy sets are not the bulking option. If anything they are the slower route to size. They are in her program for the reasons Strength and skill gives, not for tissue. The moderate work she thinks of as toning is the more efficient growth stimulus of the two.

Do not deliver that as a gotcha. It is a correction to the premise, not to her.

No rep count changes anything. Nothing here states a number at which a set stops toning and starts bulking, because no such number exists. Do not supply one.

Where the fear underneath is the real question. The thick-waist page takes over on aesthetics. Body image and the referral line takes over if the language has darkened. Muscle and growth's "the same program does not produce the same result" is the pre-frame that stops the question arising in week twelve.

Watch for

Programming her heavy work lighter to reassure her. The heavy sets were never the bulking risk. Keep them for what they are for.

Terms on this page

motor units: One nerve cell and the bundle of muscle fibres it switches on together. A muscle is made of many of them, from small to large. The harder a set gets, the more of them the body calls in.
