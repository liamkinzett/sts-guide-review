What it's for

The straight-arm pulldown is single-joint shoulder extension through a long arc with the elbow held still. It biases the lat the whole way. The shoulder blade is not being trained to pinch back here, and nothing about it is coached with a squeeze at the finish.

It has three distinct jobs, and they pull the exercise in different directions.

Three jobs

# A builder

sub: Long-arc shoulder extension loading the lat

Why: The fixed elbow takes the biceps out of the equation, so the lat does the work.

# A lat warm-up and mobility tool

sub: Programmed for an upper back (thoracic spine) that will not open, as well as for lat work

Why: The load instruction attached to this use is lighter than normal. It opens the upper back and the shoulder under a light load.

# A gate

sub: The named prerequisite for barbell deadlifting

Why: A cable pullover is a hinge-like position where the line of pull helps rather than fights. The arc is large and the lat is easy to find. If she cannot find it here she will not find it under a palms-away grip on a bar drifting in front of the shins.

Which position it loads depends on the equipment, and that is the core selection decision. On a cable, tension is continuous across the whole arc. On a dumbbell, resistance peaks with the arms out behind the head in the fully stretched position (lengthened). It falls to nearly nothing at the top of the arc. STS programs the cable version.

Where it sits in the session. It is a pre-exhaustion tool placed before a rhomboid-focused row (pre-exhaustion). Traps and lats otherwise overshadow the rhomboids. That is the mechanism; Building a session owns the order.

Terms on this page

lengthened: The point in the rep where the working muscle is at its longest while it carries the load. It describes the muscle's length, not whether she feels a stretch.

pre-exhaustion: Tiring one muscle with an isolation exercise first, so that it is the limiter in the compound lift that follows.

thoracic spine: The upper and middle back, the part of the spine the ribs attach to. It is the part of the spine that has to extend for the arms to get overhead cleanly.
