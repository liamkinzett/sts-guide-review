Glutes · E-GLU-LUNGE#When to program it

When to use it

The problem you notice. Her back leg never gets behind her, so she hinges over as she lunges. Or every lower-body day is on a fixed-plane machine. Or you need to see what she can actually coordinate. The lunge answers each one, and it is a loaded exercise, not a remedial one.

Reach for it when

# Her back leg never gets behind her

sub: She hinges over as she lunges, or dumps her weight forward into the front leg

Why it happens: True hip extension only happens when the knee gets behind the hip. She has never loaded that position, so her body finds range by folding forward instead.

Do this: A stationary lunge, loaded. Drills and mobility work are where most coaches stop. The loaded lunge is the step they never reach, and it is the one that teaches the position. The reverse lunge and the FFE split squat load the same back-leg job.

What it gets her: True hip extension (hip extension), under load.

# Every lower-body day is fixed-plane

sub: A Smith machine or FFE split squat is carrying the single-leg slot

Why it happens: A fixed plane gives up side-to-side hip stability (frontal plane). Without it the muscle on the outside of her hip (TFL) tightens up and she drifts posterior-dominant.

Do this: A walking or stationary lunge with an opposite-hand load (contralateral load), on another day, specifically to restore frontal-plane hip function.

# You need to see what she can actually coordinate

sub: Two-legged loading keeps hiding it

Why it happens: Under a bar the strong side and the trunk cover for everything. The walking lunge is probably the most complex movement you will take her through, yet almost anyone can attempt it.

Do this: A dumbbell walking lunge, as an assessment. It reports her gait mechanics and her weight transfer side-to-side and in rotation. It shows whether her rib cage stays over her pelvis, and whether she can decelerate at all. You get data instead of a failed test.

# She loses a month two or three times a year to a cranky knee or hip

sub: Avoidable, and it keeps happening

Do this: A stationary lunge variation on every lower-body day is a good general rule. It keeps the hip working. In STS programming that is the reverse lunge, or the FFE split squat, which loads the same back-leg hip extension. A step-down is the bonus.

The walking lunge is less loadable, and that is the reason to pick it, not the price of picking it. Less loadable does not mean less important. If output is what you are after, go to a stationary lunge, the Smith machine or a single-leg press instead.

The presentation that means stop, not coach

Wobbling, frantic corrective steps, calves and lower back recruited just to stay upright. She is currently weaker than gravity.

Regress to stable machine leg pressing until her relative strength improves, then bring the lunge back.

Coaching cues will not fix a strength deficit. Don't keep cueing.

Terms on this page

hip extension: Opening the hip so the thigh travels behind the body line. It is the glute's main job, and true hip extension only happens when her knee gets behind her hip.

frontal plane: Side-to-side movement, like a lateral lunge or a side raise.

TFL: The tensor fasciae latae, a small muscle at the front and side of the hip that runs into the band down the outer thigh. When the glutes stop controlling the hip sideways it takes over and tightens up.

contralateral load: The weight is held in the hand opposite the working leg.
