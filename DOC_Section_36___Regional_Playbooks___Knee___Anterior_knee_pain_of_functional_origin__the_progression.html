Front-of-knee pain, cleared: the seven-rung progression

For a client whose front-of-knee pain has been assessed and cleared, this is the order you rebuild in. It is a starting point, not a protocol. You pick up wherever she can load without provoking.

Verdict

Whether her knee pain is functional or structural is not yours to decide.

Sorting the origin of pain is grading. If she has not been assessed, the pain rules own her before this list does.

The principle. External support first, then restore function through isolated ranges, then isolate the knee's own mechanics, then reintegrate into walking.

The seven rungs, in order

Seated hip adduction machine, pulling the thighs in toward the midline (adduction), for the rotational vector. STS does not program the hip abduction machine, which pushes the thighs out to the side (abduction), so the adduction machine does this rung.

45 degree hip extension, isolating lower-back and pelvic stability so the glute works as the primary hip extensor.

Hinge, at whatever level of one-leggedness her symptoms allow: two legs, staggered, B-stance, one leg.

Single-leg kneeling hamstring curl. One of the best tools available. It limits load and forces control of the lower back and pelvis.

Isometric lunge, a lunge held still at the bottom (isometric), dialled with front-foot elevation, a goblet load, or no load.

Endpoint: loading the rear leg's quad at length, with a load held in front and pelvic stability held. This is what trains tendon stiffness.

Then full-range lifting work, then change of direction.

The progress marker here is not load. She will shake like a leaf. The marker is the shaking stopping at the same load. Pain rules and when to refer owns progress markers.

Terms on this page

B-stance: A split stance where the back foot sits behind on its toe as a kickstand and the front leg does the work.

adduction: A limb travelling in toward the midline of the body: the leg pulled in by the inner-thigh muscles, or the arm brought in to the side.

abduction: A limb travelling out and away from the midline of the body: the arm in a lateral raise, or the thigh moving out to the side.

isometric: A hold. The muscle works against the load but the joint does not move.
