Setup

Nine points, in order

Jump stance. Stand as she would to jump for a basketball dunk (jump stance). Naturally quite close, and not wide. Toes very slightly out, not too much. Knees track the same direction as the toes.

Bar in line with the midfoot. That puts it over her centre of mass. On a conventional pull the shins are about an inch from the bar.

Shoulders just in front of the bar, over the midfoot. Not behind it, not way out over it. Find the sweet spot that is just right.

Grip outside the legs, arms straight down. The least distance travelled is the most efficient. Leave enough room to very slightly push her knees out against the inside of her locked elbows. Do not exaggerate it.

Her proportions override point 4. For a lifter with narrower shoulders and wider hips, which is common in women, leg stance and hips take priority over grip width. Forcing the arms straight down blocks leg drive and puts the legs in a worse position. This applies to most STS clients.

"Bum down" enough to include leg drive, and no further. Too deep is a fault; see Common faults.

Lats on before the pull. Pull the shoulder blades down towards the back pockets. *Down, not back and down.* Said three ways: "pull your armpits towards your pelvis", "pull your scaps into your back pockets", "shoulder blades down". Why: of all the core muscles, the lat covers the longest span of the spine. It also attaches to the pelvis through the sheet of connective tissue across the lower back (thoracolumbar fascia). Lats on is spinal protection as well as bar path.

"Crown of the head to the ceiling." This corrects posture up-and-down rather than back-and-forward. It extends the upper back (thoracic extension), and the shoulder blades settle down and back on their own.

Big breath and hold before she initiates. See Breathing under load.

Sumo differs in four places.

Sumo setup

# Shins on the bar

sub: Not an inch away

Why: The knees track outwards rather than forwards, so the shin never pushes the bar away from her centre of mass.

# Feet at roughly 45 degrees

sub: If it is 46 or 44, that is fine

Why: The angle opens the hips so the knees can sit over the feet.

# Arms hang exactly straight down

sub: The knees are not in the way

Why: With the knees out wide there is nothing for the arms to clear, so the shortest line is available.

# Open the hips until the shin is vertical

sub: Knees directly above the feet, shin perpendicular to the ground

Why: Sit deep enough for leg drive, but not so deep that the posterior chain component is lost.

Terms on this page

jump stance: The foot position she would naturally take to jump as high as she could. Close, toes only slightly out. It is the conventional deadlift stance.

thoracolumbar fascia: The broad sheet of connective tissue across the lower back that the lats, glutes and deep trunk muscles all pull on.

thoracic extension: Straightening the upper back so the chest comes up and the rounding between the shoulder blades disappears.
