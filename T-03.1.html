Fatigue and recovery · T-03.1

Sleep as a training variable

A client who is not sleeping is not absorbing the training. Treat sleep as a programming input, not a wellbeing aside.

Sleep affects far more than one hormone pathway. It shapes her perceived effort, her focus, her mood and her coordination. It shapes how much discomfort she can tolerate and the force she can produce, on top of the recovery environment underneath all of it.

One night without sleep measurably reduces the muscle-building response to a meal (muscle protein synthesis). The muscle becomes less responsive to the growth signal (anabolic resistance). The same night pushes the stress hormone (cortisol) up and testosterone down. The figures live in Assessing a new client.

Why it works. Sleep is usually something a coach mentions and moves past. It feels outside the remit. The measurement puts it inside. The session was performed, and part of the adaptation was not available.

Hold the rule at the right scale. What one bad night measurably costs is the signalling side of the adaptation. It is not the numbers in her logbook.

Over a longer run the picture is gentler than the single-night figures suggest. Under mild habitual restriction, with training continuing and protein adequate, strength and size outcomes have held up. Fix sleep because it is a programming input, not because a poor week has cancelled her training.

Read it as recovery capacity. Someone sleeping five hours on rotating shifts cannot absorb the volume of someone sleeping eight on a fixed schedule. Assessing a new client reads sleep, stress and work for exactly that reason.

Watch for

Adding volume to a client who is under-slept. When she stalls, volume is fourth on the list, behind asking whether she is training hard enough. Poor sleep is a second reason that more work is not the lever.

Say it like this

If sleep is the thing that's off this month, that's the thing we fix. I can write you more work but you won't get the benefit of it.

Terms on this page

muscle protein synthesis: The process that builds new muscle protein after training. It is the repair and growth signal a session is meant to switch on.

anabolic resistance: The muscle responds less to the growth signal from protein and training than it normally would.

cortisol: A stress hormone. It rises with poor sleep and heavy stress, and it works against building tissue.
