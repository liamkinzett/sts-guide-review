Common faults

Five common faults on the 45 degree hip extension

# Pad in the wrong place

sub: A coach-side fault. The setup, not her

Why it happens: The pad was left where the last person had it, or set too high. The pelvis cannot travel over it.

Do this: Set the pad below the bony front points of the pelvis (ASIS) so the hip can travel.

# Lumbar finish (lumbar finish)

sub: Overextending at the top. Lower-back extension standing in for hip extension

Why it happens: The name says hyperextend, and the lower back is happy to add range the hip has finished supplying.

Do this: Finish at neutral. Use the first cue: "When I get to this neutral position, that's when I'm done."

# Position degrading across the set

sub: The stack is lost progressively, on the way up first

Why it happens: Fatigue. The brace goes before the glute does, and each rep finishes a little more from the back.

Do this: Stop the set. The standard is position held across the whole set.

# Gross rounding at the bottom

sub: The back visibly rounds at the deepest point

Why it happens: She is chasing range past what her hips own, so the spine folds to find it.

Do this: Stop in active range. This is the injury caution, not a preference.

# Load too light to be a stimulus

sub: A coach-side fault. Performed as a warm-up when it is programmed as work

Why it happens: A hugged light plate feels like enough because the movement burns. It is not enough for an intermediate lifter.

Do this: Load it. A dumbbell or a preloaded straight bar.

On the rounding fault

You cannot see the onset of spinal flexion on video. You can see gross rounding. Screen for the gross version only. Faults and what you can actually see explains the difference.

Terms on this page

ASIS: The two bony points you can feel at the front of the pelvis, one each side, just below the waistband. The hip extension pad sits below them.

lumbar finish: Finishing a hip movement by arching the lower back instead of extending the hip. Her back takes the load meant for her glutes.
