How to do it

Big toe, knee, sternum in line. That is the most repeated standard in single-leg work. The hip must travel outside the knee. That is the frontal-plane check (frontal plane) and the success criterion for every rep. If the hip never gets outside the knee, the movement is not doing its job, however good the rest looks.

The rest of the standard.

  • The knee tracks over the middle to the inside of the foot. Deliberately not straight ahead, and deliberately not outside the foot.
  • The pelvis rotates toward the working leg, never away from it.
  • Rib cage stacked over pelvis, with a very subtle forward lean.
  • Full foot pressure held throughout.
  • Lower the hips under control until the front thigh is close to parallel, then drive through the front foot.

    • Rest between legs. Resting between legs does not undermine the exercise. After the first leg she is already starting in a fatigued state. Let that fatigue wash off before the second leg, so she actually does the exercise. What undermines the second leg's set is cutting the rest to keep the session moving.

    Leg order on the split squat. Most people run the dominant leg first by habit. The cost is a systems failure. Fatigue of the whole system has already built up, so the second leg can't get as close to failure as it should. The fix is adequate rest between legs.

    On this exercise there is no rule about which leg goes first, so don't insist on weaker-first or stronger-first. The step-up and the kickback each carry their own instruction on their pages.

    Terms on this page

    frontal plane: Side-to-side movement, like a lateral lunge or a side raise.
