Common faults

Six common faults

# Elbow off its line

sub: Elbows flaring outward. Also the cable offset sideways from the elbow

Why it happens: The line of force is running across the joint, not through it. It is a fit problem, not a discipline problem.

Do this: This is a front-view check specifically. Film from the front and look at whether the cable runs through the elbow. Fix the stance or the handle until it does.

# Body English, torso sway

sub: The trunk rocks to help the weight down

Why it happens: The load is more than the triceps can move with the trunk still.

Do this: A chest-supported setup, which removes the sway rather than cueing against it.

# Shoulder dumping forward

sub: Shoulder blade rolling forward, getting shruggy

Why it happens: The blades were not set back and down before the first rep, and the load pulls them forward.

Do this: Hold the shoulder back.

# Body English, the straight bar version

sub: Hunkered over a short bar, sternum down, upper arms turned fully inward (internally rotated), bodyweight over the bar as a lever

Why it happens: Leaning over the bar turns her bodyweight into the force that moves it. It is called a "T-Rex jam".

Do this: Stand up. Sternum up, ribs stacked over pelvis. Expect the load to fall a long way. That drop is the real trade.

# Momentum at the turnaround

sub: Rushing the return on the cross-body version

Why it happens: The stretch at the bottom is uncomfortable and she bounces out of it.

Do this: Control it.

# Elbow working on a fixed-arm movement

sub: Turning a straight-arm pulldown into a pushdown

Why it happens: The elbow bends because it makes the weight easier to move.

Do this: Fix the elbow angle. If a straight-arm movement was prescribed, the elbow stays straight.

Balance and coordination trouble on the single-arm version is not on this list on purpose. It is not a load fault, and the fix is not to reduce the weight. Balance trouble on one arm is not a load problem explains.

Terms on this page

internally rotated: The upper arm turned inward in the socket, so the palm faces back or down.
