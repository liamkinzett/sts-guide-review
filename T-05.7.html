Training after menopause

After menopause, heavy compound work in the 6-8 rep range stays in her program. Bone and tendon need load, and load cannot be replaced with effort.

Why. Falling oestrogen causes a selective loss of her fast, powerful muscle fibres (Type II fibres). That is where her power lives.

Bone and tendon are the first reason. Both need load magnitude. How much mineral is packed into her bone (bone mineral density) rises with heavy load. So does her tendons' tolerance of load. Getting close to failure with a light weight cannot buy either. That is why heavy work stays.

Get the fibre argument right. You will hear that light, high-rep work cannot reach her fast fibres. That is wrong. Light work taken close to failure does reach them. The nervous system raises its drive to hold the force and brings in the higher-threshold fibres. Muscle and growth covers that mechanism. So heavy work is not there to reach her fast fibres. It is there for her bone and tendon.

Why this matters at the bench. A client who cannot tolerate 6-8 rep compound loading is not cut off from her fast fibres. A joint issue or a pending osteoporosis screen might be the reason. She is cut off from the bone and tendon stimulus. That is a different and more specific problem. It tells you what to solve.

Why it works. The instinct with an older client is to lighten everything. That instinct removes precisely the stimulus her physiology has stopped supplying on its own.

Watch for

Reading "heavy" as "unsafe". Heavy means heavy relative to her, prescribed by the same rules as anyone else. Effort and intensity sets the effort. Volume sets the sets.

Say it like this

We're going to keep some genuinely heavy work in here. It's the part that protects your bone and your power, and it gets more important with age, not less.

Terms on this page

Type II fibres: Fast-twitch muscle fibres. They produce the most force and power, and they are the first to shrink with age and disuse.

bone mineral density: How much mineral is packed into her bone. Higher density means stronger bone that is harder to fracture.
