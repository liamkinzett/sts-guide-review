What it's for

The pull-up is STS's vertical pull. It biases the lats and it is loaded heaviest in the stretched hang (lengthened). Bodyweight resistance peaks toward the stretch and through the mid-pull. It falls away at the top, which is why we stop the finish short rather than chasing it.

It is not primarily a size exercise. It is a shoulder-function exercise. Her spine hangs in space with nothing anchoring it to the pelvis but her trunk. Her body is free rather than fixed (open chain). The upper arm turns in and out in the socket (internal rotation) (external rotation) while her weight pulls the arm away from the socket (distraction).

Hanging like that trains the shoulder blade turning fully upward (upward rotation), plus that turning in and out, in an unstable setting. That is what makes it the vertical movement for shoulder health.

It is also a standard rather than a builder. It reads her ability to organise the trunk, her body awareness and her relative strength in one movement.

Where it sits in the session. We put grip-burning work at the end of a session so the hands are not pre-exhausted. The pull-up is the exception. Take it fresh. Building a session owns the order.

The pull-up and the chin-up are different movements.

Compared

columns: Pull-up | Chin-up

Grip: Palms facing away (pronated grip), shoulder width or wider. | Palms facing her (supinated grip), shoulder width or narrower.

Legs: Straight or subtly tucked behind. | Slightly ahead, hollow or arched, as a counterbalance.

Range: Longer. A full dead hang is available. | Shorter. Correctly so.

Bias: Upper back, lats and teres. | The same, plus more biceps.

Terms on this page

lengthened: The point in the rep where the working muscle is at its longest while it carries the load. It describes the muscle's length, not whether she feels a stretch.

open chain: The hand or foot on the working limb is free in space rather than fixed to the floor or a pad, like a leg extension or a curl. Hanging from a bar, her body is free and only her trunk ties the shoulder to the pelvis.

distraction: A pull that draws the joint surfaces apart. In a hang her own bodyweight pulls the upper arm away from the shoulder socket.

pronated grip: Palms facing down or away from her, knuckles up. It is the pull-up grip.

supinated grip: Palms facing up or toward her. On a bar overhead it is the chin-up grip, and it brings in more biceps.

internal rotation: Turning a limb inward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face back or down, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more toward the middle. An arm held that way is internally rotated.

external rotation: Turning a limb outward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face forward or up, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more to the side. An arm held that way is externally rotated.

upward rotation: The shoulder blade turning so its outer corner rises and the socket tilts upward. It is what lets the arm travel overhead.
