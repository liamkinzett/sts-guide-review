Glutes · E-GLU-DEADLIFT#⚠ Spinal position. Where the corpus contradicts common practice

Spinal position

Do not coach a rigidly locked spine, and do not coach flexion. Both extremes are wrong, and the second is the one you must never drift into.

The first extreme is the idea that a neutral spine (neutral spine) means being locked in exactly firm, in a perfect line. It does not. The strongest deadlifters in the world finish pulls near 1,000 lb with visible spinal flexion. Some flexion during a heavy pull is not by itself a fault. A forward tilt of the pelvis (anterior pelvic tilt) is not the devil either.

Draw the line by mechanism, not by permission. When she is getting far too much movement through the spine, look elsewhere. She is almost certainly not getting movement somewhere she should be. Usually that is the hips. Read a moving spine as a sign that another joint has stopped doing its job, and fix that joint.

What this does not change

STS does not coach spinal flexion under load. The above tells you not to panic at a spine that is not a ruler. It does not give you permission to teach rounding. Mobility and stretching holds what STS says about spinal flexion under load.

You cannot see the onset of flexion on video. Only gross rounding shows. Screen for the gross version, and read Faults and what you can actually see before you call a spinal fault.

Terms on this page

neutral spine: The spine held in its natural curves, neither rounded nor over-arched, and braced there. It is a zone she stays inside, not a single locked line.

anterior pelvic tilt: The pelvis tipped forward, so the lower back arches more.
