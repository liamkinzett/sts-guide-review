Fatigue and recovery · T-03.15

Why the second session of a new movement hurts less

The first session of a new movement is the sore one. The second identical session hurts far less. Tell her that before the first session, not after.

One session of an unfamiliar movement, or of heavy lowering work (eccentric), leaves damage and soreness. The second identical session produces far less of both (repeated bout effect). Protection appears after a single exposure.

Why it works.

A program change often spikes her soreness not because the new exercise is better, only because her body has not adapted to that demand yet.

Several mechanisms have been proposed for the protection that follows, and none has been shown to account for it on its own. The muscle may add contracting units along its length (sarcomeres), so the same load lands on a more stable part of the fibre. The connective tissue and the cell membrane may toughen, leaving less shear on the second exposure. The inflammatory response is smaller on repeat exposure. Which of them dominates has not been shown, and it does not change what you do.

Say it in advance. A movement coming back after a layoff hurts more in the first session back than in the ones after it. A client warned of that reads it as normal. A client not warned reads it as a sign she is unfit or that the program is wrong.

Say before her first session of anything new

The first session of a new movement is the sore one. The second one, same movement, hurts a lot less. That's your body adapting, not a problem.

Watch for

Stretching this into an argument for long-length training. The sarcomere change here is about repeat exposure, and it was seen mainly in animals. Muscle and growth covers the case for training at long lengths.

The page on soreness explains why soreness is not feedback in the first place.

Terms on this page

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.

repeated bout effect: The protection a muscle builds against the soreness and damage of work it has done before: the next session of the same movement produces far less of both.

sarcomeres: The tiny contracting units a muscle fibre is built from, laid end to end in a long chain.
