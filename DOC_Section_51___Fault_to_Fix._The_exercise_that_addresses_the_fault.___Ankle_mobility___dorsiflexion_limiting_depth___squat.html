Ankle range limiting squat depth: front squat her first

Before you reach for a heel, front squat her. Most lifters hit depth perfectly the moment the load is in front. If her ankle range were the limit, it would not have changed with the bar position.

This is not one of the named faults. It surfaces as weight into the heels or as range cut short. It has more exercise-level fixes than any named fault, so it earns its own page.

Work through it in this order

Front squat her. Usually she hits depth. Her ankle range did not change, so it was never the limit.

Take her shoes off. For the sensory feedback only. It is not a barefoot doctrine.

Elevate the heels with a fractional plate, not a bumper. A 2.5 kilo fractional plate under each heel, for a very slight slope. Minimum effective dose, with its removal planned.

Train the ankle in the position the squat uses. Machine seated calf raise with the knees actively pushed forward in the machine. Put it early in the session, before the squat.

On a pendulum squat, use a squat wedge or weightlifting shoes to get the knee to full bend.

Why each one works

# The front squat

sub: Regression, and the test

Why it works: Moving the load in front changes where her weight sits. She no longer has to reach forward at the ankle to stay balanced.

# Heel elevation

sub: Setup

Why it works: It is not cheating. It is an intervention on her centre of mass that makes the position easier to manage.

# The seated calf raise, knees forward

sub: Accessory

Why it works: It pairs a bent knee with the ankle closing (dorsiflexion), which is what a squat asks for. Trained in the machine, with the knees driven forward, it builds strength in that position.

Exercises: Machine seated calf raise

Watch for

A heel she never comes off. The long-term goal is to make up the deficit, not to rely on the wedge.

Too much heel. An excessive heel elevation tips her forward and produces the hips shooting up. Fractional plate, not bumper.

Terms on this page

dorsiflexion: The ankle bending so the shin and the top of the foot move closer together, as when the toes pull up toward the shin or the shin travels forward over the foot. A deep squat needs it at the bottom.
