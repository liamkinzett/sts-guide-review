Variations

The deadlift is one lift with seven common variations: conventional, sumo, trap bar, deficit, block pull, rack pull and snatch-grip. STS programs three of them. The conventional pull on every path. The sumo on the strength path only. The trap bar deadlift for learning, rebuilding or coming back from injury.

The other four you will read about in Deadlift variations. None of them is a default for a general client, and nothing on this page says they suit one.

The seven variations

# Conventional deadlift

sub: The lift this page teaches. Programmed on every path

What it is: Bar over the midfoot, shins an inch from the bar, "bum down" enough to include leg drive and no further. Setup and How to do it are written for this pull.

Do this: Prescribe it whenever the goal is a number off the floor.

# Sumo deadlift

sub: Programmed on the strength path only

What it is: Wide stance, shins on the bar, feet at roughly 45 degrees. It pre-shortens the glutes, so it is a poor glute builder and a sound strength lift. Like the conventional pull it is hardest off the floor.

Do this: Prescribe it for a client on the powerlifting-strength path to move a bigger number. Never prescribe it to build glutes.

# Trap bar deadlift

sub: Programmed to learn, rebuild or return to the deadlift

What it is: The pull done inside a hexagonal bar, with the load beside her rather than in front. Her back carries less tension than in the conventional pull.

Do this: Prescribe it when she is learning the deadlift, or when her conventional technique keeps breaking down. Prescribe it too when she is coming back from an injury and her back should carry less tension. Coach it to the same hinge standards as the conventional pull. Move her to the conventional pull when the pattern holds and the injury allows.

# Deficit deadlift

sub: Read Deadlift variations before you consider it

What it is: The conventional pull from a raised platform of 1–2 inches, for more range. Deadlift variations covers its purpose and its carryover, and carries a pain warning.

Do this: Read that page first. This one gives it no sets, reps, load or selection rule.

# Block pull

sub: Read Deadlift variations before you consider it

What it is: The bar raised on blocks, shortening the pull (partial). Deadlift variations rates the sumo block pull. It says nothing about the conventional one.

Do this: See Deadlift variations for partials as a class.

# Rack pull

sub: Read Deadlift variations before you consider it

What it is: The bar starting from pins in a rack. A partial from knee height or above.

Do this: See Deadlift variations.

# Snatch-grip deadlift

sub: Not a training lift at STS

What it is: A conventional pull with a very wide grip, which lowers the start and lengthens the pull. Its only STS use is inside a single rehabilitation protocol.

Do this: Do not prescribe it as a training lift.

Terms on this page

partial: A lift done through part of its range rather than all of it, cut short on purpose by a box, a board, blocks or pins. A block pull or rack pull is a deadlift that starts higher than the floor.
