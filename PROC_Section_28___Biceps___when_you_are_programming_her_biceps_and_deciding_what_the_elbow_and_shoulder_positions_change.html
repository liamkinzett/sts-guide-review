Programming her biceps

You have a biceps slot to fill and three exercise pages to fill it with. This page is the Biceps topic's pages in the order you use them. Nothing here is new doctrine.

1 · Screen her before any two-arm dumbbell curl

Run the external-rotation screen before you prescribe any bilateral dumbbell curl. She stands straight with the arms straight forward. She then turns the arms outward as far as she can (external rotation). Watch whether the elbow stays in line with the wrist and the shoulder.

The screen is active, and you judge it by alignment. You are not measuring an angle, and nobody holds the joint. That is what keeps it inside what a coach may call. Standing curl has the full screen.

2 · Read the screen, then read the two dials

Check

Arms straight out in front, turned outward as far as she can. Does the elbow stay in line with the wrist and the shoulder?

yes: Bilateral curling is fine.

no: Bilateral curling is contraindicated. She curls single-arm, permanently rather than as a temporary measure.

Forcing a two-arm curl on a failed screen puts the hand on a diagonal while the load falls straight down. That mismatch is the named cause of medial elbow pain in curls.

Then read her position question the way this topic splits it.

The grip is the flexor dial. Hammer and neutral grips shift the work to the brachialis and the brachioradialis (brachialis) (brachioradialis).

The shoulder is the length dial. The long head of the biceps crosses the shoulder (long head). Full length needs the elbow straight and the arm behind the body at the same time (shoulder extension). Only a cable, an incline or a behind-the-back setup reaches it. A standing dumbbell curl loads the mid-range with no load off the bottom, because gravity passes straight through the elbow there.

3 · Decide how much direct work she gets

For a female client, direct arm work is priority-gated. The default is one curl variation, and direct arm work only when she has made arms a priority. A male client's pattern is two curl variations, a heavier mid-range plus a lighter one, with direct arm work close to always programmed. We coach women. A program that looks light on arms is usually correct.

Key figure

value: 4 to 6

unit: direct arm sets a week

note: The working number when arms are not a stated priority. It is low because her pulling is doing real work

Her pulling counts toward her biceps, and you count it deliberately. Vertical pulling counts close to fully. A program carrying real vertical pulling is giving her biceps close to what a curl would. Rowing counts about half, as a working figure rather than a constant. A program carrying only rows is giving her something, but less than the set count suggests. Neither is a licence to stop counting.

A worked example. A client has two hard sets of chin-ups and four hard sets of rows in her week, and arms are not a priority. The chin-ups count close to fully. The rows count about half. Her biceps are already near the four to six direct sets a week that is plenty. One curl variation is the right amount of direct work for her.

Two failures, in opposite directions

Adding male-pattern arm volume to her program by habit.

Leaving arms at four sets for a client who has told you plainly that she wants them. The second is worse. She said what she wanted and the program ignored her.

Reading the pulling credit as permission to drop direct work. It is the opposite. It tells you which programs can afford minimal direct arm work and which cannot, and you only know by counting.

When she asks why there are so few curls

Your arms are getting hit hard on every push and pull day already. From a growth perspective they're already covered. If you want them to be a focus, say the word and we'll build it in properly rather than just tacking on curls.

4 · Pick by job

Do, in order

# The default mid-range mass builder

Take: The standing curl.

Exercises: DB bicep curl; DB hammer curl

# The lengthened slot

Take: The face away cable curl. A dumbbell cannot load that position. No cable means the slot is unavailable, not substitutable. Losing the cable loses the reason the movement was programmed.

Exercises: Face away cable bicep curl; Single arm face away cable bicep curl

# A preacher variation

Take: Pick the implement on availability and comfort. It is not a growth lever.

Exercises: Single arm DB preacher curl; Machine preacher curl; EZ bar preacher curl

# The finisher

Take: The shortened group, loaded by what the shortened position can hold rather than by what the mid-range could.

Exercises: Concentration curl; Spider curl; High cable bicep curl

# She cannot hold the palm-up position at all

Take: The hammer or neutral grip.

Exercises: DB hammer curl; Alternating DB hammer curl

5 · What moves around the choice

  • Sequencing is the hard rule. The preacher curl is never the first movement of a session. It goes second or third at the earliest. Biceps failure work is late-session work. Building a session owns the order.
  • Set counts belong to Volume. Tempo and pause placement belong to Tempo and pauses, page by page.
  • Balance the forearm. Accumulated forearm flexion fatigue is a primary driver of elbow pain. Standing curl's Watch for has the wrist-extension counter-measure.

    • What does not change: the screen's verdict is permanent, and the preacher's lockout rule stands. Never lock out.

    6 · When to send it up

    Medial elbow pain on curls is a stacking problem before it is a tissue problem. The wrist, elbow and shoulder are out of line. Treat the stack first. It is not a swap. On the cable curls, both alignment checks gate the load rather than being optional.

    Pain on the outside of the elbow with the wrist held palm-down is usually the brachialis, not the wrist extensors. Change the grip, not the load. Turn the palm up (supinate), or go neutral. Pain itself is Pain rules and when to refer, and the elbow playbook is Region by region.

    Terms on this page

    external rotation: Turning a limb outward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face forward or up, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more to the side. On the screen it is what she does with the arms held straight out in front.

    brachialis: The muscle under the biceps that bends the elbow. It carries arm thickness and the separation between biceps and triceps.

    brachioradialis: The muscle on the thumb side of the upper forearm that bends the elbow. A hammer or neutral grip puts more work on it.

    long head: The head of a muscle that crosses the shoulder joint as well as the elbow: on the triceps it only stretches with the arm raised, on the biceps only with the arm behind the body.

    shoulder extension: The upper arm travelling back behind the line of the body.

    supinate: Turn the forearm so the palm faces up. Of the foot, roll it onto its outside edge so the arch lifts. On a curl it puts the work back on the biceps and takes the twist off the elbow.
