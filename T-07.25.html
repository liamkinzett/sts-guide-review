The phases · T-07.25

Three peaking styles, chosen on training age and need

There are three ways to run a peak: linear, block and conjugate. Choose on her training age and what she needs from the phase.

The three styles

# Linear

sub: Reps down, weight up, every week

Who it suits: A less experienced lifter who still responds to straightforward loading (linear peak).

Why: Least friction to run and easiest to track.

# Block

sub: Accumulation, then transmutation, then realisation

Who it suits: Someone more developed who no longer progresses in a straight line (block peak).

Why: Each block trains a different quality and exercise selection changes with it. The most complete peak when there is a fixed test date to work back from.

# Conjugate

sub: Near-maximal work every week on a rotating variation, with separate speed work

Who it suits: An advanced lifter with identifiable weak points (conjugate). Also one who cannot tolerate running the same thing for four weeks.

Why: The most demanding to program and the least forgiving of poor recovery.

Full twelve week progressions for squat, bench and deadlift in all three styles are in Peaking for a meet.

Terms on this page

linear peak: Reps come down and weight goes up every week, in a straight line, until test day.

block peak: The strength phase is run in three blocks, accumulation, transmutation and realisation, each training a different quality.

conjugate: A peaking style that rotates a near-maximal variation of the lift every week and runs separate speed work alongside it.
