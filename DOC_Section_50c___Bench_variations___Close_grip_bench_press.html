Close grip bench press

Close grip means her own shoulder width, and the grip is a decision about the plane the shoulder moves in, not a triceps preference.

A narrow grip trades elbows-out (abduction) for rotation, and for flexion and extension. The shoulder sits in a more structurally stable position, and the triceps becomes what runs out first (rate limiter). A wide grip does the reverse: more abduction, less rotation, with stability supplied by the secondary flexors and extensors rather than by the rotator cuff. That is why the characteristic complaint of a very wide bencher is pain in the long head of the biceps.

Finding close grip. Arms at her sides, then pushed straight out in front of her into the bar. That is close grip. Thumbs together is not a closer version of it. It is a different and worse thing.

If she asks whether closer is better

Close grip is your own shoulder width. If close is good, closer isn't better.

The floor under the grip is the line straight down from the top of her shoulder (AC joint). Hands inside it force the wrist to bend sideways (deviation) to compensate. The injury it produces is to the cartilage on the little-finger side of the wrist (TFCC). Elbow bend is reduced too, because the hand runs into the chest. Wrist pain at the bottom of a press is the wrist leaving neutral in the deviation plane. Buy it back with hand position, not a wrist cue.

Where it earns a slot

# The novice default

sub: A new presser starts close

Why: On tissue tolerance at end range rather than on muscle bias. A tucked grip is less compromised by the upper-back and shoulder-blade prerequisites most desk-bound clients fail.

# The de-specified variation in a strength block

sub: The same motor pattern at a lower technical demand

Why: Grouped with chains, boards and the floor press. That is what a supplemental slot needs.

# The conjugate plate, specific phase

sub: Weeks 5 and 6

Why: Close grip bench worked to a 1RM in weeks 5 and 6, as the variation moves closer to the tested lift.

# The strength system's main second-day variation

sub: For a competitive powerlifter

Why: The second bench exposure of the week is normally a close grip or an incline.

Do not pair it with a big arch

The close grip's asset is bar speed. Cutting the range with an arch cuts the runway the bar has to accelerate into the point where it slows (sticking point).

The implement upgrade. Where a close grip forces her palms to turn down (pronation) and drives the elbows out, swap the implement. A Swiss or neutral-grip bar, or dumbbells, lets the hands stack directly above the elbows. That is an upgrade rather than a compromise.

Volume. Close grip and similar shared pressing counts half to chest and half to triceps. Close grip pressing is triceps work rather than a substitute for it. It does not need direct triceps work stacked on top.

Tempo. `2110`, with the pause at the chest, because of the bounce risk.

Terms on this page

sticking point: The part of a lift where the bar slows or stops, the hardest point of the range for her.

abduction: A limb travelling out and away from the midline of the body: the arm in a lateral raise, or the thigh moving out to the side. On the bench, how far her elbows sit from her body.

rate limiter: The muscle or position that gives out first and so decides where the set ends.

AC joint: The acromioclavicular joint, where the collarbone meets the top of the shoulder blade. You can feel it as the bony bump on top of the shoulder. Its line straight down the body sets the narrowest sensible grip.

deviation: The wrist bending sideways, toward the thumb (radial) or toward the little finger (ulnar).

TFCC: The triangular fibrocartilage complex, the cartilage and ligaments on the little-finger side of the wrist.

1RM: The heaviest weight she can lift once with good form.

pronation: Turning the forearm so the palm faces down or away. Of the foot, rolling inward so the arch flattens and the ankle drops in.
