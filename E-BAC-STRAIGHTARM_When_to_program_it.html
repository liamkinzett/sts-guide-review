When to use it

The problem you notice. She cannot find her lat on anything. The pulldowns and the rows all feel like arms. Or she is about to be given a barbell deadlift and has never shown you she can control her lat. The straight-arm pulldown puts the lat on a platter: move the arm in a circle and the lat should feel something.

Reach for it when

# She cannot find her lat on anything

sub: Pulldowns and rows all feel like arms

Confirm it: Ask where she feels a pulldown. If the answer is biceps or forearms every time, she has not found the lat.

What it gets her: A position where the lat is unmissable. The cable's line of pull helps rather than fights, the arc is large, and the lats are on a platter for her.

# She is about to be given a barbell deadlift

sub: And has not demonstrated lat control

Confirm it: Watch her on this movement. If she cannot find and hold the lat on a rope and pulley, she is not deadlifting yet.

What it gets her: A gate cleared before the bar is loaded. If she cannot find the lat here, she will not find it under a palms-away grip on a bar drifting in front of her shins.

# Her upper back will not open

sub: The upper back (thoracic spine) stays rounded when she reaches overhead

Confirm it: Ask her to reach overhead and open the chest. The upper back does not extend with the arms.

What it gets her: The upper back opened and shoulder mobility gained under load. Use lighter weight than normal to make this move part of her mobility training.

# A rhomboid-focused row is coming and her traps and lats will take it over

sub: A pre-exhaust before the row

Confirm it: On her upper-back rows she reports the traps and lats doing the work, and the rhomboids never fatigue.

What it gets her: The rhomboids reachable in the row that follows. Tiring the lats first (pre-exhaustion) stops them overshadowing the smaller muscle.

Two load instructions, and they are opposite

The builder use and the mobility use carry opposite loads. As a lat builder it is loaded. As a mobility tool it is deliberately lighter than normal. Name which of the two you are programming. Never average them.

The only injury instruction here is a load instruction. Going too heavy over a long range is what compromises the shoulder. The answer is a lighter load, not a different exercise.

Setup. High pulley, long arms, a big step back so the cable stays under tension. Setup has the full list.

Terms on this page

pre-exhaustion: Tiring one muscle with an isolation exercise first, so that it is the limiter in the compound lift that follows.

thoracic spine: The upper and middle back, the part of the spine the ribs attach to. It is the part of the spine that has to extend for the arms to get overhead cleanly.
