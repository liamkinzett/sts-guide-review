Writing her first program from the intake

Program one is itself the test. Write it so a wrong classification shows up rather than doing damage, then read week one in a fixed order. This page is the whole topic in the order you use it. You use it on the day her form arrives, and again when week one's footage comes back.

Test. What you actually have. A form she filled in about herself, a call summary, and no footage. Assessing a new client already marks that intake classification provisional. So you write the program the classification implies, and you write it so that being wrong is visible. The real observation arrives at the end of week one, as footage and a logbook.

Do. Write the program.

Writing program one

Start from the provisional classification. Write the program it implies. Do not write the program you hope she is ready for.

Aim off on all three variables, in the same direction. Effort below the intent. Load below what you think she can do. Volume one set per exercise below her band, in week one only. The rows below carry each one.

Look up the block itself in The phases. What priming is for and when she leaves it. Which movements go in it. How the split is built. Look it up rather than reconstructing it.

Aim off on all three

# Effort

sub: Written below the intent

What you write: Below 3 RIR where a machine holds her position. 2-3 RIR where her own bracing holds it. She under-delivers on effort by one to two reps at this stage.

Where the figures live: Effort and intensity carries the overshoot figure and the reasoning. Look them up rather than working from memory.

# Load

sub: Below what you think she can do

What you write: Pick the weight you believe fits, then go under it. Too light costs one week of a measurement block. Too heavy costs technique, which is the one thing this block exists to protect.

# Volume

sub: One set per exercise under her band, week one only

What you write: Week two goes to target and stays there. This is an on-ramp, not a ramp. Volume does not climb on a schedule. Her band is in Volume.

Read. When week one's footage and logbook arrive. Week one is read in one order, and it is not the usual one. For an experienced client you check effort first and technique second. For her, deliberately, execution comes first. Her effort ratings do not mean anything yet. Execution is the only week-one signal that does not depend on her judgement of herself.

After week one, in this order

Execution. First, and sometimes only. If execution is poor, volume, reps and effort are irrelevant. A set performed badly is a different exercise, not a lighter version of the same one. Fix it, cue it or regress the movement.

Load. Now you know what she did, correct what you aimed off. This is usually up.

Volume. To target.

Effort. Last, and mostly not yet at all. She earns an effort prescription by proving she can rate one.

Whatever the footage shows, she gets one cue. Pick the one that unlocks the most. Write the rest of the list down for yourself.

Watch for

A logbook that looks excellent while the video looks poor. That is not a client progressing. That is a client getting better at the wrong movement.

The aim-off becoming permanent. The gap closes as she calibrates. A coach still writing the beginner's discount in month four is under-training her.

Running the week-one on-ramp on a returning client. If the logbook already holds her baselines, she does not get it.

Escalate.
