Reshuffling days is negotiating the calendar, not the split

A client who reshuffles which day carries which session is negotiating the calendar, not the split. Read it through the pattern count, not the diary.

Split design counts patterns (pattern), not exercises. A week where Tuesday's session moves to Thursday still delivers the same coverage, provided the patterns still land. On that measure alone, moving a day is not a problem. The alternating A and B rotation absorbs a missed session on the same logic. The structure survives when a day moves.

The actual limit is fatigue crossing between sessions, not how often she asks (crossover fatigue). Splits fail when sessions that do not look related start competing. Heavy back work landing the day before a hinge session is one example. The hinge session underperforms for a reason that is not in the hinge session. That failure is invisible in the session it damages. Reshuffling is too much exactly when it collapses the spacing the split was built to protect. Not when it simply happens often.

Check

Does the new arrangement still keep the competing sessions apart, the way the split was built to?

yes: Grant it plainly.

no: Decline it plainly, with the reason named.

Ask that one question when the requests keep coming. Split design's page on what breaks a split governs the answer.

Say it like this

Moving Tuesday to Thursday is fine. Moving it to the day before your deadlifts puts heavy pulling right in front of them. That's the one gap I need to keep.

Terms on this page

pattern: A family of movements that load the body the same way, like squats, hinges, presses and pulls. Frequency is counted per pattern, not per exercise.

crossover fatigue: Fatigue that shows up in a session other than the one that caused it. Heavy back work the day before a hinge session limits her ability to brace.
