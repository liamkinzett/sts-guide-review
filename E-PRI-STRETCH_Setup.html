Priming lifts · E-PRI-STRETCH#Setup

Setup

Do not stretch a completely cold muscle. She moves first, with some dynamic work, and the stretch goes inside the warm-up rather than in front of it. Warming up owns the warm-up.

The stretches STS programs

# PNF lat stretch

sub: Deep breathing throughout (PNF)

Setup: Turn the wrist palm-up (supinating). That turns the shoulder outward (external rotation) and lengthens the lat completely. It only counts as PNF if she contracts against resistance at end range. A held stretch with breathing is a static stretch.

# Couch stretch

sub: Kneeling, rear foot up behind her

Setup: Kneeling on one leg with a couch or bench close behind. Front leg at a right angle, rear ankle or the top of the foot on the edge.

# Pigeon stretch

sub: Front shin under the body, back leg long

Setup: Front leg's shin and foot under the body, opposite leg flat behind. Sternum to the midline of the front thigh, hands squared, ribs tucked for a neutral lower back. Hinge slowly.

# Seated pigeon

sub: The figure-four on a bench. The low-knee-load version

Setup: Seated on the edge of a bench, feet flat, knees at a right angle, one ankle on top of the opposite knee.

# World's greatest stretch

sub: A deep lunge with a rotation

Setup: Step into a deep lunge and sink into the front hip. How to do it has the rotation.

Stretches you will see that STS doesn't program

# Supinated lat stretch

sub: Hooked under a bar, palms up (supinated)

What it is: The palms-up grip turns the shoulder outward, which puts the lat fully lengthened. Sit the hips back, throw the weight away, then rotate the pelvis away to stretch it from its pelvic origin.

# Dowel child's pose

sub: Kneeling, elbows on a bench, dowel held palms up

What it is: Facing a bench on all fours, palms-up grip on a dowel, elbows on the bench edge, hips back to the heels, head below the bench.

# Lat stretch over a plyo box

sub: Kneeling, elbows on a box

What it is: Kneeling in front of a low-to-medium box, both elbows on the box edge, dowel held underhand. Shoulders square, core braced, rib cage and hips stacked.

# Half-kneeling hip flexor stretch

sub: Kneeling, no couch

What it is: Kneeling on one knee, front leg at a right angle, hips square and pointed straight ahead, core braced, no rib-cage flare.

Priming the hips: include direct adductor work

A hip-priming answer that lists only stretches is incomplete. The inner-thigh muscles (adductors) are one of three hip extensors a coach tracks, alongside the glutes and the hamstrings. They are loaded incidentally all over her program. Direct work is the only way they are trained as a prime mover. The programmed direct option is the machine hip adduction, the machine that pulls the legs in toward the midline (hip adduction). Prescribe it for the contraction rather than for a resistance profile. Calves and adductors has the page.

Terms on this page

PNF: A stretch where she takes the joint to end range, pushes against resistance for a few seconds, then relaxes into the new range. The contraction is what makes it PNF.

adductors: The muscles of the inner thigh that pull the leg in toward the midline. They also help extend the hip.

supinating: Turning the forearm so the palm faces up.

supinated: Palm turned up or forward, knuckles back. On a lat stretch it turns the shoulder outward and lengthens the lat fully.

external rotation: Turning a limb outward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face forward or up, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more to the side. On a lat stretch it is what a palms-up grip forces.

hip adduction: Bringing the thigh in toward the midline of the body. The inner-thigh muscles do it. The machine hip adduction trains them directly.
