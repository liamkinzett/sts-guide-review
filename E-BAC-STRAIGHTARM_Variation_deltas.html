Variations

STS programs one version: the straight arm cable lat pulldown. The choice inside it is the attachment, a rope or a straight bar.

What the attachment changes. A rigid straight bar locks both hands to one width and one plane for the whole rep. As the bar approaches the body at the bottom of the arc it collides with the thighs, which caps the range. A rope lets the hands split and travel past the hips. That extends the shoulder further, and it lets the wrist and shoulder rotate along her own joint path instead of a fixed one.

Compared

columns: Rope | Straight bar

Use it when: Her shoulder mobility or elbow tolerance is limited. | She gets a strong, pain-free contraction on it.

Why: The hands follow her own joint path, so nothing is forced at the elbow or shoulder. | A fixed grip lets some lifters drop the shoulders and feel the lat harder.

Watch for: Nothing in particular. It is the default when alignment is the question. | A fixed grip that does not match her anatomy forces an unwanted twisting load (torque) at the elbow and shoulder. If either complains, move to the rope.

Do this. Where her shoulder mobility or elbow tolerance is limited, the rope is the default. Where she gets a strong, pain-free contraction on a straight bar, there is no reason to change it on alignment grounds alone. Feel and alignment are two different reasons to choose an attachment, and here you let the lifter in front of you decide.

Terms on this page

torque: The turning force on a joint. The further the load sits from the joint, the more of it there is.
