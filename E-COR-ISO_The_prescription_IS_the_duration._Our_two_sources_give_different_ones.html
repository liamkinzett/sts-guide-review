Core · E-COR-ISO#The prescription IS the duration. Our two sources give different ones

How long she holds

The prescription for a hold is its duration, and there are two of them because they answer different questions. Pick by the question you are asking, and record which one you used.

Compared

columns: Back-pain protocol | Healthy-athlete endurance

Hold: 5 to 10 seconds | 30 to 60 seconds, up to 90

Then: Repeat short holds. Fatigue is not what is being trained | Progress to loaded versions, not to a longer clock

Use it when: Her back is the complaint and the risk is fatigue, not weakness | She is healthy and the slot is endurance or a systems check

How long she holds

Back-pain protocol | 5-10 | Fatigue is not what is being trained

Healthy-athlete endurance | 30-60 | Then loaded versions, not a longer clock

The ceiling | 90 | Never a longer plank than this

The short figure exists so that fatigue is not the thing being trained. A hold that runs past the point her position degrades is training the compensation.

What a plank cannot do

Holds do not fix how she loads her spine in a lift. A bad loading pattern in her squat or hinge is fixed in the squat or hinge. Bird dogs and curl-ups are drops in the ocean against it.

A static plank does not scale. The spine is built to move. Once she holds the endurance figure, the next step is a loadable movement, not more seconds.

The side plank has three different purposes. Mobility and resisting shear. Compressing the rib cage from the sides before overhead work. Resisting the rib cage tipping sideways. Know which one you are programming. They progress differently.
