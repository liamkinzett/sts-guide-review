Programming her triceps: does the angle matter

You are filling her triceps slots and wondering whether the angle of the arm actually matters. It does, and the mechanism is the shoulder rather than the hand. This page is the Triceps topic's three exercise pages in the order you use them. Nothing here is new doctrine.

1 · Test: two looks before you pick movements

Establish her comfortable limit of overhead reach before you load anything overhead (shoulder flexion). That limit sets the bottom of the rep, not a textbook depth. See Overhead cable extension, Setup.

Then look at the session she is walking into and ask which shoulder positions it already loads. Grip changes nothing at the tricep. Turning the palm up or down is a biceps action. The attachment is a shoulder-position dial, which makes grip a session-level choice against redundancy rather than an exercise-level ranking. See Triceps pushdown, What it's for.

2 · Read: the angle is a length question, and it decides the slots

What each movement loads

The long head at length | Overhead cable extension. The shoulder is in flexion and the long head is stretched

The mid-to-stretched end of the elbow's range | Skull crusher. Shoulder close to vertical, hardest at the bottom

The mid-range, heavily | JM press. The heaviest tricep-biased free-weight option

The mid-to-shortened end | Pushdown. Shoulder neutral, so the medial and lateral heads do the work

The long head crosses the shoulder and only shoulder flexion loads it at length. No pushdown reaches that position (long head). The pushdown is therefore the medial and lateral head slot, with tension peaking in the mid-to-shortened range. The skull crusher loads the mid-to-stretched end and the JM press overloads the mid-range.

A program that never takes the shoulder into flexion never loads the long head at length. That is what the angle changes.

3 · Do

First, how many triceps movements she gets. One, by default: overhead or not overhead, not both. A male client gets both. Direct arm work only when she has made arms a priority, and four to six direct sets a week when she has not. Volume holds the rule. The rows below tell you which one to pick.

Do this, in order

# Fill the length slot with the overhead cable extension

Do this: Full elbow bend is the standard. A rep that stops near a right angle at the elbow is not this exercise.

Exercises: Single arm overhead cable tricep extension

# Slot the pushdown as isolation late in a push or arm session

Do this: Building a session decides the order. See Triceps pushdown.

Exercises: Cable tricep extension (neutral grip); Cable tricep extension (pronated grip); Single arm cable tricep extension; Dual cable tricep extension

# Where she needs a heavy, loadable triceps press between a compound press and true isolation

Do this: That is the JM press. See Skull crusher and JM press.

Exercises: Smith machine JM press

# Pick the grip at session level

Do this: The palms-down (pronated grip) pushdown is the right call where the session's other shoulder positions are not already palms-down. It is the wrong call where they are. See Triceps pushdown.

# Where the torso sways or grip fatigue is building late in the session

Do this: Take the machine pushdown. It is the braced setup pre-built.

Exercises: Tricep push down machine

4 · Change: what moves and what does not

Tempo and pause placement follow each page's Where it's hardest, and the count is Tempo and pauses's. Set counts are Volume's. The dip stays in Chest.

Her overhead limit does not change to suit the exercise. Ribs flaring on the overhead extension is a shoulder-mobility flag rather than a bracing one. Cueing harder is the wrong response. Regress the variation. See Overhead cable extension.

5 · Escalate: the elbow

The skull crusher's first instruction is a stopping rule. Flare or pain on the first unloaded reps is a joint-fit verdict, answered by changing the implement rather than the cue. See Skull crusher and JM press.

Elbow pain mid-set is something you ask about rather than see. She regrips repeatedly, or she sets the bar down early. Ask, then Pain rules and when to refer.

A tendon that has become irritable over weeks (tendinopathy) is not screenable from a video, and the answer is a movement swap. If the elbow hurts on skull crushers and not on pushdowns, the variable is the shoulder position. Keep the load and change the position. Pain rules and when to refer holds the order of levers. Terms on this page

shoulder flexion: The arm travelling forward and up in front of the body, all the way to overhead.

long head: The head of a muscle that crosses the shoulder joint as well as the elbow: on the triceps it only stretches with the arm raised, on the biceps only with the arm behind the body.

tendinopathy: A tendon that has become painful and irritable from more load than it has adapted to. It builds over weeks, not in one rep.

pronated grip: Palms facing down or away from her, knuckles up.
