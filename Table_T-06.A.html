The classification table: beginner, intermediate, advanced

Row one sets her level. The rest confirm or contradict it. Read every row before you decide, and when the rows disagree, classify down.

# So her program gets

sub: Linear progression · block progression · phase structure

Beginner: Linear progression, few exercises repeated often, no intensifiers

Intermediate: Block progression with planned variation, set-extending techniques (intensifiers) used sparingly

Advanced: Phase structure, specialisation, deliberate deloads

Where the volume bands come from. For most trainees, roughly 10 sets per muscle per week is where the benefit lands. Trained lifters sit higher, at 12 to 20. Each extra weekly set adds a little more growth, but less than the one before it.

Weekly hard sets per muscle, by training age

Beginner | 10-15 | She grows on almost anything

Intermediate | 15-20 | On the muscles targeted for growth

Advanced | 10-15 | Each set does more work, so she needs fewer

Volume is a curve, not a ladder. Advanced sits back at 10–15 rather than climbing. As she gets better her sets get heavier and closer to true failure, so each one does more work. She needs fewer of them. The intermediate years are the peak. She can push hard enough to earn the volume, but not yet efficiently enough to get the same result from less.

More sets does not always mean more growth. Treat the numbers above as sensible bands rather than a dose you climb.

Terms on this page

RIR: Reps in reserve. How many more reps she could have done when the set stopped. Zero RIR is failure.

intensifiers: Techniques that make a set harder than a plain straight set: drop sets, rest-pause, partials past failure and the like.
