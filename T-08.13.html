Program the least that works, not the most she survives

Find the least work that keeps her progressing and hold it there.

That is the minimum effective dose (minimum effective dose). Some systems program toward the most volume a client can recover from (maximum recoverable volume). We do the opposite. Every set past the least that works costs recovery and returns nothing.

Junk volume is real and it has a definition. Sets that add fatigue without adding stimulus (junk volume). They come from two places:

  • Sets taken too far from failure to count as hard sets.
  • Sets stacked past the point where the session has stopped paying, beyond the six to eight per-session range.

    • A high total assembled from either is not a bigger dose. It is the same dose with more cost attached.

    Why we do not chase the ceiling. Three reasons, and they compound.

  • The gap between the least volume that works and the most she can recover from narrows as she advances. The strategy gets less useful exactly as it gets more dangerous.
  • Living near the ceiling leaves nothing in reserve for a bad week. Every client has bad weeks.
  • A dose set at her limit cannot be progressed. There is nowhere left to go.

    • What this means in practice. When her program is working, the question is never "could she handle more". It is "is any of this not earning its place". That is the opposite instinct to the one most coaches arrive with.

    Watch for

    Reading this as a licence to under-dose. Minimum effective is a real threshold with a real floor. A client sitting below her band is not being efficiently programmed. She is being short-changed. The rule is to find the bottom of what works, not to see how little you can get away with.

    Say it like this

    We're not trying to do the most we can handle. We're trying to find the least that still works. Everything past that is just fatigue you're paying for. When it stops working, we add. Not before.

    Terms on this page

    minimum effective dose: The least amount of training that keeps her progressing. Every set past it costs recovery without adding growth.

    maximum recoverable volume: The most training a client can recover from before performance drops. Some systems program right up to it. We do not.

    junk volume: Sets that add fatigue but no extra growth, because the muscle has already had all the productive work it can use. Either too far from failure to count, or stacked past the point where the session stopped paying.
