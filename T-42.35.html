Guardedness is a quiet you can still hear. Name it early

Guardedness is a quiet you can still hear. Name it early, before it hardens into how she talks to you permanently. This is the usual choice.

One-word answers are a smaller silence. They are not the silence the previous page was written for, but they carry the same signal in a smaller register. Something is being withheld, and you cannot read from the outside whether it is caution or disinterest. The instruction is the same. Say what you have noticed. Ask what is going on.

Naming the wall

I've noticed your answers have got shorter over the last few weeks. Is something not working for you here?

Why early. The timing argument is the conduct floor's, borrowed. Set at the start, a boundary or a check-in is a policy. Left until month four, the same words land as an accusation. Waiting for rapport to arrive on its own before naming the wall runs the same risk in reverse. The longer guardedness goes unnamed, the more it reads as simply how she is, rather than something worth asking about.

What this page does not do. It does not tell you how long is too long, only that waiting is not neutral. Nor does it give you the previous coach's side of the story. That is not yours to reconstruct.

Watch for

"She's just quiet." Maybe. Ask once and find out. Quiet by nature and quiet by caution answer that question differently.
