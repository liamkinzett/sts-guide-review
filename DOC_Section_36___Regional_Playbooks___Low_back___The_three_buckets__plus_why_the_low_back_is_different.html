Low back: the three buckets clinicians think in

This page is a reading aid. It is not a sorting tool for you. It tells you what the people who do diagnose are thinking about. Use it to describe what you see usefully, and to know when you are out of your depth.

What you may and may not do with it. You may name your best guess and label it a guess.

You do not sort her pain into the three buckets: structural, functional or stress-driven (psychosomatic). That is naming the origin of her pain, which is a clinician's diagnosis.

You do not decide whether it is minor or major. That is grading, and a coach never grades.

Two things come before any of this. Anything sudden and recent (acute) refers. Anything still consistent between three days and one week escalates to a referral.

The clinician's question is how much of each bucket is present and where they overlap.

The three buckets

# Structural

sub: Something is damaged

Contents: A disc, a ligament, an actual muscle tear.

# Functional

sub: The movement pattern is the problem

Contents: Muscle spasm, or asymmetrical movement that is not caused by a structural abnormality.

# Psychosomatic

sub: Stress

Contents: Stress driving the pain, and the pain driving the stress.

The third bucket is different in the low back. It is a loop rather than a cause. The stress she is under makes the symptoms worse, and the symptoms create more stress. That loop is far more a low back thing than a shoulder, knee or hip thing.

A worked case. A high-stress period, 70 kg on the bar, crippling pain down both legs, no incident. Nothing went wrong in the gym that day. The loop had been building for weeks. Pain down both legs is a referral on its own, whatever you think is behind it.

This is why you explain the why out loud

Showing her that you understand what provokes her pain and what relieves it is treatment of the third bucket. It is not admin. Coaches internalise the plan and skip the why. Say it.

Terms on this page

psychosomatic: Pain that stress is driving or amplifying. In the low back it runs as a loop: stress worsens the pain and the pain adds to the stress.

acute: Sudden and recent. Something went wrong, fast, in one moment, rather than creeping in over weeks.
