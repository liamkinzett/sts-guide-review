When to use it

The problem you notice. Her heels lift, or her torso folds forward, as she reaches depth in a squat. Or a newer client tells you her knees hurt when she squats. Or she says her calves never grow, and you find them at the end of a brutal leg day. The calf raise is the answer to all three, and each one uses it differently.

Reach for it when

# Her heels lift or her torso collapses as she reaches squat depth

sub: The ankle runs out of bend before the hip runs out of depth

Confirm it: Elevate her heels first. If she can now squat to the floor, it is not the ankle. It is where her weight sits, a balance problem (centre of mass), and the calf raise will not fix it. If the heel lift changes nothing, it is the ankle.

What it gets her: Ankle bend she owns (dorsiflexion), reached before the squat asks for it. Put the seated raise early in the session. It pairs a bent knee with a bent ankle the way a squat does, so it improves ankle mobility before she squats. The barefoot calf raise on a block does the same job when there is no seated machine.

# A newer client's knees hurt when she squats

sub: She has not yet found the positions the squat needs

Confirm it: The pain is with squatting, not at rest. Pain rules and when to refer decides anything more than that.

What it gets her: Better positions later in the same session. Run the seated calf raise as the first exercise, paired with the seated hamstring curl. The raise is not a great driver on its own. It is there so she reaches the positions. The result coaches report is bigger quads on the hack squat that follows.

# She is stuck on the outside edge of her foot

sub: She cannot roll onto the inside of the foot (pronate) during the rep

Confirm it: Watch the foot from the front. The weight stays on the outer edge and the arch never drops.

What it gets her: Foot pressure changed under load rather than stretched. Run the bent-knee calf raise first, then a split-squat hold, then a landmine B-stance RDL, as one sequence.

# She says her calves never grow

sub: They are trained at the end of a brutal leg day

Confirm it: Read the program. Find where the calf work sits.

What it gets her: Growth that is not spent before it starts. Train calves first, two to three times a week, where they are a priority. Training them exhausted at the end of a leg day is the thing that halts growth.

A selection tell you can see in the rep

Her knees travel backward as her heels drop on a standing raise. She is hinging the raise, not lifting through the ankle. Move her to the seated machine. Seated, it is a lot easier to stabilise the knee joint, because you have taken her body out of the equation.

Setup. Knee angle first, then the load for the range you want, then full range every rep. Setup has the list.

Terms on this page

centre of mass: The single point her bodyweight and the load balance around. The further it sits from her feet, the harder she has to work to stay balanced.

dorsiflexion: The ankle bending so the shin and the top of the foot move closer together, as when the toes pull up toward the shin or the shin travels forward over the foot. A squat needs it at the bottom.

pronate: The foot rolling inward so the arch drops and the inside edge takes weight.
