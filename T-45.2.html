Breathing under load · T-45.2

Rep work is a breath in and out on every rep

The switch point is whether the set spans a breath cycle. If it does, she inhales and exhales on every rep. One breath cycle per rep, never one breath per set.

A set of 10 to 15 squats cannot be done on one breath, and she should not want to. Across a set like that she has to breathe in a way that gets oxygen into the working tissue. Otherwise she cannot finish the task.

The rule it produces. Anything that is repetition work, where she is going through a breathing cycle, is a breath in and a breath out across each rep.

This is the re-teaching target and it is one sentence long: one breath cycle per rep.

Notice what the reason is. It is oxygen delivery so she can finish the set. It is not spinal safety. The brace argument belongs to the single heavy rep, and it is a different argument.

Compared

columns: One heavy rep | A set of reps

Breathing: One deep breath, braced and held for the rep | A breath in and a breath out on every rep

Why: Maximum stiffness for one maximal effort | Oxygen to the working muscle so she finishes the set

The risk accepted: Lightheadedness, because it is one rep | None. Holding it across a set is the fault

Say when you re-teach it

Breathe in on the way down and out on the way up. Every rep gets its own breath.
