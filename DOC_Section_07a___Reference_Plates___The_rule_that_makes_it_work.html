The rotation rule, choosing a program, and accessories

Never max on the same max-effort exercise more than two weeks in a row before week 9. Rotating the movement is the whole engine of the conjugate system. It is what lets her go heavy 12 weeks straight without burning out.

Use the conjugate peak for an advanced lifter who stalls on linear. Also for one who genuinely will not run the same thing for four weeks. It also needs the kit: bands, chains and boards. Without them it loses a lot of what makes it work.

Choosing between the three

# Linear

sub: She wants the least friction

Choose it when: It genuinely works well for intermediates, and she will never be confused about what to do.

# Block

sub: There is a fixed test date to work back from

Choose it when: It is the most complete peak. The selection rotation between blocks keeps progress moving.

# Conjugate

sub: She is advanced, recovers well and has bands, chains and boards

Choose it when: She stalls on linear or will not tolerate four weeks of the same thing.

Accessories are the same across all three. The main lift is the program and the accessories support it.

Accessories in a peak

# Two to three accessories per session

sub: Two to three sets of 8-12 each

Do this: Enough to feed the main lift, never enough to compete with it.

# Every muscle through its whole range across the week

sub: Stretched, mid and shortened

Do this: Check the week, not the session, for each muscle's lengthened, mid and shortened range.

# Cut a set from everything in weeks 9 to 12

sub: Accessory volume comes down in the final block of all three programs

Do this: She cannot recover from full accessory work while pulling near-maximal singles.

# Arms come mostly from the compounds

sub: Rows and pulldowns cover biceps. Pressing covers triceps

Do this: Four to six direct arm sets a week is plenty. That figure comes from Volume.

Around the twelve weeks. The phases owns these rules; they are here so the plate is not read alone.

Around the twelve weeks

# Test day format depends on what she wants from it

sub: Three formats

Do this: Powerbuilding: all lifts tested in the final week, spread across the sessions. General population peaking for the enjoyment of it: one lift per session, each fresh. The full experience: all lifts on one day, run like a meet, attempts and commands included. Some women want the day itself as much as the number.

# After the test, deload. Then build again

sub: A maximum costs real fatigue

Do this: The week after a maximum is deliberately easy, then back into hypertrophy. Skip it only when the test came in well under her expected numbers and did not cost what a genuine maximum costs. Record the tested numbers; they are the benchmark her next strength block is measured against.

# Two realisation phases a year

sub: Cap the peaks, not the blocks

Do this: Beginners and intermediates can run strength blocks close together. What is capped is realisation, because that is the part that costs. Three peaks a year throws away eighteen weeks in a state of no adaptation. Counting the deloads either side, each peak costs more like eight to 10 weeks.

# When the calendar will not give you twelve

sub: Eight weeks is the floor and sixteen the ceiling

Do this: Take the missing weeks out of accumulation, then transmutation, never out of realisation. That is where the fatigue comes off. If the calendar will not give you eight, do not compress the block at all. Run a hypertrophy phase and start the peak when there is room.
