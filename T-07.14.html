The phases · T-07.14

Holding phases: shortened range, supported, moderate load

A holding phase uses intelligent, less fatiguing selection. Lean toward the contracted end of the movement (shortened position) and toward supported movements (externally stabilised), at moderate loads.

Mid and lengthened work still appears; Choosing exercises has the detail. No PRs, and nothing that needs a big warm-up to approach.

Key figure

value: 10–15

unit: hard sets per muscle per week

note: 10 to 15 sets per muscle per week, for maintenance only

When you may go lower. It is further than you think. This number keeps her training properly rather than ticking over. It sits far above what maintenance strictly requires. Muscle and strength have been held for 32 weeks on one set per exercise, once a week. One session a week has held a squat max in athletes mid-season, while dropping to once a fortnight cost about 10%. One or two sessions a week have held strength where stopping entirely lost 68%.

So when life genuinely compresses, a holding phase can run well below this figure and still hold. What it cannot do is stop.

Watch for

Reading those floors as targets. They tell you how little can hold tissue. They do not tell you what keeps a young woman building muscle training properly. Treat them as a floor you know exists, not a number to aim at.

Terms on this page

shortened position: The muscle is working while it is bunched short. For the glute that is full hip extension, standing tall or locked out on a thrust.

externally stabilised: The machine, bench or support holds her position so she does not have to. Leg press, hack squat, chest-supported row, cable work.
