When to use it

Reach for trunk flexion when she can hold every position you ask for and the trunk still gives when the load goes up. Her coordination is fine. Her capacity is not. Holds cannot give her capacity; loading the trunk through a range can.

Reach for it when

# She holds every position and the trunk still gives under load

sub: Coordination is fine, capacity is not

Confirm it: Her planks and side planks are solid. Her trunk still folds when the squat or the press gets heavy.

What it gets her: Capacity, which holds (isometric) cannot give her. A hold coordinates the capacity she already has and caps out there. Work that lowers and lifts through a range increases it. If you were trying to get stronger quads, you would not do only isometric training.

# She is deconditioned and you are about to start her on a squat pattern

sub: A heavier squat later, by not squatting first

Confirm it: She has little training behind her and no trunk to speak of.

What it gets her: Rope crunches for six weeks and leg press for six weeks, then the squat pattern. That gets way more weight on the bar 12 weeks down the road. Treat it as a sequence you may borrow rather than a rule.

# She has reached the plank's ceiling

sub: The rung after the hold

Confirm it: She holds thirty seconds to a minute with position intact.

What it gets her: The trunk loaded through range rather than held at one length. A loadable flexion or extension movement is one of the two exits from the isometric. Pick it when capacity, rather than load tolerance, is what is missing.

# It is a push day and rib-cage awareness is the limiter on the first working set

sub: Core work first on an upper-body day

Confirm it: Her ribs lift and her back arches as soon as the press starts.

What it gets her: Her sternum under her own control before the press asks for it. She moves through the rib cage and the upper back (thoracic spine) first, then stands up and presses with a more static rib cage.

# She lives in extension

sub: Ribs up, lower back arched, and no flexion to give back

Confirm it: Standing, her ribs flare and her lower back arches. Ask her to round her back and very little happens.

What it gets her: The two directions back in balance. She cannot extend her spine well if she is constantly in an extended position.

For the client who believes her spine is fragile

Your spine is not going to explode. It is probably good for it to get good at moving under load.

That is a reassurance, not a clearance

The contraindications on Watch for still gate the movement. Spine bones that have lost density (vertebral osteoporosis) and a fixed forward rounding of the upper back (kyphosis) are hard ones.

Dynamic core work costs more to recover from than holds do. Put it in phases where the main lifts are lighter and more technical. That changes where it sits, not whether it goes in.

Terms on this page

thoracic spine: The upper and middle back, the part of the spine the ribs attach to.

isometric: A hold. The muscle works against the load but the joint does not move.

vertebral osteoporosis: The bones of the spine have lost density and can fracture under loads a healthy spine would tolerate.

kyphosis: An exaggerated forward rounding of the upper back. Loaded spinal flexion is contraindicated with it.
