What to do when she presents with elbow pain

The elbow is where torque lands when the joint above it or below it will not rotate. Torque is the turning force on a joint (torque), a twist landing on a hinge. So the first question is never how much she lifted. It is which end of the chain stopped rotating.

Work through it in this order

Test. Four things you can watch. None of them is a diagnosis.

Read. The twenty-three hours, then pain or discomfort, then the two numbers, then the side.

Do. Change the position before the load. Pull the accessories, not the main lift.

Change. Re-check the position before you change load or exercise.

Escalate. A suspected tear, pain with a sign, and any nerve symptom. None of it waits.

The consequence most coaches get backwards. Tendon pain here is positional rather than load-dependent. Reducing her weights is not the first lever. Changing the position is.

Ask what she actually does before you decide which end to look at

Powerlifters suffer from the shoulders down when it comes to elbow pain. Lifters who catch bars overhead suffer from the wrist up: full wrist extension under a caught bar. The population decides where you look first.

Step 1. Test

Four things. None of them is a diagnosis. You may run a screen you can watch, never one that needs your hands on the joint.

The four tests

# The unloaded position test

sub: Can she get into the loaded position with no load on it?

Why it happens: Low bar: shoulder blades back and down, arms rotated out, hands behind her back. Front rack: can she get her elbow inside her wrist with the arm raised in front?

Do this: If she cannot get into a position unloaded, do not load it. That rule is the whole test. The tell for a fail is forearms 30 to 40 degrees off, pointing at her toes.

# The same-muscle, two-positions question

sub: Ask which version hurts, not how much it hurts

Why it happens: "It hurts at the elbow when I do skull crushers." "Does it hurt on rope extensions?" "No." Same elbow extension, same load, different shoulder position.

Do this: That difference is the finding. Keep the version that does not hurt.

# The overhead grip check

sub: A hand that goes weak overhead is reporting the shoulder

Why it happens: Arms at her sides, elbows at 90 degrees, squeeze her fist hard. Then overhead, squeeze again. Everyone is weaker overhead. The shoulder is telling the brain not to load anything beyond an unstable joint.

Do this: Read a big drop as a shoulder finding, not a forearm one.

# The bucket sort

sub: Every movement into helps, no effect, hurts

Do this: The sort is the plan, not a step toward one.

Named anti-pattern: security through obscurity

Going global and esoteric before looking at the joint that hurts. Her elbow hurts. Look at the elbow first.

Step 2. Read

The rules run in this order, and the order matters.

First, the whole history, which for this region means the twenty-three hours she is not training. A desk and a mouse produce claw hands. A movement then demands full elbow and wrist extension overhead. That is the named mechanism for pain on the inside. Then take the training week, because this region's history is a schedule. The worked case is a client with elbow pain squatting every Wednesday, who benched heavy on Tuesdays. Moving the two days apart resolved it.

Second, which of two things is it. Pain or discomfort. Not how bad.

Third, the two numbers that escalate. Seven out of ten reported consistently, or still consistent between three days and one week. Untouched by anything below.

Then the side, which changes the target completely.

SideWhat it usually is
Inside (medial), the commoner one in liftersWrist flexors and the muscles that turn the palm down (pronators) first, then the shoulder. Then ask about her desk
Outside (lateral)Often not the extensors at all. Frequently the brachialis, the deep elbow flexor under the biceps. With a fixed elbow angle and a palm-down wrist the biceps is disadvantaged and the brachialis holds the position. Named culprits: lateral raises, reverse curls, clean pulls
How far pain on the inside of the elbow reaches

The same complaint arrives from the elderly, the sedentary, the software engineer and the youth baseball player. Populations with nothing in common produce it. Your margin for error on a guess here is basically non-existent, which is why the guess stays a guess.

On elbow alignment. Women tend to have a larger natural angle at the elbow, the elbow's version of the wider hip angle. Read it as a distribution, not a rule: plenty of women have a small angle and plenty of men a large one. It is a reason not to call her elbow alignment a fault by eye. It is not a screen.

Terms on this page

torque: The turning force on a joint. The further the load sits from the joint, the more of it there is.

pronators: The muscles on the inside of the forearm that turn the palm down.
