Hamstrings · E-HAM-CURL#Role

What it's for

The hamstring curl is the knee-bending isolation for the back of her thigh. The seated and lying versions are different exercises with different jobs, not substitutes. The difference between them is the hip.

Compared

columns: Seated curl | Lying curl

Hip position: Bent to a right angle | Open, slightly extended

What that does: Pulls the hamstring long before the knee has done anything, because it crosses the hip as well as the knee | Keeps the hamstring short

Where it's hardest: The stretched-to-middle part of the range (lengthened position) | The squeezed end (shortened position)

Its job: The offseason growth choice. It reaches the stretch the lying version misses. | Muscle kept at a lower recovery cost, because it avoids the taxing stretch

So the two are not interchangeable. If the program needs the long position and the seated machine is taken, the lying machine is not the same movement. Choosing exercises owns that decision.

The standing single leg hamstring curl is its own tool, not a one-legged lying curl. You will also hear it called the standing or the kneeling hamstring curl; it is one movement. It is deliberately not very loadable. It belongs with the low-load, short-range work chosen for joint awareness rather than output. It has two uses.

Two uses for the standing single leg curl

# The knee-pain tool

sub: One of the best tools available for pain at the front of the knee

Why: It limits the load. It also forces her to control her lower back and pelvis to produce any hamstring force at all. What it trains is the hamstrings' ability to stop the shin sliding forward under the thigh (anterior tibial translation).

Also: Pain is Pain rules and when to refer. The ladder that uses this movement is in Region by region.

# The gauge

sub: A way to read how far her shin turns out through the outer hamstrings

Why: Alongside the seated curl, it shows you her outward shin rotation (tibial rotation) under load. That is a check, not a prescription.

Terms on this page

lengthened position: The point in the rep where the working muscle is at its longest while it carries the load. It describes the muscle's length, not whether she feels a stretch.

shortened position: The muscle is working while it is bunched short.

anterior tibial translation: The shin bone sliding forward under the thigh bone at the knee. The hamstrings resist it.

tibial rotation: The shin bone turning inward or outward. It happens on the thigh bone as the knee bends and straightens.
