Cueing · T-34.5

Prefer the cue that cannot be faked, then match the focus to the job

Fakeability decides first. Between cues that survive that test, choose the focus by what the set is for. External for force on this rep. Internal for upper-body muscle growth.

The two kinds of focus. An internal cue points her attention at her own body (internal cue): "press through your feet", "squeeze the muscle". An external cue points her attention at the environment or the outcome (external cue): "drive the floor away from your body", "get the weight up".

Compared

columns: Internal | External

Points at: Her body in relation to itself | Her body in relation to its surroundings

Sounds like: "Squeeze the muscle." "Press through your feet." | "Drive the floor away." "Get the weight up."

Use it for: Upper-body muscle growth. Curls, lateral raises, most arm work | Force on this rep. A heavy single, a peaking block, a strength test

Choose by the job, not by preference.

Which focus for which set

# Force on this rep

sub: A heavy single, a peaking block, a strength test

Which focus: External. It gives a small but real edge in the force she can express right now.

Say: "Drive the floor away."

# Upper-body muscle growth

sub: Curls, lateral raises, most arm work

Which focus: Internal. Over 8 weeks, arm growth came out at 12.4% with an internal focus against 6.9% with an external one.

Say: "Squeeze it here."

# Lower-body muscle growth

sub: Squats, hinges, leg work for size

Which focus: No basis to choose. The quads grew the same either way. Pick the cue that cannot be faked.

# Reps on a submaximal set

sub: Grinding out a set of ten

Which focus: A slight lean to external, worth about one extra rep. A lean, not a rule.

This is why "external is better" is too blunt to use. It is true for force production, and it is the opposite of what happened for arm growth. Most of what we program is muscle-building work.

Read the figures as a starting lean that her own response corrects. They are never a target she has failed.

Deviate for the client who reports nothing from one kind of cue. She exists, and she is not a coaching failure. Switch the focus and watch.

Watch for

"Mind-muscle connection" read as a beginner's shortcut. What she calls that connection is motor learning: practising the movement until she recruits it well. Position first; the feeling arrives with it.

Choosing the focus by habit. The words change with the job, not with your preference.

Say on a heavy set, then on her arm work

Drive the floor away.

Squeeze it here.

Terms on this page

internal cue: A cue that points her attention at her own body, like "squeeze your glutes".

external cue: A cue that points her attention at something outside her body, like "press the floor away".
