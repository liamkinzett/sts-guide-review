Variations

STS programs three machine squats: the hack squat, the pendulum squat and the Smith machine squat. They have different resistance profiles (resistance profile), none is superior or inferior, and Setup gives each its own standard. One more version comes up often enough to need a note.

The reverse hack squat. Facing the pad instead of leaning back on it. Do not sell it as a stimulus upgrade. The standard hack squat is chosen because the back pad takes over the whole bracing job. It removes the lower back and balance as bottlenecks. The quads become the only limit, loaded hardest at full knee bend.

Turning around undoes that. With forward knee travel restricted and the torso leaning forward, the work shifts from the knee to the hip. The hip now carries most of the turning force, and the lower-back erectors and the forward-lean shear come back.

Those are exactly what a coach reaches for a machine hack squat to avoid.

Do this. Sometimes hip-dominant loading with the machine holding her position (externally stabilised) is actually the goal. Then use an exercise built for that job: the hip press, or the hinge in Glutes. Do not repurpose a knee-dominant machine against its own design.

Terms on this page

resistance profile: Where in the range an exercise is hardest and where it is easiest. It is set by the line of the load against the joint, so a dumbbell and a cable can load the same muscle very differently. On a machine the cam sets it.

externally stabilised: The machine, bench or support holds her position so she does not have to.
