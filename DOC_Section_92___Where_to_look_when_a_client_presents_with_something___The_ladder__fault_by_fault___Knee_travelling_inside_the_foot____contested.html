Knee travelling inside the foot

Her front knee drifts inside the line of the foot.

Cue it and see whether it changes; a small inward drift on its own is not a fault to correct.

Where it shows, and the fix

# Lunge

sub: Front knee caves

Do this: Cue it and see whether it changes. Knee travel of up to about three centimetres is normal under a heavy load; correct what costs her the rep.

# Split squat

sub: Front knee drifts in

Do this: Cue it and see whether it changes. A small inward drift on its own is not a fault to correct.

# Machine squat

sub: Knees shift inward

Do this: Cue it and see whether it changes. Correct it when it costs her the rep, not because you can see it.

# Barbell squat

sub: Knees cave

Do this: Cue it and see whether it changes. Knee travel of up to about three centimetres is normal in a heavy squat.

There is no set cue written for this fault. Say the fix in plain words, one thing this set.

Other cues on those exercises. Not about this fault. Use them when the same set needs them.

Cues

When: Lunge

Say: Keep three points of contact on your foot

Why: The tripod, held through the whole stride. Try this one first.

When: Lunge

Say: Be mindful of your walking path. Maintain a straight line as much as possible, avoiding lateral deviation of your strides.

Why: Walking version only.

When: Split squat

Say: Big toe, knee, sternum in line.

Why: The primary organising cue. Try it first on every variation.

When: Split squat

Say: Hip outside of knee.

Why: The frontal-plane check. Where this is not happening the movement is not doing its job, however good the rest of it looks.

  • Lunge. Keep the centre of mass balanced over the base of support. Use it as a felt reference rather than as a shape. Where she is chasing balance the load or the variation is wrong rather than the cue.
  • Split squat. Two opposite correctives. Which one you use depends on her error. Too far forward over the toes with the heel lifting gets heel pressure. Too heel-dominant with the toes lifting gets drive through the big toe.
