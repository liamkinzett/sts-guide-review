Volume: what to say

Words for her on volume, by situation.

A script is only right inside its rule's conditions. Read the rule in Volume before you use the words.

For female clients, direct arm work is priority-gated.

Say it like this

Your arms are getting hit hard on every push and pull day already. From a growth perspective they're already covered. If you want them to be a focus, say the word and we'll build it in properly rather than just tacking on curls.

We program toward the minimum effective dose, never toward the maximum she can recover from.

Say it like this

We're not trying to do the most we can handle. We're trying to find the least that still works. Everything past that is just fatigue you're paying for. When it stops working, we add. Not before.

"High reps to cut, low reps to bulk" is a myth. Rep range is set by the implement in both phases. What actually changes is volume and the effort floor.

Say it like this

Toning down and bulking up aren't different rep ranges. They're different volumes and different effort ceilings. The reps you use come from the equipment, not the goal.

Her numbers drop on a diet and she thinks she is getting weaker. Three levers moved on purpose.

Say it like this

Your number's not dropping because you're getting weaker. Your program's running less volume and slightly less effort on purpose because right now the job is holding what you've built, not adding to it. A held number in a cut is the plan working.

She asks how someone smaller out-lifts her.

Say it like this

Strength isn't just muscle size. It's also how efficiently your nervous system uses the muscle you've got. Someone smaller who has trained that skill for longer can out-lift someone bigger who hasn't, on the same amount of tissue or less.
