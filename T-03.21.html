Fatigue and recovery · T-03.21

Tired versus damaged

Tired is acute and resolves fast. Damaged is structural and runs on a slower clock. A trainee can be one, the other, both or neither. Her own report carries the most weight, so ask. It is not the only signal, though. Whether her output actually held up in the set is one. How familiar she already is with the movement is another. So is how much the block has asked of her lately.

Compared

columns: Tired | Damaged

What it is: The fibre's calcium handling is suppressed. The nervous system has run out of extra fibres to recruit | Disruption to contractile and membrane protein. Mostly from unfamiliar or heavy lowering (eccentric) work. Soreness often goes with it, but does not measure it

When it shows: It ends the set, not the day. Usually gone within 24 hours | Soreness a day or two later, not at the end of the set

What it tells you: The muscle ran out today. It will be back tomorrow | Something is rebuilding on its own timeline. It is not a verdict on the session either way

Why this is your call. The two can occur together, separately or not at all. A client can be sore with almost nothing meaningfully damaged, or genuinely fatigued from a familiar movement that leaves no soreness. A coach reading one as a proxy for the other is the failure this page exists to stop.

Say it like this

Tired means your muscle ran out of gas today and it'll be back tomorrow. Sore means the tissue has reacted to the work and the pain has built up afterwards, on its own timeline. It doesn't tell us how much damage there was, and it isn't a sign either way that the session worked.

The page on lactate covers what ends a set. The page on soreness covers why it is not feedback.

Terms on this page

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.
