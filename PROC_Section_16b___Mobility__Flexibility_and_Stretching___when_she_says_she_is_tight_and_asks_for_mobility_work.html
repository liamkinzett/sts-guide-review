When she says she is tight and asks for mobility work

This page is the whole topic in the order you use it when the question arrives. Nothing here is new.

Test: three screens before you answer

Does the tightness block a position the program actually requires, today? That is the only trigger that earns mobility work programmed time. Not a joint that measures short. Not a screen flag.

Is there known vertebral osteoporosis or kyphosis? That is diagnosed thinning of the spinal bone (vertebral osteoporosis) or a fixed forward rounding of the upper back (kyphosis). The client asking for a stretching or yoga routine is exactly the client this ban is written for. Known means diagnosed. If you suspect one, you refer. You never diagnose.

Is she hypermobile? Joints that travel well past the normal range (hypermobility), often with a diagnosis attached. Tightness is not her problem and more range is the wrong lever.

Read. She is already doing her mobility work. It is called training. Full range resistance training matches stretching on measured range and changes the tissue on the way, which stretching does not.

What a few weeks of stretching buys is her nervous system allowing more range (stretch tolerance), not tissue change. It permits more range, and the gain fades when the stimulus stops. Below twenty minutes a week nothing structural changes at all, and almost every client routine sits below it.

And nothing shows stretching reduces injury. Strength does. Say that as "nothing shows it", which is exactly what it is.

Do, in order

Where a needed position is unreachable, gate the variation rather than the client. Pick the version of the pattern her position allows today.

Where every variation of a needed pattern is out of reach, mobility work earns programmed time. Attach it to the lift's prep rather than writing a standalone block.

Where she asks for a routine, write it. The reason is adherence rather than mobility. Screen it against the spine check above first. It goes in her session notes rather than her program, after training or on its own day.

For the hypermobile client, strength through the range she already has. No added mobility work.

Lead with what is working before you name what does not. Opening with the routine's limits reads as a brush-off to a client who asked a reasonable question.

Change. What does not change is most of the answer. Her training does not change. Full range work stays, and it is the mobility work.

The volume count does not change. Stretching is not a set, never enters Volume's count and never substitutes for a session. Pre-session holds stay under sixty seconds, which Warming up owns.

If you later catch yourself defending the routine on flexibility grounds, you have misread why you wrote it. The straight conversation is due the moment the routine starts displacing training.

Terms on this page

stretch tolerance: Her nervous system allowing a joint to travel further before it registers the stretch as pain. The tissue itself has not changed length.

vertebral osteoporosis: The bones of the spine have lost density and can fracture under loads a healthy spine would tolerate.

kyphosis: An exaggerated forward rounding of the upper back. It is usually diagnosed by a clinician.

hypermobility: Joints that move well past the normal range, often because the connective tissue is looser than usual. It is sometimes part of a diagnosed condition.
