No heavy-then-explosive pairing for a performance boost

Do not pair a heavy lift with an explosive movement on the claim that the heavy set boosts the explosive one (post-activation potentiation). We do not build a session on that claim.

Why. The idea circulates widely in strength-and-conditioning content, so it sounds like established sports science. It is a term, not a training tool we use. A superset or preload structure can just as easily reduce performance on the second movement. The fatigue and acid build-up the first one leaves behind do that. And focusing hard on a muscle raises its excitation while cutting the reps she can complete. That is a real trade-off. It is not a performance boost.

Watch for

A client or another coach citing PAP as settled fact because it is repeated everywhere. Widely repeated is not a reason to build her session around it.

Say it like this

There's a theory that a heavy lift primes the next explosive one. I don't build your session around it.

Terms on this page

post-activation potentiation: The claim that a heavy set primes the nervous system so an explosive movement done shortly after performs better. Often shortened to PAP.
