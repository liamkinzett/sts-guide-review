Faults and what you can actually see · T-32.4

The film has to show the angle before you can call a fault absent

Six faults are visible from one camera position and invisible from another. "Nothing showed up" is a finding only when the camera could have shown something. Otherwise the note is that it was not screened.

A coach who always asks for the same angle will report these faults absent. She will believe it, and she will be wrong every time.

Six faults and the angle each one needs

# Leaning away from the working side

sub: Single-arm pulldown, single-arm row

The angle: Invisible from the front. From behind, the bra strap or shirt seam gives it away.

# Shoulder blade tipping forward and rising early

sub: Dip, pushdown, priming work

The angle: Side, not front.

# Cable offset from the elbow

sub: Lengthened-bias curl, pushdown

The angle: Front specifically. You are checking whether the cable runs through the elbow.

# The pelvis turning away in a split stance

sub: Split squat, lunge, half-kneeling work

The angle: Three angles, three different tells. Front shows the back knee drifting out. Side shows the opposite tell. Neither alone is enough.

# Split squat depth loss

sub: Depth loss and compensation arrive together and hide each other

The angle: Filmed specifically to audit depth.

# The hips opening out (abduction) and turning out (external rotation) on the squat descent

sub: This overturns the default habit

The angle: Front. A side-on view gives ankle, knee and hip angles plus spinal position, and nothing else. It is like a flat cartoon, with no depth of field. Of the three hip ranges a squat requires, two are visible only from the front. If you get one angle on a squat, take the front.

Why it works. Restricting a coach to visible faults does no work until something decides what the camera could see. The camera position is half of that. The next page is the other half.

Watch for

The client who films from wherever her phone happens to lean. That is the default. It is nobody's fault. It will quietly produce clean screens for months.

Say when you need a different angle

Same lift next week. Film it from behind for me though. There is something I can only check from that side.

Terms on this page

abduction: A limb travelling out and away from the midline of the body: the arm in a lateral raise, or the thigh moving out to the side. In a squat, the knees travelling out over the toes.

external rotation: Turning a limb outward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face forward or up, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more to the side.
