When she wants chest shape, not a bench number

A physique goal uses this topic's movements in a different order from a strength goal. Nothing here is new doctrine. It is the six chest pages, assembled for the client who wants the shape of her chest to change.

Work through it in this order

First establish that the slot exists at all. STS deprioritises direct chest work, and whether her program carries direct chest slots is Split design's call, not this topic's.

Then establish that the goal is genuinely physique. The comp style bench press exists for a powerlifting-goal client. A physique-goal client has no reason to take it.

Then look at her structure. Assess her rib-cage shape and how her chest expands before you assign a pressing angle.

Assemble the session from the table below. The dumbbell press or a highly braced machine as the primary press. The pec fly after the presses, for the range they miss.

Choose the bench angle for the client and record it numerically, so the exercise is repeatable. Never promise her an angle that grows one region of the chest more than another. Choose, record, move on.

Check the push day for grip redundancy. A palms-forward machine press, a palms-forward overhead press and a palms-forward dip is one shoulder position three times.

Set her up retracted and depressed. Mid-set, the cue is depression, never retraction.

What each movement gives a physique goal

# Dumbbell bench press

sub: The primary chest press for a physique goal

Why: Preferred to the barbell for chest growth: a converging path, free rotation and an unfixed range. The triceps give out later on dumbbells, so more of the set is limited by the chest.

# Machine and cable chest press

sub: The braced press

Why: More stimulus for the fatigue it costs than a free-weight barbell press. It lets her reach genuine failure with minimal risk of the joint breaking down.

# Pec fly

sub: The only chest movement that loads the squeezed end

Why: Presses give nothing at the squeezed end. The fly covers it, after the presses. Which end it loads depends on the machine: pec deck stretched, cable fly squeezed.

# Barbell bench press

sub: The loading ceiling and the fixed path

Why: The heaviest press, and orthopaedically unfriendly for a large population. Optional for a physique goal.

# Push-up and dip

sub: Competence and shoulder function, more than chest shape

Why: The push-up is foundational competence rather than a chest exercise competing with the others. The dip is the press into shoulder hyperextension, treated as a shoulder-function movement as much as a chest one.

Two session rules travel with the pressing choice

A locked-down barbell bench needs overhead pressing or a pulldown later in the same session to restore the shoulder blade's upward turn (upward rotation). Building a session holds the rule.

No heavy lat rowing immediately before pressing. It compromises shoulder-blade stability and drops pressing performance. Building a session holds this one too.
