Building a session · T-12.3

When the joint is the limit, an isolation can go first

For a client strong enough that her joints, not her muscles, limit her training, an isolation can open the session. Opening with a heavy compound spends joint capacity she does not have to spare. This is a trade you make deliberately, for a specific client.

The method. Put an isolation for the target muscle first, on short rest, then the compound (pre-exhaustion). Three sets of leg extensions before squatting. She will squat less weight. She will still take the muscle to failure. She will do it with far less load through the joint.

The trade, stated plainly. You are giving up mechanical tension on the compound, the heavy load through the muscle, to buy joint longevity. That is a bad trade for most clients. It is a good one for the client with enough absolute strength that her knees, hips or shoulders limit her training, not her muscles.

Watch for

Using this because a client finds squatting uncomfortable. Discomfort is not the same as being strong enough to be joint-limited. The answer for the first is usually technique, load or exercise choice rather than a reordered session.

Terms on this page

pre-exhaustion: Tiring one muscle with an isolation exercise first, so that it is the limiter in the compound lift that follows.
