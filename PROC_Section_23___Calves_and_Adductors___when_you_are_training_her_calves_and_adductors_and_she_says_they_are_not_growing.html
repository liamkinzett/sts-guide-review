When she says her calves are not growing

Start with her logbook, not the machine. "My calves won't grow" is almost always a dosing problem. What she will ask you carries the full answer to that complaint. This page tells you what to look at in her calf and adductor training, in order.

Work through it in this order

Read the program. Where does her calf work sit in the session, and how is it loaded? Calves trained at the end of a brutal leg day are the most common reason they stop growing.

Watch a set. Does she reach a fully stretched position at the bottom and a fully squeezed, fully contracted position at the top? One load across the whole range cuts off the end it does not suit.

On the adductor machine, run the two-part test. Can she bring the pads together, and can she control the return? If either half fails, the load is too heavy.

Fix the dose before anything clever. Move calf work earlier in the session where position degrades. Give her two loads rather than two exercises. Set counts come from Volume.

Still stuck after that? The answer is still dosing and position, never fibre type, tendon shape or daily loading. Never reach for a physiological story. Give her the dosing answer.

What this topic adds to the dosing answer.

Do these, in order

# Calves: two loads rather than two exercises

sub: Light enough to reach full shortening some of the time, heavier for the stretched end the rest

Do this: Some sets light enough that she fully shortens and squeezes the calf. Other sets heavier, loading the stretched bottom.

Why: The calf gets much harder to lift through at one end of the range than the other. A single load under-trains whichever end it does not suit.

# Full range, every rep

sub: The one instruction that changes what she does on the machine

Do this: Every rep reaches the full stretch at the bottom and the full squeeze at the top. An inch lost is 25% of her calf range.

# Pick the knee angle against the job

sub: Straight knee and bent knee are different exercises

Do this: Straight or slightly bent for the calf raise that grows the calf. The seated version is picked for a job: ankle stiffness, tibial rotation, or ankle preparation before a squat. Growth is never its job.

Why: Never sell the seated or the standing version to her as a specialisation for one calf muscle. Choose it for the job.

# Adductors: the setup decides the set

sub: Back against the pad, no lower-back arch, start at a light stretch

Do this: Back firmly on the pad. No arch in the lower back. Starting width at a light stretch, never an end-range one.

# No machine: the Copenhagen hold

sub: It needs a partner

Do this: Use the Copenhagen hold when there is no adductor machine. It needs nothing else, but it does need a partner to hold the top leg.

What changes in the program. Move calf work earlier when position degrades across the set. Calves before squats is a named option, for ankle preparation rather than to tire the calf first (pre-exhaustion). Adduction work (hip adduction) stays isolation. It must never push out a loadable compound lift; Building a session owns the order.

Set counts are Volume's and tempo is Tempo and pauses'. Do not invent a quantity here. The calf raise has no pause point and no rep-range preference of its own.

When it is pain, not growth

A calf, Achilles or ankle problem is not a programming fix. Pain rules and when to refer decides the referral. Region by region carries the Ankle and Achilles playbook, including the return-to-impact criterion and the insertional-Achilles referral. Go there. Do not build a calf regression yourself.

A groin or hip problem goes the same way. Region by region carries hip and groin. Never turn the Copenhagen hold into a rehabilitation plan.

When she asks why they will not grow

Your calves aren't the problem. Where they sit in the session and how we load them is. We're moving them earlier and giving them two loads.

Terms on this page

pre-exhaustion: Tiring one muscle with an isolation exercise first, so that it is the limiter in the compound lift that follows.

hip adduction: Bringing the thigh in toward the midline of the body. The inner-thigh muscles do it. The adductor machine loads it.
