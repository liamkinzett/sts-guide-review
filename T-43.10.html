Body image and the referral line · T-43.10

Attribute the thought to the illness, never to her

Attribute the thought to the illness, never to her. Separate the eating disorder from the person. This is a judgement call, and it is the one verbal technique this topic holds for a client already in treatment.

A woman in recovery describes what a clinical day programme taught her family. When she had a fearful thought, her mother would explain that it was the eating disorder talking, and not her.

Separating the thought from her

That's the eating disorder. That's not you.

Read the source of it carefully. It comes from a clinical programme and was delivered by a parent. It is offered to you because it is consistent with not taking control away, and because it is the only technique of its kind here. It is not a licence to treat. Referral still governs.

The mechanism it counters is identity. She was known as the small one. People came to associate her with being small, and that became her identity. Gaining weight threatened who she was to other people, not just how she looked. That is why arguing with the mirror does not work.

What that sounds like from a client

I liked myself better when I was underweight.

Do not argue with that thought. Separate it from her, then move the focus to strength and fuelling rather than to the mirror.

Watch for

Using this instead of referring. It sits alongside treatment, never in place of it.
