Low bar back squat

The bar sits roughly 5 to 6 cm lower than high bar, on the shelf built by the rear delts. It is the fifth rung of the ladder, the most loadable squat, and the tested lift on the strength path.

What it changes. The grip is narrower. She squeezes the shoulder blades together to build a shelf between the rear delts and the spine of the shoulder blade. Ribs down, pelvis underneath, knees and hips start the rep together, lats tight, chin back, elbows forward. At low bar her torso sits where it would in a trap bar deadlift. Keep pushing in that direction and the squat turns into a deadlift.

When to use it. It is the fifth rung of the ladder and the most loadable squat. It is the tested lift on the strength path. Intermediate and advanced clients go straight to high bar or low bar, including in the opening phase where she learns the lifts (priming).

When not to

Her low back hurts on a high bar squat and the cause is forward slide. That slide is shear (shear). Switching to low bar is wrong there, because low bar increases it.

Poor shoulder mobility pushing her hands out to the collars. Try the setup's own accommodation first: hands as close together as her shoulders allow. If the shoulder still will not tolerate the position, step back to high bar, which demands less turning-out of the upper arm (external rotation).

Chasing a lower bar position for a heavier lift. That puts the bar on the bony bump at the base of her neck (C7). Reset it on the trap shelf.

Two faults with a setup fix

# She has to throw her hips back to start the rep

sub: The bar is too low on her back

Do this: Raise the bar until she can start the rep with hips and knees together.

# The bar drifts forward off the midline

sub: A setup fault before it is a cue problem

Do this: Reset her midfoot position and re-tighten the lat brace before touching the cue list.

The elbow. Pain in the low bar rack position gets the unloaded position test. She retracts and depresses the shoulder blades, turns the upper arms out, and puts her hands behind her back. If she cannot get into that position unloaded, do not load it.

Do not stretch it. She reports the position as tight, but it is unstable, and stretching an unstable position is the wrong answer. And do not pull the squat first. Pull back the accessories that make the elbow hurt. If the squat itself has to go, choose a lower-body movement that does not load the elbow rather than a lighter squat.

Elbow pain on the bench that is worst the day after heavy low bar squatting is a shoulder external-rotation deficit torquing the elbow. Move the two sessions apart first, then add external-rotation work.

Tempo. `20X1` as the tested lift. `2110` otherwise, with the pause in the bottom of the squat (the hole).

Terms on this page

priming: The opening phase of a client's program, where she learns the lifts and earns her positions before the load is pushed.

shear: A force that slides one part of a joint sideways across the other. In the lower back it is one vertebra sliding forward on the one below.

external rotation: Turning a limb outward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face forward or up, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more to the side. Racking a low bar squat needs a lot of it.

C7: The seventh neck vertebra, the bony bump at the base of the neck. A bar sits below it, never on it.

the hole: The bottom of the squat, where she turns around and drives up.
