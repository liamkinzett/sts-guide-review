Tempo and pauses · T-15.10

Tempo is part of the context you hold still

Progression lists what has to stay fixed for a logbook number to mean anything. The slot in the session, the rest period, the exercise before it, the phase. Tempo belongs on that list.

Why it works. Standardising tempo is what makes progressive overload measurable at all. When a set gets painful near failure, lifters speed up and shorten the range without deciding to. A counted tempo is what makes a week-on-week load increase mean tissue adaptation rather than momentum.

What it looks like. Her seated row goes from 45kg to 50kg in a fortnight. If the lowering half (eccentric) also went from two seconds to a drop, the number is measuring the drop, not her. Same load, same tempo, more reps is progress. More load at a faster tempo is a different exercise with the same name.

It is the same argument as the rest of the context, with one more variable in it. That variable is the one most likely to move without anybody writing it down.

Terms on this page

tempo: The four-digit code that sets how many seconds each phase of a rep takes: lowering, pause, lifting, pause.

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.
