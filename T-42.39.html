The coaching relationship · T-42.39

Changing a decision when new information arrives

Changing your mind between June and October is not the failure the apology page governs. Say so plainly. Do not apologise for having learned something.

Two different events. A mistake is something you got wrong on the facts you had. A revision is a position that changed because the facts changed. Reaching for the apology script for a revision teaches her that changing your mind is itself the offence.

Use the transparency mechanism instead. State plainly what you knew in June. State plainly what changed it. The admission is what buys the authority, not the excuse for it.

Updating a position

In June I told you X, and that was the right call on what we knew then. Since then Y has changed, so I'm changing the plan. Here's why.

Why it works. A coach who updates on new information reads as more credible, not less. It is the same register as naming the edge of your own knowledge.

Where the June advice was simply wrong. Wrong on the facts available at the time is the apology page after all. Lead with the impact of having followed it, then the fix. The distinction is which of the two actually happened, not which one is more comfortable to claim.

Watch for

Calling a mistake a revision because it is easier. Ask which one it was before you pick the frame.
