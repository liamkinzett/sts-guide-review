Board and block press

The board removes the bottom of the press, and that is the whole point. It deliberately takes out the stretched position and leaves mid-to-shortened. So it loads the squeezed end (shortened position), where every other barbell press gives nothing.

Who. Long-limbed lifters, and lifters who bench in a supportive shirt, whose triceps is what runs out. Triceps strength is overloaded where the lockout actually needs it.

Watch for

She becomes a good board presser and a poor bench presser. The board is a tool for the lockout, not a replacement for the lift.

Where it sits in a peak. The conjugate plate runs it as a max-effort variation in the variety phase, then again as the funnel narrows.

The board in the conjugate plate

Week 1: 3-board press to a 3RM. Thick board, early in the plate.

Week 2: 3-board press to a 1RM. Then it rotates out.

Week 7: 1-board press to a 1RM. Thinner board as the tested lift approaches.

Thicker board early, thinner as the tested lift approaches. That is a placement in a template, not an indication.

Do not reach for a board when

She relaxes on the chest. A technical fault, not a strength fault. Touching the board reinforces the relaxation.

The bar slows a few inches off the chest. That point where it slows (sticking point) is almost always lost upper-back tightness on the chest, and the board reinforces it.

The lockout problem is really an upper-back tightness problem. The strength system rejects a board press for it.

The bottom position is the one being trained. The board removes it. For a competitive powerlifter, the strength system rejects range-cut lifts (partials) used as fixes. They solve a problem they are not causing. The board in the conjugate plate above is a different job, a heavy rotating variation rather than a fix.

Terms on this page

sticking point: The part of a lift where the bar slows or stops, the hardest point of the range for her.

shortened position: The muscle is working while it is bunched short. On a press that is the top, arms locked out.

3RM: The heaviest weight she can lift three times with good form.

1RM: The heaviest weight she can lift once with good form.

partials: A lift done through part of its range rather than all of it, cut short on purpose by a box, a board, blocks or pins.
