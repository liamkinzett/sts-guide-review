Writing program instructions · T-40.3

One cue per movement, the same one you would say out loud

Each movement gets one cue in the notes, and it is the cue you would say to her face. A written note is not exempt from the cap because writing is cheap.

Cueing caps a coach at one cue, at most two, in a session. The same cap applies on paper. Pick the cue with the largest downstream effect and write that one.

And the written cue must pass the same test as a spoken one: try to do it wrong. If she can do what the note says while the target muscle sits out, the note has failed exactly as the spoken cue would have.

This is the usual choice, and there is one reason to depart from it. Some clients need the reasoning before they will execute anything. Where she has asked for the why, give it. That is a deviation with a reason, not a licence to write more.

Why it works. A cue works by giving her one thing to hold in her head during the rep. Four cues give her nothing to hold. The note is read once, at the rack, in the seconds before a set.

Watch for

The note that lists everything you noticed. Cueing's anti-pattern is a coach who names thirteen things. A written note is where that instinct is least resisted.
