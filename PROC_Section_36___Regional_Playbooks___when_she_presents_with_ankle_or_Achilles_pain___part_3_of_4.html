Ankle pain, continued: your working read

Your working read. Name it, label it a guess

This region has a specific bar on top of the usual one. You may name. You may not stage. Calling a tendon reactive rather than degenerative is grading it. So is calling it anything rather than torn. Somebody qualified names the stage.

Verdict

Achilles staging is conventional clinical practice. It is still barred for a coach.

Nothing in this region overrides that. A named guess is a guess, a physio confirms it, and it never delays a referral.

Do not copy the habit of grading a sprain

People describe their own injuries as "a pretty serious grade two sprain". That is a grade. You never say it, of her or of anyone.

What you may say, in this region. You may name a rolled ankle a suspected sprain, and you refer it regardless. You may name long-standing mid-tendon pain a suspected tendinopathy, and a physio confirms it before you load it. You may name pain at the heel attachment as a suspected attachment problem, and you refer it. You may name a sudden pop with an inability to push off as a suspected rupture, and she goes the same day.

What you may not say. Which stage a tendon is at. How bad a sprain is. Whether she needs a scan, or which one. That last is the clinician's call. Your job is to get her to the clinician, so she does not waste a week on the wrong one.

Where a presentation appeared after a layoff or a ramp-up, it is a load-history problem before it is a tissue one. Look at the last block before you look at the joint. The program changed, the recovery changed, life changed.

How a working read is said

My best guess, and it is a guess, not a diagnosis, is a sprain of the ligament on the outside of your ankle. A physio confirms it and tells us how bad. Meanwhile we keep everything else going and work your hips.
