Biceps · E-BIC-LENGTH#Where tension peaks

Where it's hardest

Where it is hardest depends on the version, so this page has three answers. The pause on the preacher is the one that surprises people.

Across the range

title: Face away cable curl: hardest in the stretch

note: Mid-to-lengthened, the arm behind the body and the long head at its longest (lengthened)

from: Arm behind the body, elbow straight

to: Fully curled

mark: 25

label: Hardest

Across the range

title: Preacher curl on our steep pad: hardest in the middle

note: About 46% to 69% of the way up for the EZ bar and single arm dumbbell preacher. The biceps is long at the elbow and short at the shoulder (shortened position)

from: Bottom, slight bend

to: Fully curled

mark: 55

label: Hardest

Across the range

title: Incline dumbbell curl: hardest in the middle

note: Mid-range, forearm at a right angle. The shoulder is extended, so the biceps is long, but the lever peaks at 90 degrees whatever the shoulder is doing

from: Arm hanging behind the body

to: Fully curled

mark: 50

label: Hardest

On the preacher, the pause goes at peak contraction, not the bottom. This is counterintuitive. The bottom of a preacher is the highest-torque, most stretch-loaded position in the whole biceps group (torque). That is exactly why a pause is not placed there. Tempo and pauses owns the pause.

How the preacher's peak was worked out. The load is heaviest where the resistance sits at a right angle to the line of gravity. On a conventional preacher bench at about 45 degrees, that lands at roughly 45 to 50 degrees of elbow flexion, early in the rep.

Our pad is not that bench. The upper arm is near vertical, or at a steep angle around 60 degrees. On that pad the same mechanism moves the peak to the middle of the range. That is also where a cable preacher peaks, and it matches the incline dumbbell curl's mid-range tag.

Do not carry that onto the machine preacher

A machine runs on a cam, and a cam is a property of the machine, not of the movement. Machines built for the same movement can be hardest at opposite ends. The pec deck and the cable fly are the chest example. Read the machine in front of her rather than assuming it matches the free-weight version.

The incline curl's pause is in the pause table. Tempo and pauses lists the incline dumbbell curl at `2110`, with the pause at the end of the lowering, arm behind the body. That is the loaded stretch. Look the movement up there rather than copying a tempo from another curl.

Keep the muscle and the exercise apart. A preacher curl's bottom is the stretched position of that exercise. It is not an anatomically lengthened biceps, because the shoulder is flexed over the pad and that shortens the long head (long head). Lengthened, on this page, always says which of the two it means.

Terms on this page

lengthened: The point in the rep where the working muscle is at its longest while it carries the load. It describes the muscle's length, not whether she feels a stretch.

shortened position: The muscle is working while it is bunched short.

torque: The turning force on a joint. The further the load sits from the joint, the more of it there is. It is the force times its moment arm, so the same force can produce very different torque at different joint angles.

long head: The head of a muscle that crosses the shoulder joint as well as the elbow: on the triceps it only stretches with the arm raised, on the biceps only with the arm behind the body.
