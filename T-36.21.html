A one-sided shrug on the overhead press is compensation, not a cue problem

One shoulder rides up toward her ear on every rep of the overhead press, painlessly. That is a shoulder blade that is not rotating, and a cue alone will not fix it.

Test pain first. If she reports pain, this rule does not apply. The pain rules and the bucket method take over. What follows is for a painless, visible shrug, one side, every rep.

Mechanism. Reaching straight overhead, to 180 degrees, runs on a two-to-one rhythm between arm and shoulder blade. About 120 degrees comes from the upper arm and 60 degrees from the blade.

The muscles that turn the blade upward (upward rotation) are the serratus anterior and the lower traps. When they are weak or switched off, the blade cannot rotate smoothly against the rib cage.

The nervous system over-recruits the upper trap on that side to force the arm up. The shrug is the compensation. It is not the fault itself.

A second mechanism produces the identical shrug. Pressing with the arm flared out to the side compresses the space under the bony roof of the shoulder. The body shrugs to buy the arm some clearance. Tucking the elbows into the plane of the shoulder blade recovers 30 to 40 degrees of comfortable range and removes the impulse to shrug. Both mechanisms can be present in the same client. Check her pressing plane before you assume it is a serratus problem.

Check the chain before you treat the shoulder alone. Core and pelvis, rib cage on pelvis, shoulder blade on rib cage, then the joint. A shrug that survives fixing everything upstream is the one this rule addresses.

The fixes

# Serratus press, prone on the forearms

sub: Push the chest off the floor by pushing the blades apart

Do this: Reactivates the upward rotator that is not firing.

# Overhead cable face pull

sub: Start with the arm turned inward (internal rotation) and the blade slid forward, pull into retraction and depression

Do this: Trains active control of the blade through its full path.

# Dumbbells or cables in the plane of the shoulder blade

sub: Instead of a fixed barbell

Do this: A fixed bar locks both hands and can mask one-sided instability.

# Staggered-stance single-arm row

sub: Builds the anti-rotation and rear-sling stability the shoulder is borrowing from

Do this: Program it alongside the press, not instead of it.

Why pinning the blades back is not the answer. The shoulder blade has no ligament holding it on. Retraction lifts it off the rib cage rather than stabilising it. The shoulder pages above carry that mechanism, and Chest carries the bench press exception.

What we do not give her. No count of reps or weeks for when the correctives should produce a visible change, and no claim about which side it favours. You watch the press and re-test.

Terms on this page

upward rotation: The shoulder blade turning so its outer corner rises and the socket tilts upward. It is what lets the arm travel overhead. A muscle that does it is an upward rotator.

internal rotation: Turning a limb inward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face back or down, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more toward the middle. The muscles that do it are the internal rotators.
