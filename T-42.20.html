The coaching relationship · T-42.20

You can walk back a boundary you set badly

You can walk back a boundary you set badly. Say it was yours to hold. The responsibility is in one sentence, and it is not on her.

Whose fault it is. As a coach, it is also your fault that you are replying. If you set the boundary that you do not reply outside work hours, then replying outside work hours is your breach, not hers.

What she has learned. A client messaging at eleven at night who keeps getting answers at eleven at night has been taught that the boundary is not real. This is the rule for undoing that.

Re-asserting the boundary

I told you that this is how we do things. This is the boundary that we set. This is the way we communicate, and this is the response you get.

What usually happens next. Nine or ten times out of ten she takes ownership of having overstepped. The blowout you were dreading has already been pre-empted, because the terms were hers too.

The structural half. Have a second number. It creates a physical boundary. Create working hours for yourself. Book everything in. A boundary that depends on your willpower at eleven at night is not a boundary. One that depends on which phone is switched on is. The 24 hour window sits inside these working hours.

Why it works. She was not testing you. She was reading you. Every answered late message was a data point saying the window did not exist. The re-assertion replaces that data, and the second number stops you generating more of it.

Watch for

Blaming her for the pattern you taught her. The re-assertion works because it takes ownership. An accusation does the opposite.
