Rib flare on the squat: not a belt

Rib flare's direct answer is the cue "ribs to hips". A belt does not prevent it, and the one accessory is indirect. Treat the accessory as the weakest row in this topic.

Her ribs splay and her lower back arches. Wearing a belt has little or no effect on it: the over-arch happens with or without one. A belt is not a fix for rib flare, or for finishing the rep with a lower-back arch (lumbar finish).

Rib flare on the squat

# The Copenhagen hold

sub: Accessory, and an indirect one

Why: A belt covers the same territory and the same fibre direction as the external oblique, and leaves that muscle out in the cold. The Copenhagen hold trains it. It is the one core exercise to keep for squatting.

Do this: Program it. Core has the page. Do not expect it to remove the flare by itself; the cue does that work.

Exercises: Copenhagen hold

# The belt, if she wears one

sub: Setup

Do this: Do not cinch it maximally. Put it on for the last warm-up set so the sensation is familiar before the working sets. Breathing under load covers when a belt earns its place.

Cues

When: Before the rep, and whenever the ribs lift

Say: Ribs to hips.

Terms on this page

lumbar finish: Finishing a hip movement by arching the lower back instead of extending the hip.
