How to do it

This is the lat-biased standard, for the neutral and palms-facing grips. Steps one to four and seven apply to every grip. The palms-away grip works the upper back through a wider arm path. Its own finish is a head coach's call. Coach the shared steps and send the rest up.

The rep, in order

Head still. Excess head movement means range is being stolen from a joint that is not the working one.

Slow the rep down. Tempo is a correction in its own right here. Stack the rib cage, then remove the excess movement. Tempo and pauses owns the prescription.

Going overhead, tuck the pelvis and keep a tall spine. Do not arch to reach. How long the lat gets depends on where the rib cage sits in space.

Coming down, drive the arm down and back while the shoulder blade stays down (depression). Do not dump the shoulder forward.

Hold the lat bias for the whole set. Shoulder blades down and locked, elbow tucked to the torso, driving toward the hip crease. No hard pinch of the blades at the finish on the lat-biased grips.

Do not pull back too far. Overshooting the lat's useful range and letting the shoulder roll forward are the same fault from opposite directions.

Expect the corrected rep to look smaller. Handle travel drops because the spine has stopped adding travel, while range at the working joint goes up. Warn her before it happens. Otherwise the fix reads as a step backwards.

Single-arm version for the arm coming in toward the body. She sits on the floor turned toward the cable column so the shoulder blade lines up with the pulling path. The point is fitting the path to her own bones.

Say before you correct her

The fixed rep is going to look smaller. That's the point. Your spine has stopped moving, so the lat is doing all of it now.

Terms on this page

depression: The shoulder blade pulled down, away from the ear. The opposite of a shrug.
