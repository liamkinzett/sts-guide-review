Volume · T-08.10

Volume drops in a deficit. Load and effort stay

When she is dieting, her weekly volume drops to ten to fifteen hard sets per muscle. Her load and her effort do not drop.

When she eats less than she burns to lose fat (deficit), the job is holding muscle, not growing it. That figure holds regardless of training age.

The part that catches people. Muscle is surprisingly cheap to hold when she is eating enough. One set per exercise, once a week, above eighty percent of her heaviest single (1RM), has held muscle for thirty-two weeks. A coach who reads that and concludes she can hold tissue on almost nothing during a cut has made a specific error.

That cheap dose only works for a fed lifter. Under-eating for her training and her life is low energy availability (low energy availability). Under it, the minimal dose stops working. The deficit is exactly the context in which the cheap dose is not cheap any more. That is why the retention figure sits at ten to fifteen, not at the far lower floor a fed lifter could hold on.

Why it works. A deficit takes away the recovery that made the minimal dose enough. She needs a real dose to hold the tissue. She also needs enough load and effort to keep telling the muscle it is still needed.

Watch for

Cutting volume and effort together. The phases keep her between three and one reps in reserve (RIR) through a deficit for this reason. Volume is what comes down. The load and the effort protect the tissue. They stay.

Terms on this page

deficit: Eating fewer calories than she burns, so that she loses body fat. Recovery is reduced and the training job becomes holding muscle.

1RM: The heaviest weight she can lift once with good form. Eighty percent of it is a heavy working load.

low energy availability: Not enough food coming in to cover both her training and the rest of what her body needs to run. Growth stalls, recovery suffers and injury risk climbs.

RIR: Reps in reserve. How many more reps she could have done when the set stopped. Zero RIR is failure.
