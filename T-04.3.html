Levers and moment arms · T-04.3

Does muscle length predict where a movement is hard?

Muscle length does not tell you where a movement is hard. Where the load peaks does. That is why exercise pages tag range of motion by load position. Muscle length gets its own line.

A muscle's ability to produce force changes with its length. This is called the length-tension relationship (length-tension relationship). Part of the explanation is mechanical: the contractile structures inside the muscle have to overlap in a way that allows efficient force production. That production is less favourable when a muscle is very shortened or very lengthened. Passive structures also contribute at longer lengths.

You will hear that a muscle is strongest at a middle length and weaker when very long or very short. That is true of a muscle held still at one length. It does not predict the turning force on the joint (torque) through a moving rep.

What showed it. In the wrist extensors, force fell away at long lengths. It looked like a muscle-length effect. Part of it turned out to be a 50% drop in the muscle's own lever at the joint, the internal moment arm (internal moment arm). Read that twice. A curve that looked like muscle length was mostly geometry.

What it means for coaching. Where the load peaks decides tempo, pauses and session order. At the moment you ask a lifter to hold, is the muscle actually loaded? You answer that from the resistance curve, never from muscle length. Do not analyse an exercise by saying only that the muscle is stretched there. That may be part of the picture, but it ignores the leverage side of the problem.

On every exercise page. The range-of-motion line is the load position, where the external load peaks. Where the muscle is longest is a separate line. This topic gives you the mechanics. The exercise pages apply them.

Watch for

A pause placed where the muscle is longest rather than where it is loaded. On some lifts those are the same point. On others the long position carries almost no load, and the pause trains nothing.

Terms on this page

length-tension relationship: How much force a muscle can produce at each length. It produces less when very short or very long, with its best force in the middle of its range.

torque: The turning force on a joint. The further the load sits from the joint, the more of it there is.

internal moment arm: The perpendicular distance between the joint and the muscle or tendon's own line of pull. It belongs to the strength curve, not to the load.
