A holiday week is a deload she did not plan

A holiday week is a deload she did not plan. Use it or ignore it, but decide deliberately, and say which you chose.

The phases owns deload design (deload), and this rule does not compete with it. What this page adds is that an unplanned week off is information. Either it lands where a deload was due, in which case take it as the deload. Or it does not, in which case it is a missed week and nothing is made up.

Her holiday week

? Is it long enough to be a layoff (two consecutive weeks of missed sessions)?

yes: Returning to training. Run the layoff return.

? Does it land where a deload was due?

yes: Take it as the deload, and tell her that is what it is.

= It is a missed week. Nothing to make up. Zoom out to the month.

Why this is your call. Whether the timing is close enough to be useful is a judgement, and you make it out loud. A holiday that lands where her performance had been sliding is close enough. A holiday in a block that was climbing cleanly is a missed week.

Plan for it before it happens. Assessing a new client asks about her upcoming holidays and events for exactly this reason. Knowing the trip is coming lets you decide which of the two it will be before she leaves, rather than salvaging a block after it.

The check-in does not stop. Her holiday flexes the check-in deadline by a day. It does not cancel it, and the flex is not a standing offer. The coaching relationship has the rule.

Before she leaves. Tell her the week is already priced in, as a deload or a missed week, never as a debt. Motivation and adherence has the pre-framing that stops a post-holiday spiral before it starts.

Say it like this

This week off lands right where you needed an easier one, so that's your deload. Enjoy it. We pick the block up unchanged when you're back.

Terms on this page

deload: A planned easier week that lets fatigue clear while the muscle and the skill stay. At STS it is taken on her signals, and an unplanned week off can be one.
