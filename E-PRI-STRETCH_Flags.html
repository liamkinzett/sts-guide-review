Watch for

  • A torn cartilage pad or other knee pathology, with the foot set wide on the pigeon (meniscus). The one contraindication on this page. Pain rules and when to refer owns the triage.
  • Cold tissue. Do not stretch a completely cold muscle. Integrate the stretch into a general warm-up, after she has moved.
  • Two stretches you will see prescribed over the sixty-second line. The palms-up lat stretch (supinated) and the dowel child's pose are commonly prescribed before training at a range whose upper end is over 60 seconds. STS doesn't program either. Capped under the threshold they comply. As written, they do not. Warming up owns the rule.
  • The PNF lat stretch is only PNF if she contracts (PNF). PNF sits outside the sixty-second rule at any duration, and the test is the contraction, not the clock. A lat stretch held with deep breathing and no contraction against resistance is a static stretch. Coach the contraction, or cap the hold.
  • Frog pose, which STS doesn't program. Sinking abruptly into a deep split puts joint structures at risk. Widen slowly and regulate the rocking range with the arms.
  • Calf stretch, which STS doesn't program. Keep the lead knee slightly bent to prevent hyperextension.
  • Who this is for. Weak people need to get strong. Being strong is not better for everybody. A very strong, stiff lifter genuinely does need mobility work. The answer for her is loaded variety rather than remedial drills: one-leg work and freer implements in place of pinned-down ones.
  • Return on the slot. A large slice of a limited weekly training budget spent on hip rotation drills is probably not its best use. Warming up owns the decision to run this work at all.
  • The pigeon and the figure-four work the direction people over-do. Both turn the thigh outward in the hip (external rotation). The commonest hip restriction is the other one, the thigh turning inward (internal rotation), and it gets neglected entirely. Do not build a hip routine out of pigeon work alone. The couch stretch can be performed in rotation, because turning in and then turning out hit completely different tissue. A second version anchors the foot and moves the pelvis around the thigh rather than the thigh in the socket. Hip pain itself is Region by region.
  • Stretching as a remedy is not stretching as prep. A stretch-first, then stability, cluster for a painful hip is remedial work, and Warming up still gates any static hold before a session. When a client asks for a stretching routine, Mobility and stretching owns the answer.
    • Terms on this page

    meniscus: The cartilage pad inside the knee. Twisting the knee under load is what injures it.

    PNF: A stretch where she takes the joint to end range, pushes against resistance for a few seconds, then relaxes into the new range. The contraction is what makes it PNF.

    supinated: Palm turned up or forward, knuckles back. On a lat stretch it turns the shoulder outward and lengthens the lat fully.

    external rotation: Turning a limb outward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face forward or up, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more to the side. It is the direction the pigeon takes the hip.

    internal rotation: Turning a limb inward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face back or down, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more toward the middle. It is the hip range most often short and most often neglected.
