What it's for

Soft tissue work is a short-term sensory tool. It is not a tissue intervention. STS is unusually clear on this, and it must not be softened when you explain it to her.

  • Rolling is a temporary sensory band-aid.
  • It does not break up scar tissue.
  • It does not flush toxins.
  • It does not lengthen the IT band (IT band). The band cannot be stretched. It is connective tissue doing the bidding of three hip muscles. The pain is usually the outer quad sticking underneath it (vastus lateralis).

    • How it works. The pressure stimulates sensors in the tissue (mechanoreceptors), and that turns down her sense of pain. The sensors named for this kind of work are the tendon's sensor (Golgi tendon organ) and the muscle's stretch sensors (muscle spindles).

    The slot. The lowest tier of prep work (prep work). It is step one of three. Passive work restores access, an active drill follows, then full-range loaded patterning owns the range. The warning is that people do step one well and then stop there.

    Terms on this page

    IT band: The long strip of connective tissue down the outside of the thigh, from the hip to below the knee. It is not a muscle, so it cannot be stretched or released.

    vastus lateralis: The outer quad, the big muscle on the outside of the thigh. It sits directly under the IT band.

    mechanoreceptors: Sensors in muscle, tendon and skin that respond to pressure and stretch. Stimulating them changes what the nervous system lets the muscle do.

    Golgi tendon organ: A sensor in the tendon that reads how hard the muscle is pulling. When it fires strongly, the muscle is told to relax.

    muscle spindles: Sensors inside the muscle that report how far and how fast it is stretching.

    prep work: The movements inside the warm-up slot that get the first loaded movement ready. Chosen for the session in front of you.
