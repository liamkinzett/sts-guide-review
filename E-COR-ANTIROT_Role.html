Core · E-COR-ANTIROT#Role

What it's for

Anti-rotation work asks her trunk to resist a twist rather than produce one (transverse plane). The load tries to turn her. She stays square.

What it is for

# Pallof press

sub: Resist a cable's pull while the arms press away

What it does: She stands side-on to a cable and presses the handle straight out from her chest. The cable pulls her toward the stack; her trunk refuses to turn. The lever the cable acts through (moment arm) lengthens as her hands travel further from her chest, so the demand rises with the reach.

# Bird dog

sub: The same quality on the floor

What it does: STS doesn't program the bird dog as its own exercise. It is highly remedial, an entry point rather than a destination, and you use it only to place a client who cannot organise anything yet.

# Length position

sub: Neither drill loads a length

What it does: Both hold the trunk at one length and load the twisting moment. There is no stretched or shortened position to speak of.

# Where it sits

sub: The remedial end of a session, or an early warm-up

What it does: Ongoing program content for the client whose load has outrun her awareness, alongside upper-back rotation and resisting bending, arching and twisting at the lower back.

# The rule that governs the entry

sub: A progression rule, not a technique rule

What it does: Exercise selection is STS's first lever of load management. The ladder on Swaps is the point of this page.

Terms on this page

transverse plane: Twisting movement around the long axis of the body, like a chop, a throw or a rotational lunge.

moment arm: The perpendicular distance between the joint axis and the line of force.
