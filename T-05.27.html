Reading a bone scan result or diagnosis label

Whether a scan result or a diagnosis label puts her inside the vertebral osteoporosis rule is a clinical reading. You ask the clinician who ordered the scan. You do not infer it.

The rule is worded narrowly: known thinning of the bones of the spine (vertebral osteoporosis). Two presentations sit either side of that wording. You are not the one who decides which side they fall on.

Two presentations either side of the line

# Two past stress fractures, current bone scan (DEXA) normal

sub: The scan does not erase the history

Why it is not your call: A years-old event is looked into rather than assumed resolved or assumed still live. Reading "DEXA normal" as "history irrelevant" is exactly that assumption. You do not grade how much a past finding still matters clinically.

Do this: The history stays in her intake and informs what you watch for. It does not answer the bone-rule question either way.

# Lower bone density in the spine (osteopenia), heavy deadlifting requested

sub: Osteopenia is not the diagnosis the rule names

Why it is not your call: Whether the forward-flexion and explosive-twisting restriction reads across to osteopenia is a genuine clinical question. Deciding it "counts" or "doesn't count" is grading a condition, which you never do. The deadlift itself is a hinge, not loaded forward flexion or an explosive twist, so the rule as written may not even apply. That reading still belongs to a clinician.

What you do with either

Take the full history regardless of what the scan or the label says.

Send the specific question to whoever ordered the scan, in these words: does this finding put her inside or outside the loaded-forward-flexion and explosive-twisting restriction?

Train everything else on ordinary terms while that answer is pending.

Why this is a judgement call. The instruction is fixed: ask the clinician, do not infer. What counts as ambiguous enough to ask about is yours to read. When a scan result or a label sits next to, rather than squarely inside, a locked rule's own wording, that gap is a referral question. It is not a training decision.

Verdict

A finding beside the rule's wording is a question for the clinician, not a training decision.

Ask in the rule's own words. Train everything else while you wait.

Say it like this

Your scan and your history are for the person who ordered the scan to read. I'm going to ask them one specific question, and while we wait we'll train everything the answer doesn't touch.

Terms on this page

vertebral osteoporosis: The bones of the spine have lost density and can fracture under loads a healthy spine would tolerate.

DEXA: The scan that measures bone density. It is how osteoporosis and osteopenia are diagnosed.

osteopenia: Bone density below normal but not yet low enough to be called osteoporosis.
