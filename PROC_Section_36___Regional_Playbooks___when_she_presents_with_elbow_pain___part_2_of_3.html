Elbow pain, continued: what to do and your working read

Step 3. Do

Change the position before you change the load. Build mobility and stability toward where you intend to load her, not toward where she is now. Pass where the player is going to be, not where he is.

Do not pull the main lift first

Elbow pain in the low bar squat: do not pull back the squat. Pull back the things that make the elbow hurt. The accessories are the adjustable part. If the squat itself has to go, the alternative is a lower body movement that does not load the elbow, not a lighter squat.

First lever by presentation

# Hurts on one triceps movement and not another

sub: The shoulder position, not the elbow

Do this: Keep loading the version that does not hurt.

# Pain on the inside

sub: Wrist flexors and the palm-down muscles (pronators), then the shoulder

Do this: Then ask about her desk.

# Pain on the outside

sub: Suspect the brachialis

Do this: Look for a static elbow angle under a palm-down grip.

# Pain benching, worst the day after a heavy squat

sub: Read the week

Do this: Move the two sessions apart before you change either one.

# Pain in the low bar or front rack position

sub: The unloaded position test

Do this: If she cannot hold it empty, do not load it.

# Pain appearing overhead first

sub: The early warning

Do this: Treat it as the alarm it is, not as a reason to drop the movement.

# Pain arriving with poor sleep, high stimulant use, no rest day

sub: Not a position fault

Do this: Check the week before you change the technique. This region is a recovery signal too.

# Pain plus numbness, tingling, or a hand that gives way

sub: Straight out

Do this: Refer immediately. There is no elbow nerve read to give.

Use the single arm overhead cable tricep extension as the early-warning system. It puts the shoulder in an unstable position and tests the elbow at full overhead range. Training only at 90 degrees misses 90 degrees of information. If pain starts there, you know what is coming. The load is not the point.

Your working read. Name it, label it a guess

First fork, and it decides everything after it: a sudden event, or an accumulation? Tendon conditions accumulate. An elbow that failed in one identifiable moment is a different case entirely, and it goes out.

Red flags

title: Two that leave the gym today, not after the session

A sudden searing pain in the arm during a lift, most often the palm-up arm on a mixed-grip deadlift. On an alternating grip that biceps is asked to stabilise the shoulder and the elbow at once. A torn biceps at the elbow is a surgical timeline. Refer the same day

An elbow driven into hyperextension by a failed lift: a missed front squat where the elbow went into the knee, or a clean caught wrong. Mechanism is trauma. Refer. Once a ligament tears it is never going to be 100% again

emergency: The two numbers and the red flags are untouched by any of this.

Then the named reads. Say it as a guess and send her to a physio to confirm it.

What you see, what you might guess, what changes on Monday

# Pain on the inside of the elbow, into the wrist flexors. Desk worker, mouse all day

sub: Working read: medial epicondylitis (golfer's elbow)

Why it happens: A day of claw hands on a keyboard, then a movement asking for full elbow and wrist extension overhead. It is not what she did in the gym.

Do this: Put something between the desk and the bar. Bridge the position before you load it.

# Pain on the inside in a lifter who catches bars overhead

sub: Working read: wrist-flexor strain, medial side

Why it happens: The catch, not the pull. Full wrist extension under a bar locked out fast.

Do this: This one is wrist-up. Do not go hunting at the shoulder first. Reduce the catching exposure, and refer for the loading plan, because we do not hold one.

# Pain on the outside of the elbow, worst on pushdowns

sub: Working read: lateral epicondylitis (tennis elbow)

Why it happens: The pushdown is being done without the shoulder blades set.

Do this: Try the shoulder before the weight. Set the lats, shoulders back and down, and re-test the pushdown. The pain often goes on the spot.

# Pain on the outside with a static elbow angle under a palm-down wrist

sub: Working read: brachialis overload, not the extensors

Why it happens: Lateral raises, reverse curls, clean pulls, plate front raises. The biceps is disadvantaged palm-down, so the brachialis holds the position.

Do this: Change the grip, not the load. And buffer it upstream: force that the shoulder did not take lands here.

# Elbow pain on the bench, worst the day after heavy low-bar squatting

sub: Working read: a shoulder external-rotation deficit torquing the elbow

Why it happens: The shoulder cannot turn the upper arm outward (external rotation), so the elbows take the twist. The bench arrives before it has settled.

Do this: Move the two sessions apart first. Then add the external rotation work that gets dropped in a peak. 20 minutes of it is not taxing.

# Elbow pain in the rack position itself, reported by her as "tight shoulders"

sub: Working read: not tight, unstable

Why it happens: The biceps is cranked at its shoulder attachment and the elbows pay for it. What she calls tight is a shoulder she cannot stabilise.

Do this: Do not stretch it. Run the unloaded position test, and if she fails it, do not load it. Then double down on improving the position rather than avoiding it. Where it hurts in the front rack it will hurt in high bar, then in low bar.

# Elbow pain in a lifter whose only shoulder and wrist range is the range her lifts demand

sub: Working read: a range-of-motion deficit, not a tissue problem

Why it happens: If her shoulder, shoulder blade, wrist and elbow only move as far as the bench press asks, she is in a world of hurt.

Do this: Add a position she does not currently own, unloaded. Screening range and training range are different jobs.

# Elbow pain arriving with poor sleep, high stimulant use and no rest day, positions unchanged

sub: Working read: a recovery signal, not a position fault

Why it happens: Elbows come into effect with poor sleep, which is often over-stimulation and under-recovery.

Do this: Check the week before you change the technique. And check the program for heavy spine-loading work, which is the first thing to look at when pain starts settling into elbows and knees.

Two reads that are not diagnoses and are worth saying out loud anyway. The elbow can be the first sign of a shoulder problem. Clients with shoulder pain often had a twinge in the elbow weeks earlier, and used that specific word. Log the word she uses.

And an elbow that stops a bench session may have done her a favour. That could have been the day she tore her pec if the elbow had not hurt. Say it to a client who is frustrated about pulling a session.

Two things are barred

You do not grade it. No "mild", no "grade 2", and for this region no reactive-versus-degenerative call. Somebody qualified names the stage. Tendon problem or attachment problem does not matter to what you do next. Name it if she asks, and do not let naming it change what you do.

You do not put hands on the joint. Every read above is something she reports or something you watch.

How a working read is said

My best guess, and it is a guess, not a diagnosis, is golfer's elbow, the tendon on the inside. A physio confirms it. Meanwhile we change the position that's loading it, not the weight.

Terms on this page

pronators: The muscles on the inside of the forearm that turn the palm down.

external rotation: Turning a limb outward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face forward or up, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more to the side. Racking a low bar squat needs a lot of it.
