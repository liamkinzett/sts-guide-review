What it's for

The standing curl is STS's default mid-range mass builder for the muscles that bend the elbow (elbow flexors). It loads the middle of the range and nothing at the bottom.

Where the load sits. Tension peaks with the forearm at a right angle to the upper arm. That is where the lever the weight acts through on the elbow is longest (moment arm).

Expect no load off the bottom. With the arm hanging, gravity passes straight through the elbow. The turning force at the joint is zero (torque), so the stretched range is effectively unloaded.

That is the defining limitation of this movement, and the reason a separate lengthened slot exists. See Lengthened-position curl.

The grip is the flexor dial. Hammer and neutral grips shift the tension to the outer arm and upper forearm (neutral grip). They bias the brachialis and the brachioradialis (brachialis) (brachioradialis). The brachialis carries thickness and the separation between biceps and triceps. Because those two grips train both muscles, a reverse curl is redundant. A more neutral grip on a standing or preacher curl is the lever for brachioradialis development.

The slot it fills. It is the archetype of single-joint isolation with no torso sway (isolation). It is also one of the few movements comfortable at a low set count. It is lower-skill, single-joint work, with her position fixed by the setup rather than by her trunk. Volume owns the count.

It is always available. A dumbbell curl is low complexity, and the load limits itself. She cannot pick up more than she can control. That is why it can be given to a client at any stage, including one still earning the movements her own trunk has to hold. Choosing exercises explains that ladder.

One curl you will see and STS doesn't program: the drag curl. Coach the dumbbell versions on this page instead.

Terms on this page

elbow flexors: The muscles that bend the elbow, mainly the biceps and the brachialis underneath it. Curls train them directly and pulling trains them indirectly.

moment arm: The perpendicular distance between the joint axis and the line of force.

torque: The turning force on a joint. The further the load sits from the joint, the more of it there is. It is the force times its moment arm, so the same force can produce very different torque at different joint angles.

neutral grip: Palms facing each other, or facing in toward her body. On a curl it is the hammer grip, and it shifts the work to the brachialis and brachioradialis.

brachialis: The muscle under the biceps that bends the elbow. It carries arm thickness and the separation between biceps and triceps.

brachioradialis: The muscle on the thumb side of the upper forearm that bends the elbow. A hammer or neutral grip puts more work on it.

isolation: An exercise that works one muscle across one joint, like a leg extension or a cable curl. Nothing else can take the set over.
