Strength and skill · T-02.8

How much of early strength is nervous system, and how much is muscle?

Never quote a figure for how much of a beginner's early strength was nervous system and how much was muscle. STS quotes none. Neither do you.

A coach may be tempted to turn early progress into a percentage split: "the first gains are 80% neural," for example. That is too precise for what a coach can responsibly teach.

Both contributions are clearly present. The nervous system and skill improve quickly, and muscle can begin adapting from early in training. What is missing is a simple, reliable percentage to hand a client as though it were a universal truth.

The deeper version, if you want it. The split can be measured in a lab. An electrical twitch added on top of someone's hardest effort shows how much force they are not yet switching on (voluntary activation). Electrodes on the skin show how many bundles of fibres, each with its own nerve, they recruit (motor units). A scan shows how much the muscle grew, and a strength gain that outruns the size gain is read as the neural share.

Those tools give a number for one person on one day. They do not give a figure you can hand to a client generally.

Watch for

Anyone quoting a specific split, such as "the first gains are 80% neural". No figure like that stands behind what STS teaches.

Terms on this page

voluntary activation: How much of a muscle's available force can be switched on by trying. The rest is capacity the nervous system has not yet recruited.

motor units: One nerve cell and the bundle of muscle fibres it switches on together. A muscle is made of many of them, from small to large.
