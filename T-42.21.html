The coaching relationship · T-42.21

Dreading her name on your phone is data

Dreading her name on your phone is data. Rule yourself out first, then use the drain test. This is the usual choice, because a single draining session is not the signal. A pattern is.

The first move is aimed at you, not her. Evaluate yourself and make sure you are not the problem. You might be the issue. It might be your attitude as a coach, or that you are not yet very good. That check comes first and it is not optional.

Then the test, which is about the session rather than the person.

Check

When you are done with her sessions, do you feel you accomplished something?

Do you feel good about yourself and about how the session went?

Or is she completely draining, every time?

yes: The relationship is working. The dread is about something else. Find it.

no: A pattern of drain is real data about the fit. Read the page on ending it.

Where it resolves to ending it, the worked case is short. A coach asked a client why she kept coming. She said, because I've paid you money. He said, okay, here's your money back. We're not working. Problem solved.

What this page adds. Ending it is already legitimate and refunding is already the named option. This page adds only the diagnosis.

Taking a holiday yourself. A service does not exist without you, and coaches confess to never taking one. No two-week protocol exists here. What you can do is prepare the handover. Before you go, write down her invisible half: what she avoided, what hurt, what she asked for. Then the cover coach is not reconstructing it by inference. Inheriting a client holds that rule.

Watch for

Diagnosing her after one bad session. Everyone has one. A pattern of dread is the finding.
