The competition squat is the reference point

The low bar back squat is the tested lift. Every other squat here is measured against it.

On the strength path it carries its command in the tempo: `20X1`. Read a tempo as four counts: the way down, the pause at the bottom, the way up, and the pause at the top. X means as fast as she can.

So `20X1` is two seconds down, no pause in the bottom of the squat (the hole), then drive up. Then one second stood still at the top. That second rehearses standing for the command. It is written in from the first week of the strength phase, never added in the final weeks.

At its hardest the tested lift holds one rep in reserve (RIR) and never crosses it. The lighter sets that climb toward the working weight (feeder sets) run one to eight reps on the tested lifts. The reps trend down as the load climbs.

For a competitive powerlifter, frequency is three numbers, not one.

How often the squat is trained

The muscle | As often as her weekly sets require

The movement pattern | Twice a week on the lower body, by any implement

The exact competition lift | Once a week, two to three sets

Two to three sets a week on the main lift is adequate, not low, because the accessories carry the muscle volume. That number counts exposures to the competition lift. It is not a muscle-volume prescription. A coach who reads it as one will under-train her client.

For a general client the back squat is the most loadable squat she owns. Use it when the goal is the most load and strength, including every strength-block squat session. Use it when you want her glutes working alongside her quads in one lift: her hips travel back, so the glutes share the work. And use it only once she already owns the goblet and front squat positions. No barbell on her back before that.

Watch for

Her lower back starts to complain. Move her to the front squat.

Every barbell squat is hardest at the bottom.

The bottom is where the leverage against her is longest, so the twisting load on her hips and knees (torque) is highest. Depth is as deep as she can go while her pelvis stays level, neither tucked nor tipped (neutral pelvis).

Low bar sits naturally shallower. Its hips-back mechanism reaches the end of the inner-thigh muscles' stretch (adductors), and the pelvis rotating limits the descent. Force her past that point and her pelvis tucks under (butt wink).

Hamstrings do not grow from squatting

Never list them as a muscle any squat variation builds.

Never count squat sets toward hamstring volume. Squat variations count as quads.

Terms on this page

the hole: The bottom of the squat, where she turns around and drives up.

torque: The turning force on a joint. The further the load sits from the joint, the more of it there is.

neutral pelvis: The pelvis level and still, neither tucked under nor tipped forward, so the lower back keeps its natural curve.

adductors: The muscles of the inner thigh that pull the leg in toward the midline. They work hard at the bottom of a squat.

RIR: Reps in reserve. How many more reps she could have done when the set stopped.

feeder sets: Light sets done before the working sets to rehearse the movement and find the day's load. They tune the pattern and the load without adding fatigue.

moment arm: The perpendicular distance between the joint axis and the line of force.

butt wink: At the bottom of the squat her pelvis tucks under and her lower back rounds. It usually shows up when she runs out of hip range or loses her brace.
