When to use it

The problem you notice. You need an upper-body standard and she has the competence for it. Or her low traps are the target and you have run out of raises. Or nothing in her program takes the arm behind the body under load. The dip answers each of these, and it is not for everyone.

Reach for it when

# You need an upper-body standard and she has the competence for it

sub: She owns the push-up and her shoulder is quiet

Confirm it: Clean floor push-ups, no shoulder pain, and she is past the novice stage.

What it gets her: A single movement that reads strength and stability at end range, trunk organisation, body awareness and relative strength. A dip and a pull-up are very expressive of shoulder function, and also good at challenging shoulder function.

# Her low traps are the target

sub: You have run out of Y, T and I raises

Confirm it: The raises are done and she needs the same muscle under real load.

What it gets her: Low-trap work under real load. When her arms are behind her, the shoulder blades want to get shruggy. So she is actively pulling the blade down as she goes through shoulder extension. One of the best exercises for low trap function is a dip.

# Nothing else in her program takes the arm behind the body under load

sub: Shoulder hyperextension trained, not just avoided

Confirm it: Read the program. No movement loads the arm behind the torso.

What it gets her: The only chest movement STS programs that goes there.

# She has plateaued at bodyweight and was told dips do not progress

sub: A row that keeps climbing

Confirm it: Bodyweight reps have stalled for a block.

What it gets her: People think dips can't be loaded very well, like it's not a long-term progressive exercise. Dips are an excellent exercise to load up. A kettlebell hung from a belt, or a cable-loaded dip.

It is not for everyone

Not everyone needs to do a dip. As an assessment it could be a terrible idea for a very novice or even intermediate client, or a client who experiences shoulder pain. For her, scale to a horizontal press or a supported dip. Assess the same four things there: end-range strength and stability, trunk organisation, body awareness, relative strength.

Depth is earned, not assumed. Sinking deep without control can damage the front of the shoulder capsule when the lats and rotators are weak. Regress the load before adding range.

What the blade does is on its own page. What the shoulder blade does on a dip covers it. Nothing above depends on it; every row is a reason to select the movement.

Setup. Weight directly under her, whole hand on the bar, spine long, torso still before rep one. Setup has the list.
