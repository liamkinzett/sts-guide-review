Head off the post-travel spiral before she leaves

A predictable post-travel spiral is headed off before it starts. Before she leaves, pre-frame the return week as a planned deload and name the trip as an expected pothole, not a new road.

The move is asking, before she goes, what is happening in the time she is away. Then telling her what the week is.

Before the trip

That week of training is just going to be a deload. Let's get the body moving, and then we're just still back on the path. It's a pothole in the roadmap, and we carry on from there.

Why the pre-frame and not the rescue. Unplanned, the trip leaves her feeling she is always working from a deficit, because nobody accounted for it. Planned, it is a pothole and the same plan continues. Two things carry it. Set the expectation of variability before it happens, so a bad stretch reads as expected rather than as personal failure. Then use trajectory language for the dip. Going the wrong direction a little does not mean changing directions.

Applied to travel, say three things before she goes:

Before she leaves

The scale will move. Say it now, so the number on return is the number you predicted.

The plan resumes unchanged on return. The week away is already priced in as a deload (deload), not a debt to repay.

The trip is a pothole, not a new road. Named in advance, the weight on return does not get relitigated as failure. Sprung on her afterwards, it does.

The coaching relationship holds the three questions to ask before the trip. Training around life owns what a holiday week does to the block, and the client whose every holiday starts the same spiral. This page is the pre-frame meant to stop one starting.

Watch for

A second and third trip with the same spiral. That is a pattern, not a trip. Training around life names where it goes.

Terms on this page

deload: A planned easier week that lets fatigue clear while the muscle and the skill stay. A holiday week can be one if you name it as one in advance.
