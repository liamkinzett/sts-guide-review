Common faults

Seven common faults

# Shruggie

sub: The shoulder blades ride up

Why it happens: The pec is running out and the traps take over. Read this as a range signal as well as a form fault.

Do this: Stop the rep and end the set there.

# Rib flare

sub: The rib cage flares off the pad

Why it happens: The ribs were never stacked before the set, so the arch comes from the lower back.

Do this: Ribs to hips.

# Spine doing the scapula's job

sub: She comes off the pad

Why it happens: The shoulder blades (scapula) cannot hold the position, so the spine arches away from the pad to help.

Do this: Stay on the pad.

# Tension dumped at an end range

sub: Locking the elbows out

Why it happens: She rests on the joint at the top and the pec switches off.

Do this: Stop short of lockout.

# Grip that escapes the demand

sub: Hands working like little typewriters

Why it happens: She is pressing through the heel of the palm with the fingers idle.

Do this: Fix the grip. Squeeze the handles with the whole hand.

# Head as a release valve

sub: Head tipping forward at the bottom, or flaring up

Why it happens: The head moves to release tension the shoulder blades should be holding.

Do this: Head neutral.

# Position degrading across the set

sub: Sliding the hips back in the seat mid-set

Why it happens: She is past the position she can hold and is hunting for leverage.

Do this: End the set.

Terms on this page

scapula: The shoulder blade. The flat bone on the back of the rib cage that the arm hangs from.
