Levers and moment arms · T-04.4

Can moment arms be measured?

A coach reasons about a muscle's own lever rather than measuring it. That lever is the internal moment arm (internal moment arm). Reason from the geometry you can see and say so. Do not dress it as measurement.

Limb and torso length differ from person to person, and that changes how a movement feels for a given client. It is the geometry, not effort, that shifts.

What you can see. The resistance curve of a machine or cable setup. Where the cable pulls from, where gravity pulls a dumbbell, where the cam sits. That is the exercise's external moment arm, and it is far more observable than the muscle's own internal moment arm. It is what you reason from.

Why this is a judgement call. Diagrams of levers stop meaning much once a person moves in three dimensions. Two coaches reasoning well can still land differently. You are reasoning, not measuring. Say so.

Her own levers. Her limb and torso lengths change how a lift looks on her, so good form is not uniform. Judge her against her own structure, and reach for structure as the explanation last, after the tests. Diagnosing from video covers reading it.

Say it like this

I can't measure exactly where your shoulder is working hardest. I can see where the cable is pulling from, and that tells us where the load is heaviest.

The deeper version, if you want it.

You cannot see an internal moment arm inside a joint, and measuring one is hard even in a lab. Measuring from the joint's centre can under-read it once a person moves in three dimensions. Measuring by how far the tendon travels is thrown off by tendon stretch and friction. And the measurements that do exist cover the hip, knee and ankle, not the shoulder.

Terms on this page

internal moment arm: The perpendicular distance between the joint and the muscle or tendon's own line of pull. It belongs to the strength curve, not to the load.
