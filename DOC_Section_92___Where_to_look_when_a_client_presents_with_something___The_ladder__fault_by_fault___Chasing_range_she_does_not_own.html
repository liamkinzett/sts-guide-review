Chasing range she does not own

She goes past the range she can control under load.

Stop where she owns it.

Where it shows, and the fix

# Dumbbell bench press

sub: Exceeding active range at the bottom

Do this: Stop where she owns it

# Dip

sub: Depth past what the shoulder owns

Do this: Limit the depth

# Romanian deadlift

sub: Hips stop, torso keeps folding

Do this: Stop where the hips stop

# Stretch

sub: Chasing discomfort as though intensity were the point

Do this: Back off to a position she can hold

# Barbell squat

sub: The go-lighter-and-squat-deeper depth trap

Do this: Depth is what she owns under load

There is no set cue written for this fault. Say the fix in plain words, one thing this set.

Other cues on those exercises. Not about this fault. Use them when the same set needs them.

Cues

When: Dumbbell bench press

Say: Stop half an inch off your chest.

Why: The first cue to reach for. It is a landmark relative to the implement rather than a feeling. It cannot be faked.

When: Dumbbell bench press

Say: Keep those scaps down. Cue more depression than retraction.

Why: For the client who shrugs at the top or rides the blades up as she fatigues. On the barbell bench press, retraction is a set-up position, not a mid-set cue. If she needs one mid-set it is depression.

When: Dumbbell bench press

Say: Standardize the repetition and control the transition.

Why: For the client whose reps drift as the set goes on.

When: Dumbbell bench press

Say: Top of the head to the ceiling, like you're pulling yourself up with a string.

Why: For the sternum-stuck-down client who cannot find a stacked rib cage lying back.

When: Dip

Say: Shoulders away from ears.

Why: The first cue. It is the one with a documented outcome behind it. It targets delayed scapular elevation, which is the fault both sources agree on.

When: Dip

Say: Don't get too shruggy, don't let the shoulders roll forward, full hand pressure, get your feet kind of underneath you.

Why: A mixed cue, internal then external. A full form reset rather than a mid-set correction.

Terms on this page

scapular: To do with the shoulder blade, the scapula.
