Variations

STS programs the Romanian deadlift six ways: barbell, dumbbell, trap bar, Smith machine, shrug machine and the split-stance dumbbell version (B-stance). The first five are the same movement with the load moved closer to her; Choosing the equipment covers when to move between them. The B-stance is different enough to need its own notes.

The B-stance RDL. Some coaches call it the kickstand RDL. B-stance is the STS name. The load is held in the hand opposite the working leg (contralateral load). The split stance lets her hip shift sideways over the front leg. That shift is what puts the glute into its longest position (lengthened position). It is why this version, and not the two-legged one, fills the lengthened glute slot in her program.

It has two named failures.

  • The back heel drops and the weight shifts back. At that point it has quietly become a two-legged RDL and the lengthened bias is gone. The back foot is a kickstand: toe contact only.
  • She starts already bent at the hip. That cuts the range before the first rep. Stand her tall before every set.
    • Where it sits in the session

    Never open a session with it. The lengthened glute slot is a stretched, paused movement, and a movement like that is never the first thing she does. Warm the hinge up and prime first. See Warming up and Building a session.

    STS doesn't program the single-leg RDL. It trains the same lengthened slot; the B-stance RDL is the version STS uses for it.

    Terms on this page

    B-stance: A split stance where the back foot sits behind on its toe as a kickstand and the front leg does the work.

    contralateral load: The weight is held in the hand opposite the working leg.

    lengthened position: The point in the rep where the working muscle is at its longest while it carries the load. It describes the muscle's length, not whether she feels a stretch.
