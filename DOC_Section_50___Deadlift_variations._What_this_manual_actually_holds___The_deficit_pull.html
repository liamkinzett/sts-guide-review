The deficit pull has three jobs

The deficit pull does three different jobs, and an answer that gives only one of them is incomplete. It is a variation in the first block of a peak. It is the wrong lever for pain off the floor. And it is one back-rehabilitation protocol.

The three jobs

# A variation in the first block of a peaking cycle

sub: Weeks 1 to 4, alongside the pause squat and the tempo bench

What it does: Mostly variations in that block: pause squat, tempo bench, deficit deadlift. Higher reps, plenty of tissue built, low joint stress. It drops out from week 5, when the tested lifts become the main movement. So the stated use is building tissue and work capacity early, while the competition lift is not yet the point. Peaking for a meet has the plate.

# The wrong answer for a deadlift that hurts off the floor

sub: Pull from blocks, then progress down

What it does: Nothing good. A deficit lengthens the range at the exact position that already hurts. Blocks shorten it. Start on blocks and progress down toward the floor. It is the same reason a low-bar squat is wrong for a back problem driven by forces that slide the spine (shear).

# A back-rehabilitation protocol

sub: One coach's protocol, with specific numbers

What it does: 10 sets of 6 on a snatch-grip deficit deadlift for injured backs. A five-second lowering, run at roughly 45 to 60% of her conventional one-rep max (1RM) and climbing across the block. The long range plus the slow lowering forces the load down far enough to reduce spinal compression while she still accumulates volume. Run it with straps. Without them she will not be able to do it effectively.

A deficit pull is the wrong lever for a deadlift that hurts off the floor

Pull from blocks, then progress down. This is the one a coach is most likely to get backwards.

Her deadlift hurts off the floor: pulling from a deficit is wrong for the same reason. Pull from blocks. Then progress down.

Two cautions on the rehabilitation protocol

Its five-second lowering goes past STS's tempo ceiling. Tempo and pauses sets the ceiling, and this protocol is one of the two places a reference program exceeds it.

It is a rehabilitation protocol, not a general prescription. Pain rules and when to refer decides whether any of it is appropriate for a client in pain.

One implement note. A deficit stiff-leg barbell hinge is one way of biasing the adductors on a Romanian deadlift, and Glutes covers it. That is a different movement from a deficit conventional pull. Nothing on this page transfers to it.

Terms on this page

shear: A force that slides one part of a joint sideways across the other. In the lower back it is one vertebra sliding forward on the one below.

1RM: The heaviest weight she can lift once with good form.
