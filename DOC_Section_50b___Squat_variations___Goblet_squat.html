Goblet squat

The goblet squat is the beginner's first loaded squat, the second rung of the ladder. The load sits close to her base of support, so it can be heavier than a counterbalance.

What it changes. Elbows under the wrists. The stance is slightly wider than her counterbalance stance. Ribs tucked. She starts the rep with the knees.

When to use it

# The beginner's ladder

sub: Goblet, then high bar, then low bar

Do this: Each step is earned. The work happens inside the opening phase where she learns the lifts (priming).

# Pass 3 of the butt-wink test

sub: The heels-elevated goblet squat

Do this: Elevating the heels takes the ankle out of the question. Holding the load in front moves the weight forward. If the wink goes, you have learned what it was.

# Teaching the squat as an interleaved set

sub: Three movements in turn

Do this: An unloaded hold in the bottom of the squat, a light leg press, then a heels-elevated goblet squat.

# Her back hurts and the day still needs a squat pattern

sub: The anterior-loaded end of the ladder

Do this: Counterbalance or goblet, heels elevated if needed. The load in front moves her pelvis vertically rather than horizontally, which is what spares the back.

# A cranky knee

sub: The goblet box squat

Do this: It restricts range while still loading the knee, and still trains control of the point her weight balances around (centre of mass).

When not to

She has already shown she can hold position. There is nothing to be gained from a goblet squat for her. Intermediate and advanced clients skip it.

Terms on this page

priming: The opening phase of a client's program, where she learns the lifts and earns her positions before the load is pushed.

butt wink: At the bottom of the squat her pelvis tucks under and her lower back rounds. It usually shows up when she runs out of hip range or loses her brace.

centre of mass: The single point her bodyweight and the load balance around. Where it sits decides how upright she can stay.
