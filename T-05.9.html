A coach's scope in pregnancy and postpartum

STS coaches pregnant and postpartum clients. The line you never cross is an internal examination or a diagnosis.

Exercise in pregnancy is recommended, not merely permitted. You program it, coach it and watch it. What you never do is examine her or name a condition.

What you actually track as it progresses. Comfort, balance, fatigue and nausea. Exercise tolerance and pressure management. And whether a position or movement has become impractical or poorly tolerated. None of that is a fixed trimester rule. It is what her report gives you to work with, session by session.

What a coach does

# Program and coach an uncomplicated pregnancy

sub: No contraindications present

Why: Exercise in pregnancy is recommended, not merely permitted.

# Ask how she feels, session by session

sub: Her report is the instrument

Why: The calendar only tells you what to ask about. What comes out of the program is decided by what she reports.

# Watch her move and count her reps

sub: An external movement screen and reps-to-fatigue tests sit inside a coach's scope

Why: External means you watch her move and count reps. Nothing is palpated, nothing is graded. STS does not run them as formal tests. Her training is the assessment.

# Watch for signs her trunk is not transferring load

sub: Doming, midline bulging, trunk shift or rib flare

Do this: Swap the movement, then refer. Failed load transfer (failed load transfer) is the name for this group of signs. Faults and what you can actually see covers doming on its own.

What doming looks like. A ridge or cone rises down the midline of her abdomen (doming). It appears under load, not at rest. You do not measure it, press on it or grade it. You see it and you change the set.

Verdict

Under no circumstances do you perform an internal pelvic floor examination or attempt to diagnose pelvic organ prolapse.

Grading pelvic floor strength and measuring prolapse belong to a pelvic health physiotherapist. Your version of those tests is one word. Refer.

Who clears what. Medical clearance and the six-week check belong to the obstetrician, GP or midwife. Assessing the pelvic floor and any dropped pelvic organ (pelvic organ prolapse) belongs to a pelvic health physiotherapist. Every postpartum mother should be offered a specialist pelvic health assessment from six weeks. Pain rules and when to refer carries the routing.

The six-week check is not a green light

It is a general medical screen, not a tissue-capacity assessment.

The connective sheet of her abdominal wall sits at roughly half its tensile strength at that point.

Half of new prolapses appear after it.

Terms on this page

failed load transfer: Her trunk cannot hold pressure and position under a load. It shows as doming, midline bulging, a trunk shift or the ribs flaring.

doming: A ridge or cone that rises down the midline of the abdomen under load. It is the main visual tell during and after pregnancy.

pelvic organ prolapse: One or more pelvic organs has dropped from its normal position and presses into the vaginal wall. It is felt as heaviness or dragging.
