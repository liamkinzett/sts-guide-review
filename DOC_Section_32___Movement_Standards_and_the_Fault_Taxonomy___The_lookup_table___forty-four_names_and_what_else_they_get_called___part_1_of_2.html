The lookup table: forty faults and what else they get called

Every fault has one canonical name. Find the phrase you heard in the middle column and write the name from the second. The last column names where the fault is not a fault.

Rows marked "judge in context" are ones where whether it is a fault depends on depth, structure or the movement. The notes after the table say how.

A few aliases use anatomy words. Each one is explained in the terms at the end of the page.

  • The lower back (lumbar) and the upper back (thoracic).
  • The shoulder blade (scapula).
  • Palms turned down (pronated).
  • The pelvis tipped forward (anterior pelvic tilt).
  • A thigh or arm turned outward (external rotation).
  • Knees falling inward (knee valgus).
  • The lowering half of a rep (eccentric).

    Terms on this page

    lumbar: The lower back: the part of the spine between the ribs and the pelvis. When a lift "goes to her lower back", this is where.

    scapula: The shoulder blade. The flat bone on the back of the rib cage that the arm hangs from. Where it sits decides what a press or a pull trains.

    pronated: Palms turned down, or facing away from her, as on a standard barbell grip.

    anterior pelvic tilt: The pelvis tipped forward, so the lower back arches more. In the gym it looks like a big arch with the ribs up.

    external rotation: Turning a limb outward in its socket: the upper arm at the shoulder, so the palm turns to face forward or up, or the thigh at the hip, so the knee points more to the side.

    eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.

    thoracic: The upper and middle back, the part of the spine the ribs attach to. Rounding "over the pad" on a row happens here.

    knee valgus: Her knees fall inwards, inside the line of her toes, as she squats or stands up.
