Volume · T-08.8

More sets is a weaker lever than it looks

More weekly sets does grow more muscle, but each extra set adds only a little. Effort is the bigger lever. A coach who does not know that reaches for volume as the first answer every time.

How small the gain is. Ten or more weekly sets does better than 5 to 9. Past that, each additional set adds a small amount of extra growth, and the amount shrinks as the sets pile up. Trained lifters on 3, 6 or 12 weekly sets end up growing about the same. Past 12 to 20 weekly sets, quads and biceps gain nothing extra. Triceps are the one muscle that keeps responding past 20.

So how we use the bands. The bands in the table are sensible places to sit. They are not a ladder to climb. The distance between 15 and 20 sets is small. The distance between a set stopped at three reps in reserve (RIR) and one taken to zero is large. Effort is the bigger lever. Volume is the one that is easier to count, which is why it gets reached for first.

A worked example. A client sits at 15 weekly glute sets and has stopped progressing. Adding three sets moves her from 15 to 18 and buys very little. Finding out that her sets stop at 4 RIR, and fixing that, changes every one of the 15 she already does.

Why it works. A set only grows muscle in proportion to how hard the muscle had to work in it. Three lazy sets add fatigue and little else. One set taken close to failure does what the volume figures assume every set is doing.

Watch for

Adding sets to a program that is not progressing. Work the stall list, later in this topic, before you do it. Effort is checked first.

Terms on this page

RIR: Reps in reserve. How many more reps she could have done when the set stopped. Zero RIR is failure.
