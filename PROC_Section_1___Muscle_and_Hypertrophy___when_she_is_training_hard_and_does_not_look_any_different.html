When training is hard and nothing looks different

A client is training hard and says she looks no different. This page walks Muscle and growth in the order you use it, inside that conversation. Most of what you do here is talk. The program levers live in Volume, Effort and intensity, Choosing exercises and Tempo and pauses.

Test. Five checks, and each one changes what you say.

Ask these five things first

# Are her lifts climbing while her body sits still?

sub: Open the logbook before you say anything

Why it matters: That is the nervous system improving before the muscle does. Strength and skill explains it. Come back here for the growth conversation.

# Is she in a deficit?

sub: Building muscle while dieting depends on where she started

Why it matters: A new or returning client can grow in a deficit. A trained client often cannot. Never promise it to a trained client.

# How long has she actually been at it?

sub: Growth runs on a timescale of a year, not twelve weeks

Why it matters: Twelve weeks is not the timescale growth happens on. Her expectation, not her program, may be the problem.

# What does "training hard" mean on her light-load work?

sub: On a light load, the big fibres only work close to failure

Why it matters: A light set that stops well short of failure never reached the big fibres. Ask for video. Her reps slowing on the last reps is the audit. A rating of effort is not. Effort and intensity owns that dose.

# Is "not looking different" a comparison with someone else?

sub: The same program does not produce the same result

Why it matters: Her training partner's result says nothing about hers. The coaching relationship covers comparison as a problem in its own right.

Read. A working read, never a verdict.

  • Heavy load buys recruitment outright. Light load only buys it with fatigue. However hard it felt, a light set only reaches the big units (high-threshold motor units) close to failure. A lifter cannot decide to recruit differently.
  • Trained and in a deficit? The missing growth may not be a program problem. Building muscle while losing fat (recomposition) is common in beginners and returning clients. It is not something every body does.
  • Do not read anything off how a session felt. Pump, soreness, activation and the burn (metabolic stress) are the same argument in a different costume. What a session feels like is not an outcome. The building happens in the hours and days after the session, not during it.

    • Do. The conversation, in order.

    Have the conversation in this order

    Give her the twelve-week point, not the pounds. Growth is measured in years, and clients want it in twelve-week cycles. Never present an annual gain figure as a target.

    Tell her the same program does not produce the same result, before she compares. Nothing changes in what you write. What changes is that she heard it at the start, not at week eight watching someone else.

    Do not promise growth in a deficit to a trained client. Say it depends on where she is starting. It is far more likely if she is new or returning.

    Where the effort audit failed on her light-load work, the fix is closeness to failure. Effort and intensity owns that dose.

    Change. What moves in the program is owned elsewhere. Volume in Volume, closeness to failure in Effort and intensity, selection in Choosing exercises, tempo in Tempo and pauses. This topic does not touch it.

    What explicitly does not change

    No fibre-type programming. A program built on converting slow fibres to fast ones rests on nothing. Neither her sets nor her effort move on it.

    No rep-range split for "sarcoplasmic" against "myofibrillar" growth. Higher reps for one and lower for the other is a sentence with no basis. Do not use it.

    No chasing sessions that "produced more growth signal". A protocol justified by a spike in protein building is justified by nothing.

    Hold every number at its real strength. Most growth figures were worked out in men. Treat a number as a starting point for her, not a fact about her.

    Escalate.

    Where it goes

    # The deficit itself: calories, protein, what she eats

    sub: Not a training question

    Where it goes: The Nutrition Manual. How this library works draws the boundary.

    # The comparison has become the problem

    sub: She measures herself against someone else every week

    Where it goes: The coaching relationship.

    # Not a training question at all

    Where it goes: The coaching relationship.

    # Pain anywhere in the picture

    Where it goes: Pain rules and when to refer.

    # Shape questions: bicep peak, filling a gap near the elbow, spot reduction

    sub: Do not answer from general knowledge

    Where it goes: Say only what is certain. Fat loss is systemic and muscle growth is local. Nothing STS teaches supports training the shape of a muscle.

    Three things not to invent

    A measurement protocol. This topic gives you no photo, tape or scale cadence for judging a visible change. Do not invent one to prove a point.

    A date for the visible change. Give her a measurement and a horizon, never a date she will look different.

    Why her training partner grows on the same program while she stalls. Say that people vary enormously on the same program. Do not name a mechanism you cannot support.

    Terms on this page

    high-threshold motor units: The large motor units that drive the biggest, fastest fibres. They switch on last, only when the demand for force is high or the smaller units have tired.

    recomposition: Building muscle and losing fat at the same time, so her body changes shape while her weight barely moves.

    metabolic stress: The build-up of by-products such as lactate, hydrogen ions and phosphate in a working muscle. It is felt as the burn.
