The implement sets the rep range

The tool she is lifting with decides where a rep range can sit. The prescription is always a two or three rep band inside that territory.

Where each implement's rep range sits

Barbell | 1-8 | Fine loading, hard bracing

Pull-up variations | 6-10 | Limited by grip and position

Bent-over row variations | 6-10 | The same limits as the pull-up

Dumbbell | 6-15 | Jumps in large steps as it gets heavy

Cable | 8-20 | Stable path, small increments

Machine | 8-20 | No stabilising demand

Rep range by implement

# Machine

sub: 8 to 20

Written as: 8 to 10, 10 to 12 or 12 to 15

Why: The machine removes the stabilising demand entirely. High reps are tolerable here and miserable on a barbell.

# Cable

sub: 8 to 20

Written as: 8 to 10, 10 to 12 or 12 to 15, the same way as a machine

Why: Stable path, small increments, little to brace against.

# Dumbbell

sub: 6 to 15

Written as: Usually 8 to 10 or 10 to 12

Why: A dumbbell jumps in larger relative steps as the weight climbs. Very low reps there become a coordination test rather than a strength one.

# Barbell

sub: 1 to 8

Written as: A two or three rep band inside it

Why: A barbell can be loaded in small increments and braced hard. That makes low reps productive and safe.

# Pull-up variations

sub: 6 to 10

Written as: A two or three rep band inside it

Why: Limited by grip, position and bracing rather than by the target muscle.

# Bent-over row variations

sub: 6 to 10

Written as: A two or three rep band inside it

Why: The same limits as the pull-up. Pushing lower turns it into a different exercise.

Why it works. It follows how finely the tool can be loaded and how much of her it asks to stabilise. Fine loading and hard bracing make low reps productive. Coarse loading or no stabilising demand pushes the useful range up.

A worked example. Her leg day holds a back squat, a dumbbell split squat and a leg extension. The squat is written 5 to 6. The split squat is written 8 to 10 per leg. The leg extension is written 12 to 15. Three tools, three territories, one two-or-three-rep band in each.

Watch for

Pull-ups and bent-over rows sitting at 6 to 10 rather than with the other barbell work. Both are limited by grip, position and bracing, not by the target muscle. Pushing them lower turns them into a different exercise.
