Finding out whether her sets are as hard as you asked

This page is the whole topic in the order you use it. Nothing here is new.

The first half of the question is short. How hard a set should be is always written in reps in reserve (RIR). The target is just short of failure, where the growth stimulus peaks.

Compounds never go below 1 RIR. True failure belongs only to movements where something other than her own bracing holds her, judged by the stability test rather than the exercise's name.

The per-phase numbers live in The phases. Week one of a block deliberately starts several reps short.

The rest of this page is the second half, which is where the work is.

The five moves, in order

Test. Verify her reps in reserve before you build anything on them.

Read. Turn what you saw into a working read, never a verdict delivered to her.

Do. Fix the load, run a diagnostic, or aim off for a beginner.

Change. Know what holds still while you recalibrate her.

Escalate. Know what this audit does not cover.

Test. Verify before you build. Three methods, in ascending order of how much they tell you. Never ask her whether she trained hard. She will say yes and she will believe it. Every method below works without relying on her answer.

Three ways to check

# The rest test

sub: Sixty seconds, then the same set again

What you do: She stops a set she calls one rep short. She rests sixty seconds and repeats it at the same weight.

What it tells you: If she comfortably repeats it, she was not where she thought she was.

# The video audit

sub: The last two reps against the first two

What you do: Compare the last two reps of a set against the first two of the same set. She already sends footage for the movement screen, so this costs nothing.

What it tells you: Whether the set was close, on target, or past it.

# The logbook over time

sub: Loads against reported effort

What you do: Watch what her reported reps in reserve does while her loads climb.

What it tells you: Either she is calibrating as she gets stronger, or she reports a number she has settled on rather than one she observes.

Read. A working read, never a verdict.

What you see, and what it means

# Last reps the same speed as the first

sub: The set was not close

Read: Speed that does not change means reps in reserve that did not run down.

# Last rep visibly slower while the pattern holds

sub: That is the target

Read: The slowing is the signal of a genuine near-failure set.

# Last rep slower and the pattern changes

sub: She went past it

Read: The set should have ended where form broke.

# She comfortably repeats the set after sixty seconds

sub: She was not where she thought she was

Read: A true one-rep-short set does not repeat comfortably a minute later.

# Loads climb steadily while her reported RIR never moves

sub: Calibrating, or reporting a fixed number

Read: Either she is getting stronger and judging it correctly, or she has settled on a number to write down.

# Twenty hard sets a muscle a week and she feels fine

sub: One of those numbers is wrong

Read: Usually it is the effort.

Facial strain is noise. It reads temperament rather than closeness to failure. A less experienced lifter misses in a known direction. She believes she is closer to failure than she is.

Do.

What to do with the read

The reps and the effort do not land together at her weight. Change the load. It is the output, never the input. Tell her plainly that the weight is hers to adjust and the reps are not. Otherwise she will resolve the mismatch herself and destroy the signal.

Her reported effort and her logbook disagree on an isolation movement. Use an as-many-reps-as-possible set (AMRAP) as the diagnostic. Never on a compound. It happens with a question attached or it does not happen.

She is a beginner. Aim off deliberately. Prescribe below 3 RIR on externally stabilised work and expect her to land around 3. Hold internally stabilised work at 2 to 3 RIR, where the overshoot is not free. She earns the written number by passing the rest test, not by serving time.

Form breaks before the number arrives. The set is over. The standard is the rep she can still perform to the same depth at the same speed, not the rep she can complete.

The audit says she trains comfortably everywhere. Do not add sets. Solving an effort problem with volume makes it worse in both directions.

Change. What holds still while you recalibrate her.

  • The prescription. Rep bands stay two or three reps wide, inside the implement's territory. Every mismatch is fixed at the load, never by widening the band.
  • The phase numbers. They live in The phases. This topic owns nothing that varies by phase.
  • In a strength block, the number that moves is the estimate. Sets at 80% moving like sets at 90% mean the estimated max is too high. The estimate is what you correct.
  • The compound floor does not bend, except for a test. A tested single at the end of a strength phase is not a working set. It is the thing the block was built to express. A test week is not a breach.

    • Escalate. What this audit does not cover.

  • Any pain goes to Pain rules and when to refer. Nothing in this audit covers it.
  • A set she logged as near-failure that was not is also a repeat-set case in Faults and what you can actually see. That topic routes it back to the compound floor here.
  • Percentages have no conversion table from reps in reserve. Check a written percentage by how the sets move, on video and in the logbook. Not by arithmetic.
  • A first estimated max comes from a heavy clean triple. A client who cannot yet safely test one has no verified number to take percentages from. Keep her in reps in reserve until she does.
  • The effort figures were worked out mostly on men. Her logbook outranks any general figure on any individual client.
    • Terms on this page

    RIR: Reps in reserve. How many more reps she could have done when the set stopped. Zero RIR is failure.

    AMRAP: As many reps as possible. She keeps going until she cannot complete another clean rep, so it is a set to failure by definition.
