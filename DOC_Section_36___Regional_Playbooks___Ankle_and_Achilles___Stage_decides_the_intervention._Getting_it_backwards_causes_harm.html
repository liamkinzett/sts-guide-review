Achilles: the tendon continuum and what each stage needs

A tendon moves along a continuum: normal, then reactive, then failing to repair, then degenerative. The common clinical picture is a reactive flare on top of a degenerative tendon. Somebody qualified names the stage. You never do.

StageGoalWhat helpsWhat harms
Reactive, and an acute flare on a degenerative tendonCalm the tissue downUnloading, a change of joint angle, holds against a load (isometric) for local pain reliefHigh-load lowering work (eccentric), plyometrics and aggressive hands-on therapy. They keep the pain signal firing and can speed up the breakdown
Degenerative, long-standing and quietBuild tissue upProgressive heavy loading, then plyometrics to restore the spring

Why loading works without reversing the damage. Degenerated tissue is mechanically silent: it cannot transmit or sense load. Loading builds the healthy aligned tissue around it instead. Treat the doughnut, not the hole.

Watch for

Loading an angry tendon hard because tendons need load. They do, at the other stage. Getting the stage backwards is the harm this page exists to prevent.

Terms on this page

isometric: A hold. The muscle works against the load but the joint does not move.

eccentric: The lowering half of a rep, where the muscle lengthens under load.
