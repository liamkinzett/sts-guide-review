A missing machine is a substitution problem the exercise page owns

Do not redesign the program when the equipment changes. Every exercise page from Glutes to Priming lifts carries a substitution section, and the answer to a missing machine is there.

Know what you will find. All 44 exercise pages carry a substitution ladder (substitution ladder): a ranked list of swaps, best first. Go to the page first. Read down the ladder to the first rung that is both available to her and a movement STS programs. Some rungs name a movement STS does not program, and a few tiers are empty. If no rung fits, the fall-back below is not optional. It is the answer.

The fall-back is Choosing exercises' selection logic. Name the quality the movement was buying. Then pick whatever available movement buys that quality best. Strength and skill says the same thing from the physiology side: specificity is about the adaptation, not the appearance.

Home gyms. The substitution sections name home options for a handful of movements. Where a page names none, run the fall-back: the quality the movement was buying, bought with what she has.

Watch for

Substituting on appearance. A movement that looks like the one she is missing may buy something else entirely.

Rewriting the week for a broken machine. One missing piece of equipment changes one slot, not the split.

Say it like this

Nothing about your program changes this week. Where the gym doesn't have the machine, use the swap I've written in the notes.
