The linear phases, and the block program

In a linear peak the three phases do three jobs: build tissue, build strength, then shed fatigue and show it. Each phase is four weeks and the fourth week of each is a deload.

The three linear phases

# Weeks 1 to 4, volume

sub: Lots of reps at a manageable weight

What she is doing: Building tissue and grooving technique. Nothing should feel scary here.

# Weeks 5 to 8, strength

sub: Fives become threes so the bar can get heavier

What she is doing: This is where the strength actually gets built.

# Weeks 9 to 12, peak

sub: Very few reps, very heavy

What she is doing: She is not building any more. She is teaching her nervous system to handle a near-maximal weight and shedding fatigue so she is fresh on test day.

Use the linear peak for an intermediate. Also for anyone who wants a program she can read once and never think about again. It is the simplest to run, the easiest to track and the most predictable.

The block peak, in one line: three separate blocks that each train a different quality. Size, then strength, then the expression of that strength.

The block peak

Accumulation | 4 | High volume on variations. Deload in week 4

Transmutation | 4 | The tested lifts take over, volume converts to load. Deload in week 8

Realisation | 4 | Near-maximal singles and doubles, then the taper and the test

The three block names are the ones you will hear in every strength conversation: accumulation (accumulation), transmutation (transmutation) and realisation (realisation). The next two pages say what each one does and how exercise selection changes across them.

Terms on this page

accumulation: The first four weeks of a strength phase, where volume is high and the load is moderate.

transmutation: The middle four weeks of a strength phase.

realisation: The last four weeks of a strength phase.
