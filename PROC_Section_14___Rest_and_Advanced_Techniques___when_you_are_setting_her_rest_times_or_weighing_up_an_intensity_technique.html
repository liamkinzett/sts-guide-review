Setting her rest, and weighing up an intensity technique

This page is the whole topic in the order you use it. Nothing here is new. What happens between the sets is prescribed, not preferred.

The five moves, in order

Test. For rest: the movement, the load, the phase. For a technique: what it buys.

Read. Read the rest table by demand. Let the movement pick the tool.

Do. Rest from the table. Attach any technique to the final set only. Run it to spec.

Change. Every technique comes out in a cut. A session that does not fit gets a changed program.

Escalate. Know what belongs elsewhere.

Test. Two questions gate everything.

  • For rest: what is the movement, what is the load, what phase is she in? The rest period is part of the prescription. It is not a preference.
  • For a technique: what does it buy that an ordinary straight set would not? The default is that no technique is in the program. Most of the time the true answer is that another straight set would do.

    • And check the logbook for the client who trains in a hurry. She is not doing your program faster. She is doing a different program.

    Read. Read the rest table by demand rather than by category. A movement that is heavy, loaded through a long lever and held by her own trunk needs longer than a supported machine. That holds whatever either is called. If the rests look too long against the standard advice, the advice is what is wrong. One to two minutes for hypertrophy is too short. Shorter rest does not grow more muscle, and the top of each range is the better end.

    For a technique that passed the test, the movement picks the tool:

    The movement picks the tool

    # Load changed in seconds, pattern holding as she fatigues

    sub: Machines and cables

    Tool: Drop set.

    # Impractical to strip the load quickly

    sub: Plate changes take too long

    Tool: Rest-pause.

    # High local fatigue, near-zero whole-body cost

    sub: Calves, rear delts, side delts, pec deck

    Tool: Myoreps (myoreps).

    # One muscle, one machine, no positional demand

    sub: A variation tool, occasionally

    Tool: Sarcoplasmic stimulating training (SST). Never a giant set, whose fatigue is whole-body and which Building a session bans in a building block.

    Do.

  • Rest from the table. Big compounds 3 to 5 minutes. Loadable movements her own trunk holds (internally stabilised) 3 minutes. Machines, cables and isolation 2 to 3 minutes. Above 85% of her one rep max (1RM), as needed, which is often 5 or more.
  • Attach any technique to the final working set only. Never the first, never all of them. That keeps an intensifier from becoming volume the audit cannot see, and it keeps the working sets comparable week to week.
  • Run it to its spec. Two drops of about 20% with no rest between them. Fifteen seconds then one set to as many reps as she can (AMRAP), one extension not a series. Myoreps recorded as total reps per set, the only number comparable week to week.
  • Supersets run by structure, and only where being continuous is the mechanism. Fifteen seconds inside a same-muscle pair. 30 to 45 seconds for complementary patterns. 2 to 3 minutes before looping. The fifteen seconds is a transition, not rest. The superset is rested. The sets inside it are not.

    • Change.

  • In a fat-loss phase every technique comes out. No drop sets, no rest-pause, no myoreps, no SST. The training she is already doing is what protects her muscle. The page on techniques in a cut has the words to say to her.
  • A session that does not fit her time gets a changed program, never shortened rest. Never a superset either. Building a session owns that problem.
  • What does not change: the working sets. The technique sits after them rather than changing them.
  • How long a technique stays. Ask the entry question again at every phase change: what is it buying that a straight set would not? If you cannot answer, it comes out. A cut takes it out regardless.

    • Terms on this page

    myoreps: After the final full set, short high-effort mini sets performed back to back with minimal rest, on a low-fatigue isolation movement.

    SST: Sarcoplasmic stimulating training. Many sets of the same isolation or machine exercise on very short rest, so the fatigue lands in one muscle.

    internally stabilised: Her own trunk and bracing hold the position under load.

    1RM: The heaviest weight she can lift once with good form.

    AMRAP: As many reps as possible. She keeps going until she cannot complete another clean rep.
