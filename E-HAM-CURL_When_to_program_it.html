When to use it

The problem you notice. Her knees complain when she squats, or she arrives with ten minutes less than the session needs. Or her Romanian deadlift keeps finishing in her lower back. Each of those is a reason to reach for a curl, and each gets her something different. The hamstring curl bends the knee under load with the hip held still, and the machine holds everything else in place.

Reach for it when

# Her knees hurt when she squats

sub: And the day still has to start somewhere that will not provoke it

Confirm it: She reports the pain on squatting movements, and it is a nuisance rather than an injury. Pain itself is Pain rules and when to refer.

What it gets her: The knee bending under load before anything else asks it to. The curl is safe, it trains the tissue, and it calms the knee. The hamstrings working quiets the quads' pull on the joint (reciprocal inhibition) and eases the ache. Superset it with a wall sit or a split squat hold (isometric) and she will feel good going into the rest of the day.

Do this: Start her day with hamstring curls. This is a starting block, not a diagnosis. The ladder for pain at the front of the knee is in Region by region.

# She arrives late, or the session is short

sub: And a separate warm-up is not going to happen

Confirm it: Look at the clock. A laundry list of warm-up drills will not fit.

What it gets her: The warm-up and the first working exercise as one thing. The seated curl takes her through a bent hip and a bent knee. It adds the thigh turning in (hip internal rotation) and the shin turning on the thigh (tibial rotation). Those are the demands of the hack squat and the split squat that follow it.

Do this: Take an extra warm-up set or two on the curl instead of running through the drills.

# Her Romanian deadlift keeps finishing in the lower back or the glutes

sub: The hamstrings never become the limiter

Confirm it: She reports the back or the glutes fatiguing first, set after set, with her position holding.

What it gets her: The hamstrings made the true limiting factor of the hinge. Tired first, they cannot be taken over by the lower back or the glutes.

Do this: Program the seated curl before the Romanian deadlift, so the hamstrings are already tired when the hinge starts (pre-exhaustion). This is an advanced exception, not the default order.

# She is in a deficit or a prep

sub: Recovery, not stimulus, is the thing running out

Confirm it: She is eating below what she burns (deficit), and her recovery is the limit on the week.

What it gets her: Muscle kept at a much lower recovery cost. Both curls preserve muscle equally well. The lying version avoids the taxing stretch at the long end of the range.

Do this: Swap the seated curl for the lying curl for the deficit. Seated is the offseason growth choice.

What STS does not use here. STS doesn't program the Nordic hamstring curl. None of the situations above is a reason to reach for it.

Terms on this page

reciprocal inhibition: When one muscle works hard, the muscle that pulls the other way on the same joint relaxes a little. Hamstrings working quiets the quads' pull on a sore knee.

isometric: A hold. The muscle works against the load but the joint does not move.

tibial rotation: The shin bone turning inward or outward. It happens on the thigh bone as the knee bends and straightens.

pre-exhaustion: Tiring one muscle with an isolation exercise first, so that it is the limiter in the compound lift that follows.

deficit: Eating fewer calories than she burns, so that she loses body fat.

hip internal rotation: The thigh turning inward at the hip. The seated curl asks for a little of it, and so do the hack squat and the split squat that follow.
